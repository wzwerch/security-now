# Cellebrite

# Summary

Cellebrite supplied unreleased software to the FBI to open the Samsung phone of Trump's would-be assassin, while leaked matrices show it could not unlock iPhones on iOS 17.4 or later.

Mentioned as certain to exploit checkm8 quickly for phone extraction.

NIST tested JTAG and chip-off extraction with Cellebrite Physical Analyzer and found deleted data recoverable from physically damaged Android phones.

Cellebrite is developing a roadside tool to display a driver's recent phone activity after a crash, under a New York proposal; Steve doubts phones will support it. In SN 613 he also explains how forensic tools evade retry limits by restoring nonvolatile snapshots.

Forbes reported that Cellebrite's mail-in service could break iPhones from the 5 to the X; details remain secret.

Invoices showed large payments to the firm, though the FBI would not name the vendor; the FBI director said the tool works on a narrow set of older phones.

SN 595 reports a 900 GB leak of customer and product data; SN 598 notes leaked tools appeared to be repackaged jailbreak code.
