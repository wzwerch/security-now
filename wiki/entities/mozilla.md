# Mozilla / Firefox

# Summary

Episodes track Firefox 135 (CRLite rollout, PDF editing, AI sidebar), Firefox 137 tab groups and the address-bar calculator; Steve uses Firefox with uBlock Origin and Privacy Badger.

Supports XSLT removal, launched paid Firefox support for organizations, collaborated with Anthropic and opposes Chrome's Prompt API.

Mozilla distrusted TrustCor's roots in December 2022, followed by Microsoft, Chrome and Android. Steve also moved to Firefox as main browser.

Cited for the Cure53-audited Mastodon flaw, Firefox features, and the 2023 report finding every reviewed car brand fails on privacy.

Mozilla wrote its own open letter against Article 45 with Cloudflare, ISRG and others.

Mozilla developed CRLite (cascading Bloom filters), keeps supporting Manifest V2 in Firefox, patched an exploited animation-timeline zero-day in 25 hours, and drew criticism for its add-on review process which led uBlock Origin Lite to leave Firefox.

Mozilla's policy debates in 2019 covered DarkMatter, Australia's TOLA law, Windows root store use, letterboxing, and a banned-obfuscation rule for add-ons.

Mozilla shipped Firefox 67 with tracker, cryptominer and fingerprinter blocking, defended DNS-over-HTTPS against UK ISP criticism, distrusted DarkMatter's certificates and explored a Tor mode add-on.

Led the staged DoH rollout via Cloudflare, rebutted Comcast's lobbying, announced Firefox Private Network and OpenPGP in Thunderbird, and hardened internal about: pages.

Mozilla appeared for the Firefox Private Relay email alias service, reversing TLS 1.0/1.1 removal for COVID-19 sites and the DoH rollout that overloaded providers.

Firefox Quantum performance work and Firefox Send; trust policy actions against WoSign.

Mozilla proposed requiring secure contexts for new web features and, with Cloudflare, piloted encrypted DNS in Firefox.

Mozilla added DoH support and tracking-protection options.

Mozilla tracked sites still using Symantec-chained certificates and announced default tracker blocking and cross-site cookie stripping for Firefox, plus the joint TLS 1.0/1.1 deprecation.

Mozilla followed Google on CNNIC, added opportunistic encryption, planned to deprecate plain HTTP, disabled RC4, shipped tracking protection and joined the WebAssembly effort.

Episodes covered Firefox 42 private-browsing tracking protection, the end of its advertising Tiles experiment, the Focus content blocker for iOS and SHA-1 deprecation plans.

Sponsored new-tab tiles and libpkix work.

Decided in 2014 to support DRM video through an Adobe plugin in a sandbox.

Lost about 5 percent of downloads when mozilla.org switched to SHA-256 certificates, and co-founded Let's Encrypt.
