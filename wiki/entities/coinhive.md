# Coinhive

# Summary

Coinhive offered a JavaScript miner for monetising site visitors, but was widely abused through compromised sites and routers. It closed after the Monero hash rate and price fell; Malwarebytes still saw tens of thousands of daily requests to its domains later.

Used by sites and by attackers; its DNS was hijacked via a reused Kickstarter password, and it kept 30% of proceeds.

Coinhive, Crypto-Loot and JSECoin dominated Check Point's malware rankings in early 2018. Coinhive created AuthedMine with enforced user consent and asked blockers not to block it, while Steve argued JavaScript mining yields little.

Its mining-based link shortener was planted into compromised sites via hidden iframes.

Coinhive scripts were injected into pages by compromised MikroTik routers and sites. A USENIX study found it produced about 1.18 percent of mined Monero blocks, worth roughly a quarter million dollars per month to the service.
