# SpinRite

# Summary

SpinRite 6.1 is used for pre-deployment testing of drives, rewriting SSDs to restore read performance (Level 3) and recovering marginal data. Episodes cite a bad new 8TB drive, a laptop cured of blue screens, a Kindle revived and SSD charge-leak explanations.

Listener results include restoring SSD read speed (HD Tune before/after) and reviving drives from a TrueNAS system. SpinRite 6.1 handles drives up to 144,000 TB and works on raw encrypted data.

SpinRite 6.1 received positive reviews (Dave's Garage, AskWoody); a Level 3 scan made a Samsung 850 EVO SSD uniformly fast again and revived a dead Kingston portable SSD. Steve moved GRC servers back to spinning disks after SSD failures.

SpinRite comes up in listener feedback: it treats encrypted drives as opaque blocks, flash devices need exposing as a USB drive and Level 3, and a Level 3 refresh is suggested before archiving SSDs. SpinRite 7 is planned as a Windows app. BYTE's 1988 review helped establish the product.

Mentioned via recovery stories (RAID 0 NAS, drives in Afghanistan, ISS), SSD Level 3 rewrite advice and the awaited SpinRite 7.0.

Episodes track 6.1 alpha testing: USB BIOS bugs that corrupt memory above 137 GB, defensive coding against misbehaving BIOSes and a linear-congruential noise trick for finding failure points. Steve plans native USB support in SpinRite 7.

GRC's DOS-based drive recovery and maintenance tool. During these episodes SpinRite 6.1 was in alpha testing with several hundred testers; core recovery code was rewritten, a division overflow bug in 6.0 was patched, and results on SSDs and thumb drives showed large speedups. SpinRite 7 is planned to move beyond DOS and BIOS.

Episodes 929-941 follow SpinRite 6.1 testing, RTOS-32 plans for version 7 and how its confidence tests exposed fake flash drives.

SpinRite is GRC's commercial drive recovery and maintenance tool. Episodes 943 to 957 follow the end of SpinRite 6.1 development: pre-release testing, SSD read-speed recovery, on-the-fly EV code signing, troubled-drive timeouts, USB BIOS workarounds and the plan for SpinRite 7 with native USB.

Version 6.1 was declared finished and became GRC's sold version in April 2024. Episodes cover Linux/Mac boot-image tooling, a built-in RAM test prompted by a faulty ZimaBoard, and the finding that rewriting old SSDs restores read speed by resetting drifted cell voltages. Windows Defender false positives and Microsoft's EV certificate change complicated distribution.

Mentioned for its per-download code signing and listener reports of SSD speed recovery.

SpinRite 6.1 rollout, SSD slowdown/refresh stories, and benchmark quirks from never-written regions recur; Steve notes reinstalling an OS also rewrites an SSD.

Steve reports on 64-bit sector addressing, device discovery, a debugging war story on an AMD Phenom II board, and SSD behaviour after Level 3 passes.

During 2021 Steve rewrote SpinRite as 6.1 with a new I/O abstraction layer and benchmarks, testing pre-releases with the GRC newsgroup community.

Weekly updates describe a new BIOS-bypassing hardware layer, hunts for old-chipset quirks (ASUS/AMD DMA, PIIX4, ASRock AHCI), RAID-controller detection and a mini benchmark.

These episodes track SpinRite 6.1 development: pre-release builds, the native hardware drivers that bypass the BIOS, a built-in speed benchmark, and Intel Mac support.

GRC's flagship product. During mid-2022 Steve rebuilt it for 6.1 around a device-independent storage abstraction with no BIOS dependence, got it running again in July 2022, and described splitting code into a second 64 KB segment and plans for a later 32-bit rewrite for UEFI and NVMe. A listener reported recovering PLC floppies with SpinRite 5.

This batch follows SpinRite 6.1 from driver testing to the first alpha release on 25 Nov 2022.

In 2019 Steve outlined 6.1 (fast direct AHCI), 6.2 (USB) and pre-release access for owners, with free upgrades for all v6 owners.

Listener success stories and 6.1 speed expectations (about three hours for 2 TB at Level 2) appear across these episodes.

After finishing SQRL in December 2019 Steve returned to SpinRite, rebuilding his DOS toolchain, planning native AHCI support first, then USB, NVMe and UEFI.

Steve reported progress on native AHCI drivers, a SpinTest utility and benchmark, a from-scratch USB boot preparation system and plans for a UEFI-bootable SpinRite 7.

Episodes track SpinRite 6.1 development: bare-metal AHCI/IDE drivers, benchmark findings on SSD behaviour and the read-verify command, and UEFI plans.

Each episode includes SpinRite testimonials. Steve explains how it reports cabling CRC errors, SMART data and ECC counts, notes USB drives must be attached at boot, and plans SpinRite 6.1 for existing owners after SQRL.

Recurring listener stories involve data recovery, speeding up failing SSDs and drives, and RAID recovery. Roadmap: 6.1 with direct hardware access and large buffers, later USB support and a version 7 rewrite after SQRL.

Weekly testimonials in these episodes describe recovering NAS drives, SD cards, phones and SSDs; the 6.x roadmap covers AHCI support first, then USB hardware support.

Each episode closes with listener recovery stories (a dentist's X-ray machine, a novelist's manuscript, a Korg synthesizer SSD). Steve explains Levels 1, 2 and 4, plans a faster SpinRite 6.1 and a Python UI for SpinRite 7, and notes he returns to it after SQRL.

Listener stories and Steve's explanations about error correction, SSD charge decay, SMART and spare sectors recur; SpinRite 6.1 is planned at roughly half a terabyte per hour and a separate wiping product, Beyond Recall, is announced.

SpinRite is GRC's drive maintenance and recovery product, which Steve promotes in every episode and which funds his work. In 2015 listeners shared many recovery stories, Steve explained its green R recovered-sector display, and he described plans for version 6.1 with direct AHCI access, later 6.x releases for USB and NVMe, and a rewrite as version 7.

Recurring product; episodes include testimonials, an explanation of partial sector recovery and ECC readouts, how it is tested with a thrasher, and corporate licensing.

Most episodes include listener recovery stories (TiVo, Dish DVR, laptops, SSDs). Steve discussed version 6.1 performance (about two hours per terabyte), SSD awareness and the tip to check the SMART screen after repairs.

Listener stories show recovery of failing drives. Steve explained that drives read ahead, that SpinRite works on SSDs at Level 2 because writes fatigue flash, that SpinRite 6.1 will bypass buggy SATA BIOS code and also handle hybrid drives, and that all owners will get 6.1 free.

SpinRite is GRC's commercial drive recovery and maintenance tool, discussed in testimonials nearly every week. In these episodes Steve explains SMART readouts, error-rate baselines, drive orientation, interface CRC (cabling) error counting, how it exercises drives to cure RAID timeouts, and why CHKDSK can destroy data SpinRite might have saved.

Episodes 400-413 carry listener recoveries (SSDs, a four-week laptop run, Macs) and Steve's plan for a free SpinRite 6.1 that boots on Intel Macs, bypasses the BIOS with its own AHCI/IDE drivers and extended memory manager, and uses 32 MB transfer buffers.

Listener recoveries, use inside VirtualBox, SSD error findings, 6.1 plans (BIOS-free, Mac support, free upgrade), SpinRite 7 rewrite idea and the planned Beyond Recall wiper.

Listener stories in these episodes: recovering files from failing drives, clearing slow SSD sectors, and repairing a drive enough for DBAN to wipe it; Steve explains modern drives rely on constant ECC.

Testimonials and explanations: it revives SSDs by forcing drives to rewrite weak sectors, runs faster in virtual machines, works on BitLocker or TrueCrypt disks, and SN 469 recounts its origin in tower-drift repair and interleave optimisation. SpinRite 6.1 and 7 plans discussed.

In these episodes Steve explains how Level 2 scans make drives repair growing defects, why it cannot see behind hardware RAID, how it handles uncorrectable sectors, SSD and RAID recoveries, and plans for 6.1, 6.2 and a native-booting version 7.
