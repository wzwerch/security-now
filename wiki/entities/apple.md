# Apple

# Summary

Topics: UK demands for iCloud Advanced Data Protection access, Safari's default fingerprint randomization, Declared Age Range API for Texas SB2420, Memory Integrity Enforcement, and Steve's dislike of iOS 26 Liquid Glass.

Apple appears for the iOS 14 kernel redesign that ended public kernel exploits, the DNG zero-click flaw CVE-2025-43300, the iMessage nickname dispute, the FIDO-based credential exchange, the UK Advanced Data Protection fight, and Memory Integrity Enforcement in A19 chips.

Appears for Advanced Data Protection, Security Keys for Apple ID, iMessage Contact Key Verification, BlastDoor image sandbox and WebKit zero-day patches.

Apple appears for iOS zero-days patched, the Rapid Security Response mechanism, AirTag and the unwanted-tracker draft with Google, WWDC 2023 privacy features and its refusal of backdoors. Steve repeatedly credits its strong security design while noting that the same design hides targeted exploits from forensics.

Covered for opposing UK encryption demands, refusing iCloud CSAM scanning, the Security Research Device program, removing PostScript from macOS and TriangleDB-era iOS fixes.

Apple adopted RCS, began reporting push-notification demands after Wyden's letter, and shipped iOS 16.6 to close an undocumented hardware path used in Operation Triangulation.

Apple appears for its EU Digital Markets Act browser-engine changes, the PQ3 post-quantum iMessage protocol, the GoFetch attack on M-series chips, the abandoned HomeKit secure-router programme and the DOJ antitrust suit. Steve praises its engineering but criticises marketing claims against Signal and its closed ecosystem.

WWDC 2024 introduced a standalone Passwords app with Passkeys and OTP, and Private Cloud Compute, a stateless attested server design Matthew Green called very thoughtful.

Apple's iMessage hardening, ATT tracking consent and the Trinity College finding that iPhones send nearby MAC addresses appear.

Episodes covered Apple CSAM Detection, exploited iOS zero-days, researcher complaints about the bounty programme and the Apple Pay with Visa transit-mode flaw.

In this batch Apple patched its third (WebKit use-after-free), fourth (Intel graphics driver) and fifth (AVD decoder) zero-days of 2022 and ran at a pace above its 2021 total of 12.

Episodes cover Apple's AppleAVD patch, iCloud Keychain passkey sync, the PACMAN research on Apple M1 Pointer Authentication, and Lockdown Mode with a $2M bounty.

Apple patched iOS zero-days, strengthened Safari tracking prevention and supported link ping as a UX improvement.

Fixed the iOS exploit chains found by Project Zero, disputed the report's scale, faced the unpatchable checkm8 boot ROM flaw, and shipped iOS 13 with a lockscreen bug.

Apple and Google released exposure notification, Apple dismissed the ZecOps iOS Mail claims, paid a Safari bounty and was criticized by the FBI after the Pensacola phone unlock.

Apple introduced iOS 14 privacy features, paid large bounties for web service bugs, patched three iOS zero-days in 14.2 and shipped the M1 chip with x86 emulation helpers.

Topics in this batch: the macOS IOHIDeous zero-day, battery throttling and its removal in iOS 11.3, the iBoot source leak, moving Chinese iCloud data to Guizhou-Cloud, the unfixed QR code spoof, and the Telugu Unicode crash.

Handled Chinese iCloud data transfer to a state-owned operator, a macOS synthetic click zero day, the Safari Ripper crash and denied the Supermicro report.

Apple fixed an iPhone PIN counter race, left Rootpipe unpatched on older OS X, suffered an iMessage text-rendering crash and slow response to the XARA research, and left older MacBooks with writable firmware after sleep.

Apple resisted the All Writs Act order, argued the case on law and policy, saw the FBI drop it after buying an unlock tool, fixed iMessage attachment weaknesses in iOS 9.3, and clarified Error 53 as a factory test.

Touch ID, iOS 7 lock screen bugs, CoreText crash bug, touch patent upheld.

iCloud Keychain design, Touch ID and its over-training behaviour, iBeacon over Bluetooth LE, the DropoutJeep iPhone implant era, and best score in the retailer password survey.

Goto fail bug, extensive iOS Security white paper, iMessage trust tradeoff, iCloud Keychain P-256 and an iOS 7.1 attachment protection gap.

Fixed Find My iPhone rate limiting after the iCloud photo leak, launched Apple Pay on tokenization, shipped iOS 8 with weak MAC randomization and the Shellshock patch for OS X, and claimed iMessage keys are not held by Apple.

Capped open bug reports per researcher amid AI-generated submissions; its latest updates carried about five times the usual number of fixes (#1091).
