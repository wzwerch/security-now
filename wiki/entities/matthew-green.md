# Matthew Green

# Summary

His Mastodon thread analysed Apple's Private Cloud Compute, praising the design while questioning opt-out and hidden flaws.

Green argued in SN 602 that mobile devices, especially iPhones, are safer than desktops for journalists, and in SN 603 dismissed Confide's cryptography.

Appears for the Libsodium audit, the IETF draft on data-center static Diffie-Hellman in TLS 1.3, and a team proof of HMAC-DRBG correctness in mbedTLS.

Green analysed the Canon-printer evidence linking RSA BSAFE, Dual EC and NSA's Extended Random draft, and tried to broker a TLS 1.3 compromise.

Called eFail a masterpiece of exploiting bad crypto and criticized PGP's lack of default authentication.

His blog post 'Why I'm Done With Chrome' objected to Chrome 69 signing users into the browser without notice, prompting Google to add a toggle in Chrome 70.

Green summarised the TrueCrypt phase two results and commented on backdoor proposals and weak protocol design.

Green wrote on research ethics around the Tor attack, criticised Microsoft's disk-key upload and described the Juniper Dual_EC discovery.

Published research showing flaws in iMessage's encryption design and tweeted TrueCrypt's reported origin; argues we barely know how to make encryption work.

His blog on Bullrun and RSA BSAFE; co-organized the TrueCrypt audit.

Led the TrueCrypt audit drive and wrote the BULLRUN brainstorm used in Episode 433.

Found the TrueCrypt results unalarming.

Argued PGP needs replacement and rebutted FBI claims about data access.

Explained that Mythos's HAWK attack is significant while its reduced-round AES result is a small, impractical improvement (#1091).

In "Everything is about to go dark" argued AI will soon remove exploitable bugs and revive the backdoor debate (#1095).
