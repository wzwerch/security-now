# Zoom's security overhaul and E2EE plans

# Overview

How Zoom reacted to the 2020 security and privacy scrutiny.

# Timeline

* **#761 (2020-04-07)** — Zoom usage jumped to 200 million daily users; Zoom bombing, a UNC-link NTLM leak, ECB encryption and keys routed via China were reported; Jitsi Meet recommended as an alternative. ([source](../sources/sn-0761.md))
* **#762 (2020-04-14)** — Meeting IDs hidden from title bars, security controls consolidated, waiting rooms and passwords made default, a shareholder lawsuit filed, and Alex Stamos engaged. ([source](../sources/sn-0762.md))
* **#763 (2020-04-21)** — Luta Security hired, advisors and audit firms added, AES-GCM promised, meeting credentials sold cheaply and two rumored Zoom zero-days offered at a high asking price. ([source](../sources/sn-0763.md))
* **#766 (2020-05-12)** — Zoom acquired Keybase to build end-to-end encrypted meetings with host-controlled keys for paid accounts. ([source](../sources/sn-0766.md))
* **#769 (2020-06-02)** — A 25-page design paper laid out four phases toward real E2EE; interim AES-GCM per-stream keys were already deployed. ([source](../sources/sn-0769.md))
* **#770 (2020-06-09)** — The CEO said free users would not get E2EE for law enforcement cooperation; Stamos and a spokesperson then gave conflicting explanations; Cisco Talos path traversal flaws in chat were fixed. ([source](../sources/sn-0770.md))
