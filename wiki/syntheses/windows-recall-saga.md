# Windows Recall saga

# Overview

Microsoft's screen-history feature, its security criticism and delay.

# Timeline

* **#976 (2024-05-28)** — Steve introduces Recall as a 50 GB privacy bomb: periodic screenshots stored locally for about three months on Copilot+ PCs. ([source](../sources/sn-0976.md))
* **#977 (2024-06-04)** — Kevin Beaumont shows the SQLite database can be read by other users and infostealers; Steve theorises Recall feeds a personal local LLM. ([source](../sources/sn-0977.md))
* **#978 (2024-06-11)** — Microsoft makes Recall opt-in with Windows Hello gating and just-in-time decryption; Beaumont and Steve remain unconvinced. ([source](../sources/sn-0978.md))
* **#979 (2024-06-18)** — The 18 June release is pulled and Recall moves to Windows Insiders first; the Wi-Fi flaw is cited as proof Windows is too fragile for it. ([source](../sources/sn-0979.md))
* **#980 (2024-06-25)** — Listeners raise third-party leakage; Steve argues Microsoft would use Recall locally for ad relevance and faces a harder task than Apple. ([source](../sources/sn-0980.md))
* **#981 (2024-07-02)** — A listener suggests employee monitoring; Steve calls Recall ethically neutral but likely to be pushed for AI and ad profiling. ([source](../sources/sn-0981.md))
* **#983 (2024-07-16)** — A listener catalogues Microsoft's history of forcing settings and predicts Recall will be forced on; Steve promises a blocker utility. ([source](../sources/sn-0983.md))
* **#985 (2024-07-30)** — PKfail is used as further evidence that the PC ecosystem cannot protect a full activity history. ([source](../sources/sn-0985.md))
