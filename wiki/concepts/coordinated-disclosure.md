# Coordinated disclosure

# Summary

Calif's four-to-six-day embargo on the HTTP/2 Bomb and AI-found zero-days dumped on GitHub led Steve to argue responsible disclosure is eroding.

Episode 942's Exim case shows a 15-month wait ending in a zero-day advisory; BitForge and TSSHOCK used 90 days (ep. 936).

Cases: a listener reporting an old nginx banner (CERT declined; HackerOne owner explained banner backports and PoC rules) and the D-Link NAS backdoor published at once, followed by attacks. Steve urges quiet reporting or silence for unpatchable gear.

The CUPS case exposed friction between a researcher and volunteer maintainers, with early proof-of-concept release triggering mass scans.

Wyze's three-year silence in 865 illustrates the trap researchers face.

Debate sparked by CTS Labs' 24-hour notice and Dropbox's policy.

Google Project Zero's 90-day rule collided with Microsoft's monthly cycle; in February 2015 it added a 14-day grace period.

PHP2Shell's two-month embargo collapsed two days early through a CVE leak, an accidental video and Chainguard; OpenAI proposed a five-stage coordination framework (#1102).
