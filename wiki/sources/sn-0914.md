# Summary

A popular "Get Cookies.txt" Chrome extension (around 160,000 users) was found to track every page visited and then to upload users' entire cookie files, prompting Steve to ask how extension vetting could ever work. Germany plans to exclude Huawei and ZTE from 5G. The annual DFIR report shows phishing at about 69% of initial access, with Microsoft's macro blocking having visibly shifted attacks. The main technical topic is Kudelski Security's "polynonce" attack, a generalisation of ECDSA nonce-reuse recovery that finds private keys when nonces follow a low-degree polynomial recurrence; run on the Bitcoin blockchain it only found 773 already-emptied wallets that had reused nonces, but shows how brittle ECDSA is. Steve also explains how SpinRite 6.1 uses a linear congruential generator to locate drive failures, confirms the Plex Media Server flaw (CVE-2020-5741) behind the LastPass breach, and covers Sony Music Germany's court win forcing the Quad9 DNS resolver to block a piracy site globally, a worrying precedent for DNS operators.

# Key points

- "Get Cookies.txt" in the Chrome Web Store: version 1.5.0 sent each visited URL plus a unique browser ID to the developer's domain, and a later update uploaded all cookies. It was removed after a Reddit report; users should log out of sensitive sites to invalidate exfiltrated session cookies. Steve argues extensions are powerful yet cannot be deeply vetted, and that one-time-use extensions should be removed after use.
- Germany reportedly will ban Huawei and ZTE from its 5G networks, joining the US, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Sweden and the UK; Steve is uneasy about evidence-free xenophobia but acknowledges equipment cannot be proven safe.
- DFIR report: phishing 69.2% of intrusions, drive-by compromise 15.4%, exploitation of public-facing applications (mostly Exchange) 7.7%, valid accounts (brute-forced RDP or SQL Server) 7.7%; macro-based Word and Excel attacks dropped after Microsoft disabled macros by default. Steve would like email run in a sandboxed virtual machine.
- Polynonce: ECDSA signatures are only safe if per-signature nonces are unpredictable and never reused; Kudelski showed that with four or more signatures from the same key whose nonces satisfy a low-degree recurrence, the private key is recoverable by polynomial root finding. A weak PRNG such as a linear congruential generator is an example of what would be vulnerable.
- Applied to Bitcoin (about 763 million signatures from 424 million public keys, 92% used once), the sliding-window attack on 128 cores took under three days and about 285 dollars and broke 773 wallets, all already at zero balance because of simple repeated-nonce flaws; roughly 484 BTC may have been stolen earlier. Fix: use fresh keys per transaction and a sound nonce generator (or deterministic nonces). Steve asks whether such a finicky algorithm should guard hundreds of billions of dollars; he and Leo note Curve25519/EdDSA is different.
- SpinRite 6.1 transfers 16 MB at a time; when a failing drive aborts without reporting the failure point, it prefills the buffer with a linear congruential noise pattern and finds the first sector still matching the noise to locate the problem sector.
- CISA added CVE-2020-5741, a deserialization flaw in Plex Media Server for Windows, to its KEV list; Steve explains this is the three-year-old bug that let attackers into a LastPass developer's home machine. Attackers likely maintain exploit databases indexed by vendor and scan developers' home IPs; some reports pointed at North Korea.
- Sony Music Entertainment Germany v. Quad9: the Leipzig Regional Court (1 March 2023) ordered the Swiss non-profit resolver to block piracy-site domains (linked to an Evanescence album) globally, treating Quad9 as liable. Sony had failed to reach the site's hosting provider. Quad9 complies via geo-IP blocking in Germany for now, will appeal, and argues DNS resolvers are not the place for content blocking and should be covered by the Digital Services Act's liability exemptions. Steve suggests Sony should have gone to the .to registry (Tonga) instead.
