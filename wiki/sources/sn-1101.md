# Summary

Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday fixed a record 1,411 flaws (70 critical, 677 elevation of privilege, 317 RCE) plus 348 earlier in the month, yet broke little beyond Smart App Control's toggle; it also introduced experimental "risk cohorts" that group fixes by area. Steve praises MDASH and expects a peak followed by a sharp drop, and notes Windows 10 benefits from fixes without new features. Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 describes an extortion attacker whose AI agents compressed two weeks of red-team work into under 10 hours: web foothold, repository secret harvesting, secrets-manager takeover, CI/CD abuse and even use of the victim's own AI endpoints, leaving an 80-page audit behind. Steve fears enterprises are not ready, because agents exploit organizational laxity, not just bugs.

Attackers bought genuine Ledger wallets, fitted a hidden board with a GSM modem and eSIM that read the 24-word recovery phrase off the display, and returned them to the "official" reseller CryptoBilis; on 9 October they drained about $102 million from 335 wallets across six chains in an automated sweep. Joe Grand also found implants in Trezor devices, which Trezor denies. Steve suggests self-built options like SeedSigner or Blockstream Jade. A listener enjoys AI coding and proposes a "continuous SpinRite" driver; Steve hints he has plans. The main topic: an unreleased Claude model spent under 30 minutes showing that the NSA-supplied NIST curve seeds came from the C library's `lrand48` seeded with 1997-98 Unix timestamps, which actually raises confidence there is no backdoor, and alarms Steve about what such AI could do for attackers.

# Key points

- Other October patchers: Adobe, Atlassian (exploited Data Center flaw), Cisco NX-OS root RCE, Citrix, Dell, Docker Hub, Fortinet, GitLab, Ivanti, Kiteworks (126 fixes), Progress, SAP and SonicWall. Windows 11 24H2 Home/Pro reached end of updates.
- Unit 42's agentic indicators: parallel calls to several frontier models, Markdown hand-off files between agents, AI-generated scripts; over 50 MITRE ATT&CK techniques; overlapping persistence via SSH keys, serverless functions, container restart policies, identities and pipelines; a Terraform backdoor blocked by branch protection. Defenses: automated synchronized containment, inventory and rate-limit all AI endpoints and MCP gateways, hunt for 401/200 bursts, enforce multi-party review and branch protection.
- Ledger's Donjon lab confirmed the implant reads the screen without touching the secure chip. Bitquery traced one actor practicing for two weeks, then emptying 111 Bitcoin wallets in one block and rekeying 30 TRON wallets before draining them; one wallet was refilled with $70,000 and drained again. Ledger blames distribution, not its product, and forbids reselling returned devices.
- ECDSA seeds: Claude scanned 34 old PRNG variants, spotted all-zero top bits typical of `lrand48`, recovered 48-bit states (a lattice problem for truncated output), and stepped back to `srand48` initializations that are Unix timestamps from May 1997 to April 1998 (binary-curve seeds with a halved clock). P-256's seed was the 1,842nd 20-byte draw. Base points for six curves came from Arjen Lenstra's FreeLIP generator. The earlier "Give Bob and Jerry a raise" hash story was wrong.
- Steve's conclusion: patching bugs is not enough; people, old credentials and archived secrets are the real attack surface, so organizations need AI defenders and per-user local AI "guardians" against ClickFix and phishing. He expects high-profile agentic breaches before defenses catch up, but no end of the world.
