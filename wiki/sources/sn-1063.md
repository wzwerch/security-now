# Summary

Steve starts with the Notepad++ news that broke after his show notes went out: state-level (believed Chinese) attackers compromised the hosting infrastructure behind the editor's update mechanism from about June to early December 2025 and used it for targeted malware delivery; this fits his long-standing complaint about Notepad++'s constant updates. He pairs it with the eScan antivirus supply-chain incident of January 20, where a regional update server pushed a malicious Reload.exe, and uses it to repeat his advice against third-party antivirus on Windows. Apple's coming iOS 26.3 adds a "limit precise location" cellular setting, though Leo notes it needs carrier participation. The big story is AI-driven vulnerability discovery: AISLE's AI system found all 12 OpenSSL CVEs in the January 27 release (and three of four in the autumn release) plus five in cURL, while cURL ended its bug bounty because of a flood of AI-generated junk reports. Steve and Leo discuss the stock-market jolt over AI disrupting software and argue about whether bounty removal is wise. Listener items correct last week's claim about Ireland (it is only a government proposal heading for a "General Scheme", not a passed law), warn about an AI coding agent wiping a project, and describe the fake Cloudflare "verify you are human" paste-into-Run attack that Steve says Microsoft should counter. The main topic is the MongoDB ransom wave documented by Flare: script-kiddie bots wipe exposed, unauthenticated databases and leave a worthless 500-600 dollar bitcoin ransom note. Steve's conclusion is that such stories stem from insecure defaults and lazy configuration which AI will not fix, and that only deliberately public servers should ever face the internet.

# Key points

- Notepad++ (author Don Ho): update infrastructure was compromised by a likely Chinese state actor for roughly six months in 2025; attacks were highly targeted at specific users (mainly in Asia), not widespread; the fault was at the hosting provider and in weak update verification. Fixed in v8.9.1; the author advises a manual download and reinstall, and Steve advises switching off auto-update where possible.
- eScan antivirus (MicroWorld Technologies, India): on January 20, 2026 a regional update server delivered a malicious Reload.exe with an invalid signature; it blocked later fixes by editing the hosts file, persisted via a scheduled task, and loaded PowerShell with an AMSI bypass. Kaspersky saw hundreds of victims, mostly South Asia. A 2024 breach of the same update system by North Korean actors makes this a repeat incident.
- Steve explains the hosts file (older than DNS, taking priority over all name lookups) and how malware abuses it to block security updates.
- Steve and Leo do not use third-party antivirus on Windows; Windows now ships its own and a kernel-privileged AV that turns hostile is worse than none.
- Apple iOS 26.3 (beta) adds a Cellular Data Options setting that coarsens the location cellular networks can derive; it does not affect emergency calls or app Location Services, but only participating carriers support it (reportedly just Boost Mobile in the US so far).
- AISLE: AI "cyber reasoning" system that scans, triages, writes and verifies patches; found 12 OpenSSL CVEs disclosed January 27, 2026 (including a rare high-severity one), 13 of 14 OpenSSL zero-days of 2025 in total, and pre-release bugs such as an OCSP double-free; OpenSSL's leaders publicly praised report quality. Steve now expects software bugs could eventually be largely eliminated.
- cURL ended its HackerOne bug bounty at the end of January 2026 after a surge of bogus AI-generated reports; Daniel Stenberg threatens public ridicule of time-wasters. Leo thinks this is a people problem and the bounty should have stayed; Steve fears independent research on cURL will dry up and expects reputation systems for reporters.
- Market reaction: software and legal-data stocks fell sharply after Anthropic announced legal tools for its co-work assistant; Leo cites "hyper-personalized, disposable software" and AI agents (OpenClaw) as both thrilling and a security risk.
- Correction: Ireland's lawful interception measure from last week is only a Cabinet-approved proposal to draft a "General Scheme"; a listener translator notes the 1993 Act needs no court order, so the proposal would add judicial authorization and an independent examiner.
- Listener report: Gemini's VS Code agent mode deleted a project's files; Steve advises separate backups the AI cannot touch and Leo advises committing to Git after every change.
- ClickFix-style social engineering: fake Cloudflare "prove you are human" pages instruct users to paste clipboard content into the Windows Run dialog; Steve says Microsoft should track clipboard origin and warn before pasting web-originated content into Run.
- Listener idea: ISPs could sell a real-time "who is at this IP" lookup to trackers; Steve finds it plausible and notes carrier-grade NAT or a VPN would defeat it.
- MongoDB ransom wave (Flare Systems): bots scan for MongoDB instances (default port 27017) without authentication, drop collections and leave a ransom note of about 0.005 BTC; of about 200,000 internet-visible MongoDB servers, 3,100 were fully open and 1,416 already wiped, and about 98% of notes used one bitcoin wallet, implying one dominant actor. Insecure Docker Hub images (763 found, two with 15,000+ pulls each) and leaked credentials propagate the problem; payment does not return data because it was deleted.
- Steve's rule: only servers meant for anonymous public access (web, mail, DNS) should be exposed; anything requiring a login should sit behind network-level restrictions, not rely on authentication alone.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a spoof age-verification dialog offering "search for my ID in existing breaches" (powered by a KidID logo), a joke on how widely personal data has leaked.
- Steve's upcoming ThreatLocker talk is titled "The Call Is Coming From Inside the House"; GRC's eCommerce system (written in assembly in 2003) is being reworked; a listener reports Gemini recommended GRC's DNS Benchmark; SpinRite 7.0 is still anticipated.
- Banter on Steve starting show preparation earlier in the week, a long house remodel, and Leo's vibe-coded news-prep tools.
- GRC's mailing list (NuevoMailer) delivered about 19,900 emails with no Outlook/Hotmail bounces this time.
