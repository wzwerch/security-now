# Summary

The show opens with a "caption this photo" contest on a tangle of overhead wires. News starts with Meta announcing that end-to-end encrypted Instagram DMs will end after May 8, 2026 (following TikTok's refusal to add E2EE), which Steve reads as a possible retreat from default strong encryption on mass platforms, while WhatsApp adds parent-managed accounts for pre-teens. Residential proxying is a growing theme: law enforcement seized SocksEscort, a malware-built proxy network (AVrecon, 369,000 IPs rented, over EUR 5 million earned), and Steve urges never exposing router management to the Internet. Meta hired the Moltbook founders, the EU Parliament extended voluntary chat scanning to August 2027 with limits after the mandatory Chat Control failed, and a DigitalMint ransomware negotiator was accused of running BlackCat attacks against the very clients he negotiated for. CISA ordered federal agencies to upload Cisco SD-WAN logs to its CISA CLAW cloud. Microsoft details Storm-2561, which uses SEO poisoning, fake Pulse Secure and Fortinet VPN installers hosted on GitHub and validly signed with a since-revoked certificate to steal VPN credentials; Bitdefender, Kaspersky and ThreatBook report malicious sponsored results for installing Claude Code and OpenClaw. Researcher Christopher Aziz's "Zombie ZIP" trick (mislabeling deflate data as stored) evades 50 of 51 AV engines on VirusTotal. Steve explains why he will not yet use AI to write his production code, quoting Uncle Bob Martin's "liars and cheats" account of AI instability, balanced by a listener who calls AI life-changing. The main topic is Steve's successful, free enrollment of GRC's IP block in CISA's Cyber Hygiene Vulnerability Scanning, which flagged only SWEET32 (Triple-DES) support, now being removed.

# Key points

- Instagram will drop E2EE DMs on May 8, 2026 citing low opt-in; Reuters reported Meta had been warned in 2019 that encryption would hinder CSAM detection. Steve doubts consumers will miss default encryption and notes Signal remains available. WhatsApp parent-managed accounts let parents lock settings and approve contacts without reading messages.
- SocksEscort takedown by FBI, Europol and Dutch police: botnet of infected modems/routers (AVrecon, first described by Lumen's Black Lotus Labs in 2023) used for ransomware, DDoS and CSAM distribution; difference between ORB networks and commercial residential proxy services; earlier takedowns included 911 S5, Anyproxy, 5socks, RSOCKS, Raptor Train, KV Botnet, Moobot and VPNFilter. Rebooting a router often clears RAM-resident proxies; never expose router admin interfaces.
- Meta hired Moltbook's founders (Matt Schlicht and Ben Parr) into Meta Superintelligence Labs.
- EU Parliament voted (March 11, 2026) to extend the temporary derogation allowing voluntary scanning to August 2027, restricted to known material and suspected users, excluding encrypted chats and AI/text grooming scans; no agreed permanent law. Steve favors OS-level age assertion via Apple/Google APIs as the right chokepoint; Leo notes Meta funded advocacy for such laws.
- DigitalMint negotiator Angelo Martino was accused of using an ALPHV/BlackCat affiliate account to attack companies (five paid) and leaking negotiation details; combined ransoms of $75.25M.
- CISA directed federal agencies to upload Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN logs (exploited via CVE-2026-20127, CVSS 10.0, zero-day activity since 2023) to CISA's Cloud Logging Aggregation Warehouse (CLAW) by March 23.
- Storm-2561: fake VPN clients (Pulse Secure, Fortinet, Ivanti lures) with DLL side-loading deliver the Hyrax infostealer; files signed by "Taiyuan Lihua Near Information Technology Co., Ltd." Steve questions how a malware signer passed CA vetting.
- AI-lure malware: sponsored Google results for "download claude code" led to malicious pages.
- Zombie ZIP: header method set to "stored" while data is deflate-compressed with mismatched CRC; scanners see noise; a custom loader decompresses it; 50/51 VirusTotal engines missed it (Kingsoft detected). It is a smuggling technique, not an end-user extraction flaw.
- AI coding views: Aakash Gupta cites 41% of 2025 code being AI-generated with 1.7x defect rate and the end of author-owned code; Uncle Bob Martin describes AI features breaking older features and mitigating with massive tests and mutation testing; Steve predicts specialist code AIs and says he won't yet use AI for his hand-written assembly; Leo notes things changed after late-November 2025 model releases; a listener (Craig) reports building a home-lab agent infrastructure.
- CISA Cyber Hygiene Vulnerability Scanning (CyHy VS): free for qualifying organizations with static public IPv4 addresses; enroll by emailing the CISA vulnerability intake address, create a Login.gov account, fill out the organization profile; acceptance in about a day; scan frequency depends on severity (12 hours for critical to 90 days for dark space); weekly PDF reports; Steve's first report flagged only SWEET32 on port 443 (a theoretical birthday attack needing about 705 GB over 18.6 hours), and he disabled 3DES suites. A listener says it replaced a $6,000/year insurer scan; CISA repositories are public on GitHub.
