# Summary

Fosstodon had to move behind Cloudflare after a week-long DDoS attack, illustrating that no standalone server can withstand modern attack volumes; NetScout data shows the December takedown of more than 50 booter services cut daily attacks but they remain frequent. Steve examines the TikTok ban push, noting that 28,000 apps embed TikTok SDKs, that data collection is ubiquitous and that blocking TikTok at DNS level would be technically simple but politically a great-firewall move. A DHS inspector general report found Secret Service and ICE used cell-site simulators without proper warrants. The new US National Cybersecurity Strategy contains Strategic Objective 3.3, shifting liability for insecure software onto vendors, which Steve calls huge news even though it is only a strategy. Smaller items cover GitHub's free secret scanning, a CISA red team that went undetected in a critical-infrastructure network, Russia's ban of foreign messengers in finance, the growth of CISA's KEV catalog, TPM 2.0 reference library flaws, a WordPress plugin and Russia fining Wikipedia. The main story is the Chick-fil-A breach of 71,473 accounts, which Steve argues was a Facebook-credential reuse attack enabled by the "Login with Facebook" option, highlighting the downside of single sign-on.

# Key points

- Fosstodon (largest Mastodon instance for open-source users) suffered a prolonged DDoS and placed its web front end behind Cloudflare; mastodon.social was also hit. Federation limits the blast radius, and Steve says only large DDoS protection services can absorb today's attacks.
- NetScout: the Europol/FBI takedown of 50+ DDoS-for-hire services in December 2022 cut attacks from peaks near 3,500 per day to about 1,000 on 22 January, with no immediate replacement services. Steve and Leo note ISPs still fail to apply egress filtering against spoofed source addresses.
- TikTok: Gizmodo found 28,251 apps using TikTok SDKs; Brookings argues bans do not guarantee safety because data brokers hold huge profiles on Americans. Steve notes a DNS blackhole of five or so TikTok domains would cut off federal networks, and that sites like msnbc.com and foxnews.com load dozens of third-party domains.
- DHS Inspector General: the Secret Service and ICE deployed stingray cell-site simulators in 2020-2021 without always obtaining court orders and without documenting approvals and data deletion; Steve cites it as an example of abuse once surveillance capability exists.
- National Cybersecurity Strategy, Strategic Objective 3.3: shift liability to software makers who fail a duty of care, prevent them from disclaiming liability by contract, create a safe harbor tied to secure development frameworks such as NIST's SSDF, promote SBOMs, coordinated disclosure and memory-safe languages. Steve notes lobbying will resist it but welcomes the precision of the diagnosis.
- GitHub made secret scanning alerts generally available and free for public repositories (100+ partner providers); one consultant found over 1,000 secrets in 14,000 repositories.
- CISA red-team exercise against an unnamed critical-infrastructure organization: initial access via phishing, persistence and lateral movement, with 13 measurable events that went undetected.
- Russia banned nine foreign messengers (including Telegram, WhatsApp, Viber, Threema, Teams) in financial institutions and state companies; iMessage and Android Messages were not listed.
- CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog nearly tripled in 2022 (331 to 868 entries), mostly old unpatched bugs, one dating from 2002.
- Quarkslab found two buffer overflows in the TPM 2.0 reference library used by multiple vendors (IBM, Red Hat, SUSE and others); patches issued late February; Steve calls it a monoculture risk.
- The All in One SEO WordPress plugin (over three million sites) needed an update; Wordfence reported the issues. Russia fined the Wikimedia Foundation 2 million rubles for refusing to remove war-related articles.
- Chick-fil-A reported 71,473 customer accounts compromised between 18 December and 12 February via "credential stuffing". Steve doubts a blind attack would reach this many accounts of such an unlikely target, and points to the site's removal of "Login with Facebook" as evidence that attackers used stolen Facebook credentials (such as from malicious apps that phish Facebook logins, per a Bloomberg report) to take over accounts, which were then sold. Lesson: single sign-on is itself credential reuse; use unique passwords and 2FA and audit reuse in your password manager.
