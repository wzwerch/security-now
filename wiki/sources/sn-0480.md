# Summary

Steve begins with a correction: last week he misread Adam Langley's POODLE remark. The same-origin policy stops scripts from reading other domains, but a script can still make the browser send requests to any domain, and since the POODLE attacker sits outside on the network and only needs to observe and tamper with those requests, same-origin rules are irrelevant to it. News items: the CurrentC payment consortium MCX suffered a leak of its testers' email addresses from its email provider, its CEO acknowledging retailer exclusivity with Apple Pay but no exit fees; AirHopper, research showing malware on an air-gapped PC can emit data via FM signals from the video subsystem to a nearby phone at about 60 bytes per second; an unpatched Mac OS X local root escalation; a successful formal audit of TextSecure (an unknown key-share issue found and mitigated) and the EFF messaging scorecard where only a few tools (TextSecure, Cryptocat, RedPhone and Silent Circle products) met all criteria; and a Virginia judge ruling that police cannot force disclosure of a passcode although fingerprints can be compelled. Steve warns about smart TVs' broad data collection and plugs a JavaScript Spritz implementation and the movie Ex Machina. A listener shares how SpinRite fixed a laptop's boot problem after a Samsung SSD maintenance tool broke it. The Q&A covers whether proxy VPNs avoid carrier tracking headers, how big sites stay secure, DV/OV/EV certificate types, secure erase, SQRL for payments, long-term archival storage and SMART with SpinRite.

# Key points

- POODLE correction: the script in the victim's browser need not read other origins; it only needs to cause traffic to the target, which an outside attacker then manipulates.
- CurrentC/MCX: tester email addresses leaked via a third-party email provider; retailers are contractually exclusive to MCX now, though no exit fee exists and Apple Pay may be allowed in the future; MCX launches only in 2015.
- AirHopper: infected air-gapped computers can modulate video output to broadcast FM data to a phone with an FM receiver within a few meters; Steve compares it with old Tempest emissions and notes a networked machine would have simpler exfiltration paths.
- A Mac OS X local privilege escalation allowing admin accounts to become root without a password was demonstrated but not published; Apple's fix was expected in January, and a workaround is to use a separate admin account and demote the daily account.
- TextSecure's cryptographic protocol was audited and formally analysed; an unknown key-share attack was found and a mitigation accepted by the developers.
- The EFF scorecard rates messaging apps on encryption in transit, encryption from the provider, verifiable contacts, forward secrecy, open code, security design and independent audit; TextSecure, Cryptocat, RedPhone and Silent Circle's products met all seven.
- Virginia court: a fingerprint is like a key or DNA sample and can be compelled, while a passcode is testimonial and protected; Steve notes Touch ID needs the passcode after 48 hours or repeated failures.
- A privacy lawyer's article describes a smart TV with a camera, microphone and tracking policy; Steve is wary of connected devices.
- Spritz has a small reference JavaScript implementation on GitHub.
- A listener's Samsung 840 EVO "performance restoration" tool left a laptop unbootable and a SpinRite Level 2 scan restored it.
- Paul's VPN idea: any encryption (VPN or HTTPS) prevents the carrier from inserting its tracking header, but there is no separate carrier cookie, only header insertion on plain HTTP.
- Large sites stay up because professional teams harden configurations, patch quickly and design for security, whereas small sites often use default setups with exposed services.
- Certificates: DV only proves control of a domain, OV adds organisation checks, EV involves extensive vetting; cheap certificates are DV.
- Secure erase differs from wear levelling: translation layers hide old data in unmapped areas, so firmware-level erase and later a Steve-built erase tool ("Beyond Recall") may be addressed in future.
- SQRL could authenticate credit card payments by signing a server's random challenge, but existing payment systems already address it; Steve still aims it at website logins.
- For ten-year retention, archival-grade optical discs stored in the dark are the best physical option, combined with multiple media or redundant major cloud providers; one cloud storage company announced it was shutting down.
- SMART only reports on what the drive experiences; SpinRite makes it useful by exercising the whole drive, and SpinRite also repairs SSDs.
