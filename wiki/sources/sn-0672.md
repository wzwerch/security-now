# Summary

The episode opens with two new speculative-store variants, Spectre 1.1 and 1.2, found by researchers affiliated with MIT who earned a 100,000 dollar Intel bounty; their approach bypasses the software mitigations built for Spectre 1.0. Steve argues the real fix may be hardware: either tag all predictor and cache state per process or partition cores between mutually hostile virtual machines. He then covers July's Patch Tuesday (53 Microsoft fixes, 112 from Adobe, almost all in Acrobat and Reader) and a Windows fix for the Lazy FP State Restore leak. A joint paper from Virginia Tech, a Chinese university and Microsoft Research shows a roughly 223 dollar GPS spoofer that can feed believable false turn-by-turn routes; 38 of 40 test drivers noticed nothing. GitHub extended its dependency-vulnerability alerts to Python, and Chrome's Strict Site Isolation was said to be rolling out widely. A hacker who used Shodan to find Netgear routers with a default FTP login pivoted into a US Air Force base network and sold Reaper drone manuals. The Portuguese provider Bitcanal was cut off by the Internet community for deliberately hijacking BGP prefixes to sell to spammers. The main segment walks through the 29-page DOJ indictment of 12 Russian GRU officers for the 2016 DNC/DCCC hacking, marveling at how much forensic detail the US could reconstruct.

# Key points

- Spectre 1.1 and 1.2 extend the original read-based attack to speculative stores; Intel and ARM acknowledged the problem, AMD was silent; no current mitigation.
- Steve's view: safe sharing of a core among hostile tenants may be impossible, so cloud systems may need per-VM core partitioning or fully tagged caches; end users were never at much risk.
- Patch Tuesday: Microsoft fixed 53 flaws across 15 products; Adobe fixed 112, with 104 in Acrobat and Reader (a large interpreter), prompting a remark that more features mean exponentially more bugs.
- Lazy FP State Restore leak fixed by dropping the optimization in Windows, as other operating systems had already done.
- GPS spoofing research ("All Your GPS Are Belong to Us") uses a software-defined radio, a Raspberry Pi and a battery, with 40-50 m range, and steers drivers to a wrong destination undetected; implication for autonomous trucks and cargo.
- GitHub's dependency graph and security alerts now cover Python as well as Ruby and JavaScript; visible only to repository admins.
- Chrome Strict Site Isolation (one renderer process per site, out-of-process iframes) costs about 10-20 percent memory; Google considered relaxing Spectre timer mitigations such as SharedArrayBuffer restrictions once it ships. Steve and Leo still had it disabled by default.
- A hacker used Shodan to find roughly 4,000 exposed Netgear Nighthawk R7000 routers with default FTP credentials, then pivoted from one at Creech Air Force Base to steal Reaper maintenance material; documents sold for 150-200 dollars. Steve wants routers to generate random credentials at first boot.
- Bitcanal (AS3266, Portugal) announced about 130 hijacked small prefixes totaling 224,512 IPv4 addresses, apparently resold to spammers; peers finally dropped it.
- The DOJ indictment details the GRU spearphishing of the Clinton campaign chairman via a fake Google security alert and shortened links, X-Agent malware on at least 10 DCCC machines, the DCLeaks and Guccifer 2.0 personas, and bitcoin purchases of infrastructure (over 95,000 dollars laundered).
- The methods were ordinary social engineering; what stunned Steve was the minute-by-minute attribution (even failed domain registrations and search terms), implying very broad Internet traffic collection.
- Leo notes Senator Wyden's inquiry about election-vendor ES&S having installed remote-access software on counting machines.

# Other segments

- SpinRite and RAID: a listener tip that mdadm write-intent bitmaps let a drive be pulled for SpinRite and rejoin with only a few minutes of resync.
- Geocaching shout-out and the "All your base" meme digression.
- Leo cites Zimmermann's observation that law enforcement sees nearly everything, with only a few dark pixels.
