# Summary

Steve is joined by Jason Howell (Leo is away). The first story is NetSpectre, a remote Spectre variant 1 attack that leaks data purely through network response timing, with and without a cache channel, and can also derandomize ASLR; it works on a LAN and between Google Cloud instances but is far too noisy to work across the Internet. Next is the Death botnet, which grabs about 1,200 old AVTECH devices through a long-patched flaw, a continuation of the flash-botnet theme. Senator Wyden's letters pressed the Department of Defense to use HTTPS and HSTS with publicly trusted certificates and asked agencies to remove Flash; Chrome 68 began marking HTTP sites as not secure. Steve also warns about adware-laden clone sites registered in other country TLDs, Google Play's total ban on on-device mining, a supply-chain attack that slipped a miner into a font package, and Google's Titan FIDO keys. He ends with the Bluetooth pairing flaw, which he judges theoretical because the attacker would have to intercept and alter the key exchange.

# Key points

- NetSpectre leaks roughly 15 bits per hour via cache timing (about 60 with an AVX-based channel), enough to pull a 256-bit key over a LAN in around 17 hours; also derandomizes ASLR in about two hours. Infeasible over the Internet because of timing jitter, but relevant on LANs and inside cloud networks; no in-the-wild Spectre use was known.
- Death botnet (author handle EliteLands) targets AVTECH DVRs, cameras and recorders whose password field leads to shell command execution; devices expose plaintext credentials and the patch was over a year old.
- Steve repeats that Internet-facing appliances must update themselves and unpatched ones should age out.
- Wyden told DOD CIO that many DOD sites lacked HTTPS or relied on the DOD root certificate unknown to browsers; OMB memo M-15-13 and DHS BOD 18-01 required HTTPS and HSTS; DOD replied it aimed to finish by year end.
- Chrome 68 labels HTTP pages Not Secure; per Cloudflare data, 542,605 of the top million sites do not redirect to HTTPS; mixed forms (for example a search form posting to HTTP) can downgrade the HTTPS indicator.
- Strict Site Isolation was still disabled by default in Chrome 68 for Steve and Jason; Firefox is developing a similar project named Fission.
- Wyden also asked NIST, NSA and DHS to remove Flash from federal sites by August 2019; Chrome users meeting Flash daily fell from 80 percent (2014) to 8 percent, and about 4.9 percent of sites used it.
- Clone domains such as .fr and .es versions of KeePass, 7-Zip, Audacity, GIMP and others bundled downloads with adware; legitimate TLS certificates make them look trustworthy; VirusTotal can check downloads.
- Google Play policy now bans apps that mine cryptocurrency on the device (remote management of mining is allowed); mining abuse can physically damage phones.
- Microsoft Defender ATP flagged a supply-chain attack where a PDF editor fetched font MSI packages from a cloned server, one tampered package installed a miner; the unsigned package gave it away.
- Google launched Titan USB/Bluetooth FIDO U2F security keys; Steve contrasts FIDO (a second factor) with SQRL (a single-factor identity with lifecycle management).
- Bluetooth: the Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman pairing flaw (invalid curve, only the X coordinate validated) needs a man-in-the-middle at pairing time that blocks and rewrites radio traffic; fix is to validate that the received point is on the curve. Android's Bluedroid stack and chips from Qualcomm, Broadcom and Intel were affected; Windows was not (no LE Secure Connections pairing).

# Other segments

- SQRL: Steve is adding SQRL login to XenForo-based public forums; plans a skeptical interview-style launch presentation.
- SpinRite testimonial about reviving an ATM hard drive and cloning it to an SSD in three hours.
- Aside on Yubico's origins on the podcast and Steve's SQRL demo to Yubico.
