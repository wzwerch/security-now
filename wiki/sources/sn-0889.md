# Summary

Steve covers September 2022 Patch Tuesday (63 flaws, one actively exploited Common Log File System driver privilege escalation, CVE-2022-37969) and Lloyd's of London requiring cyber policies to exclude nation-state attacks from March 2023, a response to the NotPetya-era Merck dispute and spillover risks from the Ukraine war. The Uber breach is explained from Uber's own updates: a contractor's stolen password plus repeated MFA push prompts that the contractor eventually accepted, then access to internal tools by an attacker linked to Lapsus$, who also leaked GTA 6 footage from Rockstar Games. LastPass published its final investigation results: a four-day intrusion confined to a development environment physically separated from production, with no customer data or vault access and no code tampering. A WPGateway WordPress plugin zero-day was exploited against some 280,000 sites, and a VMware engineer showed Retbleed mitigations in Linux 5.19 cost large performance. Google's MiraclePtr/BackupRefPtr reference-counting technique aims to neutralize about half of Chrome use-after-free bugs. The main topic is "spell-jacking": with Chrome or Edge enhanced spellcheck on, text from form fields, including passwords revealed with the show-password button, is sent to Google or Microsoft.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday: 63 CVEs including 30 RCE and 18 privilege escalation; CVE-2022-37969 (CLFS driver) exploited in the wild and reported by four vendors; some group policy issues after patching.
- Lloyd's will require nation-state attack exclusions in new or renewed cyber policies from 31 March 2023; Merck won $1.4 billion from insurers for NotPetya, and AcidRain collateral damage hit a German wind turbine operator; insurers are tightening and cover may become unaffordable for about half of firms.
- Uber breach: external contractor's password bought after malware infection, MFA fatigue (repeated prompts until accepted), then lateral access to G-Suite, Slack, HackerOne and finance tools; no evidence of production or customer database access; Uber attributed it to Lapsus$ and strengthened MFA policy. Steve's lesson: pair MFA with lockouts on repeated failed attempts.
- Rockstar Games: more than 90 GTA 6 development videos leaked by a persona called "Teapot", possibly the same actor; Rockstar confirmed authenticity.
- LastPass final report with Mandiant: four-day activity window; attacker used a compromised developer endpoint and impersonated the developer after MFA; dev and production are separate; developers cannot push to production; no evidence of code poisoning. Steve concludes the zero-knowledge architecture held.
- WPGateway plugin zero-day (CVE-2022-3180) lets unauthenticated attackers add a rogue administrator; Wordfence blocked 4.6 million attempts against 280,000 sites in 30 days; Steve recommends a WordPress application firewall for sites with plugins.
- Retbleed mitigation (IBRS) in Linux 5.19: VMware measured about 70% less compute performance, 30% less network throughput and 13% less storage bandwidth in VMs; Steve argues end users have little exposure to microarchitectural attacks and datacenters bear the real risk.
- Google MiraclePtr (BackupRefPtr in PartitionAlloc): over 15,000 raw pointers rewritten to raw_ptr, enabled for the browser process in Chrome 102, expected to protect roughly half of use-after-free bugs, which make up about half of exploitable Chrome bugs.
- Spell-jacking (Otto-js): with enhanced spellcheck on, Chrome and Edge send form text to cloud suggestion services; 29 of 30 tested sites leaked PII, 73% leaked passwords when "show password" was used; only Google properties (partially) and Auth0 had mitigated by setting spellcheck="false"; LastPass and AWS fixed theirs quickly. Steve found his own Chrome had the feature enabled and advises turning it off.
- SQRL discussion: its domain-bound keys and client-provided session defeat reverse-proxy phishing such as EvilProxy.
- Listener notes: Security Now! listening can count as CISSP CPE credit; the Picture of the Week may actually show a bypassed electricity meter; Dutch regulator Bert Hubert thanked the show for coverage of his resignation.
