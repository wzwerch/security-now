# Summary

Steve notes that Michaels and Neiman Marcus joined Target as victims of point-of-sale malware that infects any Windows XP Embedded terminal, and shares his method for making Apple's Touch ID reliable: repeatedly touching an enrolled finger on the Touch ID settings screen silently adds training samples and widens the recognised area. Two new, smaller CryptoLocker samples are poorly detected by antivirus, which he uses to argue for whitelisting over blacklisting. A government review panel called bulk phone-record collection illegal and ineffective, an NSA slide on mobile apps (Golden Nugget) appeared, and Facebook's Android update demanded SMS and calendar permissions. The main technical item is that Bluetooth Low Energy pairing modes (Just Works, six-digit PIN) lack elliptic-curve key agreement, so a passive eavesdropper can brute-force the temporary key in under a second, undermining later encryption unless out-of-band pairing is used. He also recommends HowsMySSL, IsTrueCryptAuditedYet, and describes his SQRL write-up of chained Scrypt ("EnScrypt"). Q&A covers keeping XP alive in a virtual machine, flash wear, femtocell security, remote-desktop alternatives to Hamachi, hibernation files and DMA attacks, MailStore tips, why SpinRite is 6.1 not 7, known-plaintext attacks on CryptoLocker and why servers do not lock out password guessing.

# Key points

- Point-of-sale malware (the Target tool) is vendor-agnostic and hits any XP Embedded terminal; Michaels and Neiman Marcus confirmed or suspected breaches, reported by Brian Krebs.
- Touch ID: the fingerprint list on the settings screen is a training opportunity; repeated presses at the edge of the finger expand the feature map and eliminate "finger fade".
- New CryptoLocker builds (about 300 KB) were detected by only a few antivirus engines; Steve hosts samples for corporate testing.
- Browser store problem: successful extensions and apps are sold to ad or malware operators and then updated without re-review.
- Review panel: phone-metadata programme was illegal and produced no concrete counterterrorism result; Verizon disclosed about 370,000 government requests in a year.
- Facebook's Android app update asks to read SMS/MMS, calendar and call logs; permissions are all-or-nothing and unexplained.
- Bluetooth LE pairing: Just Works uses a temporary key of zero, PIN pairing uses 0 to 999,999 padded to 128 bits, and the confirm values are sent in the clear, so Mike Ryan's attack recovers the key in under a second; only out-of-band exchange (NFC, QR) gives strong pairing. Fine for thermostats or toy remotes but not payments such as the Coin card or iBeacon uses.
- HowsMySSL shows what a browser offers in its handshake (Chrome rated best, Firefox criticised for a legacy suite); IsTrueCryptAuditedYet tracks the audit.
- SQRL's EnScrypt page explains password-hashing history; the reference tool benchmarks memory bandwidth by iterating Scrypt for a set time.
- XP after April 8: disable networking in an XP-mode virtual machine; Microsoft will keep updating the malicious-software removal tool until July 2015.
- Flash memory is not used as main memory, so Scrypt's memory-hardness does not wear it.
- Femtocells: a 2010 Vodafone UK flaw let attackers load firmware; fundamentally no worse than the cell link itself.
- Hamachi/LogMeIn free tier ended; alternatives are dynamic DNS with port forwarding, TeamViewer or paid services.
- Hibernation files hold an unencrypted memory image unless whole-disk encryption covers them; Thunderbolt and FireWire DMA tools (Inception) can read RAM.
- SpinRite 6.1 will be a speed and compatibility release with free upgrade; SpinRite 7 would be a paid rewrite with file-aware recovery.
- Known-plaintext attacks do not help against AES-based CryptoLocker; account lockout thresholds are rarely enabled by default because of support burden, teaser for next week's password survey.
