# Summary

The show has a theme of technology colliding with the status quo. First, the UK ISP association nominated Mozilla for an "Internet villain" award over DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH), arguing it bypasses UK filtering and parental controls; Mozilla pushed back and the nomination was withdrawn during the episode. Steve explains DoH (RFC 8484), how to enable it in Firefox, and that SOHO routers (pfSense, OpenWRT) can offer it network-wide. He then discusses reporting that the FBI and ICE run facial-recognition searches against state driver's-license databases without legislative approval, bipartisan congressional unease, San Francisco's ban, and CNN's coverage of license-plate scanning. Amazon's Project Kuiper filing and rival satellite constellations raise censorship-circumvention questions. SQRL gets a WordPress plugin, and listener feedback includes an anonymous IT worker explaining that the Florida ransomware used a Word macro lure and that geoblocking Russia stopped it. The Ruby segment dissects a hijacked "strong_password" gem version 0.0.7 whose four-line backdoor fetched code from Pastebin. Finally Steve argues about GCHQ's ghost proposal that the debate should be about what society wants, not a false claim that it is technically impossible.

# Key points

- DoH: tunnels browser DNS over HTTPS to a provider such as Cloudflare, Google or Quad9, hiding queries from ISPs and tampering; UK ISPA nominated Mozilla as villain; GCHQ and the Internet Watch Foundation criticized DoH; Mozilla said no plans to enable it by default in the UK; ISPA withdrew the nomination.
- DoH options for networks: pfSense (via DNS over TLS), OpenWRT; DNSCrypt (OpenDNS) is an older pre-standard approach.
- Facial recognition: GAO counted over 390,000 FBI searches since 2011 of federal and state databases including DMV photos; Georgetown Law's research prompted a bipartisan House hearing; Utah records show 1,000+ FBI/ICE searches; license plate scanner networks can reconstruct a car's history; Steve links to the earlier Sensorvault location-data discussion.
- Satellites: Amazon Kuiper filing for 3,236 satellites; SpaceX Starlink first 60 launched; OneWeb and Facebook plans; Leo raises Kessler syndrome and Steve raises censorship implications.
- SQRL for WordPress plugin (by the Android client author) released; the SQRL protocol could work without TLS but is deliberately restricted to HTTPS; the browser extension runs on Chromium-based Edge.
- Feedback: the Lake City attack lure was a Word macro disguised as an error dialog; Russia-geoblocking prevented infection elsewhere; recommendation to keep cold backups and tighten firewalls; Spamgourmet's operator is ill.
- SpinRite 6.1 timing: Level 2 scan of 2 TB in about three hours, Level 4 much longer; faster-spinning drives will be faster.
- Ruby strong_password gem: attacker took over the maintainer's old RubyGems account (no 2FA, likely reused credentials), published 0.0.7 on 2019-06-25 with a hidden thread that periodically fetched and evaluated code from Pastebin; discovered by a developer diffing releases against GitHub; CVE-2019-13354; remedy is rolling back and checking systems, using unique passwords and 2FA.
- Ghost Protocol: GCHQ's Levy and Robinson proposed silently adding a law enforcement participant to encrypted group chats; a 47-signatory open letter opposed it; Steve says it is not technically a weakening of encryption but a policy question, and that sophisticated criminals would just use other tools.
