# Summary

The episode begins with market news: DRAM prices are up 60 to 70% and AI buyers have claimed most of this year's supply, so enterprise firewall vendors such as Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks and Check Point face higher costs, and Steve suggests buying planned equipment early. Anthropic is giving $1.5 million over two years to the Python Software Foundation, partly to fund automated proactive malware review of PyPI uploads. The FTC finalized its order against General Motors and OnStar over selling geolocation and driving-behavior data without consent (five-year ban on sharing with consumer reporting agencies, 20 years of consent and deletion duties). DHS is finalizing ANCHOR (Alliance of National Councils for Homeland Operational Resilience) to replace CIPAC, with liability protections still unresolved. A draft German law would give the Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND) full Internet interception, six-month retention, hacking of foreign ISPs and the "federal trojan". Grubhub is reportedly being extorted by ShinyHunters using credentials from the Salesloft Drift thefts to reach Zendesk data. Let's Encrypt made 6-day (160-hour) and IP-address certificates generally available, and Steve notes revocation checking now works in browsers. Iran appears to be planning a permanent Internet disconnection. A SpinRite before/after HD Tune graph and several listener topics (Roku hidden advertising ID screen, cloud MongoDB defaults, a ScreenConnect remote-takeover incident, Claude AI coding experiences) fill the middle. The main topic is new LayerX research showing GhostPoster is much larger than Koi found: 17 additional extensions, over 840,000 installs, beginning on Edge in February 2020 and spreading to Chrome and Firefox.

# Key points

- RAM shortage: Micron exiting the consumer market, SK Hynix considering similar moves; firewall makers' margins and prices affected; Steve already bought replacement servers and a Lenovo small PC early.
- Anthropic-PSF partnership: $1.5M over two years for PyPI and CPython security, including automated proactive package review and a malware dataset reusable across package ecosystems. (Leo clarifies CPython is the reference interpreter written in C.)
- GM/OnStar FTC order: finalized January 2026 after a January 2025 complaint; opt-in consent, data access and deletion, ability to disable collection; Hyundai, Honda and Toyota reportedly also share driving data with Verisk. Steve compares to the Aureate spyware controversy behind his OptOut tool.
- ANCHOR: DHS body replacing the disbanded CIPAC (information-sharing with liability shield for one-to-many industry/government conversations); the 2015 CISA information-sharing law only protects one-to-one sharing.
- Germany's BND draft law: full content interception, six-month storage, offensive hacking of foreign providers including US platforms, surveillance of foreigners in Germany, physical entry to install state malware. Steve: strong encryption remains the defense; NSA's Utah data center suggests "harvest now, decrypt later".
- Grubhub breach: ShinyHunters demand Bitcoin to suppress older Salesforce (February 2025) and new Zendesk data; credentials stolen in the August 2025 Salesloft Drift OAuth-token campaign (about 1.5 billion records from 760 companies per ShinyHunters; Google tracked as UNC6395).
- Let's Encrypt (January 15, 2026): opt-in 160-hour certificates and IP-address certificates; default lifetime falling from 90 to 45 days; Steve still questions the need for short certificates but will donate to Let's Encrypt after migrating; revoked.grc.com test shows browsers now enforce revocation.
- Iran: offline since January 8, 2026 with signs of permanence (messaging apps limited to internal use, satellite dishes confiscated, tooling to detect Starlink traffic).
- SpinRite 6.1 Level 3 on a Crucial 480 GB SSD restored HD Tune read speed (previously deep dips); trimmed regions explain the apparent full-speed areas.
- Roku advertising ID is on secret screen 2 (Home x5, then Up, Right, Down, Left, Up), useful for the California DROP form.
- Listener points: cloud one-click deployments of MongoDB may be unintentionally public (like early Cisco defaults); a reader was hijacked via ScreenConnect while away and PayPal transfers were sent, Steve suggests per-transaction biometric confirmation; Claude Code anecdotes include context-limit tips, Superpower and Ralph Wiggum plugins, and Karpathy's remark he cannot keep up; Leo notes AI hallucination can hide errors rather than fix them.
- GhostPoster expansion (LayerX): 17 more extensions across Edge, Chrome and Firefox, over 840,000 installs, about 890,000 cumulative; first Edge extension February 2020, Firefox from March 2020, Chrome from October 2022; some stayed live for five years. New variant "Instagram Downloader" (3,822 users) hides its payload after a "<<<<" marker in an image, stores it in browser storage under "instlogo", and sleeps about five days before contacting the server. Top downloads were fake translation tools (522,000 and about 160,000 installs). Removal from stores does not uninstall existing copies. Steve's advice: install as few extensions and programs as possible, stick to known vendors.
