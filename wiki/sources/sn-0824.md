# Summary

Chrome fixed its seventh in-the-wild zero-day of 2021 (CVE-2021-30554, a WebGL use-after-free), reported anonymously and patched two days later. Citing Proofpoint's report on initial access leads, Steve describes how ransomware is specializing into initial access brokers who sell footholds to affiliates, and predicts that after the Colonial Pipeline and JBS backlash gangs will prefer many small, low-profile ransoms to headline-grabbing infrastructure attacks. He explains the iOS bug where a specially named Wi-Fi network made an iPhone's Wi-Fi unusable until network settings were reset, attributing it to the old design habit of mixing control characters with literal text in format strings. Miscellany covers the leaked Windows 11 developer ISO (how to verify its SHA-256, TPM requirement, a trick for local accounts on Home, a registry toggle for the classic Start menu) and a listener who drives raccoons from his attic by playing the podcast. Steve then gives a detailed progress report on the rewrite behind SpinRite 6.1. The closing segment is a Russian-language-intelligence view (AdvIntel) of Avaddon: after a year, about $87M in ransom revenue, and the gang shut down, released 2,934 decryption keys through BleepingComputer, and likely did so out of fear of US and Russian state pressure after Colonial Pipeline.

# Key points

- Chrome 91.0.4472.114 fixed four flaws including CVE-2021-30554 (WebGL use-after-free, exploited in the wild); it is the seventh Chrome zero-day of 2021 (earlier dates in Feb, March, April, early June). Steve is puzzled by a Google TAG director's remark about "commercial providers".
- Proofpoint "The First Step: Initial Access Leads to Ransomware": a seven-step chain from phishing with malicious Office macros to a backdoor, sale of access by an initial access broker, Cobalt Strike lateral movement, Active Directory domain compromise, and ransomware deployment to domain workstations. "Big game hunting" targets are picked by open-source surveillance of ability to pay.
- Steve's prediction: Colonial Pipeline and JBS (REvil, $11M) were strategic mistakes that triggered government attention; gangs will favor many modest attacks (a $1M each rather than a single large one), more escrow services, and specialization.
- iOS Wi-Fi bug: connecting to a hotspot whose SSID contained printf-style format specifiers broke Wi-Fi on iOS devices; the fix was Settings > General > Reset > Reset Network Settings. Steve blames in-band escape sequences as lazy language design (comparing HTTP query separators); Leo notes the `%n` specifier writes to memory and that C and C++ allow this.
- Windows 11 dev build ISO (about 4.8 GB) leaked; Steve will not share links but gives the SHA-256 start and end (b8426650... 6da60dcb) and the built-in ways to compute hashes (`certutil -hashfile`, `Get-FileHash`). Windows 11 requires a TPM (tpm.msc shows status); Home edition forces a Microsoft account, but a listener found Alt+F4 on the network dialog allows a local account; the classic Start menu can be restored via the `Start_ShowClassicMode` registry value under the Explorer Advanced key.
- Listener humor: playing Security Now! on a loop drove a raccoon out of an attic.
- SpinRite 6.1 progress: Steve is rewriting large parts, introducing 64-bit sector addressing, a single IO abstraction over five drive access classes (BIOS only through AHCI hardware), flat real mode, restartable large block transfers (up to 32,768 sectors per transfer), simpler sequentially numbered log files, and a line count of about 27,556 lines (SR.ASM at 8,652). The post-6.1 plan is a new 32-bit real-time OS with UEFI support, native USB and NVMe drivers, and detection and rewriting of slow solid-state regions. Steve plans to release the source one day.
- Avaddon: RaaS since 2019, more aggressive from June 2020, used malicious JavaScript email, RDP and VPN credentials, data theft, leak site and DDoS; victims included AXA's Asian operations and an Australian telecom partner. Avaddon had announced a private, narrower mode in May 2021, then on June 11 gave BleepingComputer 2,934 keys; Emsisoft released a free decryptor. Coveware reported an average demand around $600K and unusual pressure to close deals in the last days.
- AdvIntel (Kremez and Boguslavskiy): Avaddon built an ecosystem of RDP brute-force sellers, access auctions, exfiltration malware and negotiators; victims' lifetime revenue totalled about $35B; ransoms estimated by a "5x5" rule (about 5% of estimated annual revenue, lower percentages for large firms); total take roughly $87M with over 70% going to affiliates and about $26M for the core team. They attribute the shutdown to fear after Colonial Pipeline, US moves to treat ransomware like terrorism, forum bans, and Russian political de-escalation before the June 16 Biden-Putin summit (one affiliate reportedly arrested in Russia).

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: four-panel "Windows 11" cartoon about pointless changes to justify employment.
