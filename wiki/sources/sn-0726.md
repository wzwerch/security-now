# Summary

Steve first corrects a false alarm: a Forbes column claimed Facebook was building a client-side wiretap into WhatsApp, Bruce Schneier amplified it, then retracted after learning it was extrapolation and WhatsApp denied it. He then covers continuing government ransomware attacks in Georgia (Department of Public Safety, Henry County, Lawrenceville), pushing officers back to pen and paper, and advice to educate and phish-test employees. The Five Eyes ministers issued a communique urging tech firms to provide lawful access, and Steve and Leo again debate whether a warrant-compatible approach is technically feasible only for provider-managed systems and what an eventual law would force. Microsoft reported Russia's Strontium group (APT28/Fancy Bear) using a VoIP phone, printer and video decoder with default or outdated credentials as footholds, prompting a segment on VLAN network segmentation. Chrome 76 stops remembering per-site Flash permission and hides incognito mode from the file-system API probe. The main segment is Steve's multi-month journey syncing working files between two locations: FreeFileSync, Google Drive, OneDrive (merge duplicates), then Dropbox, with notes on Boxcryptor, ownCloud/Nextcloud, Tresorit and Synology Drive.

# Key points

- Forbes WhatsApp story: claimed on-device moderation scanning would funnel plaintext to Facebook; Schneier posted "Facebook plans on backdooring WhatsApp" then corrected on Aug 2; Steve notes Facebook plans to extend encryption to Messenger and Instagram.
- Five Eyes meeting in London (hosted by the UK Home Secretary) reiterated that tech firms should provide readable access with legal authority; a 2018 statement threatened legislative measures; Leo argues governments object mainly to true end-to-end encryption and may eventually target endpoints or force provider-managed encryption.
- Steve's recurring distinction: policy (what society wants) versus technology (what is possible); he says a provider-managed system such as iMessage could add a hidden participant, but math-level end-to-end encryption cannot be selectively broken.
- Georgia: Department of Public Safety offline, troopers using paper tickets and radio dispatch; Henry County and Lawrenceville police also hit; the 2018 Atlanta SamSam attack demanded about 52,000 USD and Iranian suspects were indicted. Ryuk is the likely family in recent attacks; Sophos guidance includes strong unique passwords, backups, patching, locking down RDP.
- Strontium targeting: Microsoft's April-onward findings of IoT devices (VoIP phone, printer, video decoder) used for initial access, sniffing with tcpdump and lateral movement; two cases used default passwords and one lacked a patch; Microsoft to present at Black Hat; earlier VPNFilter botnet linked to the same actor.
- VLANs: 12-bit tags allow isolating IoT, VoIP and departmental traffic on smart switches; adds management overhead but can be adopted incrementally; Steve mentions his preferred routers (Ubiquiti Edge router, Netgate SG-1100).
- Chrome 76: Flash permission no longer persists between sessions (Flash end of life due end of 2020); file-system API trick to detect Incognito removed.
- Listener tool: ffsend, a command-line client for Firefox Send.
- File sync lessons: FreeFileSync works manually but not reliably in the background; Google Drive gave lock errors; OneDrive created duplicate and stuck states; Dropbox (1 TB for 10 USD a month) worked flawlessly with versioning; Steve has no encryption layer yet but considers Boxcryptor; Leo recommends Nextcloud over ownCloud, Tresorit, Synology Drive, ZFS/Btrfs snapshots.
