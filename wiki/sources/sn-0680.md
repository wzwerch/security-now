# Summary

Jason Howell again stands in for Leo. On Patch Tuesday Microsoft fixed 62 flaws including the Task Scheduler ALPC privilege escalation that ESET showed was exploited by the PowerPool group within two days of disclosure, using it to replace the Google updater binary. Microsoft announced paid Windows 7 Extended Security Updates through January 2023 while Windows 7 still held the larger desktop share. MikroTik routers returned: about 370,000 remain vulnerable to the April Winbox flaw, over 7,500 are mirroring traffic to attackers, and 239,000 carry a malicious proxy. Chrome 69 hides the "www" and scheme in the address bar and adds a built-in password generator, followed by the case of MEGA's Chrome Web Store account being used for four hours to publish a credential-stealing extension, which Steve uses to argue for challenge-response login such as SQRL. Other items cover Vodafone Czechia blaming customers for "1234" passwords, Tesla's open good-faith research policy and the KU Leuven Model S key-fob clone, the CroniX Monero miner hitting Struts servers, an OpenVPN plugin loading risk affecting NordVPN and ProtonVPN, and Trend Micro's Mac apps being removed for uploading browsing history.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday: 62 flaws, 17 critical; the ALPC zero day was patched and had already been used by PowerPool to hijack GoogleUpdate.exe for a backdoor.
- Windows 7 ESU: per-device, rising prices, for volume-licensed Pro and Enterprise only; Windows 7 share about 40 percent vs Windows 10 about 38 percent.
- MikroTik: Winbox port 8291 exposed on WAN by default; CVE-2018-14847 reportedly used by a CIA Vault 7 tool; routers configured with TZSP sniffer forwarding capture FTP, POP3, IMAP and SMTP traffic; 360 Netlab recommends blocking Winbox and WebFig from the Internet.
- Chrome 69 trims "www" and "m" subdomains from the displayed URL, and offers generated strong passwords and improved autofill; Steve cautions that a browser-based vault expands the attack surface.
- MEGA extension 3.39.4 (September 4) requested extra permissions and exfiltrated credentials and wallet keys to a Ukrainian host until a clean update four hours later; users were told to change passwords.
- Steve argues that static passwords, even generated ones, depend only on user memory, whereas signing a server challenge defeats replay and leaves servers with no secrets.
- Vodafone Czech Republic customers with "1234" portal passwords were hit by SIM-order fraud totaling about 27,000 dollars; the portal only allowed 4-6 digits; Vodafone sought repayment from customers.
- Tesla published a safe-harbor research policy (no legal action under CFAA or DMCA for registered good-faith researchers); KU Leuven showed Model S fobs using a 40-bit cipher could be cloned in 1.6 seconds with a 6 TB lookup table; newer fobs and an optional PIN mitigate.
- CroniX (F5 Labs): the Struts OGNL flaw delivers a Monero miner that kills competing malware and persists via cron and fake process names.
- OpenVPN: a user-writable config can name an authentication plugin loaded by the system-level service; NordVPN and ProtonVPN needed two rounds of fixes.
- Trend Micro's Dr. Cleaner and other Mac apps collected a day of browsing history and were removed from the Mac App Store; the company said it was disclosed in EULAs.
- Degaussing a hard drive is not reliable; drill or physically destroy, or use a secure wipe.
