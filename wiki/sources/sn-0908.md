# Summary

Steve starts with Google's plan for Android 14 to refuse installation of apps that target very old API levels, from any source including sideloading, to stop malware that deliberately targets old versions to dodge newer protections. Microsoft will block Excel XLL add-ins that carry the Mark of the Web, pushing attackers further toward ZIP containers. Riot Games refused a ransom demand after a source-code theft, and the Wormhole thief started moving a large share of the 2022 loot. Kevin Rose lost around 40 NFTs to a signature phishing attack. Meta is randomly upgrading Messenger chats to end-to-end encryption, and iOS 16.3 added support for FIDO security keys on Apple IDs. Poland's CERT scanned local-government sites proactively and found hundreds of outdated sites. The FBI revealed it had covertly infiltrated the Hive ransomware gang for months, handing out over 1,300 decryption keys, and Russia responded by blocking US agency websites. After listener feedback on passwords and Argon2 in Bitwarden, and a SpinRite status update, the main topic is Eric Biggers' warning that newer Intel (Ice Lake and later) and ARM CPUs execute some instructions in data-dependent time unless DOITM/DIT is enabled, undermining constant-time cryptography.

# Key points

- Android 14 will block installing apps targeting outdated API levels from any source (initially only very old ones, with a ramp toward Android 6.0 and beyond); a command-line override remains. Google's rationale is that malware targets old API levels to evade newer protections; Steve compares it to protocol downgrade attacks.
- Microsoft will block XLL Excel add-ins downloaded from the Internet; attackers have been shifting to ZIP archives to avoid the Mark of the Web.
- Riot Games said it will not pay a ransom over stolen League of Legends, Teamfight Tactics and legacy anti-cheat source code; Steve thinks that is the right call because crooks rarely delete data.
- The Wormhole bridge thief moved about 155 million dollars of the roughly 321 million stolen in February 2022; Steve questions how safe any crypto asset is.
- Kevin Rose was phished into signing a malicious transaction signature, losing more than 40 NFTs initially valued around 2 million dollars; Steve and Leo are sceptical of the NFT ecosystem.
- Meta is gradually and randomly migrating Messenger conversations to end-to-end encryption; Leo notes staged rollouts help manage breakage.
- iOS 16.3 and macOS 13.2 allow FIDO-certified hardware keys (including NFC) for Apple ID; Leo cautions that Advanced Data Protection forces all devices onto current OS versions.
- CERT Polska's Artemis scanner checks public services of schools, hospitals and local authorities, forwards results privately to administrators, and re-verifies fixes; the first weeks covered about 2,000 domains and found hundreds of outdated sites plus exposed config files, backups and credentials. Steve sees only upside.
- Hive ransomware: US and EU authorities seized servers and sites; the FBI had been inside since July 2022, retrieving over 1,300 decryption keys, giving 1,000 to past victims and 300 to current ones, preventing an estimated 130 million dollars of ransom. Russia blocked access to CIA, FBI and State Department Rewards for Justice sites after a 10 million dollar reward offer.
- Correction: Norton/Gen Digital is not Symantec's enterprise business, which Broadcom acquired in 2019.
- Passwords: Diceware words give about 12.9 bits each, so roughly ten words are needed to match 20 random characters; Steve recommends self-invented pronounceable non-words with capitalization and separators; Leo notes anything memorable has reduced entropy and suggests rule-based reconstruction.
- Argon2 support has been merged into Bitwarden's mobile, desktop/web and server code (contributed by a community developer, Quexten); OWASP ranks Argon2 over scrypt over bcrypt over PBKDF2.
- Steve would not store TOTP seeds in the password manager, since it collapses the second factor into the master password; he prefers keeping OTP seeds separate (printed QR codes).
- Overlay or mesh networks (Tailscale, ZeroTier, Nebula) are the answer to a listener asking for a fast VPN-like technology.
- Data operand independent timing: since Ice Lake (Core) and Gracemont (Atom), Intel CPUs can run instructions such as add, XOR and AES faster depending on operand values, breaking the constant-time assumption of cryptographic code; the fix is the DOITM bit (DIT on ARM). Linux 6.2 sets DIT in the ARM kernel only; Biggers proposed enabling DOITM globally on Intel. Intel says to evaluate the threat model and enable it selectively because of performance cost.
- Thomas Pornin's perspective: timing attacks are real and can be remote, but are rarely seen in the wild; constant-time code is only a start since side channels affect everything. Steve expects crypto libraries to toggle constant-time mode around sensitive operations.
