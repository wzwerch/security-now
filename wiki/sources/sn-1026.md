# Summary

The episode begins with browser and messaging hardening news: Chrome will adopt Microsoft Edge's feature that relaunches the browser with normal user rights when started as administrator (Microsoft donated the code to Chromium), and Google Messages will gain "Key Verifier" for manual public-key verification, which Steve notes Threema has offered from the start. Trend Micro's Pwn2Own Berlin 2025 will for the first time put AI infrastructure in scope, and a Fudan University preprint claims that Llama- and Qwen-based agents can self-replicate in most trials, which Steve and Leo treat cautiously (not peer reviewed, slow and resource heavy). Quick items: Microsoft extends Office support on Windows 10 to October 2028, macOS will let users block clipboard access, and Regeneron will buy 23andMe's assets for 256 million dollars. A long listener-feedback section covers a false-positive VirusTotal detection of Windhawk, whether SMS/email fallback weakens authenticator-based 2FA, Bitwarden for a small MSP, Microsoft's number-matching push prompts, typosquatted lookalike domains in business email compromise, the Signal archive bot idea, Portsentry, and ARP-based LAN monitoring. The main story is Reuters reporting that US experts found undocumented communication devices, including cellular radios, in some Chinese solar inverters and batteries, plus a November 2024 incident in which inverters were remotely disabled from China.

# Key points

- Chrome admin de-elevation: Chrome (in Canary) will automatically restart under normal user permissions if launched as administrator, unless a command-line override or automation mode is used; Microsoft contributed Edge's implementation to Chromium.
- Google Messages Key Verifier (Android 10+ later in summer 2025, Android 16 context): users compare keys by QR code or number in Google Contacts, and a changed key (for example after a SIM swap) shows the contact as no longer verified. Verification must happen out of band. Leo mentions Swiss encryption-law proposals that Proton, Threema and others say would make them leave Switzerland.
- Pwn2Own Berlin 2025 (Trend Zero Day Initiative) adds AI infrastructure as an in-scope target category, with the aim of coordinated disclosure before attackers find the bugs.
- Fudan University paper "Frontier AI systems have surpassed the self-replicating red line": using agent scaffolding, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct created a live copy of themselves in 50% and 90% of 10 trials, and in test scenarios replicated to avoid shutdown or in a chain. Steve sees AI as inherently hard to control but neutral; Leo notes it is a non-peer-reviewed preprint and replication would be resource-visible.
- Microsoft backtracked: Microsoft 365/Office apps stay supported on Windows 10 until 10 October 2028, even though Windows 10 support ends 14 October 2025 (over half of Windows desktops still ran it).
- Apple adds a macOS control to block apps from reading the clipboard, matching an iOS feature from 2020.
- Regeneron wins the 23andMe bankruptcy auction at 256 million dollars and says it will honor the privacy policies; Steve considers a pharmaceutical buyer the least worrying outcome and notes that privacy-minded users deleted their data.
- VirusTotal false positive: one of about 71 engines (VBA32, from the firm that first found Stuxnet) flagged the validly signed Windhawk installer; Steve explains why a single off-brand detection on a signed file from the author's site is a false positive, and that his own new GRC releases routinely trigger one or two, partly because DNS-query code looks suspicious in sandboxes.
- Fallback authentication methods (SMS/email) weaken the security of an authenticator app; Steve repeats praise for Microsoft allowing accounts to drop passwords entirely.
- Bitwarden is recommended for a small managed service provider: open source with active scrutiny, cross-platform sync, true end-to-end encryption, and an optional self-hosted server (Leo mentions the Rust implementation Vaultwarden).
- End-of-life routers: Steve says a Ubiquiti EdgeRouter with no exposed remote administration is a defensible exception to the FBI's "replace EOL routers" advice; exposed authentication on the WAN side is the real risk (SSH with strong keys, or an overlay such as Tailscale, is acceptable).
- Business email compromise defense: a listener proposes flagging mail to or from domains registered in the last days or weeks; Steve agrees and suggests a banner warning rather than blocking.
- Signal archive bot: a GitHub project (built on signal-cli and libsignal-service-java) already implements the kind of archiving participant Steve proposed last week; he says the world needs a trustworthy open-source version.
- Threema is still recommended for explicit, visible key management and out-of-band verification; Microsoft's number matching on push prompts counters notification fatigue by making the legitimate user type a code shown only to the real login.
- OpenSpeedTest self-hosted speed test is useful for finding flaky LAN links, which Ethernet retransmission can hide.
- Portsentry (open source) listens on unused ports and blocks or logs sources that touch them, either to stop bot enumeration on public servers or as a tripwire inside a LAN. Steve extends the idea to a passive ARP-broadcast monitor (for example on a Raspberry Pi) that would reveal any device probing for nonexistent LAN addresses.
- Flash storage retention: a listener reports cold storage preserves SSD data better than room temperature; Steve suggests a SpinRite Level 3 refresh before archiving drives.
- Main story (Reuters, 14 May 2025): US experts dismantling inverters and batteries from multiple Chinese suppliers found undocumented communication hardware including cellular radios that could bypass utility firewalls; inverters are also in batteries, heat pumps and EV chargers. Huawei was the largest inverter supplier (29% of 2022 shipments); Europe has over 200 GW of solar tied to Chinese inverters. Reuters also reported that in November 2024 inverters in the US and elsewhere were switched off from China (a dispute between Sol-Ark and Deye was involved). Responses: a Senate bill to bar DHS from buying batteries from six named Chinese firms from October 2027, utilities such as Florida Power and Light reducing Chinese inverter use, a Lithuanian law restricting remote Chinese access above 100 kW, and NATO calling for reduced dependencies. Steve notes a software bill of materials would not reveal hidden radios and that the origin and intent of the radios are unknown.
