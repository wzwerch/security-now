# Summary

Steve first notes that old episode links broke after the audio hosting changed and he fixed his redirects. The news covers Starbucks customers losing money because stolen passwords let attackers drain linked funding sources through automatic reload, an Internet-exposed management interface with default logins on Ubiquiti-branded modems that created a botnet of about 40,000 addresses, and the Venom flaw in the QEMU floppy-controller emulation, which he judges overhyped. He discusses researcher Chris Roberts' claim, made to the FBI according to an affidavit, that he reached aircraft control systems through the in-flight entertainment network, and expresses scepticism about the account while accepting that shared networking on aircraft is a worry. He covers carriers in Europe planning network-level ad blocking, an ESET shop bypass, Google flagging an old GRC test executable, and SQRL progress. The main topic is the Swiss ETH study of passive keyless entry and start (PKES): the cryptography works, but cars assume that being able to talk to the fob means it is nearby, so extending the car's low-frequency challenge over a longer path lets a thief open and start the car with the owner's key far away. Mitigations discussed are shielding the fob or removing its battery, and the long-term fix of distance bounding based on the speed of light.

# Key points

- Starbucks: reports of drained accounts when attackers log in with reused or weak passwords, move stored value, and let auto-reload refill from linked cards or PayPal; Steve suggests unique strong passwords or removing stored payment sources.
- Ubiquiti-branded modems exposed HTTP and SSH to the Internet with default credentials; Incapsula counted over 40,000 addresses, mostly in Thailand and Brazil, forming a self-sustaining botnet that was used for HTTP flood attacks.
- Venom: QEMU-derived hypervisors (KVM, Xen, VirtualBox) had a long-standing flaw in virtual floppy controller emulation that could let a guest with root privileges escape; VMware, Hyper-V and Bochs were not affected, and no exploitation was known. Steve calls the hype excessive compared with Heartbleed and Shellshock.
- In-flight hacking claim: a researcher's tweet about aircraft systems led to FBI questioning, device seizure, and an affidavit alleging he influenced an engine command; Steve and Leo find the story hard to believe but agree on the risk of passenger networks connecting to avionics. An airline's bug bounty explicitly excluded onboard systems.
- European carriers working with Shine to block mobile ads at the network level, which Leo expects could hurt free ad-supported apps.
- An ESET web shop flaw allowed obtaining a licence without payment; the researcher reported it and received a licence as thanks.
- Google flagged a test executable on GRC (packed with UPX) and cited undetermined malware; Steve deleted it and waited for re-review.
- Steve's SQRL milestone: identity management (disable, remove, update) moved into the client, with a last feature allowing sites to ask for out-of-band confirmation of risky actions.
- SpinRite corporate licensing: multi-seat use is handled by buying four copies.
- PKES research (ETH Zurich): ten cars from eight manufacturers could be opened and started by relaying signals from up to about 50 metres away without line of sight. The car sends a short-range low-frequency challenge; the fob answers on UHF at much longer range; the cryptographic challenge-response is sound, but proximity is inferred from the ability to communicate.
- Real-world theft reports (Toronto, Los Angeles, New York, Missouri) match the pattern; a relay device reportedly costs on the order of seventeen dollars.
- Mitigations: keep the fob in a signal-blocking pouch, or take out the fob battery so the car falls back on near-field short-range operation (a mechanical key may also exist); for fixes, manufacturers need distance bounding that measures round-trip time of the radio signal at nanosecond scale, which requires fobs with very stable response times. Leo suggests requiring a button press to activate the fob.
- Garage door openers are not affected because they need a button press.
