# Summary

Firefox 27 enables TLS 1.2 by default; Steve explains that a stale manual about:config override had held him back and that resetting it earns a "probably okay" from HowsMySSL. Adobe issued an emergency Flash update for an integer-underflow zero-day found in the wild by Kaspersky. Steve revisits the 2013 HoneyWords idea of planting decoy accounts as intrusion tripwires, and cites RiskIQ figures showing malicious advertisements rising from about 70,000 in 2011 to 384,000 in 2013, often undetected by antivirus, so scripting should stay blocked. He rejects a blog's speculation that the NSA made him drop CryptoLink, saying he stopped on his own for fear of being forced into a back door and will do it as freeware. He recommends Freelan (an open Hamachi-style VPN) and Windows Firewall Control. Point-of-sale breaches continue: Neiman Marcus said up to 1.1 million cards were exposed, and RSA found the "Chewbacca" keylogging and memory-scraping botnet that reaches its server through Tor. A Stanford paper shows a coin toss is slightly biased (about 51 percent to land as launched), relevant to using coin flips as entropy for SQRL identities. The main topic is Dashlane's reverse-engineering of the password policies of 100 top retailers: a third demand mixed characters, almost three quarters accept six characters or fewer, only seven offer a strength meter, half never lock out guessers, and only Apple scored perfectly.

# Key points

- Firefox 27: TLS 1.2 on by default and an old 3DES suite removed; HowsMySSL gives Firefox "probably okay" once user overrides are reset.
- Flash zero-day: integer underflow exploited in drive-by attacks; update Flash or block it, and Leo urges sites to drop Flash for HTML5.
- HoneyWords (Juels and Rivest): seed a password database with fake accounts with trivially cracked passwords so any login attempt signals database theft; Gizmodo's coverage was months late.
- Malvertising: RiskIQ counted about 70,000 malicious ads in 2011, 205,000 in 2012 and 384,000 in 2013; new ones evaded 44 of 47 antivirus engines; Google disabled ads from over 400,000 sites in 2013; Leo blames ad networks that allow automated ad purchases without vetting. Blocking scripts defeats most of them.
- CryptoLink: Steve denies any government contact; he halted the product pre-emptively; future plan is free software.
- Freelan: a cross-platform, correctly engineered Hamachi-like private network tool; Windows Firewall Control (free) adds quick filtering modes to the built-in firewall; the same author offers a USB-flash controller.
- Neiman Marcus: up to 1.1 million accounts potentially exposed at 77 of 85 stores between July and October 2013.
- Chewbacca point-of-sale botnet (found by RSA): keylogs and scrapes memory on XP-based terminals and exfiltrates over Tor hidden services.
- Coin tossing: researchers show a tossed coin lands on its starting face about 51 percent of the time, spun coins can be strongly biased, and an automated flipper reproduces results exactly; edge landings occur about once in 6,000 flips. So coin flips are poor entropy.
- Zero-knowledge proofs: Steve flags a breakthrough on obfuscating whole programs and points to Episode 363 on Ali Baba's cave.
- Touch ID over-training tip got heavy feedback.
- Dashlane survey: 73 percent of top retailers accept passwords of six characters or fewer, minimum lengths as low as one to four characters; 62 percent do not require letters and digits; 55 percent accept the ten most common passwords; 51 percent never block guessing after ten failures (including Amazon, Dell, Best Buy); 61 percent give no advice and 93 percent no strength meter; eight email forgotten passwords in plaintext; every site masks the entry. Only 10 percent scored well; Apple was perfect, Newegg, Microsoft and Chegg second, MLB.com, Karmaloop and Dick's lowest.
- Steve disputes lockout after only four tries, favouring ten, and dislikes masked fields without a show option; a Yahoo message that a new password was too similar implied plaintext storage.
- Dashlane's four recommendations: eight-plus characters with mixed classes, account lockout, onscreen advice, and an interactive strength check using a small embedded list of common passwords.
- SQRL would remove these problems because a stolen site database yields nothing usable; a new Key Flow page explains key management.

# Other segments

- Listener SpinRite story of a drive stuck at 2 percent for hours that then recovered and booted.
- Steve's ffmpeg scripts for HTML5 video, a SQRL user-interface update, a Super Bowl security-center photo that leaked a Wi-Fi password, EVE and TV asides.
