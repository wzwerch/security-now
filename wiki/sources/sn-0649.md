# Summary

Steve argues the Net Neutrality fight is far from lost: governors and the California Senate use purchasing power, 22 state attorneys general have sued the FCC over the repeal, and the EFF lists stronger levers California could use (broadband subsidies, utility-pole access, local franchises), though Leo notes interstate-commerce arguments may reach the Supreme Court. Windows had patches on 15 of 31 days in January, per Woody Leonard. Proofpoint's Smominru botnet, spreading by the year-old, long-patched EternalBlue SMBv1 flaw, had infected over 526,000 mostly server machines to mine Monero (about 8,900 coins, roughly $3.6 million). A new Adobe Flash zero-day was used by North Korean actors against South Korean targets, via Office files; Steve recommends Office Protected View and removing Flash. Autosploit chains the Shodan search API with Metasploit modules for mass exploitation, which the community criticizes. Firefox 59 will trim path and query data from referer headers in private browsing. A Fidelis proposal to smuggle data in X.509 certificate extensions strikes Steve as impractical. The closing segment reports that AV-TEST has collected 139 Meltdown/Spectre-related malware samples (not yet weaponized), proof-of-concept browser code exists for IE, Chrome and Firefox, and Intel has started releasing a corrected microcode for its NUC line.

# Key points

- Net Neutrality: California SB 460 passed the Senate 21-12 but the EFF thinks it is weak; 22 state AGs challenge the FCC's reversal of the 2015 Open Internet Order, which they argue lacked the changed conditions needed.
- Smominru: miner botnet since about May 2017, earning roughly 24 Monero per day; the sign of infection is a pegged CPU. Steve ties it to the argument for forced automatic patching.
- Flash zero-day (version 28.0.0.137 and earlier) spread through Office documents; update to 28.0.0.161 or avoid Flash; HTML5 has replaced Flash functionality. Enable Office Protected View for internet files, unsafe locations and Outlook attachments.
- Autosploit: pulls targets via Shodan, runs Metasploit modules (including a try-everything mode); critics say it hands mass exploitation to script kiddies.
- Firefox 59 strips URL path/query from referer headers in private browsing; about:config policies (network.http.referer.trimmingPolicy and XOrigin variants) allow stronger trimming. The EFF had found HealthCare.gov leaking age, ZIP and income via referers.
- X.509 extension covert channel: practical value doubtful because certificate contents are covered by the issuer's signature.
- Why encrypting the CPU cache would not stop Spectre: the leak is in access timing, not cache content.
- Meltdown and Spectre in the wild: counts rose from 77 samples on 17 January to 139 on 31 January; browsers (JavaScript, WebAssembly) are the main end-user vector; Steve says either updated firmware or a Spectre-mitigated browser protects users; Retpoline protects hardened code.
