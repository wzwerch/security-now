# Summary

Tom Merritt again substitutes for Leo. Steve begins with the revelation that RSA, the best known commercial crypto vendor, had made Dual_EC_DRBG the default random number generator in its BSAFE toolkits for years, and had advised customers to switch; Matthew Green's commentary and a New York Times editorial agree that backdoors endanger everyone because secrets cannot be kept. Linus Torvalds's answer of "no" while nodding to a question about government backdoor requests gets a mention. Steve then covers several iOS 7 lock screen weaknesses (Siri can enable Airplane mode, the Control Center route to photos and sharing, and Siri bypass on older iPhones), reminds listeners that Google stores synced Android Wi-Fi passwords in a form it can read, and gripes about iOS 7 control panel dimming, background app updates saturating his home network and the app switcher. The main topic is the spoofing of Apple's Touch ID by the Chaos Computer Club and security researcher Marc Rogers, which Steve calls spoofing, not a hack. He explains that Touch ID is a capacitive sensor feeding a Secure Enclave that stores a mathematical template, and agrees with Rogers that it is a convenience control better than no lock, advising a long passcode and temporarily disabling the finger at borders, since fingers can be compelled but memorized secrets are more protected.

# Key points

- RSA's BSAFE libraries (Crypto-C, Crypto-J, Cert-J, SSL-J and others) used Dual_EC_DRBG as the default generator; RSA told customers to move off it. Steve says its performance and doubts about its constants were known since 2006-07, and a predictable generator breaks all derived keys. Matthew Green warned that products built on BSAFE could be exposed to TLS interception.
- The New York Times editorial board argued the NSA's weakening of encryption damages commerce and trust; a bill by Representative Rush Holt would bar compelled backdoors.
- Steve's point: algorithms must be public and only keys secret; a weakness that depends on a secret will eventually leak or be independently discovered.
- iOS 7 issues: Siri accessible from the lock screen could turn on Airplane mode, defeating Find My Phone; the Control Center on the lock screen allowed a path to the photo sharing and thus account access; iPhones before the 5s allowed Siri to bypass the passcode. Mitigation was to disable lock-screen access to Control Center and Siri.
- Apple's own support text: Touch ID needs a passcode, which is required after a reboot, after 48 hours, or after five failed finger attempts; it stores only a mathematical representation of the print inside a hardware Secure Enclave on the A7 chip, never uploaded to Apple or iCloud. Steve notes this write-only design is plausible in hardware.
- Google's Android backup stores Wi-Fi network passwords, so a device can join known networks on a new tablet; since Google can decrypt it, authorities could request it.
- Chaos Computer Club defeated Touch ID with a photographed latent print turned into a thin conductive mold; Rogers repeated it and emphasized the effort: a clean full print, high-resolution photography, hours of work and over a thousand dollars of equipment, with only five attempts allowed. His verdict: Touch ID is a convenience control, not a strong one, but protects against casual theft and beats the 50 percent of users with no PIN.
- Steve's advice: Touch ID is acceptable with a strong passcode behind it; turn it off at border crossings; fingers can be compelled, memorized secrets are better protected legally. He would like the failure limit to be user-settable (for example one). Apple's 1-in-50,000 false match claim is compared to house keys not being unique.
- A site tracked whether Touch ID had been hacked yet.

# Other segments

- Apple iOS 7 interface complaints; auto-updating apps can congest a home or office network.
- Orphan Black, Homeland and Hover-versus-Network Solutions domain registrar marketing rant.
- SpinRite 6.1: first phase complete with 100 percent success on tested controllers (including software RAID setups), AHCI next, free upgrade for SpinRite 6 owners.
- Steve plans to present his authentication system next week.
