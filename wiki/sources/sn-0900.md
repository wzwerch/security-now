# Summary

Steve opens with Australia's new Privacy Legislation Amendment bill, which raises maximum penalties for negligent data protection from about 2.2 million to at least 50 million Australian dollars (or 30% of adjusted revenue), and which also applies to foreign firms doing business there. He notes Ireland has now fined all three main Meta platforms (Facebook 285 million euros for the 2021 scraping incident, Instagram 405 million, WhatsApp 228 million). The Medibank data from REvil was dumped on the dark web covering 9.7 million customers, and Steve observes that a raw unstructured dump is less immediately useful than an organized database. Mozilla removed the three TrustCor root certificates after reporting linked the Panamanian CA to a spyware and interception contractor ecosystem. Google disclosed that Android platform certificates from Samsung, LG, MediaTek and Revoview were used to sign adware, a puzzling misuse of extremely sensitive keys. Further items cover a South Dakota executive order banning TikTok on state systems, Albania prosecuting five IT staff after the Iranian intrusion, Android's drop in memory-safety bugs after adopting Rust, a ninth Chrome zero-day of the year, and Anker's Eufy cameras found to stream unencrypted footage via the cloud contrary to marketing claims. The main topic is LastPass's second incident in four months, in which data from the August 2022 breach was used to reach a shared cloud storage service. Steve's conclusion is that vault contents remain protected by client-side encryption, so he is staying with LastPass while waiting for details.

# Key points

- Australia's Privacy Act reform: fines up to the greater of A$50M or 30% of adjusted revenue; applies to non-Australian companies operating in Australia; enforced by the OAIC. Steve sees it as the only effective incentive but is uneasy that vendors of buggy software stay unaccountable.
- Ireland's Data Protection Commission fined Meta 285M euros over the 2021 Facebook scraping of about 530M users; Instagram and WhatsApp had been fined earlier, so all three main Meta properties have now been hit.
- Medibank (Australia's largest private health insurer) refused to pay REvil; stolen data on about 9.7M current and former customers (names, contact data, Medicare numbers for ahm customers, passports and visa details for international students, some health claim codes) was published. Steve notes the dump is raw and hard to interpret without the database structure.
- The Vatican website was knocked offline by a DDoS attack by pro-Russian hacktivists after criticism of Moscow by the Pope.
- Mozilla (and other browsers) removed TrustCor's three roots from its 166-root store; reporting tied TrustCor to Packet Forensics and interception services; Steve explains how a trusted CA can enable man-in-the-middle interception of all TLS traffic.
- Google's report on Android platform certificates: ten leaked certificates from major vendors signed adware; Rapid7 noted that state actors would not waste such keys on adware, so the incident does not add up; keys were rotated.
- South Dakota Executive Order 2022-10 bans TikTok for state agencies, employees and contractors; Steve laments the fragmentation of the Internet along national lines.
- Albanian prosecutors sought house arrest for five government IT staff accused of not patching and not detecting an Iranian intruder; Steve argues breaches can occur without negligence (citing the Lapsus$ review by the US Cyber Safety Review Board) and that prosecution will make IT roles hard to fill.
- Android 13 is the first release where most new code is memory-safe (Rust, Java, Kotlin); memory-safety vulnerabilities reported have fallen to less than half of previous levels.
- Another Chrome zero-day (type confusion in V8) patched, the ninth of the year; Black Hat USA 2022 videos are finally published.
- Eufy (Anker) cameras: despite claims of local-only storage, end-to-end encryption and on-device AI, researchers and The Verge streamed unencrypted video from the cloud with a media player; the URL combined a base64 serial number, a timestamp, an unvalidated token and a four-digit random value. Steve's lesson: unless you designed it yourself you do not know what a connected camera does.
- LastPass second incident: unusual activity in a cloud storage service shared with affiliate GoTo; attackers used information from the August 2022 incident; Mandiant engaged. The company states passwords remain encrypted under its zero-knowledge architecture. Steve stresses that a cloud sync breach should not expose properly encrypted vaults; the real risk is client-side code. Details of what was taken were not yet disclosed.
- Steve notes the passkeys ecosystem lacks a cross-platform sync service, an opportunity for password managers.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a Cisco switch whose factory-reset button sits above the RJ45 port so a rubber plug boot can hold it down.
- Hardware tip: tiny, cheap ESP32-C3 (RISC-V) modules and the LinkStar-H68K router board for about 119 dollars; Steve does not vouch for it.
- Commentary on Elon Musk's explanation of why he bought Twitter.
- Recommends the Alex Stamos appearance on This Week in Google (episode 692).
- SpinRite 6.1 alpha testing: a listener's SSD scan time dropped almost sixfold after a Level 3 rewrite pass, supporting Steve's theory that slow regions on SSDs reflect hidden error correction, a plan for future SpinRite versions.
