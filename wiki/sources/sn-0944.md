# Summary

The planned second part of the NSA/CISA misconfiguration advisory is postponed because of the HTTP/2 Rapid Reset disclosure. After ValiDrive follow-ups (27,603 downloads in ten days, now GRC's most downloaded freeware ahead of the DNS Benchmark), Steve answers listener questions about passkey export, hobby browsers, SpinRite 6.1 pre-release access and OTP apps. The main segment reviews how Cloudflare, Google and AWS disclosed a DDoS technique that abuses HTTP/2 stream cancellation (RST_STREAM) to keep a connection busy with unlimited requests, producing the largest attacks recorded (Cloudflare 201 million requests per second from only about 20,000 bots; Google nearly 400 million). Steve disputes Cloudflare's framing as a protocol zero-day: it is the abuse of an efficiency feature that is worst for multi-stage cloud proxy architectures, and the practical defence is tracking connection statistics and closing abusive connections or blocking the (non-spoofable, TCP) source IPs.

# Key points

- ValiDrive: 27,603 downloads in ten days; freebie USB drives are usually fraud against the buyer of the giveaway items first and the recipient second; example of a declared 32 GB drive with only 512 MB real storage. Runs on Windows, including in Parallels on ARM Macs; not WINE-compatible due to USB event handling.
- Passkey debate: listeners note that easy export could aid phishing, or that device-bound passkeys allow per-device revocation; Steve expects FIDO2 passkeys to replace passwords eventually but slowly.
- SpinRite 6.1 pre-release is available to owners via GRC's prerelease form (DOS executable, 800 registered testers, no data loss reported); VeraCrypt can fill a drive with encrypted noise; Steve uses OTP Auth while Leo recommends 2FAS.
- HTTP/2 Rapid Reset (CVE-2023-44487): the client opens streams and immediately cancels them with RST_STREAM, never hitting the concurrent stream limit (typically 100), while the server still does parsing and header work; cost is asymmetric.
- Cloudflare mitigated 1,100+ attacks over 10 million requests per second since late August 2023 and 184 above its previous 71 million record; the peak came from roughly 20,000 bots, whereas botnets of hundreds of thousands exist.
- Google reports HTTP/2 has been the main layer-7 DDoS vector since 2021; recommended mitigation is connection-level tracking (for example heuristics on cancelled-request ratios) and GOAWAY/closing the TCP connection rather than blocking individual streams.
- Defences: put a DDoS-mitigation service in front, patch or tune web servers (NGINX working on one), or disable HTTP/2 temporarily; IPs cannot be spoofed over TCP so offenders can be throttled or blacklisted.
- HTTP/2 is offered by about 39% of websites; HTTP/3 (QUIC over UDP) by about a quarter.
- Google also rewrites links in Calendar invites; a listener theory that Docs link rewriting protects referrer privacy is rejected because of the unique tracking parameters.
