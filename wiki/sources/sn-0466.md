# Summary

Steve corrects last week's remark that iOS 7 had not been jailbroken (Zdziarski's paper was months old), notes Apple's response to the pairing claims, and updates canvas fingerprinting: browserleaks.com saw 1,847 distinct signatures, about 11 bits, better than the study's under 6 bits but still not a unique identifier and dependent on JavaScript. Open Whisper Systems released Signal for iPhone, interoperable with Android's RedPhone, as the start of a unified encrypted voice and text platform. Bluebox disclosed the Android "Fake ID" flaw: since Android 2.1 (2010) the system did not verify certificate chain signatures on app signing certificates, so malware could impersonate privileged apps such as Adobe Flash or Google Wallet; Google patched it and found no exploitation in its store. Steve then reads the final Verizon rebuttal, which recalls Level 3's own 2005 free-peering argument against Cogent, explaining why peering expects balanced traffic. A listener found that Avast's "scan secure connections" setting substitutes its own certificate for GRC's, which Steve calls a man-in-the-middle presence that could be abused. He introduces resistive RAM (memristor, Crossbar), claiming up to 1 TB in the area of 16 GB of flash, and notes SpinRite revived a dead SSD. Q&A covers how SpiderOak can offer zero knowledge while recovering data with a password, Boxcryptor and Viivo, XP embedded updates still working for some, SQRL's "hint" quick re-authentication, router topologies for IoT isolation, minimum password strength in SQRL clients, why OpenSSL's PRNG design is weak, and using an odometer as a one-time password.

# Key points

- Signal (iOS) interoperates with RedPhone (Android) for end-to-end encrypted calls; text compatibility with TextSecure and a combined Android app are planned.
- Android Fake ID: signature verification in certificate chains was missing from Android 2.1 onward, letting a malicious app claim identity of trusted privileged ones (Flash plugin rights, NFC wallet access); responsibly disclosed ahead of Black Hat; Bluebox offers a scanner.
- Verizon's David Young cites Level 3's 2005 Cogent dispute to argue settlement-free peering needs balanced flows; Level 3 cut Cogent off then, so Steve learns why imbalance matters; Verizon and Netflix are arranging direct connections.
- Avast, like some appliances, inserts its own signing certificate to scan HTTPS; if the certificate were extracted, outside attackers could mimic it. Steve finds this alarming.
- Resistive RAM (memristor) stores bits in a two-terminal crossbar of changing resistance, is dense, stackable and fast; Crossbar claims 20 times the performance and lower power than NAND.
- SpinRite revived a dead SSD; Steve says anything SpinRite can fix, it would have prevented; Level 2 preferred for SSDs to limit writes.
- Zero-knowledge cloud storage means the password-derived key encrypts the real data key on the client, so recovery with only a password is plausible; password strength and a memory-hard KDF are critical. Snowden recommended SpiderOak.
- Boxcryptor (client-side encryption for any cloud) and Viivo (PKWARE, public-key file encryption) are Steve's current examples; Leo uses Transporter appliances.
- XP embedded update trick still works for many users while not for Steve's machine; no explanation.
- SQRL "hint": after the five-second EnScrypt (scrypt-based) unlock, SQRL keeps the first n characters re-encrypted for one-second quick reauthentication within a session; mistype once and the full password is required; flushed on screen blank, hibernation or timeout; can be disabled.
- Websites cannot see the strength of the SQRL client password; Steve built a complexity meter but cannot force other clients to enforce strength.
- Router isolation: two routers in series each have ARP spoofing risks; the strongest layout is a router behind the modem feeding two separate WiFi routers.
- OpenSSL's PRNG would benefit from using hardware sources directly (RDTSC, RdRand) as SQRL does.
- A reader suggests using a fleet card odometer reading as a monotonic one-time password; Steve calls it a nice observation but likely an accounting control.
- Congress passed a bill allowing phone unlocking after contract; awaits presidential signature.
