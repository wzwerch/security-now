# Summary

The last episode of 2025 centers on money-driven attacks. Chainalysis reports that North Korea stole about $2.02 billion in crypto in 2025 (up 51%, total about $6.75 billion), mostly via a few huge heists such as Bybit's $1.5 billion loss through a compromised Safe{Wallet} multisig provider, and increasingly through embedded IT workers and fake recruiters; Steve advises keeping crypto in offline wallets. Amazon describes a cryptomining campaign using stolen IAM credentials that launched miners within ten minutes and set termination protection on EC2 instances to hinder cleanup, while Trend Micro saw exposed Docker remote API servers hijacked to run the SRBMiner program; Steve's rule is never to rely on the strength of remote authentication. XLab's Kimwolf analysis describes an Android TV-box botnet (more than 1.8 million devices, near 30 Tbps DDoS capacity, linked to Aisuru, using DNS over TLS, elliptic-curve-signed C2 and Ethereum Name Service fallback) whose C2 domain briefly outranked Google in Cloudflare's popularity list. Steve reports on DNS Benchmark release 4, a Steam and Proton trick for running Windows apps on Linux, and a discussion of who besides Let's Encrypt offers free ACME certificates (Google, ZeroSSL with limits) and who funds ISRG. The main story is GhostPoster: 17 Firefox extensions (about 50,000 installs, e.g. "Free VPN Forever") hide a loader after a marker in their PNG logo, fetch obfuscated payloads from remote servers, hijack affiliate links, strip security headers, bypass CAPTCHAs and inject hidden iframes. Steve advises minimizing browser extensions and distrusting free VPN add-ons.

# Key points

- Chainalysis: DPRK theft of $2.02B in 2025 over fewer incidents, 76% of service compromises, 45-day laundering cycle via Chinese-language services and bridges; personal wallet compromises reached 158,000 incidents affecting 80,000 victims but $713M; top three hacks were 69% of service losses; the largest hack is now 1,000x the median.
- DPRK tactics: infiltration via IT workers and fake recruiter or investor outreach that harvests credentials, source code, VPN and SSO access. Leo notes crypto theft is a major share of North Korea's hard currency.
- Amazon GuardDuty found a campaign (from November 2, 2025) using compromised IAM credentials against ECS and EC2, miners running in 10 minutes, persistence via ModifyInstanceAttribute with DisableApiTermination, a malicious Docker Hub image with over 100,000 pulls, and boto3 scripting; no AWS vulnerability involved.
- Docker: the daemon listens on a local Unix socket by default; exposing the remote API without TLS lets attackers run containers; Trend Micro saw attackers using gRPC over h2c to evade defenses and deploy SRBMiner. Leo advises hiding self-hosted services behind a VPN such as Tailscale.
- Kimwolf (XLab): NDK-built Android botnet with DDoS, proxy forwarding, reverse shell and file management; C2 domain topped Cloudflare rankings; 2.7 million distinct IPs over three days with an estimate of more than 1.8 million devices; Brazil 14.6%, India 12.7%, USA 9.6% of infections; issued 1.7 billion DDoS commands between November 19 and 22; observed attacks near 30 Tbps and 2.9 Gpps; domain takedowns drove it to ENS; shares infection scripts and operators with Aisuru. Advice: buy TV boxes from reputable sources, update firmware, avoid weak passwords and unknown APKs.
- Free certificates: Let's Encrypt is run by non-profit ISRG funded by sponsors (Google, Mozilla, Cisco, AWS, Meta and others), not users; Google also offers a free ACME service; DigiCert's old stance against free DV certificates is outdated; all CAs must support ACME as lifetimes drop to 47 days; Steve plans to move to Let's Encrypt and donate. EV certificates (and BIMI logos which require them) have lost practical value.
- GhostPoster (Koi Security): extension reads its own logo.png and looks for a "===" marker followed by JavaScript loader; loader polls liveupdt and dealctr domains every 48 hours with only 10% chance of fetching the payload; payload is case-swapped, digit-swapped and base64 encoded; functions include affiliate hijacking on Taobao and JD.com, Google Analytics tracking injection, stripping Content-Security-Policy and X-Frame-Options, CAPTCHA bypass, hidden iframes; activation delayed six-plus days. 17 extensions with names such as free-vpn-forever, weather-best-forecast and dark-reader-for-ff; related reports of Urban VPN Proxy (8 million users) harvesting AI chats and FreeVPN.One capturing screenshots. Extensions were still live at time of reporting.
- Listener points: Jeff Root argues anonymous-age-verification apps are moot while sites track users, favoring regulation of data brokers; New Yorker "on the Internet nobody knows you're a dog" cartoon; Netdata plugin for pfSense suggested.

- GRC: DNS Benchmark (about $9.95) at release 4, Windows 11 Smart App Control briefly blocked one user; plans for more small products before SpinRite 7.
