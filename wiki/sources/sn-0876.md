# Summary

Steve first answers the previous week's puzzle: double encryption with independent 256-bit keys needs about 2^512 trials because ciphertext from a good cipher looks random, so no divide-and-conquer feedback exists (unlike the 2011 WPS PIN flaw that leaked whether the first half of the PIN was right). He then reviews the passkey landscape: press coverage and FIDO Alliance officials admit there is no bulk export of passkeys, so lock-in is a real risk, and he looks at LastPass's vague passwordless messaging and Bitwarden, which supports WebAuthn for its own login but not yet as a passkey authenticator. Mozilla made Total Cookie Protection the Firefox default, but Steve's tests with GRC's Cookie Forensics page showed third-party cookies still being accepted. News items: a record 26 million requests per second HTTPS DDoS stopped by Cloudflare, the DFSCoerce NTLM relay coercion via MS-DFSNM, a Safari WebKit use-after-free reintroduced in 2016 and fixed in 2022, a forced update of 1M+ WordPress sites for a Ninja Forms flaw, and a FastJson deserialization bug. The closing segment collects criticism of Microsoft from Tenable, Orca Security and others, and explains how the loss of in-house hardware testing shifted testing onto customers.

# Key points

- Double encryption: strength multiplies (2^256 x 2^256) rather than adds, as long as an attacker cannot tell a correct partial decryption from random data.
- Passkeys: FIDO Alliance says batch export is "a future iteration" and fears attackers would target export; articles in Fast Company and 9to5Mac warn of ecosystem lock-in. Steve wants dynamic cross-vendor sync, probably via third-party managers.
- LastPass announced a passwordless webinar with unclear detail; Bitwarden is a FIDO Alliance member and already accepts FIDO2 for two-step login, with passkey-authenticator support as a feature request. WebAuthn optionally supports Curve25519, which could allow SQRL-style deterministic keys.
- Firefox Total Cookie Protection partitions third-party cookies per site rather than blocking them; in Steve's and Leo's tests Firefox 101 still accepted third-party cookies unless all were blocked, while Chromium-based browsers behaved well. Test tool: GRC Cookie Forensics.
- Cloudflare mitigated a 26M requests/second HTTPS DDoS from 5,067 hijacked cloud servers; a separate botnet of 730,000 devices (weak IoT gear) produced under 1M requests/second. Steve advises keeping IoT devices on an isolated network.
- DFSCoerce (found by Filip Dragovic): abuses the MS-DFSNM RPC interface to coerce domain controllers to authenticate to an attacker relay, similar to PetitPotam; mitigations are Extended Protection for Authentication, SMB signing, and disabling HTTP on AD certificate services.
- Safari WebKit CVE-2022-22620 (use-after-free, CVSS 8.8): fixed in 2013, reintroduced by huge refactoring commits in late 2016, exploited in the wild and fixed in February 2022 (Project Zero).
- Ninja Forms (1M+ sites) unauthenticated object injection, CVSS 9.8, force-updated; FastJson deserialization bug (CVSS 8.1) fixed by disabling AutoType by default after a block-list approach failed.
- Microsoft criticism: Follina fixed in June Patch Tuesday after initially being dismissed; Tenable accused Microsoft of silently patching Azure Synapse issues and not assigning CVEs when no customer action is needed; Orca Security needed three patches over about five months for a cross-tenant Azure Synapse flaw. Microsoft accounted for over a third of KEV entries.
- Former Microsoft test engineer account: layoffs of Windows test teams, shift to virtual machines and telemetry from Insiders, effectively making users unpaid testers; Steve stays on Windows but expects continued decline.
- NIST's IoT definition (network plus sensor/actuator) excludes routers; Steve uses a broader notion of unattended networked devices.
- SQRL could ask sites to disable all non-SQRL authentication, something passkeys do not do; the SQRL OAuth gateway for TWiT's forum was restored.
