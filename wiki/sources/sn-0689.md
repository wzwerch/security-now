# Summary

The headline story is a 16-page paper from two Radboud University researchers who reverse engineered firmware of seven popular self-encrypting SSDs (Crucial MX100/200/300 and Samsung 840 EVO, 850 EVO, T3, T5) and found that all of them had critical flaws; in several models the data could be recovered without any password. Steve explains the correct design (a random per-drive key, encrypting the master key with a password-derived key) and notes the drives instead merely guarded a stored key behind the password. Because BitLocker defaults to hardware encryption when a drive advertises it, BitLocker users were affected too; Microsoft published guidance to force software encryption via group policy, and firmware fixes exist. Earlier items: Windows 1809 is being re-released slowly; Privacy International filed GDPR complaints against seven data brokers and ad-tech firms; the UPnP Hunter botnet abuses a Broadcom-chip UPnP flaw across many router brands, and Steve points to GRC's port probe and UPnP exposure test; a researcher disclosed a VirtualBox E1000 NAT guest-to-host escape without notifying Oracle; Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 mobile app uses DNS over HTTPS; and Microsoft warned that BitLocker keys in RAM are exposed to DMA attacks via FireWire or Thunderbolt.

# Key points

- Research "Self-Encrypting Deception": 100% of examined SSDs had weaknesses; causes include keys not cryptographically bound to passwords, constant keys due to a firmware bug, exposed JTAG on five of seven drives, firmware readily obtainable, and wear leveling leaving an old key copy readable.
- Opal (TCG) standard is overly complex; researchers urge simplicity, public reference implementations and published crypto designs.
- Hardware encryption is not inherently safer: the OS often still keeps the key in RAM for suspend/resume and drives are not hardened.
- Recommendations: update firmware; set BitLocker policy to disable hardware-based encryption (Windows 8 and later) and re-enable BitLocker; manage-bde -status shows whether hardware or software encryption is in use; on Windows 7 use VeraCrypt.
- Microsoft advisory: BitLocker with TPM-only is exposed to DMA attacks via 1394 or Thunderbolt while powered on or in standby.
- Privacy International complaints name Acxiom, Equifax, Experian, Oracle, Criteo, Quantcast and Tapad before data protection authorities in France, Ireland and the UK.
- UPnP Hunter botnet: scans port 5431 then UPnP on UDP 1900; used for spam proxying and DDoS; GRC found over 54,000 exposed UPnP routers via its test; check port 5431 and UPnP exposure.
- VirtualBox up to 5.2.20 with Intel PRO/1000 MT NAT: full-disclosure zero-day by a researcher unhappy with bug bounties; workaround is another NIC type or network mode.
- Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 app sets up a VPN-style profile to send DNS over HTTPS or TLS; Cloudflare promises no logging with annual KPMG audits; GRC's DNS Benchmark and DNS Spoofability test were revisited.
- Windows Defender sandboxing works only on Windows 10; setting the variable back to 0 disables it.

# Other segments
