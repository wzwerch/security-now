# Summary

This episode skips small news items for three larger topics. First, Google widens its Play bug bounty to every app with 100 million or more installs and adds a HackerOne-run data protection reward for data-abuse evidence in apps, OAuth projects and Chrome extensions; HackerOne also reports five more bug-bounty millionaires. Second, Google Project Zero's Ian Beer describes five iOS exploit chains (14 vulnerabilities, iOS 10 through 12) used from hacked websites for at least two years to drop a memory-resident implant that stole messages, photos, location and keychain tokens; Apple fixed the zero-day within a week of notification in iOS 12.1.4, and Leo adds that press reports tied the sites to targeting of Uyghurs. Third, the bulk of the show covers the ransomware surge: 13 new victims in a week (mostly school districts), the Rockville Centre insurance-negotiated payment, Baltimore's costs, the dental backup provider PerCSoft, and the identification of Sodinokibi (REvil) as behind the Texas and dental attacks. Steve predicts offline, ransomware-aware backup becoming standard and OS-level detection of mass encryption.

# Key points

- Google Play Security Reward Program now covers any app with 100M+ installs, with Google relaying findings to developers; the App Security Improvement program helped fix 75,000+ apps in 2018; total GPSRP payouts about $265,000.
- New Developer Data Protection Reward Program (with HackerOne) targets policy-violating data abuse; a single report could earn up to $50,000, and offending apps or extensions are removed.
- Six HackerOne hackers are now bug bounty millionaires; HackerOne cited Steve's earlier episode on the bug bounty business.
- Project Zero/TAG found indiscriminate watering-hole sites exploiting iPhones with five chains (seven browser, five kernel, two sandbox-escape bugs); Chrome on iOS would also have been affected through the WebKit flaws.
- The implant ran as root only in RAM (lost on reboot), polled its server every 60 seconds, and could exfiltrate WhatsApp/Telegram/iMessage plaintext databases, contacts, photos, live location and the keychain including long-lived Google tokens that persist access after the implant is gone.
- Beer argues the debate should focus on the low marginal cost of monitoring entire populations, not on million-dollar exploit prices; Apple was told on Feb 1 and shipped 12.1.4 on Feb 7, 2019.
- Texas: 22 municipalities, nine identified; Steve still favors a common-service-provider explanation; Bleeping Computer later puts the Texas demand at $2.5 million.
- 149 publicly reported ransomware attacks in 2019 so far, 30 against education; schools are the second largest victim group after local governments; the FBI saw 1,493 cases last year, showing under-reporting.
- Rockville Centre School District: demand of $176,000 negotiated by its insurer to $88,000 with a $10,000 deductible; ProPublica argues insurers fuel the trend. Baltimore (RobbinHood) faces a clean-up above $10 million.
- PerCSoft / Digital Dental Record (DDS Safe): ransomware hit remote management software used for backups at about 400 dental practices on Aug 26; a decryptor was obtained, with reports that the ransom was paid.
- Sodinokibi (REvil): fourth most prevalent family at 12.5% (behind Ryuk, Phobos, Dharma); run as ransomware-as-a-service with 60-70% affiliate splits, avoids CIS countries, likely linked in some way to the defunct GandCrab operation; network-wide decryptors averaged $150,000.
- Steve's forecast: nightly offline image backups, ransomware-aware backup products and OS behavior-based blocking of bulk file encryption; he describes hiding an offline backup volume as his own practice.
