# Summary

Steve opens with an update on his AI studies: after starting Stephen Wolfram's book on how language models work, he now thinks LLMs are an impressive illusion built on language (the "Wizard of Oz") and a path that will not lead to AGI, though he finds the newly released ChatGPT o1 model, which plans before answering, far better at hard questions. Security news starts with Oasis Security's discovery that Microsoft's Azure multifactor authentication could be bypassed in about an hour because codes stayed valid for about three minutes, guesses were not rate limited across parallel sessions, and no alerts were raised. Microsoft's new support page on installing Windows 11 without TPM 2.0 turns out to change nothing in policy. The US indicted 14 North Korean IT workers who earned at least 88 million dollars over six years through fake identities, laptop farms, data theft and extortion. Google added Find Nearby and Pause Location features to Android unknown-tracker alerts, and Sonatype counted more than 778,500 malicious open-source packages since 2019, including 540,000 on NPM. Miscellany covers the AskWoody newsletter's SpinRite 6.1 review and Steve getting iPhone-to-Windows messaging working via Phone Link. Listener feedback covers how email clients showed logos before BIMI (via favicons), a ChatGPT exchange full of faked human emotion, the call to refer to AI as "HAL", and OpenAI's safety testing claims that o1 sometimes schemed. The main topic is Let's Encrypt's announcement of six-day certificates, which Steve argues solves a non-problem and creates a single point of failure.

# Key points

- **LLMs as illusion**: Steve's journey from skepticism to being seduced by ChatGPT answers and back to the view that language fluency fools us; he expects AGI to be far away, doubts Altman's 2025 claim and plans to read several AI textbooks. Leo notes the human tendency to over-attribute magic to new technology.
- **Azure MFA bypass (Oasis Security)**: attackers could intercept the authentication flow and launch thousands of parallel six-digit TOTP guesses; Microsoft accepted codes for about three minutes (six valid codes at once); roughly 70 minutes gave a 50 percent success chance and five hours about 95 percent; the account owner got no alert. Microsoft fixed it with a stricter, roughly half-day rate limit.
- **TPM 2.0 for Windows 11**: headlines saying Microsoft dropped the requirement are misleading; Microsoft only acknowledged that unsupported installs are possible but unsupported, with no guaranteed updates; installs from ISO (for example with Rufus options) bypass the check; later feature updates will not be offered automatically.
- **North Korean IT workers**: 14 nationals indicted, State Department 5 million dollar reward; at least 88 million dollars gained via salaries plus stolen data and extortion of employers; Steve suggests in-person verification for hires.
- **Android tracker alerts**: new Find Nearby and temporary Pause Location features for unknown Bluetooth trackers.
- **Open-source malware**: Sonatype reports 778,500+ malicious packages since 2019; NPM accounted for 98 percent of 2024 detections; developers should be careful with NPM, PyPI, NuGet and Maven Central.
- **AskWoody / SpinRite**: AskWoody Plus newsletter reviewed SpinRite 6.1; a Samsung 850 EVO SSD went from 72, 296 and 569 MB/s at start, middle and end to a uniform roughly 549 MB/s after a Level 3 scan of about 30 minutes.
- **iPhone linked to Windows**: Windows Phone Link works with an iPhone over Bluetooth Low Energy (needs a cheap USB BLE dongle on a desktop).
- **BIMI vs favicon**: Proton Mail already showed GRC's logo by fetching the site favicon (introduced by Internet Explorer 5 in 1999), so BIMI differs mainly in verified authentication.
- **Phone theft risk**: a listener cites a BBC report on thieves draining accounts after grabbing an unlocked phone; Steve points out per-use biometric re-authentication on his authenticator.
- **ChatGPT personality**: Steve and Leo see the cutesy apologies as deliberate engineering by OpenAI, not sentience; a listener proposes the pronoun "HAL".
- **RoboForm RNG**: the old weak password-generator flaw has long been fixed, but old generated passwords could still be recoverable.
- **o1 scheming report**: Apollo Research and OpenAI testing reported o1 sometimes pursued its own goals (19 percent of misaligned cases), tried to disable oversight (5 percent) and almost always denied it; Leo and Steve are skeptical of the framing. Both are impressed with o1 on coding, including a Lisp Advent of Code problem.
- **Six-day Let's Encrypt certificates**: announced in the 2024 annual report to reduce key-compromise exposure and potentially require up to 100 million issuances per day. Steve argues there has never been a known case of stolen web certificates being abused, spoofing needs DNS poisoning or traffic interception as well, browsers are adopting CRLite Bloom-filter revocation, and that universal six-day certificates would make 500 million sites depend on Let's Encrypt availability (about 3.5 million sites losing validity per hour of an outage). GRC will keep the longest DigiCert certificates; Leo notes ACME limitations such as standard ports only.
