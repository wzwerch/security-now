# Summary

Back from the SQRL speaking tour and the Boston authentication panel, Steve opens with a long ransomware update: hundreds of US schools hit in 2019, Louisiana's emergency response, the hearing-aid maker Demant facing losses up to $95 million against a $14.6 million insurance policy, an Alabama hospital system that paid the Ryuk operators, and Australian hospitals forced to manual operation. McAfee's analysis showing that Sodinokibi shares code, affiliate-ID design and infrastructure patterns with GandCrab supports the idea that the same developers continue the business. Steve then rants about Microsoft extending paid Windows 7 Extended Security Updates to businesses of any size, and covers a trivially exploitable unauthenticated vBulletin remote code execution zero-day, with a pointed remark about Zerodium customers having had it for years. The episode ends with Sophos research on WannaCry today: thousands of unpatched machines still spread it, many variants have the kill switch removed by hex editing, but all sampled variants carry a corrupt encryption payload archive, which is why no second outbreak occurred.

# Key points

- Armor counted 500+ US schools hit by ransomware in 2019 (Emsisoft counted over 1,000 in 62 incidents); Connecticut had the most districts hit; Louisiana declared a state emergency and refused to pay; highest demand reported was $1.6 million (Crowder College). A DHS cyber hunt and incident response team bill passed the Senate.
- Demant (largest hearing-aid maker) was knocked offline across production and clinics from early September; estimated loss up to $95 million; insurance cover $14.6 million.
- DCH Health System (Alabama, three hospitals) paid for a Ryuk decryption key and was restoring thousands of systems one by one while diverting new patients; seven hospitals in Victoria, Australia also went to manual operation.
- Sodinokibi/REvil: McAfee found the same affiliate-ID/sub-ID scheme, near-identical code blocks and nearly identical randomized C2 URL generation as GandCrab; top GandCrab affiliates vanished before Sodinokibi launched; GandCrab had 292 registered affiliates and kept 30-40% of proceeds. BleepingComputer concludes the GandCrab programmers likely run Sodinokibi, though it is far more secretive.
- Microsoft will sell Windows 7 ESU to businesses of any size from December 1, 2019 through January 2023 (price rises yearly); Steve criticizes Windows 10's bundled consumer apps and "post-license monetization", Leo suggests Linux.
- vBulletin: an 18-line proof-of-concept for unauthenticated remote command execution (all versions from 5.x) was posted on September 23; DEF CON took its forum offline; Zerodium's CEO tweeted that the bug had been sold for years. Steve isolates his XenForo forums on a separate machine behind its own firewall.
- WannaCry: spread via EternalBlue with a kill switch domain registered by Marcus Hutchins; Sophos recorded 5.1 million detections in Q4 2018 and 4.3 million in August 2019, almost all from unpatched hosts. Of 12,481 unique files, the original appeared only 40 times; the top variants had the kill switch removed (URL deleted or altered, check skipped or NOP'd), but every sample had a corrupt password-protected ZIP holding the encryptor, a defect traced to a copy uploaded two days after the original outbreak. The kill switch still blocks tens of millions of lookups a month.
