# Summary

The lead story is a paper presented at EUROCRYPT 2025 by five cryptographers that formally analyses Telegram's home-grown key exchange; they could prove security only by adding unusual assumptions, and their closing section is a model statement of why modern cryptography insists on modular, easily reasoned-about designs. Steve then corrects Elon Musk's claim that Ukrainian IPs attacked X (a DDoS-for-hire group, Dark Storm Team, claimed it and the traffic came from many countries), explains that the Firefox root certificate expiry only hurt people on outdated builds, and runs through AI-written malicious GitHub repos, voice-cloning tools without consent checks, Patch Tuesday (six exploited zero-days) and an Apple WebKit zero-day. The FBI warns about free online file converters that deliver malware. Google publishes a legislative proposal on age signals that contrasts with Utah's law; Kazakhstan mandates child SIM cards; Spain adopts heavy fines for unlabelled AI content. Google declined to deny receiving a UK technical capability notice. A major segment covers the Windows "best-fit" Unicode bypass behind PHP-CGI CVE-2024-4577, now widely exploited. After listener feedback the episode introduces FLIPPYR.AM, an ISO from a Chaos Communication Congress team that tests a PC for Rowhammer and optionally uploads anonymous results to measure real-world prevalence.

# Key points

- Telegram: "Analysis of the Telegram Key Exchange" (authors from King's College, ETH Zurich, Tel Aviv University and Amazon, 104+ pages) proves security only after introducing novel assumptions, citing SHA-1 use and missing proper key derivation; they find a hypothetical attack on current server behaviour. Steve: probably secure enough in practice but designed so that it cannot easily be proven; Pavel Durov's 2014 prize contest is beside the point.
- X outage: attributed by groups tracking it to Dark Storm Team; traffic came from the US, Vietnam, Brazil and others, not mainly Ukraine.
- Mozilla's embedded signing root certificate expired on 14 March 2025; Firefox 128+ and ESR 115.13+ (released July 2024) were already fixed, so only long-unrestarted or unmaintained installs (kiosks) risk add-on, DRM and content-signing breakage.
- Trend Micro: AI-generated descriptions on malicious GitHub repos spreading SmokeLoader and Lumma Stealer to steal developer credentials.
- Consumer Reports: four of six leading voice-cloning apps (Speechify, Lovo, PlayHT, Descript) lack consent safeguards; Leo and Steve dismiss voice biometrics as authentication.
- March Patch Tuesday: 58 flaws, six exploited zero-days across Win32k, NTFS, Fast FAT and Management Console; Apple patched an exploited WebKit zero-day.
- FBI warning: free document converters and MP3/MP4 downloaders that work as advertised can carry malware or harvest data from uploaded files; report to IC3.
- Meta lost its final appeal in South Korea over sharing data of 3.3 million users (about 4.6 million USD fine).
- Google's proposal: share age signals only with developers who need them, only with user or parent consent, ban personalised ads to minors, and give parents a central dashboard; it criticises Utah's approach (backed by Meta). Steve sees Apple and Google converging on similar parent-controlled designs.
- Kazakhstan requires special child SIM cards with filtering and location reporting; Spain's AI-labelling law allows fines up to 35 million euros or 7 percent of global revenue.
- UK order: Google told Senator Wyden's office it could not disclose a technical capability notice if it had one, which Steve reads as a likely sign it received one; Steve and Leo state they have not. UK civil-liberties groups and politicians ask for Apple's closed Investigatory Powers Tribunal hearing to be public.
- PHP: CVE-2024-4577 (Orange Tsai / DEVCORE) is an argument-injection bug in PHP-CGI on Windows that bypasses a 2012 fix through Windows Best-Fit character conversion; default XAMPP is vulnerable, and 79 exploit variants are reported. Fixed in PHP 8.1.29, 8.2.20 and 8.3.8 or later; FastCGI, mod_php and FPM setups are not affected. Cisco Talos and GreyNoise report broad exploitation.
- Listener items: AI interview-helper tools and fake candidates; Microsoft declining to fix DLL search-path issues in Sysinternals tools; 1Password's Secret Key vs LastPass's under-iterated PBKDF2 and (per an anonymous listener) a developer's personal machine and unenforced policy behind the LastPass breach; Juniper end-of-life routers exploited by China (Mandiant) and Steve's argument for paid extended maintenance; IceWhale (ZimaBoard) allegedly misusing emails.
- Rowhammer background: first shown in 2014, mitigations (TRR, ECC, faster refresh) repeatedly bypassed (TRRespass, SMASH, Half-Double), exploits via JavaScript and the network, DDR3 through DDR5 and ARM affected, yet no confirmed in-the-wild attacks. FLIPPYR.AM provides an open-source Arch Linux ISO (default 8-hour run, USB boot) and a raffle for contributors. Steve's ECC server came back clean; his ZimaBoard could not run all tests.
