# Summary

Steve starts with Chrome's twelfth exploited zero-day of 2021, a use-after-free (CVE-2021-37973) in the new Portals API, found by Google's Threat Analysis Group, and with Apple's emergency iOS and macOS fixes for a kernel type-confusion flaw. He repeats his complaint that "zero-day" is used for any vulnerability rather than ones seen exploited. A researcher publishes three unfixed iOS flaws after months of silence from Apple, feeding a wider complaint about Apple's bounty programme. Epik finally confirms its breach, VoIP.ms and Bandwidth.com are still under DDoS, Windows 11 heads to an October 5 release with a stricter PC Health Check tool, and September's patches cause application freezes with Exploit Protection. Google TAG describes malware that uses a malformed code-signing certificate which Windows accepts but OpenSSL-based antimalware rejects as unparseable. After a lukewarm review of Apple's "Foundation", the main story is Guardicore's research showing that Outlook and other Exchange clients, when Autodiscover fails, "fail up" through the domain hierarchy and send credentials, even over HTTP basic authentication, to domains like autodiscover.com or autodiscover.<tld>. Guardicore captured hundreds of thousands of credentials; Microsoft responded by registering dozens of Autodiscover domains, although a 2017 Black Hat Asia paper had already described the same flaw.

# Key points

- Chrome 94.0.4606.61 fixes CVE-2021-37973, an exploited use-after-free in the Portals API (a page-transition feature); this is Chrome's 12th in-the-wild zero-day in 2021, and Steve again praises Google's speed compared with Microsoft.
- Apple fixed older iOS 12.5.4 and macOS Catalina builds against an XNU kernel type confusion exploited with a WebKit RCE, found by Google TAG, plus earlier patches related to the NSO Pegasus chain.
- Denis Tokarev ("Illusion of Chaos") published proofs of concept for three unfixed iOS 15 bugs (gamed, nehelper leaks of installed apps and WiFi info) after Apple ignored reports from March to May and left a fixed analyticsd issue out of the security notes. The Washington Post and others report researcher frustration with delayed fixes and unclear payments.
- Epik confirmed its breach a week after denying it; commentators note that fast-launched sites for controversial audiences often skimp on security. The VoIP.ms DDoS continued and Bandwidth.com, a big back-end for services such as Google Voice, was also attacked.
- Windows 11 was due on 5 October 2021 with build 22000.194 in the Release channel; features such as a movable taskbar were missing. The updated PC Health Check tool rejected Steve's well-specced 6th-generation Core i7 machine as unsupported.
- September Patch Tuesday side effect: apps freezing or failing with Exploit Protection Export Address Filtering; Microsoft used Known Issue Rollback to fix it.
- Google TAG found OpenSUpdater, adware that uses malformed Authenticode certificates accepted by Windows but not parsed by OpenSSL-based third-party scanners, letting it bypass inspection; victims were mainly US users of cracked software.
- Autodiscover design flaw: clients try autodiscover.<domain>, then the bare domain, and then back off upward until they reach something like autodiscover.com, falling back from HTTPS to HTTP.
- Guardicore Labs bought many autodiscover.<tld> domains and between April and August 2021 captured 372,072 credentials (96,671 unique) as plaintext HTTP basic authentication; an attacker can force a downgrade from OAuth/NTLM by answering HTTP 401, and DNS manipulation would allow large-scale harvesting.
- Microsoft complained it had not received a coordinated disclosure, but the problem was shown in the Black Hat Asia 2017 paper on email client domain name collision (over 9.7 million requests with credentials). Microsoft registered at least 68 Autodiscover domains, which Steve calls a kludge since clients still send credentials to whoever controls the domain.
- Steve argues Microsoft only responds to force, compares the PrintNightmare delay of nine months, and recalls the MSBlast worm and raw sockets. He questions whether responsible disclosure should continue when vendors do not honour their side.
