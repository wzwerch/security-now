# Summary

Firefox 92 will block insecure (HTTP) downloads started from HTTPS pages by default, and Steve shows the about:config switch to enable it early. The T-Mobile breach is the main news item: data on roughly 54 million current, former and prospective customers, including names, dates of birth, Social Security and driver's licence numbers, and IMSI/IMEI data, with a hacker selling it. Steve's advice is to keep permanent credit locks at Experian, TransUnion and Equifax, which can now be lifted temporarily at no cost. He covers the Orange Tsai ProxyShell chain against Microsoft Exchange, now exploited by ransomware (LockFile) because tens of thousands of servers stayed unpatched, and the Razer installer issue where a system-privileged installer's folder dialog gave local users a path to full privileges, with CERT's Will Dormann noting the likelihood of similar cases. Mandiant's disclosure of CVE-2021-28372 in the ThroughTek Kalay peer-to-peer platform illustrates unfixable IoT risk for 83 million devices. Steve then surveys open and alternative overlay networks (Nebula, Innernet, ZeroTier) beside Tailscale, and ends with a labelled thought experiment: that Microsoft's delays in fixing privately reported flaws might reflect a quiet arrangement benefiting US intelligence, based on a 2013 Bruce Schneier remark, which Steve and Leo stress is speculation without evidence.

# Key points

- Firefox mixed-content download blocking: dom.block_download_insecure will become default true in Firefox 92; users can still override; copying an HTTP link into the address bar is unaffected.
- T-Mobile breach (fifth in four years): 48.6 million then about 54 million people; 13.1M postpaid accounts and 40M former or prospective customers with full identity data, plus prepaid PINs and IMEI history back to 2004; seller asked 6 BTC. Offered credit monitoring; Steve prefers permanent credit locks (the "lock" being more durable than a "freeze"), now free to lift temporarily.
- ProxyShell (CVE-2021-34473, 34523, 31207): pre-auth RCE chain in Exchange shown at Pwn2Own 2021 and patched in May and July; after Black Hat details a scan on August 8 still found about 30,400 of 100,000 servers unpatched; LockFile ransomware and web shells (140+ on 1,900+ servers per Huntress) followed.
- Razer Synapse installer: Windows Update launched a system-level installer whose folder-selection dialog inherited those privileges; the researcher was ignored by Razer and published. Steve notes Windows also has installer-name heuristics. Lesson: privileged installers are a broad class of local privilege escalation.
- ThroughTek Kalay (CVE-2021-28372, CVSS 9.6): devices register on the network with only a 20-byte UID, so an attacker who learns a UID can hijack it, capture credentials and get audio/video and RPC access; binaries ran as root without common mitigations; 83 million devices, many unpatchable. Steve repeats: keep IoT off the PC network.
- Overlay and software-defined networks: Slack's Nebula (Noise Protocol Framework, lighthouse discovery nodes, certificates and groups), Innernet (WireGuard-based, self-hosted), ZeroTier (free up to 50 devices) as alternatives to Tailscale; Hamachi recalled as the early example.
- Speculative thesis ("reasoned neglect"): Steve asks whether Microsoft passes early vulnerability knowledge to US agencies and delays patches, citing Bruce Schneier's 2013 claim that the NSA gets advance warning of soon-to-be-patched flaws; he admits no direct evidence, and Leo offers sluggishness as a simpler explanation.
