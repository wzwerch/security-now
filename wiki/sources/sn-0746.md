# Summary

Steve starts with SQRL news: TWiT's Discourse community now accepts SQRL login through a community OAuth 2 provider, with a caveat that tying SQRL to an existing password account weakens an account that used two-factor. Microsoft gave contradictory statements about whether Security Essentials signatures continue after Windows 7's end of support. A tally shows 72 school districts or institutions hit by ransomware in 2019, affecting 1,039 schools, often through managed service providers. The revised Avast/AVG Firefox extensions returned with an opt-in prompt and less data collection. Google restructured its patch rewards program to pay open-source projects up front, while Apple opened its bounty to all researchers with a top payout of 1.5 million dollars. Mozilla added NextDNS as a second Trusted Recursive Resolver partner, and Drupal admins are urged to patch an Archive_Tar flaw. The bulk of the show is a year-by-year recollection of the decade, from Stuxnet to 2019's ransomware wave.

# Key points

- SQRL login on twit.community works via the sqrloauth OAuth 2 service; SQRL clients have a setting to demand disabling non-SQRL authentication, which sites must honour.
- Microsoft Security Essentials: an official reply said signature updates continue after January 14, 2020 but the platform will not be updated; an ESU FAQ contradicted it and was to be corrected.
- Ransomware in education: 72 institutions, 1,039 schools in 2019; advice to throttle MSP admin rights.
- Avast/AVG extensions are back on Firefox with an explicit "scan web addresses" consent screen and reportedly far less data sent; Steve still advises against them.
- Google patch rewards: from 2020 grants of 5,000 or 30,000 dollars paid in advance to open-source projects for security work.
- Apple Security Bounty: open to all researchers, covering iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, watchOS and iCloud; payouts scale up to 1 million dollars for zero-click kernel execution with persistence, and a 50 percent bonus for beta or regression bugs gives the 1.5 million maximum.
- Mozilla's Trusted Recursive Resolver program adds NextDNS (founded May 2019) next to Cloudflare; requirements include deleting data within 24 hours, a privacy policy and no filtering unless legally required.
- Drupal 7.69 / 8.7.11 / 8.8.1 fix a critical Archive_Tar issue allowing remote takeover through crafted archives.
- Decade highlights: Stuxnet and Operation Aurora (2010); Sony PlayStation Network breach and DigiNotar CA compromise used for Gmail interception in Iran (2011); Snowden leaks (2013), Target POS malware, Adobe breach and Have I Been Pwned, Silk Road takedown; Sony Pictures hack by North Korea, Mt. Gox, Heartbleed, Rowhammer (2014); Ashley Madison, SIM swapping, Ukraine power grid attack (2015); DNC hack by Fancy Bear and Cozy Bear, Yahoo breaches, Shadow Brokers, Mirai (2016); WannaCry, Vault 7, exposed MongoDB ransom wave, Equifax via unpatched Apache Struts, Coinhive cryptojacking (2017); Meltdown/Spectre and the Marriott/Starwood breach (2018); the ransomware franchise model (GandCrab) in 2019.
- Steve predicts ransomware and IoT problems for 2020.
