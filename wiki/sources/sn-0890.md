# Summary

Steve starts with the European Data Protection Supervisor suing over a June 2022 law that retroactively lets Europol keep data on non-criminals it was ordered to purge, a conflict Leo says should distinguish convicted persons from others. Denmark became the fourth EU country (after Austria, France and Italy) to rule that Google Analytics cannot be used lawfully under the GDPR; the French regulator suggests an EU-hosted proxy that strips IP addresses, referrers and identifiers. A 17-year-old was arrested in the UK and is suspected to be "Teapot" of the Uber and Rockstar breaches. Mozilla issued a 66-page "Five Walled Gardens" paper complaining about OS-bundled browsers, and Chrome's Manifest V3 declarativeNetRequest API threatens advanced ad blockers. Numen Cyber Labs warned that apps embedding Chromium (for example Skype) lag behind patches. Windows 11 gets SMB authentication rate limiting, US senators propose the Securing Open Source Software Act, and CISA/FBI revealed Iranian actors sat in Albanian networks for 14 months via an unpatched SharePoint bug. A listener explains the Uber breach as MFA push fatigue. The main story is the leak of the LockBit 3.0 builder, which could commoditize ransomware.

# Key points

- Europol: EDPS ordered deletion of data on people with no criminal link by January 2023; EU lawmakers retroactively authorized retention in June; EDPS sued at the Court of Justice. Europol argues bulk data is needed for analysis; Steve notes both sides have valid points and that data minimization is the best safeguard.
- Google Analytics: Denmark joins Austria, France and Italy; CNIL guidance says IP-address settings and identifier encryption are insufficient and only breaking direct contact between visitor and Google (an EU proxy removing IP, referrer, URL parameters, fingerprintable data) would suffice; Leo questions practicality and who would run the proxy.
- Teapot/Lapsus$: City of London police detained a 17-year-old from Oxfordshire; the suspect was reportedly already on probation over the earlier Microsoft/Lapsus$ case.
- Mozilla's report claims OS vendors entrench their own browsers; browser share cited: Chrome 77%, Safari 8.9%, Firefox 7.7%, Edge 5.8%. Google pays Mozilla roughly $450 million a year for default search; Steve and Leo worry about the Chromium monoculture.
- Manifest V3: extensions lose the blocking webRequest pipeline for a rule-based declarativeNetRequest API; Google cites security and performance, critics cite ad revenue; Vivaldi's lead developer promised to keep content blocking effective; enterprise users keep V2 for now.
- Numen Cyber Labs: patched Chrome bugs CVE-2021-38003 and CVE-2022-1364 can still affect apps embedding old Chromium builds, with Skype named as an example (patch gap).
- Windows 11 Insider build adds a two-second delay after failed inbound NTLM attempts on SMB, which would stretch 90,000 guesses from 5 minutes to about 50 hours; Leo notes Microsoft's 8th-generation CPU requirement may relate to new virtualization-based security features.
- Securing Open Source Software Act (Senators Portman and Peters) would have CISA build a risk framework for open source use, hire open source experts, and set up a software security subcommittee following the Log4j hearings.
- CISA/FBI: Iranian military-linked actors were inside Albanian government networks for 14 months, entering through an unpatched SharePoint flaw (CVE-2019-0604), stealing email in 2021, spreading in May 2022, then deploying ransomware in July with anti-MEK messages. Steve's lessons: patch and assume compromise, so intrusion detection matters.
- Listener Greg (who works in MFA) clarifies the Uber attack was push-notification MFA fatigue rather than code brute-forcing, and recommends number-matching "verified push" and phishing-resistant FIDO2.
- LockBit 3.0 (the leading ransomware group, with 1,029+ attacks since 2019 and 68 victims in August) had its builder and key generator leaked by "3xp0rt"; Cyberint confirmed it is genuine; experts expect rebranded copies, as happened after the Babuk and Conti leaks; Recorded Future counts 150+ new ransomware groups this year and over 100 leak sites.
- Steve's closing view: cryptocurrency plus porous enterprise security made extortion lucrative, and tools are now falling to low-skilled actors.
