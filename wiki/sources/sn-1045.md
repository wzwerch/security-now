# Summary

A listener-feedback-heavy episode. Steve first investigates why Gmail briefly sent GRC's weekly mailing (and a listener's test mails) to spam: Google's Postmaster Tools showed spam reports uncorrelated with GRC's Monday mailings, which pointed to spoofing of grc.com, so he tightened DMARC to strict SPF/DKIM alignment and enabled report collection, while concluding the weekend spike was most likely a Google-side glitch. He then reports that iOS 26 and macOS Tahoe turn on Safari's advanced fingerprinting protection for all browsing by default, so EFF's Cover Your Tracks sees a randomized fingerprint, while his Firefox is still unique. A long segment explains why SNMP is inherently insecure and how about 2.3 million Cisco IOS devices expose SNMP on the public Internet to an actively exploited stack-overflow bug. Windows 10 ESU enrollment is rolling out (with a known manual trick to force the offer), and the EEA gets a year of no-strings ESU. Steve found 2-year, $12 domain-validation certificates from a budget reseller and explains how to time them against the shrinking maximum TLS certificate lifetimes. Other stories: the Jaguar Land Rover ransomware shutdown and UK 1.5 billion pound loan guarantee, the Neon app that pays users to have calls recorded for AI training, Bluesky using Kids Web Services for age verification in more US states, and more than 10,000 publicly reachable Ollama instances. GRC's DNS Benchmark upgrade has reached release-candidate status.

# Key points

- Gmail spam spree: Google user-reported spam rate for grc.com showed no correlation with GRC's weekly 19,000-email sends; spoofers exploited GRC's relaxed DMARC alignment. Steve switched to strict alignment and DMARC reporting. SPF lists valid sender IPs, DKIM signs headers with a public key in DNS, DMARC sets the enforcement policy.
- Safari in iOS 26 / macOS Tahoe 26 enables advanced tracking and fingerprinting protection in all tabs by default; older Safari users should set it to "All Browsing". Cover Your Tracks: Firefox unique among 301,784 browsers (18.2 bits), iOS 18 Safari shared its fingerprint with 1 in 2,340.
- SNMP (RFC 1988-era, cleartext community strings over UDP, v3 added crypto) is "Security Not My Problem"; a Shodan search found 2,303,370 Cisco devices answering on UDP 161 to an actively exploited SNMP stack overflow in IOS. Advice: patch, disable SNMP unless needed, and use ACLs so only known clients can reach it; Cisco services bind to all interfaces by default.
- Windows 10 consumer ESU: offers rolling out gradually; community scripts on GitHub can force the enrollment prompt; limit of 10 enrollments per Microsoft account; EEA users get free ESU without conditions after Euroconsumers pressure under the DMA; business users pay and 0patch is cited as a cheaper alternative.
- TLS certificate lifetimes: 398 days until March 15, 2026, then 200 days, 100 days in 2027 and 47 days in 2029 (CA/B Forum); Steve bought a two-year, $12 domain-validation plan, to reissue just before each cutoff, and will need ACME automation (Let's Encrypt) afterwards. DigiCert's cheapest OV certificate was about $324.
- Jaguar Land Rover: ransomware (attributed to Hellcat per reports) on August 31, 2025 shut production for about a month, 1.5 billion pound UK loan guarantee, no cyber insurance reported, allegedly flat network; lesson is that cyber readiness is a cost of doing business.
- Neon Mobile: No. 2 social app paying up to $30/day for recorded calls sold to AI companies; one-sided recording claim to dodge wiretap laws, sweeping license over recordings, voice-cloning fraud risk. Steve uses written confirmation for any money-moving instruction.
- Bluesky adds age verification in Ohio via Kids Web Services (owned by Epic Games; methods include card via Stripe, face scan, ID scan, SSN checks). Steve hopes standardized device-side age assertion (digital driver's licenses) replaces it.
- Censys found about 10,600 publicly accessible Ollama instances; about 1,500 answered probe prompts; Ollama has no authentication, binding to localhost is its only protection.
- Listener feedback: Arm MTE versus Apple's Memory Integrity Enforcement (A19), a Microsoft Entra passkey flow that is more cumbersome than passwords, client-side responsibility for age verification, liveness weaknesses, uBlock Origin's "Block outsider intrusion into LAN" setting to stop sites probing localhost, and 0patch pricing.
- Windows 10 end of support is October 14, 2025.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: Windows 7 owners pleading "don't force me to install 10" in 2020 versus "Windows 10, don't leave me" in 2025; Steve recalls his Never10 and InControl tools.
- GRC: DNS Benchmark commercial upgrade reached release-candidate stage after about 10 months of work.
- Resources: 3Blue1Brown neural-network series and Andrej Karpathy's LLM lecture recommended for understanding LLMs.
