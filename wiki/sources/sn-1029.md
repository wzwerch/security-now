# Summary

The episode opens with a memorial for Apple pioneer Bill Atkinson (QuickDraw, MacPaint, HyperCard), who died on 5 June 2025. The first major story is a disclosed tracking technique in which Meta's Pixel and Yandex's Metrica scripts running in Android browsers talk over localhost sockets to the native Facebook, Instagram and Yandex apps, linking web cookies to real identities and bypassing incognito mode, cookie clearing and permission controls; Meta stopped using it as soon as the research appeared, and Steve argues browsers should block and announce localhost access by default. Other news: the EU launches DNS4EU filtered resolvers, Ukraine claims a DDoS on Russian Railways' DNS, the Linux Foundation backs FAIR as a decentralized WordPress package manager, a court order obliges OpenAI to preserve all ChatGPT logs including deleted chats, a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated remote code execution flaw hits the Erlang/OTP SSH server, reports claim Russia can read some Telegram traffic, Spanish ISPs wrongly block Google sites, Reddit sues Anthropic, Twitter's XChat encryption is criticized, and a GAO report finds Login.gov has no policy to verify backups. The main topic is Apple's research paper "The Illusion of Thinking", which uses controllable puzzles such as Towers of Hanoi to show that large reasoning models help at medium complexity but collapse at high complexity and cannot even exploit a supplied algorithm. Steve concludes that today's AI is useful but is pattern matching, not thought.

# Key points

- Bill Atkinson tribute: Apple employee 51, principal designer of the Lisa and early Mac user interface, author of QuickDraw (68000 assembly), MacPaint and HyperCard; Leo recalls interviewing him several times.
- Meta/Yandex localhost tracking (research disclosed late May 2025): the Meta Pixel script sends the first-party _fbp cookie via WebRTC to UDP ports 12580-12585 where the Facebook or Instagram app listens (also TCP 12387/12388), and the app then links it to the logged-in account; Yandex Metrica uses HTTP/HTTPS to ports such as 29009, 29010, 30102 and 30103, with yandexmetrica.com resolving to 127.0.0.1 and an apparent multi-day startup delay to evade analysis. About 5.8 million sites embed the scripts. Any other app could eavesdrop on the same ports and learn browsing history; Chrome, Firefox and Edge were affected, Brave blocked it, DuckDuckGo mostly. Vendors shipped mitigations; Meta stopped sending the localhost traffic the day the report appeared, though the apps still listen.
- Steve's recommendation: browsers should block or prompt on web-page scripts connecting to localhost by default, with optional port restrictions; he notes his own SQRL client used a documented localhost channel for legitimate login, and that Microsoft once tried and failed to close the localhost path in Internet Explorer.
- DNS4EU (joindns4.eu): free EU-run resolvers built under ENISA supervision and run by a consortium led by Whalebone; profiles for protective filtering, child protection, ad blocking, all combined, or unfiltered; DNSSEC, DoH and DoT supported. From California Steve measured 163-173 ms versus about 20 ms for Cloudflare, but listeners in the EU report good speeds. Leo would not use a US government equivalent.
- Ukrainian military intelligence claims a 6 Gbit/s, 2.5-million-packet-per-second DDoS on Russian Railways' DNS; Steve is unsure of its effect.
- FAIR Package Manager (Linux Foundation): a drop-in WordPress plugin that replaces WordPress.org update APIs and offers federated, mirrored distribution of plugins and themes, started by developers sidelined in the Automattic/Matt Mullenweg dispute.
- OpenAI preservation order: a New York court (Judge Ona Wang) ordered OpenAI on 13 May 2025 to keep all output logs, including deleted chats and API data, in the news publishers' copyright case; OpenAI objects on privacy grounds. Steve notes "deleted" no longer means gone and points to privacy-by-design services like Venice.ai, though o3 is far more capable.
- Erlang/OTP SSH server flaw: CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated remote code execution; fixed in versions 27.3.3, 26.2.5.11 and 25.3.2.20; workaround is to disable the SSH server or firewall it.
- First Department (Russian NGO) says the FSB reads messages sent to bots and feedback accounts of certain Ukrainian Telegram channels, mostly via compromised devices but possibly through undisclosed tools or Telegram cooperation; Steve does not recommend Telegram.
- Spanish ISPs again accidentally blocked Google domains while enforcing court-ordered blocking of illegal soccer streams.
- Reddit sued Anthropic over scraping comments for Claude training.
- A researcher's analysis of Twitter/X XChat encryption concludes it is little better than before (the company could obtain keys and see metadata) and recommends using Signal; it is also not written in Rust as claimed.
- Thundermail (from the Thunderbird team) will host EU email servers.
- GAO report: Login.gov has no policy to verify that its backups work.
- Apple's "The Illusion of Thinking": using Towers of Hanoi, Checker Jumping, Block World and River Crossing to scale complexity, it finds three regimes (standard LLMs better at low complexity, reasoning models better at medium, both collapse to zero at high complexity), a drop in reasoning effort near the collapse point, "overthinking" on easy cases, and no improvement even when the algorithm is given in the prompt. Contamination of math benchmarks (AIME) is cited as a reason to prefer puzzles. Steve concludes the models do pattern matching at scale, are useful but not thinking, and calls for non-anthropomorphic vocabulary; Leo cautions that prompting such as "use code" might change results and that models are a moving target.
