# Summary

Microsoft issued two out-of-cycle critical fixes for the Windows codecs library (HEVC video extensions), distributed through the Microsoft Store rather than Windows Update, which may leave locked-down or Store-disabled systems unpatched. iOS 14's new clipboard-access banner exposed dozens of apps reading the clipboard, including TikTok and LinkedIn, which called it a bug. Steve reviews HackerOne's annual top-10 public bounty programs (Verizon Media first, PayPal second) and Sony's new PlayStation program managed through HackerOne. After Boston's facial-recognition ban, he summarizes NIST's FRVT Part 3 study showing algorithm-dependent demographic differentials in false positives. A CVSS 10 flaw in F5 BIG-IP's management interface (TMUI) was patched on 3 July and was being exploited within days. SpinRite AHCI driver benchmarking lessons follow. The title refers to the password 123456, which makes up about one in 142 of a billion leaked credentials analyzed by a student researcher.

# Key points

- Windows Codecs Library RCE and information disclosure bugs affect the Store-delivered HEVC extension (present by default since 1809 except N and LTSC editions); fixed versions end in 31822.0/31823.0. Steve flags the problem for enterprises that disabled the Store, and Woody Leonhard called the distribution scheme a mess.
- iOS 14 shows a notice when an app reads the clipboard; many news, game and social apps tripped it. TikTok promised to stop; LinkedIn said an equality-check code path was a bug. Steve praises transparency as the fix; Leo suspects a framework side effect. LastPass clears the clipboard after use.
- HackerOne top 10: Verizon Media (5.4M paid in the year), PayPal, Uber, Intel (largest single HackerOne bounty, believed 100-200k, for a side-channel flaw), Twitter, GitLab, Mail.ru, GitHub, Valve, Airbnb. Steve concludes bounties are becoming a mainstream part of mature security practice.
- Sony launched a PlayStation vulnerability rewards program via HackerOne, paying up to 50,000 dollars or more, versus 20,000 maximum at Microsoft and Nintendo.
- Boston became the second-largest US city to ban police facial recognition. NIST tested 189 algorithms from 99 developers on 18.27 million images: higher one-to-one false positives for Asian and African American faces (10-100x for many algorithms), highest for American Indians; algorithms developed in Asia showed no such gap for Asian faces; one-to-many false positives were higher for African American women; the most equitable algorithms were also among the most accurate.
- F5 BIG-IP: unauthenticated remote exploitation of the TMUI configuration utility, CVSS 10; about 8,500 organizations were exposed per Shodan, proofs of concept appeared within two days and attacks began the next day. Lesson: never expose management interfaces, and keep a reliable contact path for vendor notices; the race is now patcher versus exploiter.
- 123456 appeared about 7 million times among a billion leaked credentials (168.9 million unique passwords); top 1,000 passwords cover 6.6%, top million 36%, top 10 million 54%; only 12% contain a special character; 34% end in digits but 4.5% start with one. A cluster of high-entropy 10-character reused passwords hints at a weak password-manager generator.
