# Summary

With Jason Howell filling in for Leo, Steve first corrects last week's claim that Firefox's Total Cookie Protection was not working: third-party cookies still exist but are partitioned per top-level site, so GRC's test page was simply not designed to detect that. He summarizes a LastPass webinar: passwordless vault login now, FIDO2 keys for vault access later in 2022, and storing passkeys for websites in 2023. Germany's government criticized the EU's CSAM-scanning regulation as crossing a line, while the EU commissioner defended it vaguely. Conti finally shut its last public Tor infrastructure after its members moved to other gangs. CISA warned that Log4Shell still gives state actors access to unpatched VMware Horizon servers, an industry panel floated a "311" cyber-incident line, and Forescout's OT:ICEFALL found 56 insecure-by-design flaws in industrial control products from ten vendors. Feedback revisits double encryption (meet-in-the-middle attacks and authenticated encryption change the analysis). The main topic is Hertzbleed, where data-dependent dynamic frequency scaling on Intel and AMD CPUs turns a power side channel into a remote timing attack that extracted keys from the post-quantum SIKE scheme, with no microcode fix planned.

# Key points

- Total Cookie Protection works as designed: a third-party cookie is bound to the site where it was set, so it cannot track across sites; Steve's test page misreported it.
- LastPass roadmap: Phase 1 passwordless vault login (available), Phase 2 FIDO2 keys and biometrics for the vault (later 2022), Phase 3 passkey storage (2023). Steve warns to check passkey export before committing to a single vendor's ecosystem.
- Germany's Digital Minister said the EU CSAM proposal crosses a line; commissioner Johansson claimed it was targeted and court-authorized. Steve notes Apple's earlier hash-based proposal was the least intrusive but still failed.
- Conti: last two Tor sites shut; leaked data had come from earlier attacks and was also posted on Hive's site; crew dispersed to other ransomware groups.
- CISA/Coast Guard advisory: state-sponsored actors still exploit Log4Shell (CVE-2021-44228) in VMware Horizon and Unified Access Gateway to implant loaders, move laterally and exfiltrate data. Old vulnerabilities remain in toolkits.
- CISA industry advisory group suggested a national "311" line for small and medium business incidents; Check Point cited roughly 868 weekly attacks per organization on average; new US law requires critical infrastructure to report breaches in 72 hours and ransomware payments in 24 hours.
- Forescout OT:ICEFALL: 56 flaws across Siemens, Motorola, Honeywell, Yokogawa, Emerson, Omron, JTEKT and others; 38% allow credential compromise, 21% firmware manipulation, 14% remote code execution; three quarters of affected families carry security certifications. Steve argues critical infrastructure software must be open to scrutiny.
- Double encryption feedback: meet-in-the-middle attacks need a known plaintext/ciphertext pair and impractical storage at 256-bit keys; authenticated modes like GCM give feedback that allows divide-and-conquer.
- Hertzbleed (CVE-2022-23823 and CVE-2022-24436): CPU frequency scaling depends on processed data, so identical-cycle constant-time code takes different wall time. Affects Intel (8th to 11th gen confirmed) and AMD Zen 2/3; demonstrated full key extraction from SIKE decapsulation remotely. Not a bug; no microcode patches planned; workaround is disabling turbo/boost features at a performance cost; vendors give software guidance. Steve anticipates a need for constant-power crypto.
- Disclosure: reported to Intel in 2021 Q3, Intel asked for extended embargo; researchers published June 14, 2022. Code is open source.
