# Summary

Two weeks after the Shadow Brokers' third leak of alleged NSA tools, tens of thousands of Windows machines were found infected with the DoublePulsar implant, delivered through the SMB exploit EternalBlue that Microsoft had already patched in March. Scans found over five million Windows machines answering on TCP port 445, and several security firms counted between 30,000 and 107,000 infected hosts. Steve also covers a GitHub hack that flips one return value to remove Microsoft's block on updates for Kaby Lake and Ryzen PCs running Windows 7 and 8.1, the debate over whether Microsoft Authenticator's password-free login is multifactor, and a Wall Street Journal report that Chrome will block ads from sites violating the Coalition for Better Ads standards. He criticizes MasterCard's thumbprint-reading card concept, covers the Bose Connect lawsuit, ten flaws found in 25 Linksys Smart Wi-Fi router models, MIT selling half of its IPv4 block to Amazon, and Brickerbot variants 3 and 4 with their author's "Internet chemotherapy" justification. The closing portion is listener feedback, including early results from the fingerprint-unlock experiment.

# Key points

- DoublePulsar is a RAM-resident kernel backdoor that hooks into SMB traffic on port 445 and lets attackers load further malware. After the Shadow Brokers leak it spread quickly, with scans finding about 30,000 to over 100,000 infected machines (Below0Day, Binary Edge, Robert Graham, Phobos Group). It does not survive reboot unless re-exploited, relies on older kernel protection, and is compared to Conficker by researchers. Steve is astonished that 5,561,708 hosts expose port 445; the advice is to be current on the March 2017 patches and never expose SMB.
- A GitHub user showed that Microsoft's March update added IsCPUSupported and IsDeviceServiceable checks and that changing one byte restores updates on seventh-generation CPUs, proving the block is a policy rather than a technical necessity. Steve does not recommend the hack.
- Microsoft Authenticator lets you sign in to a Microsoft account by approving a phone prompt without a password. Steve argues it is one strong factor, not multifactor, but good enough; Leo notes setup required a password.
- WSJ reports Google may add native ad blocking to Chrome for ad types violating the Coalition for Better Ads standards (pop-ups, autoplay video with sound, prestitials with countdowns, large sticky ads, plus extra mobile categories) and might block all ads on offending sites. Steve and Leo welcome it as pressure on advertisers.
- MasterCard unveiled a card with a built-in thumbprint reader powered through the EMV chip contacts. Steve says a fuzzy biometric match must be a software decision inside the card and can be patched or bypassed, so it is a gimmick compared with cryptographic keys; Apple Pay and Android Pay are stronger.
- A lawsuit claims Bose's Connect app collected listening history with serial numbers without consent, allegedly violating the Wiretap Act, with analytics provider Segment.io as a data recipient.
- IOActive found ten flaws in 25 Linksys Smart Wi-Fi models (EA and WRT series), six remotely exploitable without login, including denial of service, information disclosure of the WPS PIN and device lists, and root command injection after login; about 7,000 exposed routers were found, and 11 percent used default credentials. Linksys, now owned by Belkin, cooperated and issued mitigations (auto-update, disable guest network, change default password, turn off remote admin).
- MIT is selling about eight million IPv4 addresses from its 18.x.x.x allocation to Amazon to fund its IPv6 transition; IPv4 space was trading around 11 to 12 dollars per address.
- Brickerbot 3 and 4 appeared with more brick methods; the author, Janit0r, describes it as "Internet chemotherapy" to remove vulnerable IoT devices.
- Errata: Unicode has 17 planes with over a million code points, and Steve's punycode comment was about the basic plane. Chrome's punycode fix timing is unclear.
- Listener results on fingerprint unlock: one listener's 84 volunteers could not unlock his iPhone, but a child unlocked a parent's iPad and another's spouse succeeded about once in ten tries; more enrolled fingers means a looser match. Google's own Pixel UI warns fingerprints may be less secure than a strong PIN.
- Steve notes his HTTPS fingerprinting page would still detect an ISP-installed middlebox; password managers like LastPass resist punycode spoofing because they match the real domain; an OpenVPN server on Amazon EC2 was suggested as an alternative to commercial VPNs.
