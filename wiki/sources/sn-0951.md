# Summary

Leo returns from his retreat. Steve begins with ICANN's new Registration Data Request Service (RDRS), a standardized ticketing portal through which law enforcement and other legitimate parties can ask registrars for redacted WHOIS data. WhatsApp adds a "Secret Code" to Chat Lock so locked chats can use their own password and be hidden from the chat list. The Iranian-linked group Cyber Av3ngers broke into US water facilities by logging into Unitronics PLCs still using the factory password. A federal judge blocked Montana's statewide TikTok ban, and Google says RCS is on over a billion Android devices. Steve welcomes the EU Cyber Resilience Act (five-year support, default auto-updates) and CISA's first "Secure by Design" alert, laments the proliferation of new top-level domains, and reports on France ordering ministers off Signal, WhatsApp and Telegram in favour of the French messenger Olvid. The main segment revisits the EU eIDAS 2.0 Article 45 provisional agreement through commentary by Ivan Ristic and Ryan Hurst, including the finding that seven certificate authorities issue about 99 percent of web certificates.

# Key points

- ICANN RDRS: free, single-form request system that routes requests for non-public gTLD registration data to the right registrar; it does not guarantee disclosure. Background: GDPR forced redaction of public WHOIS records.
- WhatsApp Chat Lock (May 2023) moved sensitive chats behind the device unlock; the new Secret Code adds a separate password and optional hiding of the locked folder. Steve approves of a second "something you know" layer.
- Steve argues devices should ship with a random strong password rather than a shared default. Cyber Av3ngers (tied by US agencies to Iran's IRGC) hit Unitronics PLCs with default password "1111" from about 22 November; CISA asked operators to change passwords, enable MFA and take devices off the Internet; Shadowserver counted 500 to 800 exposed devices (37 in the US).
- Montana's TikTok ban (due 1 January 2024) was preliminarily blocked by Judge Molloy as likely violating the First Amendment and exceeding state power; TikTok's "Project Texas" moves US data to Oracle servers. Steve notes domestic firms and agencies also misuse personal data (data brokers).
- Google reports RCS enabled on more than one billion Android devices; Leo mentions Beeper Mini, which lets Android users join iMessage via reverse-engineered protocols.
- EU Cyber Resilience Act agreement: vendors of connected products must run vulnerability reporting, support products at least five years, ship free automatic security updates by default and report attacks; applies about three years after adoption.
- Black Basta ransomware reportedly earned over USD 107 million from 90+ paying victims out of 329 hit organizations (Elliptic and Corvus Insurance).
- New TLDs such as .meme and .zip are seen as a poor evolution of the DNS hierarchy; defensive registrations undermine their purpose.
- CISA "Secure by Design" alert on web management interfaces: manufacturers should own customer security outcomes, ship interfaces disabled by default, refuse to run while exposed to the Internet, and warn before loosening defaults. Steve suggests governments could use procurement attestations to drive adoption.
- France told ministers and cabinet staff to uninstall Telegram, Signal and WhatsApp by 8 December and use Olvid. Olvid is open source, needs no phone number or directory, and bootstraps trust with Short Authenticated Strings (SAS, from Sylvain Pasini's 2009 thesis) exchanged over an existing channel; text is free, voice calls and multi-device need a subscription.
- Article 45: the provisional agreement (8 November 2023) requires browsers to trust EU member-state qualified website authentication certificates (QWACs), show legal identity in the UI, and limits browsers' ability to distrust EU CAs or keep precautionary measures after supervisory review.
- Ryan Hurst: about 43 new organizations would join root stores, a more than 50 percent increase over 75 to 85 existing CAs, with lower standards for EU CAs. Precedents: a French agency's rogue Google certificates (2013), DigiNotar, and the 2022 distrust of e-Tugra over default admin credentials.
- Market data: Let's Encrypt 46.52 percent of unexpired certificates, DigiCert 22.19, Sectigo 11.89, Google Trust Services 8.88, GoDaddy 5.77, Microsoft 3.45, IdenTrust 0.63; these seven cover about 99 percent. Extended Validation certificates fell to about 3.8 percent and were abandoned in UI after researcher demos such as a "Stripe, Inc." EV certificate for a lookalike company.
- Steve considers disabling Firefox's new OS-root-store import and pruning roots to the main six or seven CAs, noting most users would not do so; he questions the EU's authority to dictate browser design.
