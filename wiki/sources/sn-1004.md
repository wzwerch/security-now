# Summary

The lead story is the Chinese Salt Typhoon intrusion into telecom providers (eight US carriers named by the Deputy National Security Adviser, up to about 80 worldwide per Steve's account), which US officials say is ongoing and could not be declared evicted; the FBI and CISA now advise Americans to use encrypted communications, which Steve finds ironic because the attackers abused the lawful-intercept (CALEA) systems that the carriers had installed. Steve then criticizes Microsoft's TPM 2.0 requirement for Windows 11 as arbitrary, since Windows 10 security updates will end in October 2025 and only be available for paid Extended Security Updates, and reports that the "Microsoft Activation Scripts" project claims to have defeated nearly all Windows and Office activation. Shorter items: Apple's patent on recognizing people by clothing and gait, Zoom's 18 million dollar SEC settlement offer over false end-to-end encryption claims, AWS Data Transfer Terminals, an FTC ban on selling location data by Mobilewalla, Gravy Analytics and Venntel, and GRC's new BIMI logo display. Listener feedback includes a long discussion of where to keep TOTP secrets (password manager versus separate device) framed as layered security, a new SSH-tunnel linking service, the Tor Snowflake browser extension, tools to link PCs and phones, and refilling SodaStream canisters. The main segment recounts how ChatGPT 4o first gave a wrong MASM macro default-parameter syntax, then agreed with Steve's incorrect-sounding correction, and Steve announces he will study LLM internals to explain them on the show.

# Key points

- **Salt Typhoon**: Chinese state-linked group inside US and global telecoms since late spring 2024; victims in three groups (call-record theft concentrated around the Washington DC area, a small number of political or government figures including reportedly presidential campaign phones, and copied lawful-intercept court orders); Five Eyes alert issued without the UK; officials cannot say the actors are gone; Senator Warner called it the most serious breach in US history. Steve concludes that independent end-to-end encrypted messaging (Signal; WhatsApp uses the Signal protocol) must be allowed, and Leo notes the FBI wording "responsibly managed encryption".
- **TPM 2.0 for Windows 11**: Microsoft's blog restates the requirement; Steve agrees TPM 2.0 is technically better (elliptic curve crypto, SHA-256) but sees no reason that TPM 1.2 machines must be excluded, and objects to charging for ESU security fixes after October 2025; 0patch is mentioned as an alternative.
- **Windows and Office activation crack**: the "massgrave" Microsoft Activation Scripts team claims permanent activation including Windows 10 ESU and volume keys, hosted on GitHub; Leo tested it in a VM (the described method emulates a volume-license KMS service). Steve notes Microsoft has long shown little concern about consumer piracy.
- **Apple patent**: "Identity Recognition Utilizing Face-Associated Body Characteristics" lets a camera link a known face to clothing, build and gait for later recognition; Steve takes it as a sign of an incoming flood of AI patents and criticizes obviousness standards.
- **Zoom**: offered to pay 18 million USD to the SEC over its 2020 false claims of end-to-end encryption (earlier 85 million class action and FTC settlement); renamed Zoom Communications.
- **AWS Data Transfer Terminals**: reservable physical rooms (first in Los Angeles and New York) for high-speed upload of storage devices into AWS.
- **FTC and data brokers**: ban on selling geolocation data for Mobilewalla, Gravy Analytics and Venntel, including sensitive locations and protest attendance; the Pentester-hosted National Public Data lookup shortcut was monetized so Steve disabled GRC's link.
- **BIMI at GRC**: DigiCert issued a Verified Mark Certificate and Steve published a BIMI DNS TXT record; Gmail shows the Ruby-G logo only for mail received after the record was in place (checked together with SPF, DKIM and DMARC).
- **Authenticator policy**: after a full site breach only passkeys remain safe, since TOTP uses shared secrets; against credential stuffing any TOTP location works; the only extra risk of password-manager-held TOTP is client compromise or a vault breach like LastPass. Steve's conclusion is layered security ("belt and suspenders"), and that letting a password manager supply codes is reasonable for most users.
- **Remote access and Tor**: a listener's free SSH-tunnel service (PuppetPC) is mentioned without endorsement; the Snowflake browser extension turns a browser into a short-lived Tor proxy for censored users.
- **PC and phone linking**: LocalSend, PairDrop, Intel Unison and Windows Phone Link are suggested for typing on a PC and sending to an iPhone.
- **ChatGPT anecdote**: asked how to give an optional MASM macro parameter a default, ChatGPT gave wrong syntax, then a workaround that assumed the feature did not exist; the correct form uses a colon-equals default. After Steve explained, ChatGPT thanked him. He calls AI the most transformative event of our lifetimes, says no one understands how it works, and plans to read two LLM textbooks and later explain them on the show.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a key being unlocked over a floor grate ("What could possibly go wrong?"); ChatGPT's image description impressed a blind listener.
- Best Of episode and holiday schedule discussion; Advent of Code cheating with LLMs.
- GRC's mailing went to 13,675 recipients.
- SodaStream refill tip: a bulk CO2 tank with a siphon tube and brass adapter, refilled at a home-brew shop.
- Steve is reading Peter Hamilton's Archimedes Engine novels; childhood photo of him wiring a bulb at age four.
