# Summary

Steve Gibson and Leo Laporte mark the 1000th episode of Security Now! after almost 20 years. The news starts with the Bitwarden licensing scare: a build/packaging change made the desktop client look no longer fully open source, and Bitwarden then moved its SDK code to the GPLv3 and split off a separate repository for its business secrets product. Germany drafted legislation that shields responsibly disclosing security researchers while punishing abuse of that status, and Australia announced a minimum age of 16 for social media, which prompted Steve to argue that platforms need a parent-set date of birth exposed through an API. A mislabeled update caused third-party patch tools (reported for Heimdal customers) to upgrade Windows Server 2022 machines to Windows Server 2025, and Microsoft announced an optional Windows 11 "Admin Protection" feature that replaces the classic UAC token split. Fines against Apple, Mozilla and TikTok in Russia and a roughly 15.7 million USD fine against Meta in South Korea were covered briefly. The technical highlights were Synology's zero-click RISK:STATION flaw (CVE-2024-10443) and a typosquatted PyPI package called "Fabrice" that stole AWS credentials for three years. Google's coming mandatory MFA and the Mozilla Foundation layoffs closed the news. The rest is SpinRite feedback, a backup reminder, GRC mailing-list fixes and listener mail on AI, uBlock Origin and secure remote access to home networks.

# Key points

- **Bitwarden licensing**: Rumours that Bitwarden had gone closed source arose from an SDK dependency that stopped the public desktop client from building. Bitwarden called it a packaging bug, relicensed the internal SDK under GPLv3 and renamed the old repo to sdk-secrets for its Secrets Manager business product. Steve treats it as a good example of community pressure working.
- **German researcher protection**: A draft law removes criminal liability for vulnerability research that is responsibly disclosed, but sets prison terms of three months to five years for abuse such as extortion, substantial financial damage or harming critical infrastructure.
- **Australia age limit**: Bill to bar under-16s from social media, with enforcement placed on the platforms. Steve criticises the lack of a workable mechanism (Apple's age brackets of 4/9/12/17 have no 16, and are not automatic) and proposes a parent-set birthdate exposed via a platform API. Leo notes the privacy problems of age verification and AI face-based estimation.
- **Windows Server 2025 surprise upgrade**: An update mislabeled in the Windows Update API under a Windows 11 KB number (KB5044284) was pushed by third-party patch management and upgraded Windows Server 2022 systems; Heimdal estimated about 7 percent of its customers were hit. Leo points out the fault lay with the patch-management layer; rollback depends on backups.
- **Windows 11 Admin Protection**: Optional (off by default, enabled via group policy) feature in Canary builds that moves elevated rights into a separate system-managed account and requires real authentication (password, PIN, ideally Windows Hello) instead of a Yes/No UAC prompt; intended to stop UAC bypass techniques. Details expected at Microsoft Ignite. Steve himself disables UAC and Defender on his development machines.
- **Russian fines**: A Moscow court fined Apple (two podcasts), Mozilla (add-ons) and TikTok (Ukraine-war videos) about 35,000-40,000 USD each; Steve contrasts this with the absurd Google fine of last week.
- **Meta fined in South Korea**: The PIPC fined Meta 21.62 billion won (about 15.7 million USD) for inferring sensitive traits (religion, political views, sexual orientation, North Korean defector status) of about 980,000 users and offering them to roughly 4,000 advertisers without consent, and for weak verification in password-reset requests for inactive accounts.
- **Synology RISK:STATION (CVE-2024-10443)**: Zero-click, unauthenticated remote code execution in Synology Photos / BeePhotos found by Rick de Jager of Midnight Blue at Pwn2Own Ireland 2024; one to two million devices were reachable from the Internet. Details withheld; patches released. Steve's advice: never expose a NAS to the Internet, keep it behind NAT with UPnP disabled, and use mechanisms like port knocking or overlay VPNs.
- **PyPI "Fabrice" typosquat**: A look-alike of the popular Fabric library was downloaded about 37,100 times over three years and harvested AWS access keys on Linux and Windows hosts. The episode stresses how hard it is to keep malicious packages out of public repositories (PyPI, NPM).
- **Google mandatory MFA**: Google will require multifactor authentication for all cloud service users, including Gmail, starting around early 2025 (no firm deadline given).
- **Mozilla Foundation layoffs**: 30 percent of the nonprofit Foundation was cut; Leo notes it had doubled in two years and that the browser company Mozilla is separate. Steve remains a Firefox user and finds the official statement meaningless corporate language.
- **SpinRite and Dave's Garage**: Dave Plummer's positive SpinRite 6.1 review (not sponsored) led to comments including one from Seagate's former chief technologist advising multiple independent copies, air-gapped dated backups and new drives every few years; Steve urges automatic, transparent backups.
- **Listener feedback**: AI will aid attackers too, but defenders win long term because AI cannot create vulnerabilities (Steve); uBlock Origin filter lists can remove YouTube Shorts without extra extensions; the viral "hose over train tracks" photo was a Belgian firefighters' joke; the new DNS Benchmark will keep running under WINE; for reaching Home Assistant remotely, IPv6 obscurity is weak and an overlay network (Tailscale, ZeroTier, Nebula) is recommended; AI chatbots giving differing answers reflect guardrails and are just another opinion.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a pedestrian path with weaving barriers that cyclists simply bypassed across the lawn.
- GRC mail system fix: "instant unsubscribe" links were being triggered by email providers' link scanners; unsubscribe now needs a confirmation page. Mailing went to 12,656 subscribers.
- Steve has started work on the next DNS Benchmark.
- Reflections on the show's history: shortest episode 4 minutes 12 seconds, longest about three hours (a Best Of); thanks to listeners and a former sponsor from the earliest days.
- Next week's topic teased: Sam Altman's AGI statements.
