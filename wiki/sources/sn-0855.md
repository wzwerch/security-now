# Summary

Steve opens with Log4j "background noise": SolarWinds Serv-U and Zyxel NetAtlas EMS were targeted, and The Record notes that ransomware gangs, nation-state spies, miners, access brokers and DDoS botnets all use the flaw. He then turns to cyber insurance. A New Jersey court ruled for Merck against insurer Ace American, finding the "acts of war" exclusion did not cover the 2017 NotPetya attack (about 1.4 billion dollars of losses); insurers like Lloyd's are rewriting exclusions, and one Illinois school district saw its premium rise 334% and had to adopt multifactor authentication everywhere for full coverage. Wordfence found an unauthenticated REST API flaw in WP HTML Mail (20,000 sites), and Jetpack found a backdoor injected into 40 themes and 53 plugins on the AccessPress website (used on about 360,000 sites), though the WordPress.org copies were clean; Leo stresses it is the plugin model, not WordPress core, that is the problem. The EU announced a 200,000 euro Intigriti bug bounty for LibreOffice, Mastodon, Odoo, CryptPad and LEOS, with a 20% bonus for submitting a fix. Kaspersky disclosed MoonBounce, an APT41 UEFI bootkit living in the motherboard's SPI flash, surviving OS reinstalls and disk replacement. Listener questions covered pfSense hardware, SodaStream refills and a request for full-range port scanning in ShieldsUP!. The deep dive explains the NetUSB flaw: the client declares a 32-bit byte count, the server adds 17 bytes for scratch space before allocating, a count of 0xFFFFFFFF wraps to 16 bytes allocated, and the subsequent receive of the declared amount overflows the tiny buffer. Steve notes this is the same KCodes module as the 2015 bug in episode 509, discusses missing sanity checks, and gives the lesson to validate untrusted input.

# Key points

- Log4j continues: Serv-U attacks failed against LDAP specifics; Zyxel fixed NetAtlas EMS only; Steve expects an unpatched tail that stays infested.
- Merck v. Ace American: court found the war exclusion applies only to traditional warfare; Lloyd's and others updated clauses; cyber premiums are rising and insurers demand controls such as universal MFA (a 2021 Emsisoft tally counted 2,323 hit governments, schools and healthcare providers).
- WP HTML Mail (before 3.1) had unauthenticated REST endpoints allowing script injection into email templates, giving takeover; Steve calls missing authentication inexcusable. WordPress has about 40% of websites and 62% of CMS use.
- AccessPress compromise (September 2021): all extensions downloaded from the vendor site carried a backdoor; Jetpack's team reported it and the vendor removed them.
- EU-FOSSA successor EC-OSPO funds the Intigriti program (up to 5,000 euros per exceptional bug); earlier EU rounds covered VLC, KeePass, 7-Zip, FileZilla and others.
- MoonBounce: third known SPI-flash UEFI implant after LoJax and MosaicRegressor, attributed to APT41, used in targeted attacks; other bootkits mentioned include ESPecter and FinSpy.
- NetUSB: service bound to 0.0.0.0 on TCP 20005; after a nonce/AES handshake a command loop accepts a 16-bit command; command 0x805F reads an attacker-chosen unsigned 32-bit length, adds 17, and a 32-bit MIPS build wraps to a 16-byte allocation; the full count is then read into it, a classic heap or kernel buffer overflow. A 64-bit platform would have failed the allocation instead.
- History: the 2015 SEC Consult NetUSB bug (an overlong computer name overflowing a kernel stack buffer) affected over a hundred router models from dozens of vendors, KCodes was unresponsive, and a weaponized exploit appeared on GitHub; vendors could not firewall or disable it on some devices.
- Design lessons: no sanity limit on the declared length, trust that only the vendor's own client connects, exposure on the WAN, and unchecked integer arithmetic.
- cURL's STARTTLS issue from last Patch Tuesday was confirmed by its author not to be remote code execution, contrary to Microsoft's label.
