# Summary

The episode opens with the death of hacker-turned-consultant Kevin Mitnick and Leo's update on pagefile wear from an SSD expert. Steve then covers Apple's formal response to the UK's proposed Investigatory Powers Act amendments, in which Apple says it would withdraw FaceTime and iMessage rather than weaken security. Next is Google's Web Environment Integrity proposal, labelled "Web DRM" by critics, which would let sites require attested client environments and thereby block ad blockers. An FTC/HHS warning to 130 hospital systems about Meta pixel and Google Analytics tracking, the EU Cyber Resilience Act committee vote, the nomination of Gen. Haugh promising "expeditionary cyber forces", Coveware's record-low 34 percent ransomware payment rate, MOVEit victim counts and TikTok's passkey rollout follow. Listener questions cover TOTP in password managers, how Threads blocks VPN users, wireless keyboard security (Logitech Unifying versus Bluetooth), and why Steve avoids containers. The main topic is the IEEE S&P paper "Space Odyssey", where German researchers found 13 serious vulnerabilities in the firmware of three real LEO satellites. Steve's conclusion is that satellite security is decades behind because developers relied on obscurity and assumed nobody could reach a ground station.

# Key points

- Kevin Mitnick died on 16 July 2023 of pancreatic cancer, shortly before turning 60.
- Apple's nine-page submission opposes the UK plan to require pre-clearance of security features, global effect on non-UK firms and immediate compliance with disable orders; it says it would remove services from the UK rather than weaken products globally. Signal and WhatsApp had objected similarly.
- Web Environment Integrity, proposed by four Google engineers on GitHub, uses attestation (WebAuthn-like) so sites can trust the client environment; critics see Web DRM that could block ad blockers; Steve acknowledges legitimate bank and anti-bot use cases.
- FTC and HHS Office for Civil Rights wrote to 130 hospital systems and telehealth providers that tracking tech such as the Meta pixel and Google Analytics can breach HIPAA.
- The EU Cyber Resilience Act passed its committee 61-1-10, requiring secure-by-default automatic updates, encryption and attack reporting for IoT products; the U.S. only had a label emblem plan.
- Zyxel routers with an April flaw were being enlisted into a botnet because they cannot be updated.
- NSA/CYBERCOM nominee Lt. Gen. Timothy Haugh pledged "expeditionary cyber forces".
- Coveware: the share of ransomware victims who paid fell from 85 percent in 2019 to a record low 34 percent in Q2 2023, though the average ransom rose. Emsisoft counted over 380 confirmed MOVEit victims; Coveware expected Clop to earn about $75-100 million.
- TikTok announced passkey support (rolling out outside the U.S. first) and joined the FIDO Alliance.
- Logitech Unifying receiver keyboards use AES-CTR but have known weaknesses (CVE-2019-13053) that Logitech declined to fix; Steve recommends full Bluetooth keyboards where security matters.
- Space Odyssey (IEEE S&P 2023 Distinguished Paper): 13 vulnerabilities across three in-orbit satellite firmwares (ARM Cortex-M3, AVR32, LEON3 SPARC), including unauthenticated TeleCommands for memory read/write, an unchecked packet length field (Heartbleed-like over-read), a flag-protected firmware update path, and a stack overflow in the UFFS flash file system library used on about 75 spacecraft.
- Cheap ground stations (under $10,000) and Ground Station as a Service from AWS and Azure undermine the "inaccessibility" assumption; a survey of 19 satellite developers confirmed the findings; CCSDS defines the SDLS and SPP protocols.
- Firmware updates to old satellites can take days over 9600-baud links, which limits remediation.
- A counterspace report (Secure World Foundation) covers anti-satellite tests and debris risk (Kessler effect).

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a man blowing into a coffee pot to pour coffee through the spout.
- Leo and Steve discuss Twitter renaming to X, Threads' fading traffic and trademark questions.
- Steve avoids containers and clouds for personal infrastructure, runs Syncthing natively on Synology and keeps self-owned hardware in a Level 3 data center.
- SpinRite cannot be used on phones; SpinRite 6.1 integration of the InitDisk USB technology continues.
- Allyn Malventano's view: swap on SSD adds wear but is not a concern with sufficient RAM.
