# Summary

Steve first follows up on last week's Connected Experiences scare: Microsoft told BleepingComputer it does not use Microsoft 365 customer data to train large language models and that the setting, on by default since April 2019, powers features such as co-authoring, Editor and Designer using simpler machine learning. Next is research from Ben-Gurion University and Fujitsu showing that camera-based object detectors can be confused by emergency-vehicle flashing lights (a "digital epileptic seizure"), possibly relevant to Tesla Autopilot crashes into stationary emergency vehicles. The Tor Project is asking for volunteers to run WebTunnel bridges to help users in Russia, where Roskomnadzor is blocking hosting providers and circumvention tools. Smaller items: Zello told 140 million users to reset passwords without explanation, the FTC opened a broad antitrust probe of Microsoft, and Android scareware imitates a cracked screen. Listener feedback covers whether leaving WireGuard open to the Internet is safe, a cartoon and Nature study on AI "model collapse", isolating IoT devices, and a heated exchange about Australia's under-16 social media law and Jonathan Haidt's claims, on which Leo cites expert disagreement. The main topic is Voyager 1, nearly one light-day from Earth, whose X-band transmitter was shut off by fault protection after engineers turned on a heater to try to anneal degraded transistors, and whose thrusters are clogging with silicon residue.

# Key points

- **Microsoft Connected Experiences clarification**: Microsoft says Microsoft 365 consumer and commercial customer data is not used to train LLMs and the setting is unrelated; the features (Designer, Editor, translation, online content) use simpler machine learning. Steve accepts this but notes some per-account data is retained and that he dislikes the direction of the Windows desktop.
- **"Epilepticar" research**: five off-the-shelf dash cams and four open-source object detectors lost confidence on emergency light flashes, especially in the dark; inspired by 16 Tesla Autopilot collisions with stationary emergency vehicles (2018-2021) that led to an NHTSA-driven recall; the authors propose a software fix and stress they do not know whether Tesla is affected. Steve calls independent testing vital since carmakers keep systems proprietary.
- **Tor WebTunnel**: pluggable-transport bridge that wraps Tor traffic in WebSocket-like HTTPS so it blends with normal web traffic and can share an IP address and port with a real website; Tor wants 200 new bridges by end of December 2024 for users in Russia, runnable via a Docker image; a bridge is not a relay node.
- **Zello**: password reset demanded for accounts created before 2 November 2024; no explanation given and a previous breach occurred in 2020, so a breach or credential stuffing is suspected.
- **FTC vs Microsoft**: wide antitrust investigation of licensing, cloud, cybersecurity and AI practices; uncertain future under the incoming administration; Google also filed an EU complaint about Microsoft's cloud.
- **Fake cracked-screen scareware** on Android (spotted by Lukas Stefanko) tries to push victims toward fake virus removal or tech support.
- **WireGuard exposed**: a listener runs WireGuard on OPNsense with Suricata and CrowdSec and invisible to port scans because WireGuard stays silent to unauthenticated UDP packets; Steve agrees it is almost certainly safe but will not expose any service on GRC's production network, and notes port knocking adds minimal complexity.
- **Model collapse**: marketoonist/Nature study shows image models trained on AI output lose rare dog breeds and finally produce nonsense; a related Exeter study found individual creativity boosted but collective novelty reduced.
- **IoT VLAN isolation** can block firmware updates; solutions are temporary rewiring or a separate access point.
- **Australia age limit**: law passed, effective 20 November 2025, fines up to 50 million Australian dollars, YouTube excluded; Leo argues Haidt's "Anxious Generation" claims are disputed by researchers such as Candice Odgers and that no privacy-preserving technical method exists; Steve focuses on the missing technology.
- **Voyager 1**: 15.4 billion miles away, signal travel time over 23 hours each way; fault protection swapped X-band for S-band when a heater was turned on to anneal radiation-damaged JFETs; operations resumed in November 2024; power drops about 4 W per year; silicon from fuel-tank bladders is clogging thruster capillaries and engineers consider mixed branch operation; the hardware is software controlled, a design chosen to save weight; high data rate support expected to last until about 2027-2028; probes unlikely to survive another decade.
