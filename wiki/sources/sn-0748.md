# Summary

With Jason Howell hosting in Leo's absence, Steve opens 2020 with CanaryTail, a proposed JSON standard for machine-readable warrant canaries, explaining the history of canaries after the Patriot Act gag orders and the SpiderOak confusion. A Xiaomi camera bug showed strangers' video feeds on Google Nest Hub displays, and Google temporarily blocked Xiaomi devices. Microsoft's data-science blog on detecting RDP brute force attacks reports a roughly 0.08 percent success rate across 45,000 workstations, and Steve argues the answer is to never expose RDP, not to monitor it. Point-of-sale malware hit Landry's and Wawa, Russia announced a successful Runet disconnection test, and Python 2.7 reached end of life. HackerOne's top-20 bounty payers are reviewed, and Tencent's Magellan 2.0 SQLite flaws are flagged. The show closes with Sophos's seven-class malware lexicon: keyloggers, data stealers, RAM scrapers, bots, banking trojans, RATs, and ransomware.

# Key points

- Warrant canary: concept by Steve Schear (2002) of publishing a statement that disappears if a warrant arrives; EFF's Canary Watch shut down because canaries lapsed ambiguously; CanaryTail proposes a signed JSON file with flags (warrants, gag orders, trap and trace, duress, seppuku pledge), expiry, a Bitcoin block hash for freshness and a panic key.
- Xiaomi Mijia cameras linked to Google Nest Hub showed other users' home video (a baby, a sleeping adult) in a small number of cases; Google disabled Xiaomi integration while Xiaomi investigated. Advice: unlink until patched, and treat cloud-connected cameras as high risk.
- Microsoft RDP brute-force study: attacks typically last two to three days (throttled to avoid bans), about 0.08 percent of attacked machines compromised, roughly one compromise every three to four days across the studied enterprises. Steve: put RDP behind a VPN with MFA, never publicly exposed.
- POS malware: Landry's (600-plus venues; malware active March-October 2019, possibly earlier) and Wawa (about 850 stores; March-December 2019) skimmed payment card data; check statements.
- Russia's Runet test disconnected the country's network from the global Internet for several days under the Internet Sovereignty Law, with traffic routed through government chokepoints that also enable surveillance.
- Python 2.7 ended on January 1, 2020, with one final release planned for April; distros move to Python 3, Red Hat supports 2.7 through 2024; PyPy is an alternative.
- HackerOne top payers: Verizon Media, Uber, PayPal, Shopify, Twitter, Intel and others; PayPal paid the largest single bounty (30,000 dollars) and Shopify had the fastest payout.
- SQLite "Magellan 2.0" from Tencent Blade: five CVEs fixed December 13; exploitable via Web SQL in Chrome (already fixed) and in any app exposing SQL queries externally; many apps embed SQLite, so patching is uneven.
- Malware lexicon: keyloggers (software, JavaScript, hardware), data stealers, RAM scrapers (PCI-DSS and GDPR forbid storing some data so attackers scrape memory), bots/botnets, banking trojans (web form injection), RATs, ransomware (enabled by cryptocurrency).
