# Summary

Steve opens with Chrome's eighth exploited zero-day of 2021 (a V8 type confusion) and Google's note that two of those eight came from the same commercial surveillance vendor, which Microsoft and Citizen Lab identified as Candiru. Firefox 90 ships SmartBlock 2.0, which briefly unblocks the Facebook login script on demand so "Login with Facebook" still works in strict private browsing. The iOS WiFi SSID format-string bug turned out to be a zero-click "WiFi Demon" issue that Apple had partly and silently fixed in iOS 14.4, which Steve compares to Microsoft's piecemeal fixes. PrintNightmare continues with a new spooler elevation-of-privilege bug (CVE-2021-34481) and Benjamin Delpy's signed-malicious-driver technique that Microsoft cannot fix without breaking compatibility. July's Patch Tuesday brought 117 fixes including nine zero-days. Researchers from ETH Zurich and Royal Holloway found four weaknesses in Telegram's homegrown MTProto, which Steve uses to argue against rolling your own crypto. The Pegasus Project exposed widespread abuse of NSO Group spyware against journalists, politicians and activists, and the episode ends with the sudden, simultaneous shutdown of all REvil infrastructure, which Steve expects to be a pause or rebrand rather than an end.

# Key points

- Chrome 91.x fixed seven bugs including an in-the-wild V8 type confusion (CVE-2021-30563); eight exploited Chrome zero-days so far in 2021. Candiru spyware was linked to two of them.
- Firefox 90 SmartBlock 2.0 keeps tracker blocking in Private Browsing but loads the Facebook login script only when the user clicks the button. Steve calls it a sensible, tiny privacy exception, and notes OAuth is inherently non-private (a three-party login, unlike his SQRL).
- iOS WiFi SSID bug: unsanitised percent escapes in attacker-controlled SSIDs. ZecOps showed a zero-click drive-by variant ("WiFi Demon") from iOS 14.0; Apple removed %@ handling in 14.4 without a CVE and fully fixed the rest in 14.7. Lesson: fix the class of bug, not the single proof of concept.
- PrintNightmare: new spooler privilege escalation CVE-2021-34481 (workaround is disabling the Print Spooler service); a second, by-design problem lets a signed malicious printer driver propagate laterally even with Microsoft's mitigations. Microsoft's own wording was "vulnerable by design".
- July Patch Tuesday: 117 vulnerabilities, 13 critical, nine zero-days (four exploited); also an Exchange backlog, a Windows Hello spoofing bypass and a CVSS 9.9 kernel flaw. Adobe Acrobat and Reader got 14 critical fixes, so update them as well.
- Telegram MTProto analysis (disclosed April 2021, published July 16): four flaws including undetected message reordering ("Crime Pizza"). Default Telegram chats are only client-server encrypted. Steve: use established protocols rather than inventing your own.
- Pegasus Project: leaked list of 50,000+ phone numbers, 80+ journalists in 17 outlets, Amnesty technical forensics on 67 devices, zero-click iMessage exploits against a patched iPhone 12 on iOS 14.6, questions about Apple's BlastDoor. Steve ties this to the argument against government encryption backdoors.
- REvil vanished at 8 a.m. Moscow time: Tor payment site and clearweb decoder.re went offline together while servers stayed up. A LockBit post on the XSS forum rumoured a government request; REvil's representative was banned. Steve notes the pattern of groups like DarkSide and Babuk shutting down and relaunching (REvil itself descended from GandCrab).
- Ransomwhere, a new public site tracking ransomware payments by Bitcoin address, showed about $92 million in totals.
