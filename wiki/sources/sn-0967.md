# Summary

Leo returns. Steve briefly comments on the US Department of Justice's antitrust lawsuit against Apple, expecting a years-long process and noting Apple will argue closed-system security while Signal and WhatsApp show interoperable security is possible. General Motors, after public outcry, stopped sending OnStar Smart Driver data to data brokers LexisNexis and Verisk, and customers alleged salespeople had enrolled them. Other news: a white-hat "rescue" of $4.6 million from the Super Sushi Samurai game, Apple apparently abandoning HomeKit-secure routers while the Matter standard takes over, ICANN's planned reservation of the `.internal` top-level domain for private networks, a UN resolution on AI, Spain blocking Telegram, the Pwn2Own 2024 results and China's claim that foreign spies infiltrated hundreds of its organisations. SpinRite gets a Linux-friendly boot image and a built-in RAM test after a faulty ZimaBoard exposed false drive errors. The main topic corrects press hysteria about GoFetch: it is not an unpatchable chip flaw but a side channel using Apple's Data Memory-Dependent Prefetcher to leak keys from otherwise constant-time cryptography, mitigated by a DMP-disable bit on M3 or running crypto on efficiency cores on M1/M2.

# Key points

- DOJ and 15 states plus DC sued Apple under Sherman Act Section 2; Steve expects EU rules to bind Apple first.
- GM stopped sharing driving data with LexisNexis and Verisk after the New York Times exposure covered in the previous episode.
- Super Sushi Samurai recovered $4.6 million that a security researcher had moved out of harm's way; the researcher was hired as an advisor.
- Apple's 2019 HomeKit Secure Router programme is effectively dead (router vendors say Apple stopped accepting routers); the Matter standard (v1.2, 550+ companies) is the industry path, but it targets interoperability, not security.
- ICANN/IANA provisionally determined `.internal` should be reserved for private use and never delegated in the root, finally giving internal networks a safe TLD.
- UN General Assembly AI resolution; Steve and Leo doubt AI can be restrained.
- Spain's high court ordered carriers to block Telegram after media firms complained about copyright; consumer backlash followed.
- Pwn2Own Vancouver 2024: Manfred Paul exploited Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari ($202,500, Master of Pwn); 29 zero-days, $1.1 million total; Mozilla shipped Firefox 124.0.1 within about 24 hours. Steve's model is "security is porous".
- China's Ministry of State Security said foreign spy agencies infiltrated hundreds of local entities; tax-season phishing warning.
- SpinRite: zip with a 4 GB FAT32 boot image (SR61.img) for dd on Linux that auto-"downsizes" to small USB drives; planned RAM test after MemTest86 revealed marginal RAM on a ZimaBoard (SpinRite will refuse to run on unreliable memory).
- Passkeys in phone-free environments: Bitwarden or a YubiKey (up to 25 passkeys); FIDO2/WebAuthn is algorithm-agile for a post-quantum transition, which will require regenerating passkeys.
- GoFetch: DMP in Apple M-series (Firestorm performance cores) dereferences anything that looks like a pointer in cached data. Attackers feed crafted inputs so intermediate values look like pointers only when a secret satisfies a predicate, then observe cache activity. Demonstrated key extraction: 2048-bit RSA in under an hour, 2048-bit Diffie-Hellman in about two hours, Kyber and Dilithium material in 54 minutes to about 10 hours. Affects classical and post-quantum algorithms (OpenSSL DH and Go RSA cited); a similar but more restricted DMP exists on Intel Raptor Lake.
- Earlier "Augury" research (2022) found the DMP but not a practical exploit; Apple was told 107 days before disclosure; the attack needs local code execution.
- Mitigations: M3 offers a bit to disable DMP during crypto; M1/M2 could run crypto on Icestorm efficiency cores. Lesson: performance optimisations need an off switch.
