# Summary

Steve opens with an Expel report on PoisonSeed, a phishing campaign that defeats FIDO passkey protection by relaying a victim's credentials to the real login portal and forwarding the resulting cross-device QR code to the victim, who scans it with their authenticator; the lesson is that cross-device authentication is weaker than on-device passkeys. Ransomware items include Seoul Guarantee Insurance, United Natural Foods and a Qantas injunction. Cloudflare began geoblocking pirate-site domains in the UK with an HTTP 451 page after Motion Picture Association orders, while still refusing to filter its public DNS resolver. Age verification pressure grows (Roblox face-based age estimation, Steam removing adult games under payment-processor pressure), and Steve sketches a privacy-preserving protocol using a biometric-bound signer; Microsoft announces paid Exchange 2016/2019 extended security updates; Russia criminalizes deliberately searching for extremist content; China's Massistant tool can extract data from Android phones; Fortinet FortiWeb firewalls carry web shells after a SQL injection flaw. Listener feedback covers SpinRite restoring a Kindle, whether AI is "simulated intelligence", and a hash-based age scheme. The main topic is Cloudflare's postmortem of the 14 July 2025 outage that took the 1.1.1.1 resolver offline for 62 minutes because of a topology configuration error.

# Key points

- PoisonSeed passkey bypass: fake Okta-like page on a week-old Cloudflare-hosted domain captures username and password, requests cross-device sign-in at the real portal, relays the QR code to the victim; not a FIDO flaw but an adversary-in-the-middle downgrade to cross-device flow. Steve notes domain age should be treated as a red flag and recalls that SQRL avoided this for same-device use. FIDO's new passkey import/export standard is welcome.
- Ransomware: Seoul Guarantee Insurance (hand-written guarantees during outage), United Natural Foods expects up to $400 million in lost sales, Qantas obtained an injunction against use of stolen data (Steve doubts its value).
- Cloudflare in the UK now serves "Error 451 Unavailable For Legal Reasons" for around 200 MPA-requested pirate domains, including to UK VPN exit users; transparency of the underlying orders (Lumen database) is thin. Cloudflare still resists DNS-level blocking but offers 1.1.1.2 (malware) and 1.1.1.3 (malware plus adult content) filtered resolvers.
- Age verification: Roblox requires a video selfie for trusted chat for 13+; Valve removed thousands of adult games after payment processors complained; Steve proposes in-person binding of a biometric-locked age assertion (post office, DMV, notary) and a token-signing protocol with per-session single-use tokens signed by age-asserting providers; he stresses that hashes or QR codes alone would be traded on a black market, so biometrics plus blinding are needed, and fears this ends free internet access. The Social Security number cannot legally serve as an identity key.
- Microsoft Exchange 2016/2019 end of support is 14 October 2025; a six-month paid ESU from 1 August 2025 through 14 April 2026 offers no guaranteed updates; Steve notes 0patch also covers Exchange.
- Russia passed a law fining people for deliberately searching for officially listed extremist material (about $65) and heavier fines for advertising VPNs; effective September; tied to wider Runet sovereignty and deep packet inspection.
- China: Lookout reported Massistant, a successor to MFSocket, used at checkpoints to extract location, SMS, contacts and chat data from Android phones; advice is to use a burner phone with plausible history; Leo and a listener note the U.S. does similar border searches.
- FortiWeb: web shells found on about 80 devices exploited via CVE-2025-25257 (pre-authentication SQL injection); Steve says exposed web management portals have become the new buffer overflow.
- Listener items: SpinRite Level 3 restored a Kindle's storage; Steve calls current AI "simulated intelligence", quoting Dijkstra that asking whether computers think is like asking whether submarines swim.
- Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 outage: a June 6 config error attached the resolver prefixes to a not-yet-live Data Localization Suite topology; a change on 14 July at 21:48 UTC triggered withdrawal of the BGP routes globally; resolver traffic dropped from 21:52 to 22:54 UTC (62 minutes); DoH via cloudflare-dns.com mostly stayed up; a hijack of the prefix by Tata Communications was exposed but unrelated. The legacy topology system lacked progressive canary deployment. Traffic mix on Radar: UDP 86%, DoT 7.1%, DoH 4.7%, TCP 2.2%; IPv4 queries about 62.6%, IPv6 18.8%. Anycast routing explained versus unicast resolvers such as DNS4EU. Steve advises configuring resolvers from more than one provider.
