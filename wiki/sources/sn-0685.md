# Summary

Steve notes Paul Allen's death, then reviews the continued denials of Bloomberg's Supermicro story (DHS said it had no evidence while calling the threat real) and updates on Facebook, which revised the stolen-token count from 50 million to about 30 million, in a pivot attack that started from 400,000 accounts. Microsoft's October update saga continues: the data-deleting 1809 feature update was traced to Known Folder Redirection cleanup, an HP keyboard driver and Intel audio drivers caused boot or sound failures, Edge and store apps needed IPv6, and a Jet database remote-code flaw was "fixed" incorrectly, leaving every Windows version exposed and the 0patch micropatch needing a rework. A Kuwaiti researcher published a trivial Edge remote-code-execution bug after the patch. Browser vendors jointly announced retiring TLS 1.0 and 1.1 by March 2020, Google added control-flow integrity to the Android 9 kernel (Pixel 3 first), a GAO report found Department of Defense weapon systems easily taken over, and PHP 5.6 support ends at the end of 2018. The title topic is a Russian-speaking grey hat who applied firewall fixes to over 100,000 vulnerable MikroTik routers without consent, which Steve and Leo agree is illegal and risky even though auto-update by default for routers remains Steve's goal.

# Key points

- Bloomberg's Supermicro claims: Apple, Amazon, Supermicro and DHS deny; Bloomberg stands by its reporting; Steve notes implants could be embedded within board layers.
- Facebook update: attackers started with controlled accounts, pivoted to about 400,000 users (seeing timelines, friend lists, groups), then stole tokens for roughly 30 million; 15 million had name and contact details taken, 14 million much more profile data; Facebook did not mention third-party Login with Facebook exposure.
- Windows 10 1809: the folder-cleanup logic deleted original known-folder locations still holding data; Insider feedback about data loss had been ignored; the Feedback Hub now lets users flag severity. Also an HP keyboard driver blue screen, Intel audio driver issues, brightness reset and an Edge/UWP IPv6 dependency.
- Jet database engine: Microsoft's October fix was incomplete, so the bug stays exploitable until at least November; Acros released a smaller patch for the revised code.
- Edge: Abdulrahman Al-Qabandi's remote code execution via a page and an Enter key press was patched in October, then published with working code.
- TLS 1.0 and 1.1 retired by all major browsers in 2020 (Chrome warns from version 72 and disables around version 81); current traffic is about 93 percent TLS 1.2, under 6 percent TLS 1.3, roughly 1 percent TLS 1.0.
- Control Flow Integrity (LLVM) in the Android 9 kernel on the Pixel 3, comparable to Microsoft's Control Flow Guard; Chromium builds show under 1 percent time cost and about 15 percent size cost.
- GAO: DOD weapon-system testers took control in about a day, scanning crashed systems, and known vulnerabilities went unfixed.
- EFF opposes a Chicago ordinance enabling business face recognition that conflicts with Illinois BIPA.
- DuckDuckGo traffic is rising and its result links are direct, unlike Google's redirect links.
- PHP 5.6 ends at year end; about 62 percent of PHP sites still run 5.x.
- AV-Comparatives results: Avast, AVG, Bitdefender, F-Secure, Microsoft, Trend Micro and Vipre had perfect protection scores, with Microsoft showing the most false positives.
- ACI router study: 155 of 186 routers had known flaws, 32,003 vulnerabilities in total.
- Steve's view: routers should ask at setup and default to auto-updating; vigilante changes risk breaking needed services.

# Other segments

- Feedback on OCSP and stapling, drive wiping and spared sectors, VPNFilter removal (reflash and lock down), and John McAfee's false claim about an E911 chip in alerts.
- Kevin Mitnick's malicious USB cable demonstration and the Presidential Alert aftermath.
