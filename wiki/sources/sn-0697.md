# Summary

Steve reviews January Patch Tuesday: 51 fixes including a DHCP client code-execution bug and two Hyper-V escapes found internally, but two updates broke file sharing on Windows 7 and Server 2008 R2 (a standalone fix followed), and a server-side change caused false "not genuine" warnings for volume-licensed Windows 7. During the US government shutdown, around 80 government sites had expired TLS certificates; those on HSTS preload lists became unreachable in browsers, and Steve urges renewing certificates early. A WhatsApp user received a prior number holder's message history, showing how phone-number-bound identity and recycled numbers undermine privacy; Steve coins a corollary to "trust no one": if you are not managing your own keys, someone else is. Other items: a ransomware decryptor that needs captured network traffic, Google Public DNS adding DNS over TLS, a study valuing illicit Monero mining at over 4 percent of supply, router threat reports (platform-style malware), Los Angeles suing The Weather Channel app over location data, a 51 percent attack on Ethereum Classic, a California magistrate ruling that biometric unlocking cannot be compelled, and the schedule for disabling Flash. The main topic is Zerodium, which doubled many exploit payouts, up to 2 million dollars for a zero-click iOS remote jailbreak, selling mainly to governments.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday: DHCP client memory corruption, Hyper-V guest-to-host flaws, ChakraCore and Jet fixes; KB4480960 and KB4480970 caused SMB share problems, fixed by a later update; do not leave the registry workaround in place.
- Shutdown side effect: about 80 gov sites with expired certificates; HSTS preload prevents bypass; sites needing logins could leak credentials if users clicked through; certificate authorities credit remaining time on early renewal.
- WhatsApp: account tied to phone number; recycled numbers and loose key trust can expose another person's history; FAQ tells users to migrate with Change Number; Threema-style self-managed keys are an alternative but closed source.
- PyLocky ransomware decryptable only with packet capture of its key exchange, so little practical use.
- Google Public DNS added DNS over TLS on port 853; stub resolvers exist for Android 9 and other platforms; DoT protects all system lookups while DoH protects browsers.
- Cryptomining malware study: 4.4 million samples, about 4.3 percent of Monero associated with illicit mining, around 53 million dollars; Check Point's top malware list dominated by miners (Coinhive, XMRig, JSEcoin, CryptoLoot).
- Avast threat report: 60 percent of users never update router firmware; router malware becoming a platform for pay-per-install, DDoS, mining and spam.
- Netgate SG-1100 with pfSense recommended as a verifiable perimeter router.
- Los Angeles sued The Weather Channel over undisclosed location data sales; New York Times found 75 companies receiving precise location data.
- Ethereum Classic suffered 51 percent attack with repeated reorganizations and double spends of about 219,500 coins (about 1.1 million dollars); Coinbase halted ETC transfers; size gives Bitcoin more safety.
- Court: magistrate in Northern California refused to allow forced face or finger unlocking, though rulings differ across courts.
- Flash: Firefox to disable by default in v69, Chrome in v76, Edge/IE in 2019, end of life in 2020.
- Zerodium payouts: iOS zero-click remote jailbreak 2 million dollars, one-click 1.5 million, WhatsApp/iMessage/SMS RCE 1 million, Chrome RCE with sandbox escape 500,000, Windows RCE via SMB/RDP 1 million, local privilege escalations 80,000 to 200,000; customers described as governments; weekly payment and NDA-bound research feed.
