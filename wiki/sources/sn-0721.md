# Summary

Steve tracks the wave of Ryuk ransomware attacks on small US municipalities: Lake City, Florida paid 42 bitcoin through its insurance pool after an Emotet-to-TrickBot-to-Ryuk chain, and its IT director was fired; Key Biscayne and Georgia's court administration were hit as well, and public sentiment is turning against officials who pay. He then covers a Politico report that the US administration is weighing legislation against unbreakable end-to-end encryption, with the term "warrant-compatible encryption" noted, and promises to cover GCHQ's "Ghost Protocol" proposal next week. Sophos published a video demonstrating a BlueKeep attack that swaps the accessibility button for a system console. The core segment argues that "exposed database" incidents are really publishing mistakes needing two failures (public binding and no authentication) and walks through three cases: a MongoDB with over 5 million insurance-marketing records, Attunity's open S3 buckets holding over a terabyte of backups from many large companies, and Orvibo's unsecured log of over two billion smart-home records with unsalted MD5 password hashes and precise locations. Steve jokes IoT should stand for "Installation of Trojan."

# Key points

- Lake City, FL: employee-opened email led to Emotet, TrickBot, then Ryuk; ransom demand went to the Florida League of Cities insurer; 42 bitcoin (about 400,000 USD) paid; no usable backup; city paid a 10,000 USD deductible and fired the IT director. Riviera Beach later faced about a million dollars more in remediation (per Leo).
- Key Biscayne and the Georgia Administrative Office of the Courts also hit by Ryuk; Steve and Leo stress offline, rotated image backups and note that attackers target municipalities because they are insured and publicly list staff for spear-phishing.
- Going dark: Politico reported a National Security Council deputies meeting on seeking legislation against uncrackable encryption; agencies are split (DOJ/FBI versus Commerce/State; DHS divided); experts expect Congress not to pass such a bill; the 2016 Burr-Feinstein draft died in backlash.
- Sophos Labs BlueKeep demo (not public code) shows login bypass and a system console; still no worm; Steve thinks mining is the likelier use.
- Exposed databases: MedicareSupplement.com MongoDB (over 5 million records, found via BinaryEdge indexing and Comparitech); Attunity (acquired by Qlik) S3 buckets with 750 GB of compressed email plus OneDrive backups, found by UpGuard and closed within a day of notice, customer list including Netflix, TD Bank and Ford; Orvibo (Shenzhen) logs of over 2 billion records with emails, reset codes, geolocation, device names, found by vpnMentor and unreported fixed at time of recording.
- Steve's framing: such leaks are not "breaches" but accidental publication; IoT users have no oversight over what vendors log or expose.
- Smaller items: SQRL forum moderation, a WordPress SQRL plugin prototype and other client ports; SpinRite 6.1 testing will occur in GRC's newsgroups; uBlock Origin per-site JavaScript disabling; a listener reported a worm that patches Exim servers.
