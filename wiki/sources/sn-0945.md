# Summary

The episode begins with how Amazon handled a customer's ValiDrive-backed review of a fake 512 GB drive (the review was removed and the product declared authentic). News items: Microsoft moves VBScript to a Feature on Demand ahead of eventual removal, and announces the eventual disabling of NTLM in Windows 11 with new Kerberos features (IAKerb, local KDC). curl's maintainer pre-announced a high-severity flaw (CVE-2023-38545, a SOCKS5 heap overflow found by JFrog). The big story is Cisco IOS XE: CVE-2023-20198 in the web admin UI plus a second zero-day (CVE-2023-20273) let attackers create level-15 accounts and implant a Lua backdoor on more than 42,000 devices before patches on October 16; the implant then vanished from scans, suggesting stealth rather than cleanup. Steve repeats that web administration must never face the Internet and uses a VPN or overlay network instead. Listener items include SpinRite 6.1 pre-release usage, SSD read-disturb slowdowns, passkey export plans from the FIDO Alliance, limits of ECH versus IP-based tracking, and Privacy Badger. The main segment covers the NSA/CISA misconfiguration "improper separation of user and administrator privilege".

# Key points

- Amazon removed a review exposing a fake 512 GB USB stick (really 53 GB); fake NVMe-looking enclosures can contain a hot-glued SD card.
- VBScript becomes an optional Feature on Demand; malware authors are expected to migrate to PowerShell.
- NTLM (since 1993) will be disabled in a future Windows 11; Microsoft adds logging and group policy to prepare; Kerberos needs a domain controller, hence IAKerb and local KDC. Windows 10 support ends October 14, 2025.
- curl 8.4 fixed CVE-2023-38545 (SOCKS5 hostname handling race leading to heap overflow, libcurl 7.69.0 to 8.3) and a low-severity CVE-2023-38546; embedded copies make the exposure broad, and Steve speculates governments keep databases of such library dependencies.
- Cisco IOS XE: attacks began September 28, patch October 16; Lua implant on 42,000+ devices (Censys/Shadowserver counts) then dropped to a few hundred, indicating attacker-hidden implants; a 2021 bug (CVE-2021-1435) was also exploited by a different actor.
- Advice: never expose router, NAS, Plex or other web admin interfaces to the Internet; the LastPass breach involved a Plex server at a developer's home.
- Passkey export: a 1Password employee says FIDO Alliance work on secure import/export is under way.
- ReadSpeed on an SSD showed 2.2 MB/s at the start of the drive versus 482 MB/s elsewhere (read disturb); SpinRite refresh helps, and SpinRite 7 is planned to target slow regions.
- Encrypting an already encrypted file does not stop ransomware; ECH hides the hostname but destination IPs remain visible, so Tor or a trusted VPN is needed for destination privacy.
- Privacy Badger (EFF) is an algorithmic tracker blocker (blocks a third party seen tracking on three sites) and also strips Google's rewritten search result links.
- NSA/CISA privilege item: excessive account privileges (privilege creep), elevated service accounts (kerberoasting), and non-essential use of admin accounts; an assessment team compromised a domain in under a business day via a phished admin user. Leo contrasts sudo with Windows UAC.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a barrier at the end of a paved path with a worn dirt desire path around it.
- Pronunciation debate: "lib" versus "libe".
- GRC plans to extend ShieldsUP to all 65,536 ports after SpinRite 7.
