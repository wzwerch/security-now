# Summary

Steve returns to the February Windows 10 rollup (KB4532693) after reports that some users permanently lost their profiles, and notes 0patch now covers Windows 10 1903 and 1909 for the IE JScript zero-day since many users are skipping the update. Microsoft plans optional driver updates in the Windows 10 2004 release. Researchers presented IMP4GT at NDSS, an attack on 4G LTE (and 5G) that exploits missing user-plane integrity protection and OS ICMP behaviour to build encryption and decryption oracles and impersonate a phone or the network. Firefox begins enabling DNS over HTTPS by default in the US, and Mozilla is adding the RLBox fine-grained library sandbox, and Chrome 80.0.3987.122 patched an exploited V8 zero-day. Steve updates on SpinRite, deciding AHCI support is now required. The main item is Apple's announcement at the CA/Browser Forum that Safari will distrust TLS certificates issued after August 31, 2020 with a lifetime over 398 days, a unilateral move after a Google ballot for one-year certificates failed, with Steve and Leo arguing OCSP stapling would have been a better answer.

# Key points

- Picture of the Week: misleading 3D pie charts on a TV election segment where equal values look unequal.
- Windows 10 KB4532693: some users' profiles were renamed and then reported unrecoverable, with Microsoft silent; Steve will image systems before updates. Microsoft's reduced QA staff is blamed by commentators.
- 0patch micropatches for CVE-2020-0674 added for Windows 10 1903/1909; free for non-commercial use.
- Windows 10 2004 will let drivers be marked optional and show in an Optional Updates section; Steve and Leo question whether this helps ordinary users.
- IMP4GT (impersonation in 4G networks): a man-in-the-middle with a software-defined radio flips bits to trigger ICMP replies, learns keystreams, and can send and receive arbitrary IP packets as the victim, enabling billing fraud, firewall bypass and false attribution; 5G repeats the design; application-level end-to-end encryption such as iMessage, Signal and WhatsApp is not affected, plain web traffic is more exposed; it needs physical proximity and is targeted rather than mass.
- Firefox: DoH default for new US installs from February 25 and gradually for all US users, Cloudflare by default with NextDNS as an option; the UK is excluded after ISP lobbying.
- RLBox sandboxes third-party libraries (libvorbis, libvpx, Skia style libraries) inside the Firefox renderer; arrives in Firefox 74 for Linux, 75 for Mac, later Windows.
- Chrome 80.0.3987.122 fixes a V8 type confusion exploited in the wild, Chrome's third zero-day in twelve months.
- SpinRite: Steve is rereading about 6,000 newsgroup posts from 2013, will add native AHCI support alongside legacy mode, plans UEFI support later, and notes Macs without BIOS emulation will not run it.
- Apple's 398-day rule: Safari will reject certificates issued from September 1, 2020 valid longer than 398 days; DigiCert notified customers and plans multi-year subscriptions and automation (ACME, APIs). Steve recalls lifetimes falling from 8-10 years to 3, 2, and now 1; Leo and Steve argue OCSP stapling would revoke within hours; Steve expects browsers may someday flag automated domain-validated certificates.
