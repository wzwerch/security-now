# Summary

Mikah Sargent substitutes for Leo Laporte. Steve first covers Microsoft's Windows Endpoint Security Ecosystem Summit of 10 September 2024, held in reaction to the CrowdStrike outage: it produced talk of shared safe-deployment practices and future non-kernel security APIs, but no decisions, and Steve expects slow negotiation and little change. He then discusses an Ars Technica report that Iron Mountain finds about one fifth of 1990s music-industry hard drives unreadable, using it to argue that every medium decays and data must be refreshed, and shares his own RAID 6 practice. A Navy Times story describes chiefs on the USS Manchester who secretly installed a Starlink dish and Wi-Fi network, leading to a court-martial. Steve notes ADAMnetworks' adam:ONE won an SC Award for best SASE solution, reports lessons from mailing SpinRite 6.1 announcements (email reputation, bounce rates, Apple blocking), and explains why a mostly empty SSD can appear slower after SpinRite. The main topic is the Cornell Tech/Ben-Gurion research on injection attacks against ten password managers and against WhatsApp and Signal backups. Steve judges the practical risk to ordinary users very low but values the lesson that encryption is only the start of keeping a secret.

# Key points

- Microsoft summit: vendors and US/EU officials discussed safe deployment practices (gradual, staged rollout), more joint testing, and new Windows 11 capabilities letting security products work outside kernel mode; Steve calls it largely for show, notes kernel access is needed for performance and anti-tampering, and points out Windows 10 users would be left out.
- Staged rollout is trivial to implement, and CrowdStrike's failure to do it is, in Steve's view, arrogance; EU rules that forced Microsoft to open the kernel to third parties are part of the background.
- Long-term archiving: Iron Mountain reports roughly 20% of 1990s hard drives from the music industry entirely unreadable; optical media, tape, flash and floppies all degrade; advice is to copy data periodically to fresh storage.
- Steve runs 16 spinning drives in four RAID 6 arrays, replacing about one or two per year, and has never lost data; a drive flagged bad once needed only a SpinRite sector rewrite.
- Offline SSDs lose data faster when stored hot; periodically rewriting media strengthens stored bits.
- USS Manchester: more than 15 chiefs bought a Starlink kit with personal funds and hid the Wi-Fi network (named "Stinky", later disguised as a printer) from command; the leader was court-martialed and reduced in rank. Security risk: personal devices could be a path into ship networks.
- ADAMnetworks adam:ONE (zero-trust perimeter/SASE) won the 2024 SC Awards category; Steve first described it in episode 946.
- SpinRite 6.1 mailing: sent from the separate domain SpinRite.news via Postmark to protect GRC.com's mail reputation; bounce rates climbed to about 10% going back to 2011; Apple initially bounced the mail; Exchange sometimes sent it to spam; Steve asks listeners to mark it "not spam".
- SSD benchmark oddity: an SSD that was never written reads blank regions instantly (fake high speeds); after SpinRite rewrites the whole surface the drive treats it as in use and shows true media speed; the OS's periodic TRIM restores the earlier state.
- A listener showed Perplexity.ai could produce Steve's feedback address; discussion of Google NotebookLM's AI podcast feature hallucinating a conversation about meaningless "patent" files, and of Windows Recall's privacy implications.
- Research (Fabrega, Nassi and colleagues, USENIX Security 24 and IEEE S&P 24): an attacker controlling only their own client injects data (for example via credential sharing) into a victim's client and observes encrypted state or traffic to infer secrets.
- Vault-health logging attack: password health metrics (such as duplicate-password counts) logged to servers let a server-side observer run a binary-search dictionary attack; affected LastPass, Dashlane, Zoho Vault, Keeper and NordPass; LastPass and NordPass separated shared from personal credentials in the metrics.
- URL icon fetching attack: icons fetched once per URL reveal whether the victim already stored a URL, visible to a network observer; affected Dashlane, 1Password, Enpass, Roboform, Proton Pass and NordPass; mitigation is disabling icons (default off in some products).
- KeePassXC (KDBX format) leaked through compression and attachment deduplication size changes; fixed with per-folder deduplication and random-length padding in version 2.7 (a partial mitigation).
- A companion paper showed limited leakage from WhatsApp and Signal backups (whether a specific attachment was received); Steve reads this as evidence of how strong those apps are.
- Steve's conclusion: practical risk is on the fringe, but "encrypted with a huge key" is meaningless if implementation details leak metadata; Bitwarden was not among the ten tested products.
