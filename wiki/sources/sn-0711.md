# Summary

The headline is Cisco Talos's research into a state-sponsored DNS hijacking operation it named Sea Turtle, which Steve presents under the DNSpionage label even though Talos says it is distinct from the earlier DNSpionage campaign. Attackers compromised DNS registrars, registries and ISPs to redirect targets such as ministries and energy firms, obtained certificates from automated CAs for the hijacked names and harvested credentials with man-in-the-middle servers; Steve says DNSSEC would block this. Other stories: Google's Sensorvault location store being used by police under geofence warrants; Drupal's repeated security releases; new details that Facebook, when verifying accounts by email password, also harvested 1.5 million contact lists and that Instagram passwords had also been logged in plaintext; Russia's Duma approving an Internet isolation bill; a student pleading guilty to destroying college computers with a USB Killer; Marcus Hutchins pleading guilty to two counts; heavy exploitation of a patched win32k zero-day; Edge news; and Mozilla planning to enable hyperlink auditing by default. Steve also describes relaunching his blog and the trust problems of a brand-new EV code-signing certificate.

# Key points

- Sea Turtle (Talos): active since about January 2017, at least 40 organisations in 13 countries compromised; attackers hijacked DNS by controlling registrar or registry accounts and name servers (first publicly confirmed compromise of an organisation managing a root server zone), used certificates from Let's Encrypt, Comodo/Sectigo or self-signed ones in man-in-the-middle servers, then stole the victims' legitimate certificates; initial access came from known flaws (including phpMyAdmin, Shellshock, Cisco devices, Tomcat, Drupalgeddon) or spear phishing. DHS had warned on January 24, 2019. Talos urges nations to treat DNS as off-limits.
- Sensorvault: Google's store of location data is queried under geofence warrants in stages (anonymous device IDs near a crime, then narrowed, then identities revealed); at least one innocent man was jailed; Leo doubts such fishing-style warrants are constitutional and notes Location History being off does not stop all tracking.
- Drupal: ten significant vulnerabilities in 12 months, many from third-party libraries; Steve argues widely deployed software needs reliable update notification and mentions his own prototype update checker and the SQRL client's auto-update.
- Facebook: since May 2016 some new users who verified by entering an email password had their contacts imported (1.5 million people) for ad targeting and friend suggestions; Facebook called the upload unintentional. Facebook also admitted additional logs of Instagram passwords stored readably, affecting millions of Instagram users.
- Russia: State Duma passed the bill requiring ISPs to enable routing through state-approved exchange points with a national DNS, enabling filtering and isolation; satellite Internet would remain an escape route.
- USB Killer: a $40-class device that discharges about 200 V into USB ports; an MBA graduate destroyed 59 computers and other equipment at the College of St. Rose (about $51,000 in damage) and pleaded guilty after filming himself.
- Marcus Hutchins (who stopped WannaCry) pleaded guilty to two of ten counts tied to Kronos banking malware written when he was younger; he posted a statement accepting responsibility.
- The patched win32k.sys use-after-free reported by Kaspersky is now widely exploited in APT campaigns against 64-bit Windows 7 to older Windows 10, apparently because operators rushed to use it before systems patch.
- Edge (Chromium): warns when run elevated (Bleeping Computer showed how Notepad launched as admin could spawn an admin Edge); Application Guard integration is coming; Edge will present Edge or Chrome user-agent strings depending on the site; Windows 7 end-of-support dialogs are appearing and link to a PC-sales page.
- Mozilla told BleepingComputer it will enable the hyperlink ping attribute by default and not offer a disable switch, agreeing with Apple that it is a user-experience choice; Brave continues to block it.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: an office sign-up sheet for password changes listing old and new passwords.
- Steve relaunched his blog at blog.grc.com on a self-hosted hardened WordPress, with Jetpack services, and discusses WordPress self-hosting risks.
- GRC code signing: new DigiCert EV certificate on a hardware token triggered SmartScreen and antivirus warnings until it earned reputation; re-signed DNS Benchmark, InSpectre, Never10 and LeakTest; SpinRite v6 owners will get 6.1 free and old-version upgrades may end; SpinRite works on floppies.
