# Summary

The episode is almost entirely about AI. Italy's data protection authority blocked the Chinese DeepSeek service for insufficient answers about personal data handling, while researchers reported that DeepSeek models are easy to jailbreak, and ChatGPT-4o had a "Time Bandit" jailbreak and GitHub Copilot a "sure" jailbreak. Wiz Research found an open, unauthenticated ClickHouse database at DeepSeek exposing over a million log lines including chat history and secrets. Steve explains why DeepSeek matters: far cheaper training and inference through low-precision arithmetic, mixture-of-experts (671B parameters, 37B active) and hand-tuned GPU code, all open source, which threatened Nvidia's valuation. Microsoft made OpenAI's o1 free through Copilot's "Think Deeper". Google's Threat Intelligence Group reported that state-backed actors use Gemini mainly for productivity, not novel attacks. Non-AI news covered an unpatched Zyxel router flaw exploited by Mirai, the U.S. ROUTERS Act, the FADPA site-blocking bill, Russia's 417,000 blocked sites, Thailand's scam liability law, Edge's scareware blocker and Bitwarden's new-device email verification. The main topic is Unit 42's jailbreak research on DeepSeek, from which Steve concludes that such models are knowledgeable but naive extraction systems and that controlling them is a different, harder problem than classic bugs like buffer overflows.

# Key points

- Italy's Garante blocked DeepSeek on 30 January 2025 after the firms said Italian law did not apply to them; it had earlier banned ChatGPT temporarily and fined OpenAI 15 million euros.
- DeepSeek was reported vulnerable to Crescendo, Bad Likert Judge, Deceptive Delight, DAN and EvilBOT jailbreaks (Unit 42, HiddenLayer, KELA); HiddenLayer also saw hints that OpenAI data was in DeepSeek's training. CERT/CC described the ChatGPT-4o "Time Bandit" jailbreak (anchoring to a historical period) and Apex found that starting prompts with "Sure" shifts Copilot into compliance.
- Leo and Steve agree "AI safety" via guardrails is likely a whack-a-mole exercise, especially since local models lose app-level guardrails and cheap models will exist with no controls.
- Wiz Research: unauthenticated ClickHouse instance on two DeepSeek subdomains (ports 8123 and 9000) with a log_stream table of more than one million entries including plaintext chat messages and API secrets; DeepSeek secured it after disclosure. Lesson: basic infrastructure exposure remains the main AI security risk.
- DeepSeek explained (via Morgan Brown of Dropbox and MIT Technology Review): claimed training cost of about $5-6 million versus $100 million-plus, 8-bit style reduced precision, multi-token prediction, mixture of experts, and bypassing CUDA with low-level code; these claims were not independently verified.
- Copilot "Think Deeper" gives free access to OpenAI's o1 reasoning model; Steve ties this to "query-time compute" producing better answers with less hallucination.
- Google GTIG report on Gemini misuse: no novel prompt attacks seen; actors used public jailbreak prompts unsuccessfully; Iranian actors were the heaviest users, North Korean actors used it for fake-identity job applications; LLMs speed up phishing and malware work rather than enabling breakthroughs.
- Zyxel CPE routers: CVE-2024-40891 (Telnet) and CVE-2024-40890 (HTTP) allow unauthenticated command execution; GreyNoise linked exploitation to Mirai variants; over 1,500 devices exposed per Censys; Zyxel had still not patched six months after disclosure.
- U.S. ROUTERS Act would have Commerce study routers and modems from foreign adversaries; Steve would prefer requiring CISA IoT security guidelines.
- FADPA (Rep. Zoe Lofgren) would let courts order large ISPs and DNS resolvers (revenue over $100M) to block foreign pirate sites with no-fault immunity; Public Knowledge and Re:Create call it censorship infrastructure.
- Russia's censorship list now covers about 417,000 sites; Thailand is drafting a law making banks, telcos and platforms co-liable for preventable scam losses.
- Edge gains an opt-in "Scareware blocker" (edge://settings, Privacy, search, and services) that uses machine learning to detect tech-support scam pages.
- Bitwarden will require email verification for logins from unknown devices when two-factor authentication is not enabled; Steve says real two-factor is better.
- Unit 42 jailbreak demonstrations: Bad Likert Judge (asking the model to rate harm on a scale and then produce top-rated examples), Crescendo (gradual escalation from a benign history question) and Deceptive Delight (embedding an unsafe topic between benign ones in a story) all produced prohibited output from DeepSeek; details are described only at report level here.
