# Summary

On Windows 7's final Patch Tuesday, Steve and Leo note a Crypt32.dll certificate-validation flaw affecting all Windows versions that the NSA urged people to patch, and Leo wonders aloud whether it was a deliberate weakness. The FBI again asked Apple to unlock a shooter's iPhone, prompting discussion of whether law would need to change and the difference between a lawful intercept on iMessage and decrypting a locked phone. The checkra1n jailbreak keeps maturing, Apple confirms server-side scanning of iCloud images against known abuse hashes, and Princeton researchers show five US prepaid carriers can be tricked into SIM swaps with weak authentication. Unpatched Pulse Secure VPN servers remained widespread nine months after a critical fix and were used as an entry point for Sodinokibi ransomware (including a Travelex case). Firefox 72.0.1 fixed an in-the-wild JIT zero-day. The headline item is Cable Haunt, a buffer overflow in the spectrum-analyzer service of Broadcom-based cable modems reachable through a browser WebSocket, with ISP-only firmware updates.

# Key points

- Windows 7 reaches end of support; roughly a quarter of workstations still ran it. A Crypt32.dll flaw (certificate spoofing) was fixed in the same Patch Tuesday; Steve is moving to Windows 10 on an Intel NUC.
- FBI/Apple: Apple says it helps with iCloud data but cannot unlock modern iPhones; Steve sees a legal mandate for access as the only route and distinguishes it from the technically possible silent listener in iMessage groups.
- checkra1n 0.9.1: boot ROM based jailbreak, tethered (reboot clears it), available on macOS and Windows, Linux pending.
- Apple's privacy chief said images stored in iCloud are scanned for child-abuse material; Steve explains perceptual fingerprinting such as Microsoft's PhotoDNA and the NCMEC hash list.
- Princeton study "An Empirical Study of Wireless Carrier Authentication for SIM Swaps": AT&T, T-Mobile, Tracfone, US Mobile and Verizon all used weak challenges (recent payments via refill cards, recent calls, account or device info, security questions), some allowed swaps with no valid authentication; 17 of about 140 websites could be taken over by SIM swap alone.
- Pulse Secure VPN: April 2019 patch for a pre-auth bypass; in August about 15,000 endpoints were still vulnerable and public exploits circulated; over a thousand still unpatched later; REvil/Sodinokibi attacks traced to it, including Travelex with seven unpatched servers.
- Firefox 72.0.1 fixes an IonMonkey type-confusion zero-day reported by Qihoo 360; JIT compilers inherently need executable memory.
- Credit freezes are now free and offer temporary lifts; Steve recounts unlocking for an Amazon card and for Social Security setup.
- Chrome fails to update first-party persistent cookies in GRC's cookie test page; Leo reports Google plans to phase out third-party cookies and user-agent strings within two years.
- Cable Haunt (Lyrebirds researchers): Broadcom reference firmware on the eCos OS, with no ASLR or stack protection, parses JSON for the modem's spectrum analyzer on a LAN-side port (8080 on Steve's modem); an oversized parameter allows code execution via return-oriented programming and, through browser JavaScript or any LAN device, full control (DNS changes, firmware replacement, botnet). About 200 million modems affected in Europe alone; Broadcom shipped a fix in May 2019 but ISPs must push firmware, causing brief outages. Interim mitigation: block the modem's spectrum-analyzer port from the LAN (find it with a port scanner), and note Firefox does not support that analyzer page.

# Other segments

- The SHA-1 chosen-prefix collision was to be the lead story but was pre-empted; it is mentioned only in the intro.
- Steve walked out of a 4DX Star Wars screening; talk of Disney+ documentary on the making of the original trilogy.
- Steve plugs Advanced Port Scanner (grc.sc/aps), the GRC cookie test (grc.sc/cookies) and grc.sc/win10; he keeps DigiCert over Let's Encrypt for GRC.
- Leo and Steve talk about 16 TB drives and the coming SpinRite speed gains.
