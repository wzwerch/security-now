# Summary

The show opens with a tribute to Dan Kaminsky, who died at 42; Steve recalls his 2008 DNS cache-poisoning discovery and coordinated fix, and plays the short video Dan made with his niece. April's Patch Tuesday (114 flaws, 19 critical) included four Exchange remote code execution bugs credited to the NSA and an actively exploited Win32k privilege escalation found by Kaspersky. Google Project Zero adds a 30-day grace period after a vendor patches within its 90-day (or 7-day for in-the-wild) deadline, so users have time to apply fixes. Window Snyder's new startup Thistle aims to give IoT makers a secure update foundation. Steve defends, and Leo criticises, University of Minnesota researchers whose "hypocrite commits" study of Linux kernel patch review led the kernel maintainers to ban the university. Cloudflare launches another prior-art bounty ($100,000) against patent troll Sable Networks in Judge Albright's Texas court. The main story is AS8003: a thinly documented Florida shell company began announcing huge blocks of unused Pentagon IPv4 space (starting with 11.0.0.0/8) via BGP minutes before the end of the previous US administration, growing to about 175 million addresses; the DoD later called it a pilot to deter unauthorised use and gather threat intelligence. Steve and Leo agree it is not nefarious and speculate it may also help justify keeping the space.

# Key points

- Dan Kaminsky: found DNS query entropy flaws in 2008, coordinated a silent industry-wide fix; inspired GRC's DNS Spoofability test; DEF CON held an online memorial.
- Patch Tuesday April 2021: 114 fixes; four Exchange RCEs from NSA (two rated 9.8 and unauthenticated); CVE-2021-28310 Win32k elevation of privilege under active attack, likely paired with browser exploits to escape sandboxes.
- Project Zero policy change: 90 days (7 days for actively exploited zero-days) plus a 30-day grace if the vendor patches in time, acknowledging that shipping a patch is not the same as systems being patched.
- Thistle (Window Snyder, formerly Square, Mozilla, Fastly, Intel CSO) starts with a secure update delivery platform for connected devices; failure risk of updates is why few IoT vendors patch.
- Minnesota "hypocrite commits" paper (IEEE S&P 2021) argues stealthy vulnerabilities can be slipped past kernel review; researchers say bad patches only stayed in email and were never merged; kernel maintainers banned umn.edu submissions and began reviewing about 68 prior commits. Steve judges the research valuable; Leo says committing in bad faith is unacceptable.
- Cloudflare vs Sable Networks (patents from defunct Caspian Networks about flow tagging): Project Jengo-style $100,000 prior-art contest, paid out quarterly while the case is pending; Cloudflare's earlier fight against Blackbird shows the approach works. Steve and Leo stress that settling just invites more claims.
- AS8003 (Global Resource Systems LLC, registered "GRS-DoD") appeared January 20, 2021 at 16:57 UTC announcing 11.0.0.0/8 and other DoD ranges; reached roughly 56 million then 175 million addresses (about 5.7% of the IPv4 routing table), making it the largest AS; Kentik's Doug Madory analysed it.
- DoD statement: Defense Digital Services pilot to assess and prevent unauthorised use of DoD IP space; likely goals are scaring off squatters and collecting background traffic as threat intelligence (as with Cloudflare's 1.1.1.1 prefixes).
- AP reporting linked the shell company to a person associated with Packet Forensics, a maker of lawful-intercept TLS interception appliances; the AS number had earlier belonged to a spam outfit with the same name.
- In 2019 Congress had tried to force the DoD to sell its IPv4 space (estimated $100M per /8) but the provision was dropped.
- Listener-reported FLoC cohort ID example (5393) suggests a limited number of cohorts; Microsoft's competing proposal is called Parakeet (based on Turtledove).

# Other segments

- SpinRite 6.1: third public test release found only an issue with the BIOS virtual floppy drive B prompt, now fixed by hooking INT 2Fh.
- Leo shares his own experience of patent troll demand letters.
- Banter about class A/B/C allocations, Hamachi's use of 5.x.x.x, and HP's 14./15. blocks.
