# Summary

Steve describes setting up Apple's new Digital ID in Wallet from a US passport (camera scan of the photo page, NFC read of the passport chip, and a liveness check) and sees it as a foundation for privacy-preserving age assertions. He praises Checkout.com for refusing a ShinyHunters ransom demand and donating the equivalent sum to university security research, and discusses Google's Private AI Compute as a time-sharing-style cloud service that must be judged on being secure enough rather than as secure as local compute. Google has softened its Android developer-registration plan with a hobbyist account type and an advanced install flow for experienced users. Windows 11's November 2025 update adds a passkey plugin API already used by 1Password and Bitwarden. Russia now blocks data and SMS on SIMs newly arriving or idle for three days to hinder drone navigation, and Google sued the Chinese phishing-as-a-service platform Lighthouse. Listener mail covers XSLT dependencies, a DMARC email checker, Windows 10 ESU enrolment bugs and disabling the Run dialog against ClickFix. The main topic is a Lighthouse Reports investigation of First Wap's Altamides product, which exploits the SS7 telecom signaling protocol to locate any phone number by cell tower without installing anything on the device; Steve concludes that no handset hygiene prevents it and only airplane mode or powering off the radio helps.

# Key points

- Apple Digital ID (iOS 26, iPhone 11 and later): created from a US passport by photographing the data page, reading the chip over NFC and passing a randomized face-liveness check; accepted in beta at more than 250 US airports for domestic TSA checks; bound to one device at a time; not valid for international travel. Steve expects future age-verification use. Only about 12 states plus Puerto Rico support driver's licences in Wallet.
- Checkout.com: ShinyHunters accessed a legacy third-party cloud file store (data from 2020 and earlier, under 25% of current merchants affected); company refused to pay and donated the ransom amount to Carnegie Mellon University and Oxford's cyber security centre. Payment platform and card data not affected.
- Google Private AI Compute: Gemini in the cloud running on TPUs inside "Titanium Intelligence Enclaves" with remote attestation. Steve notes cloud cannot equal local security, argues the test is "secure enough", and compares it to mainframe time sharing; he sees it as sensible for brand-name providers only. Side talk about an AI bubble and CoreWeave using GPUs as loan collateral.
- Android developer verification: Google will add a limited account type for students and hobbyists and an advanced, coercion-resistant flow allowing experienced users to install unverified apps, easing the F-Droid concern; general registration for wider distribution remains.
- Windows 11 passkey plugin API (November 2025 update): third-party managers integrate natively with Windows Hello; 1Password (MSIX build) and Bitwarden (beta desktop app) are first, alongside Microsoft's own manager. Passkeys then work without browser extensions.
- Russia: SIM cards that enter the country or have been idle for 72 hours lose mobile data and SMS for 24 hours (restorable via CAPTCHA) to disrupt drone use; Steve questions how this stops cell-tower-based navigation or voice-channel control.
- Google v. Lighthouse: lawsuit against the Chinese phishing-as-a-service platform behind SMS scam waves impersonating Google, USPS and others; over one million victims in 120 countries and 25 individuals named.
- XSLT feedback: listeners with large internal XSLT codebases and the point that 0.02% of page loads is still a large absolute number; Steve expects a rude awakening in March 2026 when Chrome flips the default off.
- DMARCLY offers a free check@ email test for SPF, DKIM, DMARC, BIMI, MTA-STS and blacklists (needs a free account); GRC's spoofed-mail count stays at zero after the strict-alignment change.
- Windows 10 ESU: KB5071959 is an out-of-band update that fixes ESU enrollment failures (including EU and Windows Backup paths) and carries security fixes up to 14 October 2025; ESU runs to October 2026.
- ClickFix mitigation tip: disable the Run dialog with the NoRun policy value (registry or Group Policy) for users who do not need it.
- A Columbia Business School study found different AI models pick systematically different products from identical assortments.
- Global tracking: the Lighthouse Reports archive of 1.5 million rows from First Wap's Altamides covered over 14,000 numbers and 1,500 identified targets, including journalists, dissidents and a celebrity; Altamides queries SS7 for cell IDs through leased Global Titles, notably via Telecom Liechtenstein, then maps cell IDs to coordinates (OpenCelliD-style data). SS7 was designed without security and 4G/5G networks keep backward compatibility; operators struggle to filter abusive queries. First Wap denies wrongdoing; the carrier suspended the relationship.
