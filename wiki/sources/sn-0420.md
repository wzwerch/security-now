# Summary

Steve opens by teasing that he has had an idea for a new website authentication system with no usernames or passwords, which he will describe after finishing the current SpinRite work. The news covers Brazil's plan for a national surveillance-resistant email service and India's plan to bar government use of US email providers, the revelation that David Miranda carried a written password for TrueCrypt-encrypted drives holding tens of thousands of UK documents, an iOS and OS X CoreText font-rendering bug that crashes Messages, Safari and Wi-Fi scanning when it meets special text, and New Zealand's near-unanimous vote declaring computer programs not patentable inventions. He reports Oracle's new Java warning dialogs can be forged because the name and location fields are unsigned, and that signed applets get full privileges by default. Other items: BitTorrent Sync gaining an iOS client without published crypto details, Google Authenticator's iOS update wiping stored accounts, a hoax slide deck claiming TrueCrypt backdoors, and a US Naval Research Lab study showing Tor users are more exposed to traffic analysis than assumed. The main topic is Bitmessage, a peer-to-peer broadcast system where every node receives all messages from the past two days and tries to decrypt each, with proof-of-work to deter spam; Steve finds the design interesting but immature and with scaling and cryptographic problems.

# Key points

- Brazil's government moved to expand its postal service email into a national alternative to Gmail and Hotmail; India planned to bar its roughly 500,000 officials from using US webmail for official business and to mandate VPNs and one-time passwords abroad.
- David Miranda was reportedly carrying a paper with the password for TrueCrypt-encrypted drives; UK authorities said this exposed about 58,000 classified documents and showed very poor security practice. Steve says a written password should be altered or incomplete so that it is useless alone.
- An Apple CoreText bug let a specific text string crash apps, including Messages via SMS or iMessage and Safari, or even a Wi-Fi network name; iOS 7 and Mavericks betas were not affected.
- New Zealand's parliament voted 117 to 4 to treat computer programs as not inventions, while still allowing patents where software improves a physical process; Steve recalls patenting software through hardware descriptions decades earlier and thinks obvious engineering should not be patentable.
- Java: Oracle's new signed-applet warning dialog displays name and location fields that can be changed without breaking the signature, and signed code runs outside the sandbox by default, so the warnings can be subverted.
- BitTorrent Sync added an iOS client but still offered no cryptographic whitepaper; Steve will not endorse it without one.
- Google Authenticator for iOS was pulled after an update lost all stored secrets; Leo saves the QR codes in a password manager.
- A hoax PDF mimicking a prosecutors' computer forensics deck claimed major encryption tools including TrueCrypt had backdoors; Steve says it was fake (with joke detective names on the last slide).
- A Naval Research Laboratory and Georgetown paper modeled realistic adversaries (relays, autonomous systems, Internet exchange points) and found Tor users more susceptible to compromise than previously thought; Steve plans to study it.
- Bitmessage: a Python-based peer-to-peer network where each user has a key pair and an address starting with BM, messages and public keys propagate to all peers who keep two days of traffic, and clients try to decrypt each message to find theirs. Proof of work limits spam; there are person-to-person messages and broadcasts.
- Criticisms: asymmetric encryption used for bulk data instead of a hybrid scheme, no block chaining, acknowledgments that can reveal recipients, scalability limits, resend back-off logic, and a de-anonymization stunt in which about 15,000 users clicked a fake announcement link. Steve says the idea of everyone receiving everything is clever but immature, and that Cryptocat-style OTR messaging suits real-time chat.
- Steve's yet-unnamed authentication idea is said to resist man-in-the-middle attacks, support multiple unlinked identities, require no third party and be free of patents; he calls the plan a brief hiatus from SpinRite.

# Other segments

- SpinRite 6.1: 32 MB ATA transfers via Ultra DMA in extended memory working, with AHCI support planned immediately after.
- Recommendation of the TV series Orphan Black, via an economist's blog.
- Steve will join Leo in studio for the 24-hour New Year's Eve event.
