# Summary

Steve starts with a series of news items: Let's Encrypt has stopped sending certificate-expiry emails and deleted the stored addresses; Microsoft is renaming Windows crashes "unexpected restarts", turning the blue screen black and adding Quick Machine Recovery, a response to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage; Microsoft will give Windows 10 users a first year of Extended Security Updates in exchange for enabling Windows Backup, redeeming 1,000 Rewards points, or paying 30 dollars; Russia will require Apple and other manufacturers to allow the RuStore app store from 1 September 2025; Lyon, France will move from Windows to Linux; and NSA and CISA published a memory-safe languages guidance sheet. Check Point found malware embedding "ignore previous instructions" prompt injection aimed at AI scanners, a new Russia-linked disinformation wave (Operation Overload) uses AI content, and Cisco ISE received a CVSS 9.8 and a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated remote code execution fix, which Steve ties back to last week's Salt Typhoon lessons. A Cybersecurity Dive report describes weakened US government and critical-infrastructure partnerships after workforce cuts and the end of the CIPAC framework. Short items cover PNG 3, Swift on Android, Samsung's inactive-account purge and the Project Hail Mary trailer. The main segment presents the first study (FPTrace, presented at the 2025 ACM Web Conference) showing that changing a browser's fingerprint changes ad bidding and HTTP syncing, proving fingerprints are used for tracking, including after opt-outs, and that known users command roughly 40% higher bids.

# Key points

- Let's Encrypt ended expiry notification emails on 4 June 2025 (privacy, cost and complexity reasons), deleted stored addresses, and suggests third-party monitors such as Red Sift Certificates Lite; it now routes any supplied email to optional newsletters. Steve reminds that certificate lifetimes are shrinking so ACME automation becomes necessary.
- Microsoft's "unexpected restart experience" (black screen, simpler UI, crash dump collection down to about two seconds) and Quick Machine Recovery, which lets Microsoft push remediations through the Windows Recovery Environment when many devices fail to boot; QMR is on by default for Windows 11 Home 24H2.
- Windows 10 ESU for individuals: October 15, 2025 to October 13, 2026 for free if the user syncs settings with Windows Backup or redeems 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points, otherwise 30 dollars; Steve criticizes the Windows 11 hardware requirements and notes Rufus can bypass checks.
- Russia's law (effective 1 September 2025) requires device makers to allow installing, updating and paying through RuStore (installed on about 80 million devices); retailers worry buyers will turn to grey-market imports. Leo expects Apple to comply as it did in the EU.
- Lyon will replace Windows with Linux, Office with OnlyOffice and SQL Server with PostgreSQL, joining Aarhus and Copenhagen; the EU also considers leaving Azure.
- NSA/CISA "Memory Safe Languages: Reducing Vulnerabilities in Modern Software Development" urges adoption of MSLs and mitigation for legacy code; Steve predicts it will become a government purchasing requirement and that Rust, Go, Java, C#, Swift, Kotlin and Python are viable alternatives to C and C++.
- Check Point reports malware that embeds "ignore all previous instructions" style text to trick LLM-based scanners into reporting no malware; the payload opens Tor-based backdoors on Windows.
- Operation Overload (Checkfirst and Reset Tech): Kremlin-linked campaign using AI-generated impersonations of over 180 people and institutions across Telegram, X, Bluesky and TikTok, targeting Ukraine, France, Germany, Poland and Moldova and overloading fact-checkers; Bluesky acted, X lagged.
- Cisco ISE: CVE-2025-20281 (CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2025-20282 (CVSS 10.0) allow unauthenticated remote code execution as root; patches exist, no workarounds. Steve repeats that management interfaces should never be reachable from arbitrary IP addresses and that Cisco's remedy is advice, not secure defaults.
- Cybersecurity Dive ("Suspended animation"): the dissolved CIPAC framework, cuts of roughly a third of CISA's staff, lost regional advisers, a dormant JCDC and canceled sector meetings have reduced information sharing with utilities, healthcare, water and telecom; commentators warn of weaker response to major attacks. Steve stresses loss of institutional knowledge and keeps his own political views out.
- PNG 3 adds animation, HDR and EXIF; Swift is being ported to Android; Samsung purges accounts inactive for two years at the end of July 2025.
- Browser fingerprinting study: the five authors' FPTrace framework varied fingerprints and observed ad bids and HTTP traffic. Bid values and HTTP chains and cookie-syncing events dropped significantly when fingerprints changed; 378 fingerprint-related cookie restorations across 90 key/host pairs were found; fingerprinting also worked despite GDPR/CCPA opt-outs through consent platforms such as OneTrust, Quantcast and NAI (tests were run from US IPs on Linux, a stated limitation).
- Header-bidding research shows advertisers bid about 40% more for users they recognize; US CPM averages 0.69 dollars versus 0.36 in France and 0.24 in Japan. Steve concludes there is strong monetary pressure to identify visitors, that blocking third-party scripts breaks sites, and that browser vendors' fuzzing of time and battery APIs does not stop fingerprinting. IP address remains the strongest short-term identifier.
- Leo describes how podcasts measure audiences with IP-based escrow matching and says Club TWiT feeds are untracked.
