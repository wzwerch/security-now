# Summary

Jason Howell co-hosts. Steve recaps Lenovo's Superfish scandal (a shared root certificate used for ad injection, now a 7.3 million dollar class-action settlement) and then covers Sennheiser HeadSetup software, which installed a shared local root certificate and its private key, enabling spoofing of HTTPS sites; Microsoft pulled trust for the certificates and Steve recommends auditing the root store with Sysinternals Sigcheck. Large breaches follow: Marriott disclosed that Starwood reservation data on up to about 500 million guests had been accessed since 2014, Quora reported about 100 million affected users, and an exposed Elasticsearch cluster leaked tens of millions of US records tied to a company called Data & Leads. Akamai described EternalSilence, where 45,000 routers with UPnP exposed had NAT rules added so attackers could reach SMB ports behind them. A PewDiePie fan spammed exposed printers, spawning a printer-spam service. The US Treasury linked two Iranian SamSam facilitators to bitcoin addresses holding over 23 million dollars. Other items: two scam iOS apps abusing Apple Pay prompts, Mozilla's DoH performance tests, rumors Microsoft will switch Edge to Chromium, and a Humble Bundle of security books. The main topic is Korean research showing GPUs do not zero newly allocated memory, so rendered web page textures from Chromium and Firefox can be recovered and used to infer visited pages with up to 95.4 percent accuracy.

# Key points

- Sennheiser HeadSetup installed a shared root CA and left it after uninstall; the fix was updated software and a Microsoft CTL update. Chrome pins Google certificates, so Google sites would still warn.
- Sigcheck with the trust-list option compares installed root certificates with Microsoft's trust list; output should normally be empty.
- Marriott breach: up to about 500 million guests, passport numbers and encrypted card data for some; Steve backs data-minimization legislation as testable and enforceable.
- Quora breach: roughly 100 million accounts; per-user salted password hashes, forced logouts and resets; Steve calls the response textbook.
- Elasticsearch has no authentication by default; an open instance exposed about 57 million records and the likely owner's website went offline.
- EternalSilence: UPnP exposed on 277,000 routers, 45,113 modified; attackers use NSA-derived SMB exploit against devices behind them; disable UPnP on the WAN.
- Printer spam: over 800,000 exposed printers listed by Shodan; printers are complex interpreters and should not be on the internet.
- SamSam: DOJ indictment of two Iranians; OFAC attributed bitcoin addresses (5,901 BTC, over 23 million dollars) and sanctioned them, making ransom payments to them illegal for US persons.
- Fake fitness iOS apps triggered Apple Pay payment prompts; iPhone X flow with double-click is more explicit.
- Mozilla DoH study with Akamai testing total page-load time via Cloudflare's DoH.
- Windows Central rumor: Edge to be rebuilt on Chromium (codename Anaheim).
- GPU research: NVIDIA and AMD GPUs do not initialize new allocations; browser textures remain; mitigation would be zeroing memory in the background as Windows does.
