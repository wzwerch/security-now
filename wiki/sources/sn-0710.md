# Summary

Steve first admits that last week's claim that the link ping attribute serves only tracking was too narrow: Imperva saw it abused for a DDoS against gaming sites, because JavaScript can edit and trigger it with no same-origin limit. WinRAR exploitation keeps growing, including a Microsoft report of the MuddyWater group using it. April Patch Tuesday fixed 74 flaws and two more win32k zero-days (Kaspersky has now reported seven), but caused boot failures, hangs and slowdowns on systems with several third-party antivirus products; Adobe patched 40 flaws and announced the end of Shockwave. Steve praises the clean Windows 10 LTSC image he tried versus retail Windows 10 Pro. Google proposed blocking high-risk downloads (executables and archives) served over HTTP from secure pages. Russia fined Facebook and Twitter token amounts over data-localisation questions, Kaspersky described the very large TajMahal espionage framework, and the main topic is Dragonblood, the first attacks on WPA3's SAE handshake, which showed side-channel leaks, a downgrade weakness in transition mode and denial of service, blamed largely on the closed development process.

# Key points

- Ping abuse: Imperva observed about 70 million requests from roughly 4,000 browsers over four hours (peak 7,500 per second) generated through the hyperlink ping attribute; Steve suggests a same-origin limit or stopping script from editing it. uBlock Origin's "disable hyperlink auditing" option blocks it.
- WinRAR: Microsoft Office 365 threat researchers tied MuddyWater to a chain that used a Word macro lure from a fake Afghan ministry to deliver a malicious ACE file; Sophos expects commercial crime to follow state use. Steve urges updating or removing WinRAR.
- April 9 Patch Tuesday: 74 vulnerabilities; two win32k elevation-of-privilege zero-days (one from Kaspersky, one from Alibaba Cloud) and fixes in GDI+ EMF parsing and Access connectivity. Conflicts with Sophos, Avast, Avira, AVG and other antivirus caused boot hangs and slowness on Windows 7, 8.1, Server 2012 and some Windows 10 builds; Microsoft blocked the updates for Sophos Endpoint users. Steve and Leo again recommend relying on built-in Defender.
- Adobe fixed 40 flaws including seven in Shockwave Player, which was discontinued on April 9, 2019, plus Flash, Acrobat and Reader issues.
- Windows 10 Enterprise LTSC (Long Term Servicing Channel) installs without consumer apps and promotions and carries a ten-year support commitment, but is not sold to consumers.
- Chrome plan (Emily Stark): treat downloads of executables (EXE, DMG, CRX) and archives from non-secure sources initiated on HTTPS pages as active mixed content and block them; Mozilla expressed interest.
- Roskomnadzor: Moscow court fined Facebook and Twitter 3,000 rubles each (about $47) for refusing to say where Russians' data is stored; a ban remains possible.
- TajMahal (Kaspersky): modular APT with a Tokyo first stage and Yokohama second stage holding up to 80 plug-ins in an encrypted virtual file system; active from at least 2014, found in 2018 on a Central Asian diplomatic target; steals from printer queues, burned CDs and USB drives.
- Dragonblood (Mathy Vanhoef and Eyal Ronen): WPA3 Personal's SAE (Dragonfly) handshake leaks password information through timing and cache side channels in its hash-to-curve encoding, allowing partitioning attacks (brute forcing eight-character lowercase passwords for under $125 of cloud time), plus denial of service against access points, downgrade and dictionary attacks in WPA2/WPA3 transition mode. The Wi-Fi Alliance said patches would fix it; researchers noted WPA3 is a certification and criticised the secret process and said a small change excluding the MAC address from password encoding would have prevented most attacks.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: retail Windows 10 Pro Start menu full of promoted apps versus clean LTSC.
- Firefox add-on Auto Tab Discard to free memory; a listener's note on viewing saved browser passwords; "Copy as path" tip repeated; SpinRite 6 owners get 6.1 and later free, and Steve is considering ending upgrades from versions older than 15 years.
