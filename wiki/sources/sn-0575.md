# Summary

Steve skips the Q&A to cover the emergency iOS 9.3.5 update. The news section starts with Dropbox forcing password resets for pre-2012 accounts after learning of an old credential set, and Opera resetting Opera Sync passwords after an intrusion, both handled responsibly. By contrast the Chinese CA WoSign issued certificates for base domains to anyone who controlled a subdomain (for example GitHub, Alibaba, Microsoft names) and did not report it, prompting Steve to argue users should rethink the trust-every-root model. WhatsApp will share data with Facebook unless users opt out. The FBI warned about SQL-injection intrusions into state election databases (Illinois and Arizona). Tavis Ormandy teases coming Dashlane and 1Password vulnerability disclosures. Two "fringe" items follow: a Berkeley AI professor's warning about autonomous micro-weapons and a magazine piece on WiFi sensing, which Steve says is overblown for ordinary routers. After errata, router guides and a note on 3D XPoint delays, battery care, and how SpinRite's single EXE doubles as a DOS stub, the main segment explains Pegasus: a one-click, one-time-link exploit chain from NSO Group that used three zero-days (a WebKit bug, a kernel address leak defeating ASLR, and a kernel memory corruption for jailbreak), persisted across reboots, hooked messaging apps before encryption, and was caught because a targeted human-rights activist forwarded the messages to Citizen Lab. The puzzle answer covers why RSA needs primes; the new puzzle asks how a vendor can keep unpatchable devices working when their SHA-1 certificate expires.

# Key points

- Dropbox: users who had not changed passwords since mid-2012 forced to reset after Dropbox learned of an old set of emails with hashed, salted passwords; no sign of misuse.
- Opera Sync: about 1.7 million of roughly 350 million users used the service; attack blocked, passwords reset; Steve notes the wording avoided saying a vulnerability was exploited.
- WoSign: free certificates for a base domain granted on proof of control of a subdomain; 33 mis-issued certificates acknowledged; no report to the root programs; Steve expects browser distrust and floats a whitelist or learning-mode approach to root trust.
- WhatsApp: new terms share the phone number and account data with Facebook; opt out via the Read More dialog or Settings, Account, within the grace period.
- FBI flash alert on election sites: attackers used scanning tools and SQL injection against state voter-registration systems; Illinois shut its system for ten days after data on up to 200,000 voters was taken; the bulletin included indicators and action items.
- Tavis Ormandy says he has upcoming Dashlane and 1Password findings; users should watch for updates.
- Stuart Russell's warning on lethal autonomous weapons; Steve says technically plausible but obstacles remain.
- WiFi sensing (FreeSense and similar): research can identify walkers or gestures in purpose-built setups, but ordinary cheap routers lack the signal detail.
- Errata: the iMessage attack complexity was 2^18, not 218, a practical figure.
- Dumb-router guide from LittleBytesofPi and a YouTube channel on EdgeRouter configuration added to GRC's Link Farm.
- 3D XPoint: no news at Intel's developer forum, analyst Jim Handy frames it as a layer between DRAM and flash and notes Intel depends on it; manufacturing the new material at volume is the challenge.
- Lithium-ion care: keep batteries roughly half to two thirds charged when sitting on AC power; modern devices manage this themselves.
- Pegasus/Trident (Citizen Lab and Lookout): targeted SMS link to a one-time URL; stage 1 WebKit exploit, stage 2 remote jailbreak tailored to device and 32/64-bit type with per-download encryption, stage 3 spyware that hooks apps (messaging, mail, calls) before encryption, installs its own root certificate, removes other jailbreak access and survives reboots and updates; supports iOS 7 through 9.3.3 and iPhone 4s onward; sold by Israel's NSO Group under a per-infection licence with NSO-run infrastructure. Three flaws were patched in iOS 9.3.5 ten days after Apple was informed.
- Steve's takeaway: high-value targets face professional cyberweapons; the exploit vendor probably has replacements ready.
- Puzzle answer: RSA's one-way property depends on the modulus having only two huge prime factors; composite factors could be peeled off one by one.
