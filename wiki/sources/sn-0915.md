# Summary

Steve opens with Google Project Zero's disclosure of 18 vulnerabilities in Samsung Exynos modems, four of which allow zero-click internet-to-baseband remote code execution knowing only a phone number; Google took the rare step of withholding those four, and Pixel devices already got fixes in the March 2023 update. He then covers Socket's "safe npm" wrapper and argues package managers should build such protections in. Privacy enforcement follows: South Korean fines on Google and Meta, and an Austrian ruling that Facebook's tracking pixel breaks the GDPR, which Steve contrasts with governments wanting access to encrypted communications. After TikTok bans in New Zealand and Scotland and a US investigation, Steve explains Google's expected proposal to the CA/Browser Forum to cut TLS certificate lifetimes to 90 days, pushing the industry to ACME automation. Smaller items are Check Point's findings in Chess.com and CISA's new Ransomware Vulnerability Warning Pilot. The main segment is a letter from a bipartisan group of US senators asking CISA to assess DJI drones, followed by GRIMM's and Synacktiv's reverse engineering of the DJI GO 4 Android app. Steve concludes that proprietary closed software, including unvetted Windows kernel drivers, makes the whole security model fragile, and that open source is the only real long-term answer.

# Key points

- Project Zero reported 18 Exynos modem flaws; four allow remote baseband code execution with no user interaction. Affected devices include Samsung S22/A-series phones, Vivo models, Pixel 6 and 7, and Exynos Auto T5123 car chips. Workaround until patched: disable Wi-Fi calling and VoLTE.
- Google made a policy exception to its 90-day disclosure rule for those four flaws because attackers would gain more than defenders; the other 14 were disclosed or scheduled for disclosure. Likely users at risk are targeted individuals (spyware vendors such as NSO Group).
- Socket's "safe npm" wraps `npm install` to flag malware, typosquats and install scripts; claims the average package pulls about 80 packages and that 94% of malicious packages use install scripts. Steve wants the package managers themselves to take on this role.
- South Korea's PIPC fined Google and Meta for lacking lawful consent for ad tracking; both are suing instead of paying.
- Austria's data protection authority ruled Facebook's tracking pixel violates the GDPR (following noyb's 101 complaints after the Privacy Shield annulment). Steve notes the irony of governments demanding privacy from tech firms while seeking access to citizens' encrypted communications.
- TikTok: New Zealand banned it for lawmakers, Scotland advised removal, Australia published a 113-page report, and the US FBI/DoJ investigated ByteDance for surveilling journalists. Leo warns that a general US ban sets a worrying precedent.
- Certificate lifetimes fell from five years to 398 days after Apple's 2020 unilateral decision. Google is expected to propose 90 days; over 50% of Web PKI certificates already use ACME and about 95% come from CAs offering it. Short lifetimes also reduce the weakness of Chrome's absent revocation checking.
- Check Point found Chess.com flaws: manipulating clocks, and the server leaking correct puzzle solutions to the client. Leo notes most online chess cheating uses engines, not hacks.
- CISA launched the Ransomware Vulnerability Warning Pilot (under CIRCIA), notifying 93 organizations running Exchange servers exposed to ProxyNotShell. Steve compares it to GRC's ShieldsUP! scanning.
- Senators' letter to CISA cites DJI's Pentagon listing as a "Chinese military company" and its large market share.
- GRIMM validated Synacktiv's analysis of the DJI GO 4 app: heavy obfuscation (custom Bangcle packing), a self-update path bypassing Google Play, an SDK able to install arbitrary apps, and collection of device identifiers sent to Weibo/MobTech. Steve notes no proven misuse, but sees a strong targeting mechanism.
- Steve's broader conclusion: unvetted ring-0 drivers and 2.65 million Play Store apps make trust in closed software unjustifiable; open source is the only long-term path.
