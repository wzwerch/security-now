# Summary

Leo returns from vacation and Steve starts with iOS 11 impressions: very buggy at launch and noticeably slow on older hardware. He summarises Apple's Face ID security guide, praising the per-device dot-projection sequence, local-only processing and the rules that force a passcode after set intervals, which mirrors the tradeoff in SQRL. The show then covers browser-based cryptocurrency mining injected into sites such as Showtime, the court ruling that the FBI need not name the vendor that unlocked the San Bernardino iPhone, and Bloomberg's reconstruction of the Equifax intrusion: a Struts flaw disclosed on 6 March, scanning four days later, an entry crew handing off to a more advanced group, and more than 30 entry points. A Broadcom Wi-Fi exploit proof of concept, Google's planned Advanced Protection Program for Gmail, DKIM spoofing without breaking the crypto, dnsmasq LAN-side code execution flaws, neglected Mac EFI firmware and an IE address-bar leak are reviewed. Listener feedback includes a SpinRite-rescued Drobo and offline storage of 2FA QR codes. The main topic is Signal's private contact discovery using Intel SGX.

# Key points

- iOS 11: unstable early release, slow on older devices; Steve plans a newer phone.
- Face ID: about 30,000 infrared dots forming a depth map, device-specific projection order to defeat replay, pupil/attention check, on-device neural processing, and passcode required after restart, 48 hours idle, about 6.5 days without passcode use, or 4 hours since last Face ID unlock. Apple claims 1 in a million false match versus 1 in 50,000 for Touch ID.
- Cryptomining in browsers: injected through ads or compromised sites (Showtime domains); Steve sees it as an annoyance comparable to other CPU-hungry web content, with browsers being poor miners. He mentions his early bitcoin mining.
- A judge ruled the FBI need not reveal the vendor or price of the iPhone-unlocking tool, reasoning that disclosure could expose the vendor and help adversaries.
- Leo notes the IRS awarded Equifax a fraud-prevention contract.
- Bloomberg on Equifax: Struts patch published 6 March 2017, public exploit within a day, vulnerable Equifax server found by scanning by 10 March, an initial crew handing off to a sophisticated team, over 30 entry points and dozens of databases, discovery on 29 July, tools and tradecraft resembling Anthem and OPM intrusions attributed to Chinese intelligence.
- A Google researcher published a proof of concept for the Broadcom Wi-Fi flaw in iPhones before iOS 10.3.3; no association with the attacker is required.
- Google reportedly will offer an Advanced Protection Program for Gmail requiring two physical security keys and blocking third-party app access, aimed at high-risk users.
- DKIM was not cryptographically broken; clients could be fooled into showing a valid signature next to content that was not what was signed.
- dnsmasq: several code-execution flaws reachable from the LAN side, affecting routers, Linux distributions and virtual networking; patch firmware when available.
- Duo Labs found many Macs running outdated EFI firmware; EFIgy checks Mac EFI status. Steve says old motherboard firmware on all PCs should now be updated, reversing the "if it ain't broke" rule.
- IE bug: script on a page can capture what the user types into the address bar when leaving.
- Signal private contact discovery: hashing phone numbers is insufficient (low entropy); Signal instead runs matching inside an Intel SGX enclave on its servers with remote attestation and a design that hides memory access patterns (cache-aware oblivious algorithm), designed to scale to over a billion users with no social-graph leakage. Matthew Green called it revolutionary.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: bees obtain the EpiPen allergy database.
- SpinRite testimonial: a Level 2 pass revived a second failing drive during a Drobo rebuild; Steve explains RAID 5 versus RAID 6 risk and Leo mentions a counterfeit Western Digital drive bought from an Amazon third-party seller.
- Listener Q and A: printing 2FA QR codes to keep them offline; Leo's Hover account email-forwarding tamper was caught and 2FA blocked a Twitter reset; travel-laptop advice (clone and encrypt the drive, or restore an image afterwards); SQRL password re-entry rationale; Payment Request API does not stop merchants storing cards; reference to the Cuban embassy hearing loss story and the "Portable Dog Killer" episode.
