# Summary

Steve opens with a follow-up to the previous week's Mythos episode and then covers a researcher using the aliases "Chaotic Eclipse" / "Nightmare-Eclipse", who in protest at how Microsoft's MSRC treated him published working proof-of-concept exploits for Windows Defender privilege-escalation flaws (BlueHammer, RedSun, UnDefend). Huntress saw all three exploited in the wild within days. BlueHammer was fixed in April Patch Tuesday as CVE-2026-33825, but RedSun still gave SYSTEM on fully patched machines when Defender was enabled. Microsoft also reported record Zero Day Quest payouts ($2.3 million). The suspension of Windows Hardware Program accounts for VeraCrypt, WireGuard, MemTest86 and Windscribe turned out to be a deliberate identity-verification enforcement, now eased by a fast-track reinstatement process. A Huntress investigation of signed adware from "Dragon Boss Solutions" showed the danger of abandoned update domains: roughly 23,500 hosts, including 324 in high-value networks, phoned home to unregistered domains that Huntress sinkholed. Leo adds that Mozilla used Mythos and fixed 271 bugs in Firefox 150. After listener feedback on securing open source repositories with AI gatekeepers and on the film Project Hail Mary, the main topic is the Cloud Security Alliance paper "The AI Vulnerability Storm: Building a Mythos-Ready Security Program", co-authored by a who's who of security leaders, which confirms Steve's view that Mythos was real and not just marketing. Steve's conclusion is that organizations must act now, like Y2K, and he shut down his own exposed SSH servers as a precaution.

# Key points

- RedSun / BlueHammer / UnDefend: three Defender-related Windows flaws leaked as PoC code by a disgruntled researcher; BlueHammer = CVE-2026-33825 (patched April 2026); RedSun abuses Defender's behavior of rewriting cloud-tagged files (Cloud Files API, cldapi.dll) to gain SYSTEM on Windows 10/11 and Server 2019+; UnDefend lets a standard user block Defender definition updates.
- Huntress reported in-the-wild exploitation (BlueHammer since April 10) in a hands-on-keyboard intrusion via a compromised SSL VPN account. The only mitigation was disabling Defender, which Steve does not recommend; he suggested checking 0patch for a micro-patch.
- Steve's reading: Microsoft's MSRC probably treated the researcher like everyone else; the researcher likely expected a bounty and may have forfeited it by releasing early. He warns the researcher threatens to release RCEs next.
- Microsoft's Zero Day Quest 2026 paid $2.3 million for nearly 700 submissions (80+ high-impact cloud/AI flaws: credential exposure, SSRF chains, cross-tenant access); the pool was $5 million versus $4 million offered (and $1.6 million paid) in 2025. Microsoft paid a record $17 million to 344 researchers in July 2024 to June 2025. Part of the Secure Future Initiative started after the CSRB criticism in 2023.
- Windows Hardware Program accounts (VeraCrypt, WireGuard, MemTest86, Windscribe) were suspended for incomplete identity verification, which Microsoft said it had been emailing about since October 2025; developers disputed receiving notices. Microsoft added a temporary fast-track reinstatement via support case with business justification. Steve links it to his own strict code-signing identity checks.
- Dragon Boss Solutions LLC adware (Chromstera and similar "browsers") used a signed Advanced Installer updater to run SYSTEM-level payloads, including a PowerShell AV-killer (ClockRemoval.ps1) that removed Malwarebytes, Kaspersky, McAfee and ESET and null-routed vendor domains via the hosts file.
- The update domains were never registered; Huntress registered them and saw 23,500+ hosts in 124 countries, 324 in high-value networks (221 academic, 41 OT/energy/transport, 35 government/utilities, 24 schools, 3 healthcare, several Fortune 500). Steve's lesson: abandoned software that phones home to expired, re-registrable domains is a ready-made backdoor, an "aging Internet" design problem akin to abandoned AWS buckets.
- Mozilla (CTO Bobby Holley) said Mythos found 271 bugs fixed in Firefox 150 (reportedly 13 high, 18 moderate, 15 low among CVEs); Mozilla says every software project will have to make this transition.
- Listener idea: independent AI auditors signing packages, a Let's Encrypt for code audits. Steve prefers public repositories running an AI agent as gatekeeper before release, with incremental review of diffs and rate limits on submissions.
- Cloud Security Alliance paper (23 pages) authored by Jen Easterly, Bruce Schneier, Chris Inglis, Phil Venables, Heather Adkins, Rob Joyce, Katie Moussouris and others: AI gives attackers an asymmetric advantage; expect waves of AI-found vulnerabilities after Project Glasswing; prioritize basics (segmentation, egress filtering, MFA, defense in depth), dependency management, AI agents for defenders, tabletop exercises for simultaneous incidents, and collective defense, including organizations below the "Cyber Poverty Line" (Wendy Nather). Short links grc.sc/mythos and grc.sc/1075 point to the PDF.
- Zerodayclock.com: average time-to-exploit fell from 2.3 years (2018) to 1.9, 1.3 years, 10.8 months, 9.7 months, 4.9 months, 56 days (2024), 23.2 days (2025) and about 10 hours so far in 2026.
- Historical context in the paper: DARPA Cyber Grand Challenge (2016), XBOW topping HackerOne, Google Big Sleep, AISLE finding 12 OpenSSL zero-days, Claude Opus 4.6 reporting 500+ open-source bugs, Linux and curl maintainers now receiving verified AI-found reports, and an AI-assisted compromise of Mexican government systems.
- AISLE objected that small cheap open-weight models could reproduce Mythos's showcased findings; Steve notes this is isolated known-bug confirmation and that Mythos is what broke through to the industry.
- Steve shut down his two deliberately exposed SSH servers at GRC (US-IP-allowed, MFA-protected) and is considering a no-open-port approach such as Tailscale-style NAT traversal.
- Steve argues software is deterministic math and could eventually be bug-free with AI help; he expects local AI models and AI-directed coding to replace human code generation.
