# Summary

Steve opens with the 12-hour AT&T outage and AT&T's opaque explanation, then turns to Nevada's attorney general, who sought an emergency order to stop Meta from offering end-to-end encrypted Messenger to minors in the state; experts call the move bizarre since encryption has been available for years and would leave only children less protected. A Cybereason survey of 1,000 IT professionals found 84 percent paid a ransom, only 47 percent recovered data intact, and 82 percent were hit again within a year. Chrome 122 gains a switch to disable the V8 JIT optimizer, a feature that originated as Microsoft Edge's Super Duper Secure Mode. Further items: DoorDash and Avast fined for selling customer and browsing data, Microsoft making 31 Purview audit-log categories free, spyware found on European Parliament security-committee phones, the multi-country takedown of LockBit (Operation Cronos) and its quick return, and Firefox 123 features. The titular story is the trivially exploitable ConnectWise ScreenConnect authentication bypass (CVSS 10), a reminder that web authentication interfaces are still not done right. Steve announces SpinRite 6.1 as released, explains how Microsoft SmartScreen reputation for his EV code-signing certificate works, and introduces the free BootAble utility. Listener feedback refines the email-link login (bind the link to cookie, IP and timestamp), alias email services, car-theft relay attacks, and a proposal for a /.well-known/ password-rules file.

# Key points

- AT&T nationwide outage of about 12 hours; DHS, CISA and FBI looked into it; AT&T gave few details.
- Nevada v. Meta: attorney general asked a federal court for a TRO and injunction against E2EE on Messenger for minors, citing predator risk and the state's deceptive-trade law. Meta replied that E2EE has existed on Messenger since 2016 and default for months, and that it cannot reliably identify Nevada minors; experts (Stanford Internet Observatory, Matt Blaze) called it the biggest US attack on encryption since 2016. A follow-up hearing was set for March.
- Cybereason survey: all respondents breached in two years; 84% paid ransom, 47% recovered data cleanly, 82% hit again within a year. Steve salutes IT staff.
- Chrome 122: chrome://settings/content/v8 lets users block the V8 optimizer (JIT) globally or per site; experimental AI features (Help me write, tab organizer, themes) also added.
- DoorDash paid $375,000 under California privacy law for selling customer data to a marketing cooperative; the FTC fined Avast $16.5M for selling browsing data via Jumpshot to over 100 third parties (2014-2020).
- Microsoft moved 31 Purview Audit logging categories to the standard tier after the Storm-0558 criticism; CISA welcomed it.
- European Parliament IT found spyware on phones of security and defence subcommittee members.
- LockBit takedown (Operation Cronos, led by UK NCA, announced 20 Feb 2024): 34 servers seized, 14,000+ accounts closed, 200+ crypto accounts frozen, arrests in Poland and Ukraine, indictments of two Russians, decryptor via No More Ransom; LockBit reappeared on new infrastructure with 12 new victims within days.
- Firefox 123: search in Firefox View, Web Compatibility reporting via the shield icon, address-bar settings; Steve explains how to switch back to a unified URL bar.
- ConnectWise ScreenConnect (SlashAndGrab, advisory 19 Feb 2024, fixed in 23.9.8): authentication bypass via alternate path (CVSS 10) plus path traversal; exploited within hours after the patch was reverse engineered; Huntress analysed it. Steve's point: web authentication portals remain a repeated weak spot.
- SpinRite 6.1 declared released (a volume-label attribute glitch in the boot image needed a Release 2); SmartScreen reputation was granted to the sr61.exe binary within hours, but the EV code-signing certificate (issued 16 January) was still building reputation after six weeks.
- New freeware BootAble creates DOS boot media (USB, CD, ISO, IMG, diskette) to test whether a PC can boot DOS.
- Cox.net mail moves to Yahoo without address changes; credit-card embossing is a legacy of manual imprinters.
- Client-side password hashing makes quality checks on the server impossible, so requirements must be enforced in the browser before hashing.
- Email-link login hardening: encrypt timestamp, client IP and a first-party browser cookie value into the link so it only works from the requesting browser, making it effectively independent of email interception risk.
- Car theft: banning Flipper Zero does not address relay/signal-extender or CAN-bus theft; German router maker AVM already requires a physical button press for sensitive changes; Leo notes Apple Car Key with ultra-wideband.
- Listener ideas: alias services integrated with Bitwarden (SimpleLogin, AnonAddy, Firefox Relay, Fastmail, DuckDuckGo, forward email) avoid email fingerprints; a /.well-known/ password-rules JSON so password managers can match site policies.
- Stories deferred: a "fingerprint from touchscreen sound" claim judged exaggerated (MasterPrint templates succeed 1.88% of the time), Voyager 1 trouble, and Apple's PQ3 post-quantum messaging protocol (next week).
