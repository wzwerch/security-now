# Summary

The first episode of year 16 starts with a fix for a WebGL use-after-free read in Chrome 85 and a reminder that Microsoft will end IE11 support for its online services in 2021. Three new ransomware victims show the extortion-first pattern: the University of Utah paid roughly 457,000 dollars (partly insurance-funded) to stop release of exfiltrated student data after only a tiny fraction of files was encrypted, Brown-Forman (Jack Daniel's) was claimed by REvil, and Carnival disclosed a breach. Coveware, Emsisoft and Recorded Future reports rank RDP as the leading ransomware entry vector, with exposed RDP ports surging during COVID. Microsoft issued an emergency patch for Windows 8.1 and Server 2012 R2 remote access flaws. The Zero Day Initiative turned 15. Steve recommends O&O AppBuster, Remote Utilities and Syncthing, then covers SpiKey, National University of Singapore research that infers a pin-tumbler key's bitting from the audio of key insertion.

# Key points

- Chrome 85 fixes a WebGL use-after-free read found by Cisco Talos, reported in May 2020; IE11 support for Microsoft online services ends in August 2021 and Teams web app support in November 2020.
- University of Utah: ransomware hit one college network (about 0.02% of data encrypted), restored from backups; payment was made to keep exfiltrated student data private. Brown-Forman reported prevented encryption but employee data impacted; REvil claimed about a terabyte. Carnival disclosed access to guest and employee data. The pattern is exfiltrate first, then encrypt, i.e. blackmail even when backups exist.
- Ransomware vectors: RDP leads over email phishing in Q4 2018 through Q2 2020 datasets; exposed RDP ports rose from about 3 million to 4.5 million between January and March 2020, and Kaspersky saw brute-force attempts jump from about 200,000 to 1.3 million daily. Leo notes the low-end Phobos ransomware-as-a-service and cheap credentials explain volume.
- RDP guidance: never expose it directly; restrict by source IP (with dynamic DNS if needed), use a VPN with multi-factor, or tunnel RDP over SSH; RDP has no native MFA.
- Microsoft's out-of-band update for Windows 8.1 and Server 2012 R2 closes two Remote Access Service privilege escalations already patched elsewhere in August.
- Group-IB: low-skilled, likely Iranian actors use Masscan and NLBrute against exposed RDP and deploy Dharma ransomware (source leaked in March), demanding one to five bitcoin.
- ZDI turned 15 (founded by 3Com, now Trend Micro): over 25 million dollars paid to 10,000-plus researchers across 7,500+ submissions, 1,500+ published advisories in total, Pwn2Own growth, and a disclosure deadline that cut vendor response times from over 180 to under 120 days.
- Tools: O&O AppBuster as a decent Windows 10 bloatware remover; Remote Utilities for remote control (TLS 1.2, MFA, brute-force lockout, one-time license; free for ten hosts); Syncthing for open-source peer-to-peer folder sync, which Leo also uses.
- Steve re-keyed GRC certificates ahead of the 1 September lifetime limit and revived the revoked.grc.com test site; benchmarking found Samsung SSDs the most consistent.
- SpiKey: a smartphone or doorbell microphone records the clicks as a key passes spring pins; the timing between clicks gives inter-ridge distances and relative bitting depths, narrowing about 330,000 possible keys to three candidates in the paper's simulation. Countermeasure: insert the key at an uneven speed.
