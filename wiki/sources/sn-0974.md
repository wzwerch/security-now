# Summary

Steve starts with a popular "Information Is Beautiful" heat map built from 3.4 million four-digit PINs gathered from data breaches, showing how strongly people cluster on repeated digits, birth years and dates; the 20 most common PINs account for 27% of all use. A listener note on GPS prompts a look at eLORAN, a low-frequency, ground-based, hard-to-jam navigation and timing system the US is testing as a complement to satellite navigation. Listener feedback on last week's Passkey discussion corrects the record: most sites do allow several Passkeys per account, though implementations vary and some are poor (for example PayPal accepting only phone-generated Passkeys, and cookie-dependent or browser-sniffing sites). The main topic covers Microsoft's pledge to put security first, driven by the Cyber Safety Review Board report on the 2023 Storm-0558 compromise of Exchange Online, which Steve reads from at length. He ties it to the long-running pattern of Microsoft ignoring warnings until a public disaster, compares today's cloud to the mainframe timesharing era, and concludes that moving to the cloud transfers security responsibility to the provider, whose contract holds it harmless.

# Key points

- Four-digit PIN data (3.4 million PINs, GRC shortcut grc.sc/pin): bright diagonal of repeated digit pairs, hot spots at 1234, 4321 and 6969, a band for years starting 19xx peaking near 1980, a cluster for month/day dates, and about 40 rarely used PINs; the top 20 PINs cover 27% of use. Advice: use longer or alphanumeric secrets where allowed.
- eLORAN (enhanced Long Range Navigation): ground-based, low frequency, high power, synchronised to UTC, accuracy of about 10-20 m, penetrates buildings, very hard to jam or spoof; proposed as one leg of a resilience triad with GNSS and inertial units with precise clocks. Hyperbolic positioning explained via pulse-timing differences between transmitter pairs.
- Passkey feedback: sites differ on how many Passkeys they accept per account; a YubiKey slot is used per service and a physical key can be registered by two people; PayPal supports multiple Passkeys but only from iOS/Android; PayPal and LinkedIn were reported to block Firefox by browser sniffing; Steve notes it is early days and typical users will likely adopt Passkeys when sites simply offer them.
- Microsoft's Secure Future Initiative progress claims: 1.7 million old Entra ID systems removed, MFA enforcement automated across over a million Entra ID tenants, more than 730,000 stale apps removed, short-term credentials for new staff and vendors, and phone-call MFA replaced with in-app approval for about 300,000 staff.
- CSRB finding: Storm-0558, assessed as PRC-affiliated, forged tokens with a consumer signing key Microsoft created in 2016 and compromised mailboxes of 22 organisations and 503 individuals, including senior US officials; Microsoft still could not say how the key was obtained; a token-validation flaw let a consumer key unlock enterprise Exchange Online accounts.
- The Board judged the intrusion preventable, Microsoft's security culture inadequate, and its public statements about root cause slow to be corrected; the State Department detected the breach itself using premium (G5/Purview Audit Premium) logging, with an alert rule nicknamed "Big Yellow Taxi".
- The Board urged Microsoft's CEO and directors to publish a security reform plan and to deprioritise feature work until security improves. Microsoft later stopped charging extra for security logging. CISA had earlier issued an emergency directive after the Midnight Blizzard compromise of Microsoft corporate email.
- Commentators (for example Senator Wyden) argue the government's dependence on Microsoft is a national-security risk; customers rarely leave because switching cost is high.
- Steve's analogy: cloud computing re-creates mainframe timesharing, concentrating value in tightly controlled places; customers moving assets to a provider also move the security responsibility, and contracts hold the provider harmless regardless of fault.
