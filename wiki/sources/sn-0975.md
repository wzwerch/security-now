# Summary

Chrome had a rough week: three more exploited-in-the-wild zero-days (CVE-2024-4947, -4671, -4761) brought the 2024 total to seven, four of them in the V8 JavaScript engine, and Steve praises Microsoft Edge's "Enhanced Security" mode, which disables JIT compilation on unfamiliar sites. He then looks at the decline of Google Search, including AI Overviews giving absurd advice, and the `udm=14` URL parameter that returns plain "Web" results. A video of an unprotected Windows XP virtual machine being compromised within about ten minutes illustrates how hostile the open Internet is and why NAT routers matter. Two Santa Cruz students found that the CSC ServiceWorks laundry app trusts client-side checks, allowing free laundry and arbitrary balances, yet the company ignored disclosure attempts. Listener feedback covers VPN firewalls as a defence against last week's TunnelVision routing trick, Netgate SG-1100 eMMC wear, a French web developer's warning that publishers are adopting first-party and identity-based tracking to replace third-party cookies, Bitwarden Passkey support now shipping, and the 300-credential Token2 security key. The main story is the open letter signed by 312 scientists from 35 countries rebutting the EU Council's revised Child Sexual Abuse Regulation proposal, arguing that targeted detection and risk categories do not fix the false-positive, circumvention and encryption-weakening problems.

# Key points

- Chrome zero-days: CVE-2024-4947 (V8 type confusion, found by Kaspersky in targeted attacks), CVE-2024-4671 (use-after-free in Visuals) and CVE-2024-4761 (V8 out-of-bounds write); Google withholds details until most users update.
- Edge Enhanced Security mode disables JIT and turns on hardware-enforced stack protection and Arbitrary Code Guard for unfamiliar sites, trading some speed for fewer memory-corruption exploits.
- Google search quality: sponsored results and video promotion crowd out answers; AI Overviews rolled out to all US users and cannot be disabled, produced dangerous nonsense such as advice to drink urine, and the "Web" filter (`udm=14` appended to the search URL) restores a clean results page. Steve recalls recommending Google to his mailing list in 1999.
- Eric Parker's Windows XP experiment: an unprotected XP VM on the Internet had a trojan, a new admin account, an FTP server, backdoors and a rootkit within minutes; Steve credits stateful NAT routers for shielding modern LANs.
- CSC ServiceWorks laundry machines (over one million units): the CSC Go app and API trusted client-side checks, did not verify email ownership, and let two UC Santa Cruz students start cycles and set a multimillion-dollar balance; CSC ignored contact forms, a phone call and a CERT/CC report, zeroed the demo balance, but left the flaw open.
- VPN follow-up: a VPN could prevent TunnelVision by using local firewall rules to block all non-tunnel outbound traffic, but many products do not, partly because firewall APIs are not cross-platform.
- Netgate SG-1100 users report the soldered eMMC wearing out (pfSense logging); Steve keeps his SG-1100 but also recommends the four-port Protectli Vault.
- Listener from a million-visitor-a-day French site: ad tech is moving to CNAME-cloaked first-party cookies, ISP-based identifiers and email/phone hashing because Privacy Sandbox pays lower CPMs; Steve notes tracking is shifting from advertiser-only to website-advertiser collusion and cannot be blocked.
- Bitwarden's Passkey support for mobile is available; Token2's FIDO2 key stores up to 300 Passkeys with selective deletion, built-in TOTP, USB-A/C and NFC; YubiKey's 25-slot limit is called a drawback. Leo and Steve rebut a listener who thought Passkeys should be non-exportable like session cookies.
- eLORAN follow-up: a Canadian listener receives test signals from three US stations (Nevada, Washington, Montana).
- 312 scientists' letter on the EU CSA Regulation (7 May 2024): the 13 March 2024 revision does not resolve earlier objections; flagging "users of interest" after repeated hits still produces millions of false positives (WhatsApp scale example gives 1.4 million per day at a 0.1% error rate and 1% sampling) and repeated hits are not statistically independent for families sharing photos.
- Risk categorisation of apps will not help: nearly all messaging services would count as high risk, abusers will migrate, and client-side scanning cannot be limited to a subset of users. Detection in end-to-end encrypted services undermines encryption; reliable privacy-preserving age verification does not yet exist.
- The ECtHR ruling Podchasov v. Russia (13 Feb 2024) held that obligations to decrypt end-to-end encrypted communications weaken encryption for all and are disproportionate. The letter recommends funding education, hotlines and proven abuse-prevention measures instead of "techno-solutionism"; the UK handled the issue by adding a "technically feasible" caveat.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a door plate that says "Pull" on a door that only pushes.
- Voyager 1 resumed sending science data (160 bits per second) after a memory workaround.
- GRC email system subscription front end needs more testing; SpinRite support for pre-6.0 upgrades has ended, with a retired FoxPro-era customer database.
- Anecdotes on coin-operated washers, a PIN heat-map follow-up noting 1701 (Star Trek) is over-represented, and a Joni Mitchell joke about "Big Yellow Taxi".
