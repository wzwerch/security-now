# Summary

This is the last episode of 2023 and Steve confirms the show will continue past episode 999. He covers Meta suing the FTC over a proposed USD 200 billion COPPA penalty and uses reporting on the Kids Online Safety Act (KOSA) and COPPA 2.0 to explain why US child-protection legislation is deadlocked and technically hard (age verification and the "duty of care" are opposed by civil-liberties groups). Chrome will disable third-party cookies for 1 percent of users in Q1 2024 and ramp to 100 percent by Q3, subject to UK competition review, mirroring Firefox's Total Cookie Protection. He summarizes Cloudflare's 2023 Internet review, including 25 percent traffic growth and 1.7 percent of TLS 1.3 using post-quantum key exchange. SpinRite 6.1 is nearly done after a hardware-security-module based on-the-fly EV code-signing system went live. Listener feedback covers quantum effects on Bitcoin and password hashing, open-source overlay networks, SpinRite hardware needs, passkeys that bypass additional MFA on GitHub, why upgrades break dependent code, and Chrome's own root store. The main topic is Cox Media Group's removed "Active Listening" marketing pages claiming to target ads from ambient conversations.

# Key points

- Meta v. FTC: FTC seeks up to USD 200 billion for alleged COPPA violations (children under 13 without parental consent, per a 41-state attorney-general suit); observers think the FTC may lack constitutional footing, underscoring that Congress should legislate.
- KOSA and COPPA 2.0 advanced in a Senate committee in July 2023: KOSA's duty of care and age gating vs EFF, ACLU and CDT objections (blocking LGBTQ and health information, forcing ID or face scans); COPPA 2.0 raises the protected age to 16 or 17 and so widens age verification. Steve concludes there is no consensus and no feasible technology, Leo says parents are the only effective control.
- Chrome 120 notice: third-party cookies disabled for 1 percent of users in Q1 2024, 100 percent by Q3 2024 pending UK CMA concerns; Google's replacement is the Topics API. Firefox's Total Cookie Protection (partitioned per-site cookie jars) has been the default since April 2023 without breakage.
- Cloudflare 2023 review: global traffic up 25 percent (likely streaming); top services Google, Facebook, Apple, TikTok, Microsoft, YouTube, AWS, Instagram, Amazon, iCloud; OpenAI most popular generative-AI service; Android over two-thirds of mobile traffic; Starlink traffic nearly tripled; about 50 percent of requests use HTTP/2 and 20 percent HTTP/3; NodeJS the top API client language (14.6 percent); 180+ outages, many government shutdowns; only a third of IPv6-capable requests use IPv6; just under 6 percent of traffic mitigated; 1.7 percent of TLS 1.3 traffic post-quantum; BGP route validity improved.
- SpinRite 6.1: remote server-side EV code signing with an HSM and a fresh three-year DigiCert certificate is working (165+ builds signed); signed builds triggered 0 of 68 VirusTotal engines versus a third for unsigned. Next release will wait 60 seconds for troubled drives to return and show a countdown; official release planned early 2024. Faster CPU or more RAM does not speed SpinRite 6.1 (about 50 MB RAM); SpinRite 7 will be multitasking.
- Quantum computing does not threaten symmetric crypto, password hashing (PBKDF) or Bitcoin proof-of-work mining, but does threaten asymmetric signatures.
- Overlay-network nuance: ZeroTier is source-available (BSL), Tailscale's control server and some clients are closed; Headscale, NetBird and OpenZiti are alternatives; Steve has not yet tried them.
- A GitHub user notes that passkey login skips the additional MFA factor, so storing passkeys in the same password manager as everything else collapses factors; Steve agrees offering both would be better.
- Upgrading libraries is feared because re-implementations can subtly change behaviour; Steve is impressed by Microsoft's backward compatibility.
- Google launched the Chrome Root Program and Chrome Root Store (announced September 2023), so Chromium browsers no longer depend on OS roots, relevant to the EU eIDAS Article 45 fight.
- Cox Media Group (owned by Apollo Global Management and Cox Enterprises) marketed "Active Listening" via CMG Local Solutions, claiming AI keyword detection from phone, smart TV and speaker audio to build lead lists in 10 or 20 mile territories, then scrubbed the pages (Web Archive copy exists). CMG later said it only resells anonymized third-party data. Steve and Leo doubt always-on microphone capture (device processing and traffic would reveal it) but note ISPs already see DNS and TLS metadata, so Steve recommends encrypted DNS for home users.
