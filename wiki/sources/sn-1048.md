# Summary

The episode opens with the long-awaited legal challenges to Texas SB2420, the App Store Accountability Act: the CCIA (whose members include Apple and Google) and a student advocacy group with two minors sued on First Amendment grounds, though Steve notes the Supreme Court's HB1181 opinion said adults have no First Amendment right to avoid age verification, so the law may survive in trimmed form. Google also posted its Play plans (new Play API for age signals, Play Console features) for Texas, Utah and Louisiana. NIST finally modernized its password guidance (SP 800-63B): length over complexity, minimum 15 characters when a password is the sole factor and 8 with MFA, no forced periodic rotation, no hints or security questions, and blocklists; Steve ties this to his 2012 "Password Haystacks" idea. Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters did not disband, having doxxed hundreds of DHS, ICE, FBI and DOJ officials. China's Ministry of State Security accused the NSA of attacking the National Time Service Center. Researchers from UC San Diego and Maryland found that about half of geostationary satellite IP links carry cleartext traffic including cellular backhaul, military, utility SCADA and corporate data. The October 20 AWS us-east-1 outage (DNS fault tied to DynamoDB) is used to argue against cloud monoculture. A rich listener-feedback section covers SB2420's scope, F-Droid, the older-brother loophole in age gating, and Discord's retention of ID images. The main technical topic is Mic-E-Mouse, a UC Irvine attack that reconstructs speech from high-rate optical mouse movement data using a retrained Whisper model.

# Key points

- Texas SB2420 lawsuits (W.D. Texas): CCIA argues it is a "broad censorship regime"; second suit by students; both cite Brown v. Entertainment Merchants Association; effective January 1, 2026 (Utah in May and Louisiana in July 2026). The law's "mobile device" definition excludes consoles, TVs and PCs; Leo notes app-store chokepoints make Apple and Google easy enforcement targets and that desktops remain an open loophole.
- Google Play: planned Play API to deliver age verification/supervision status and age ranges, Play Console tool to report significant app changes and parental revocations.
- NIST SP 800-63B update: length is the primary factor (15+ characters single-factor, 8+ with MFA, up to 64 allowed), complexity rules dropped, scheduled resets dropped (reset only after compromise), knowledge-based hints dropped, breach-corpus and dictionary blocklists required. Leo recalls NIST author Bill Burr regretted the old advice.
- Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters: 404 Media reports doxxing of nearly 700 DHS officials among hundreds of government staff, contradicting their claimed breakup.
- China accuses NSA of exploiting a foreign phone brand's messaging service in 2022 and using 42 attack tools against the National Time Service Center; no evidence published.
- "Don't Look Up" paper (ACM CCS 2025): 39 GEO satellites, 411 transponders scanned with consumer hardware; 50% of links had cleartext IP traffic, including cellular calls/SMS and encryption keys, in-flight WiFi, military vessel tracking, Walmart inventory, ATM networks and power/pipeline SCADA; hundreds of dollars of equipment suffice; researchers notified affected parties.
- AWS outage (October 20, 2025): us-east-1 DNS problem cascaded through DynamoDB and 143 services; Snapchat, Signal, Roblox, banks, Ring and more affected; experts call for cloud diversification. Steve calls it a monoculture risk like Chromium dominance and Cloudflare.
- Listeners note last week's elevator-code picture also required pressing star; SB2420 definitions are tightly mobile-focused; F-Droid arguably affected by SB2420 as an installer of apps; Discord's own support page claims ID images are deleted after verification, yet 70,000 leaked; kids will bypass age gates (older-brother loophole).
- Mic-E-Mouse (UC Irvine): optical mice with 4 to 8 kHz sampling sense desk vibrations from speech; user-space software (even web pages via WebAssembly) can read the raw movement data; a pipeline of Wiener filtering, resampling and a neural spectrogram filter plus a retrained OpenAI Whisper model yields up to 19 dB SNR improvement and roughly 42 to 61% (up to about 65%) speech recognition accuracy; no special privileges needed; code is public.
