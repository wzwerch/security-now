# Summary

A news-light week lets Steve spend about half the show on listener feedback. He opens with the 50th anniversary of BYTE magazine's first issue (and its 1988 SpinRite review), then reports that the FCC removed more than 1,200 voice providers from the US phone network for failing to deploy STIR/SHAKEN robocall mitigation. Leo interrupts with breaking news that Judge Mehta's remedies ruling in the Google search monopoly case declined to force Chrome or Android divestiture. Steve covers the Salesloft Drift breach, where stolen OAuth tokens let attackers pivot into customers' Salesforce and other services, as a warning about consolidated authentication; a Reuters report on Meta's celebrity chatbots, which he uses to argue LLM behaviour is inherently hard to control; and Vivaldi's anti-AI-browser stance. He explains how WhatsApp bug CVE-2025-55177 was chained with Apple's DNG flaw in a targeted spyware campaign, describes the Nx npm compromise that made victims' own AI CLI tools hunt for secrets, notes TransUnion's breach by ShinyHunters, a 4chan/Kiwi Farms suit against Ofcom, OpenSSH's new post-quantum warning, and a Russian-based sole maintainer of the fast-glob library found in Pentagon-approved software. Extensive feedback follows (Syncthing untrusted-peer encryption, credit bureau consent, Cheburnet, email-address validity quiz, ads and AI, defence of Microsoft) and the show ends with an author's exit from Kindle Unlimited.

# Key points

- BYTE issue 1 (September 1975) is archived on the Internet Archive; BYTE's 1988 SpinRite review helped establish the product.
- STIR/SHAKEN: STIR attaches signed certificates to SIP calls and SHAKEN extends the idea to non-VoIP networks; the FCC mandated it from June 2021 (Canada's CRTC from November 2021) but carriers had little incentive. Last Thursday the FCC removed 1,200+ of 2,411 notified providers from the network; all other providers must stop accepting their calls.
- Google search antitrust remedies (Leo): no forced sale of Chrome or Android and payments to partners such as Apple and Mozilla may continue; the ban targets exclusive-style distribution deals; Google said it will appeal.
- Salesloft/Drift: attackers used OAuth tokens from the Drift chat agent to reach customers' Salesforce data and then Google Workspace, Slack and Pardot; Salesloft revoked connections. Lesson: login-with-X and shared OAuth trust let one compromise cascade.
- Reuters found Meta chatbots impersonating celebrities (including minors) with sexual content; Steve compares LLM knowledge storage to a hologram, which makes surgical editing and policy enforcement very hard.
- Vivaldi says it will not add chatbots, summaries or agentic browsing "until better ways exist"; Leo calls it partly marketing, Steve prefers AI as a research aid despite occasional invented facts.
- Amnesty International said the WhatsApp flaw CVE-2025-55177 (incomplete authorization of linked-device sync messages) and Apple's CVE-2025-43300 were used together in a spyware campaign over the preceding 90 days. Leo adds that the current US administration reportedly allowed ICE to use Paragon spyware.
- Nx supply-chain attack (late August 2025): a stolen npm publishing token let an attacker push malicious Nx packages (about 4.6 million weekly downloads) that prompted locally installed AI command-line agents such as Claude, Gemini or Q to find secrets (tokens, SSH keys, .env files, wallets), encrypted the loot and published it to new public GitHub repos prefixed "s1ngularity-repository"; about 1,400 repos were created.
- TransUnion lost data on 4.4 million customers to ShinyHunters, another victim of the Salesforce-linked campaign that also touched Google, Allianz Life, Workday, Cisco, Chanel, Qantas and others.
- 4chan and Kiwi Farms sued Ofcom over the UK Online Safety Act; Steve notes traffic shifting from compliant adult sites to non-compliant ones, and says privacy-preserving age assertion (derived from systems like TruAge, stripped of identity) is urgently needed. Leo adds that the US Supreme Court dismissed NetChoice's challenge on procedural grounds rather than endorsing the Mississippi law.
- OpenSSH 10.1 will warn when a connection does not use a post-quantum key exchange (default since 10.0, option WarnWeakCrypto), to counter "store now, decrypt later".
- Nextgov/Hunted Labs: the fast-glob library (about 70 million weekly downloads, in at least 30 packages in the DoD's Iron Bank repository) has a single maintainer who works at Yandex in Russia; no malicious code is known but sole-maintainer risk and coercion are the concern. Context includes the July memo from Defense Secretary Hegseth about foreign influence and the earlier XZ Utils backdoor.
- Feedback highlights: Parachute Backup app for local iCloud photo backup; credit bureaus collect data under consent buried in credit agreements; a Russian listener says Google Meet blocking and the Max messenger are part of building a national "Cheburnet"; e-mail.wtf quiz on valid email address syntax; ad blockers are analogous to skipping TV commercials and to highlighting a book; AI chatbots will likely carry ads; Brave's BAT and micro-mining ideas; Common Crawl as a shared crawl corpus to reduce redundant bot traffic; malvertising delivered ransomware twice at a listener's organisation; Syncthing has supported encrypted "untrusted" peers for over four years; Sync.com disables email-based recovery by default; a listener defends Microsoft's scale, Steve replies he objects to TPM-based Windows 11 limits and paid extended security updates.
- Ryk Brown's Frontiers Saga will leave Kindle Unlimited from Part 4 because royalties fell to about 30%; Leo discusses Amazon's monopoly power and AI-generated books.
