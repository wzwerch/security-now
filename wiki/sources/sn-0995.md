# Summary

Steve opens with Ireland's 91 million euro (about $101 million) fine on Meta for storing user passwords in plaintext, a practice first reported in 2019 with some 2,000 engineers having searched those records millions of times. He then warns PayPal users that a privacy-statement change effective 27 November 2024 will share shopping-history data with merchants by default (except in California, North Dakota and Vermont) and shows where to switch it off. Cloudflare reported a record 65-second DDoS peaking at 3.8 Tbps, likely from ASUS routers compromised through CVE-2024-3080; Steve suggests checking router firmware during National Cybersecurity Awareness Month. Other items: more than a dozen blockchain firms hired North Korean IT workers, Akamai's finding that the CUPS flaws also enable DDoS amplification, a Vitamin D digression, and advice on secure consumer routers. The main topic is Chrome's Manifest V2 phase-out, which cripples uBlock Origin; Steve offers a Chrome enterprise policy that delays it until June 2025, and explains why uBlock Origin Lite left Firefox after disputes with Mozilla's add-on review process.

# Key points

- Meta fine: Irish Data Protection Commission fined Meta 91 million euros for logging and storing hundreds of millions of passwords unhashed; Steve notes five years of "investigation" and that plaintext passwords aid credential reuse attacks. Total GDPR fines against Meta exceed 2 billion euros, including 1.2 billion euros under appeal.
- PayPal: from 27 November 2024 information gathered is shared with participating merchants for "personalized shopping" (marketing use from summer 2025); on by default outside California, North Dakota and Vermont; opt-out under Settings > Data & Privacy > Manage shared info > Personalized Shopping. In the UK merchants may pass data to their service providers. Steve decided against adding PayPal to GRC's shop.
- Cloudflare: record DDoS of 3.8 Tbps and 2.1 billion packets per second for 65 seconds with sharp on/off edges, over 100 hyper-volumetric attacks in a month, previous record 3.47 Tbps (2021); likely sourced from ASUS routers exploited via CVE-2024-3080 (CVSS 9.8, about 157,000 exposed models per Censys). CDNs withstand such floods through geographic distribution.
- GRC itself was knocked offline for an hour by a flood on the preceding Sunday; Steve does not use a CDN.
- Router hygiene: update firmware, enable automatic updates, and never expose remote admin, media or file servers; Steve found his ASUS router one version behind.
- CoinDesk found a dozen-plus blockchain projects had unknowingly hired North Korean IT workers using fake identities, violating sanctions.
- Akamai: CUPS (cups-browsed on UDP 631) can be abused as a one-packet DDoS reflector; about 198,000 devices exposed, 58,000 usable for abuse, many running ancient CUPS versions; the point that "attacks only get better".
- Listener question on secure consumer routers: any mainstream NAT router is fine by default; disable UPnP and WPS, use guest networks for IoT, keep auto-update on; Steve likes ASUS (a roughly $66 model is enough), Leo mentions Ubiquiti, Synology and Eero mesh; routersecurity.org recommended.
- Manifest V3: Chrome is phasing out Manifest V2 extensions and flags uBlock Origin as soon unsupported; MV3 removes capabilities of the blocking API, so the lighter uBlock Origin Lite lacks dynamic filtering, custom filters and some cosmetic features by default.
- Workaround: Chrome policy ExtensionManifestV2Availability set to 2 (Windows registry DWORD; Steve provides the grc.sc/v2 .reg file) keeps MV2 extensions until June 2025; Edge is expected to drop MV2, Brave may keep it, Firefox says it will keep MV2.
- uBlock Origin Lite on Firefox: Mozilla's add-on reviewers sent policy-violation emails (claims of undisclosed data collection and minified code) that Raymond Hill (gorhill) says were wrong; he declined the process and discontinued the Firefox Lite edition on 26 September 2024. Others report similar slow, error-prone Mozilla reviews.
- Steve and Leo debate the ethics of ad blocking (Hill: blocking is not theft), site funding, local journalism and paywalls; Steve regrets that EU regulators pushed Google to abandon its Privacy Sandbox.
