# Summary

After a quiet news week Steve devotes most of the episode to uBlock Origin. He first closes the loop on his audio problems (a Skype relay path had been replacing his direct UDP link), reports that he now runs Firefox under reduced rights on XP using DropMyRights, and notes that uBlock Origin already blocked the ad-network domain involved in new Angler exploit kit malvertising on MSN. News items: Amazon banned Flash ads from September 1 and Chrome's default shifts to not running all plugin content automatically; Google, Mozilla and Microsoft announced the end of RC4 support in early 2016; Microsoft quietly shipped optional updates back-porting Windows 10 style telemetry to Windows 7 and 8. Steve rejects the idea that visitors owe sites their attention to third-party ads, comparing web ads to opportunistic advertising on buses and billboards, recalling how he found the first adware (Aureate) and explaining perverse incentives, tracking and performance costs. He then explains uBlock Origin's lineage (HTTP Switchboard, uMatrix, uBlock, fork to Origin by Raymond Hill, who refuses donations), its curated filter lists, advanced mode matrix, per-site sticky power switch and first-party blocking, and notes a Wired page loading ten times faster with it.

# Key points

- **Audio fix:** Skype was relaying through intermediaries; Steve restored a direct UDP path and saw zero packet loss.
- **DropMyRights on XP:** running the browser with a constrained token confines exploits of font, Flash or script flaws to reduced rights.
- **Angler on MSN:** Malwarebytes tracked another ad chain through AdSpirit and a Red Hat cloud storage hop to ad-fraud and ransomware payloads; uBlock Origin blocks the domains even when typed as first-party URLs.
- **Flash:** Amazon stopped accepting Flash ads; Chrome now defaults to "detect and run important plugin content"; Steve prefers "let me choose" or click-to-play in Firefox.
- **RC4 end of life:** Adam Langley announced Chrome will disable RC4 around January or February 2016, with only about 0.13 percent of connections using it; Mozilla and Microsoft follow; Chrome already hides RC4 unless the first connection fails.
- **Google logo update** noted as animated HTML5.
- **Telemetry on Windows 7 and 8:** four recommended updates add customer-experience and diagnostic services that connect to Microsoft endpoints and are hard to disable; Ars argues users need rigorous opt-outs; Leo stresses they are recommended, not critical.
- **Dave Winer quote:** operating systems turning social is unwanted; Steve wants a file system and a program launcher.
- **Ethics of ad blocking:** Steve sees no implied contract to load third-party content; ad ecosystem incentives reward obnoxious ads and page weight; expects sites to start detecting blockers, asking users to whitelist them, or serving ads first-party.
- **uBlock Origin features:** cross-browser; HTML firewall with default-protective filter lists (ads, malware, trackers), per-site power switch, advanced mode with matrix of domains and object types showing log10 counts of blocked/permitted requests, global and local rules, user rules, sync of settings in version 1.1; Raymond Hill's fork from uBlock; measured Wired.com load of about 46 seconds and 125 requests falling to under 5 seconds and 98 requests with 10 domains instead of 33.
- **Google router follow-up:** a listener worries about remote update paths; Steve says it is fine if done with pinned certificates and signed firmware.
