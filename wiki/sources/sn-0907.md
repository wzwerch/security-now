# Summary

Steve opens with PayPal's breach notification: a credential-stuffing campaign in early December exposed almost 35,000 accounts, with no unauthorized transactions found. This follows the previous week's Norton LifeLock case and becomes the episode's closing topic. Apple's iOS 16.3 makes iCloud Advanced Data Protection available worldwide, though it requires every device on the account to run a capable OS, which Steve finds a high bar. Two reports show ChatGPT being used to produce polymorphic malware and basic criminal tooling, lowering the skill barrier for attackers. Meta received a third EU fine of the year, a modest 5.5 million euros over WhatsApp's 2021 terms-of-service pop-ups. Bitwarden acquired Passwordless.dev (FIDO2/WebAuthn tooling) and raised its default PBKDF2 iterations, with OWASP also raising its recommendation. Listener feedback covers memory-hard key derivation, "finished" software, why vault blobs are still guarded by a login hash, and the strong-versus-memorable password paradox. After a SpinRite 6.1 status note, Steve explains credential stuffing and concludes that time-based one-time passwords plus a required persistent device cookie give the most protection with the least annoyance.

# Key points

- PayPal reported (to the Maine Attorney General) that credential stuffing in early December reached almost 35,000 accounts using valid reused credentials; no unauthorized transactions were found. Steve stresses we only learn of attacks that trip alarms.
- Apple iOS 16.3 makes iCloud Advanced Data Protection globally available (backups, Drive, Notes, Photos and more end-to-end encrypted; Mail, Contacts, Calendar excluded for interoperability), but all signed-in devices must run a supporting OS, which blocks many users with older devices.
- CyberArk researchers got ChatGPT to produce evasive, mutating (polymorphic) code by insisting on constraints; the API version appeared to lack the web interface's content filtering.
- Check Point Research documented underground forum threads where low-skill actors used ChatGPT for an infostealer script, a file-encryption tool that could be turned into ransomware, and dark-web marketplace scripts. Steve's point: easier crime means more crime.
- Meta received a third EU GDPR fine in 2023: 5.5 million euros for WhatsApp, after earlier fines of 210 million (Facebook) and 180 million (Instagram); the heavy-handed 2021 terms pop-ups drove users toward Signal and Telegram.
- Bitwarden acquired the EU startup Passwordless.dev (open-source FIDO2/WebAuthn API); passkey support is on its 2023 roadmap. Leo notes Bitwarden is a show sponsor.
- Bitwarden raised default client-side PBKDF2 iterations to 350,000, then announced 600,000 plus double encryption of stored fields; existing accounts are still under study. OWASP also moved its recommendation to 600,000. Argon2 may follow.
- Steve argues the era of non-memory-hard key derivation is over and new identity platforms should use memory-hard functions; he notes Argon2 won a key-strengthening competition and Dashlane already uses it.
- On "finished" software: the RTOS-32 kernel vendor On Time closed because it ran out of bugs to fix; Steve (who bought its source code) sees software that needs no updates as an ideal, using the OTP Auth app as an example.
- Why a vault blob needs a login hash: services keep a client-derived hash for portal authentication, so both LastPass and Bitwarden hash it another 100,000 times; real-world crypto sheds its theoretical purity.
- A strong, memorable master password is a contradiction because memorability implies patterns and lower entropy; options are random characters written down (an offline secret) or a hidden modification of the written text.
- Credential stuffing succeeds roughly 0.1% of the time; the Collection #1-5 dump holds about 22 billion pairs; bot fleets spread attempts over IPs, time and target sites to avoid alarms.
- Countermeasures: delay failed-login responses and track offending IPs, treat data-center traffic with suspicion, block email addresses as usernames, use CAPTCHAs and fingerprinting sparingly; shared front doors such as Cloudflare can see cross-site patterns.
- Best two measures: time-based one-time passwords and a persistent device cookie check ("device not recognized"), combined so that the OTP is only requested when the cookie is missing.
- Unix time (32-bit seconds since 1970) overflows on 19 January 2038, flagged by a listener as the 15-years-to-go reminder.
