# Summary

Steve starts with yet more SQL injection flaws found in Progress Software's MOVEit Transfer, bringing the count to four required patch rounds in four weeks, and notes that Huntress sees only data theft and extortion by the Clop group, not ransomware or wider network compromise. He explains the term "rug pull" using the Multichain hack, and cites SlowMist's mid-year report of over $922 million stolen from crypto platforms in 185 incidents. A Dutch consumer foundation sued Avast over the sale of user browsing data via its Jumpshot subsidiary. The show then covers China's Linux-based OpenKylin desktop, the "TootRoot" Mastodon flaw, the Firefox 115 Quarantined Domains feature, Russia's doubtful "disconnection test", a Trustwave honeypot study, EU rulings against Meta and Google Analytics use, and Apple's withdrawn Rapid Security Response update that broke user-agent parsing. After a long segment on how Steve now synchronizes Synology NAS boxes with Syncthing behind pfSense firewalls, and his purchase of the RTOS-32 operating system for future SpinRite work, the main topic is the Centauri paper showing that Rowhammer bit-flip patterns can uniquely and stably fingerprint an individual DRAM module without privileges. Steve's conclusion is that this yields an indelible, cross-browser and cross-app device identifier that no cookie control can stop.

# Key points

- MOVEit Transfer received a fourth patch round for additional SQL injection bugs (found by HackerOne and Trend Micro ZDI researchers) plus two other high-severity fixes; Steve says the architecture makes further bugs likely.
- Huntress forensics: the Clop group limited itself to the MOVEit appliance for data theft and extortion; it had publicly said it would delete data from governments, law enforcement and education.
- Multichain lost roughly $126 million (about $65 million frozen by Circle and Tether); the incident introduced the term "rug pull" for developers absconding with funds.
- SlowMist's 2023 mid-year report: over $922 million stolen in 185 incidents in H1 2023, versus $2 billion in 187 incidents in H1 2022; Euler Finance ($197 million) was the largest case and was largely returned.
- A Dutch foundation (CUIC) filed a class action against Avast over data collected by its browser extensions and sold through Jumpshot between 2015 and 2020; Steve calls it a deep breach of trust.
- OpenKylin is China's Linux 6.1-based desktop OS for x86, ARM and RISC-V, with Firefox as the browser.
- "TootRoot" (CVSS 9.9) in Mastodon let a malicious media file extension take over a server; found by Cure53 during a Mozilla-commissioned audit and patched before disclosure.
- Firefox 115 introduced Quarantined Domains, letting Mozilla remotely block specific extensions on specific sites; an extension developer objected, Steve supports it. It is the last Firefox for Windows 7 aside from security updates.
- Russia's claimed annual test disconnection of RuNet on 5 July showed no visible routing drop in monitoring data; railroad outages were attributed to DDoS attacks.
- Trustwave honeypots in six countries over six months saw 19 percent of probing traffic as malicious, 95 percent of it from IoT botnets.
- The European Court of Justice ruled against Meta's cross-service data merging under GDPR; Sweden fined Tele2 and CDON for Google Analytics use, the fifth EU state to act.
- Apple pushed and pulled a Rapid Security Response update (version suffix "(a)") because websites' user-agent parsers failed; it fixed a WebKit zero-day, the tenth Apple zero-day of 2023.
- Steve's NAS setup: Syncthing native on each Synology, no Synology cloud features, pfSense with DNS-driven firewall rules and DynDNS so each site accepts connections only from the other site's IP.
- Centauri (UC Davis, to be presented at NDSS 2024): Rowhammer bit-flip distributions give a DRAM fingerprint with 99.91 percent accuracy across 98 DIMMs, stable over 10 days, extractable in under 10 seconds and without root, defeating Target Row Refresh via tuned patterns; could run in WebAssembly in browsers.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: live mains cables stripped to power a light bulb as a "voltage tester".
- SpinRite 6.1 reached its first release candidate; Steve bought RTOS-32 (Peter Petersen's real-time OS, about 16K overhead) as the base for SpinRite 7, replacing FreeDOS.
- Listener feedback: HxD hex/disk editor for raw disks, tracking down chirping detectors (Steve's old water-leak sensor), OTP Auth and 2FAS authenticator apps, military camo uniform discussion, a "buffet overflow" joke.
- Leo's Midjourney image of Steve and Leo in "cyber camo".
