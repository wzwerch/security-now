# Summary

Because of heavy news the whole episode is a news roundup. Microsoft's Patch Tuesday brings 12 bundles (eight critical, including DNS, Office, scripting engines and both browsers) alongside an Adobe Flash update fixing 78 flaws. Steve covers Microsoft's escalating push of Windows 10 upgrades through Windows Update and the GWX Control Panel tool that fights it. Vendor-installed software returns as a theme: Dell's remaining Foundation Services web service exposed management queries on a port, Lenovo Solution Center ran a privileged local web server that web pages could drive, and AOL Desktop's decades-old FDO bytecode protocol is unauthenticated and unencrypted. Let's Encrypt entered public beta using the ACME protocol, while political news includes President Obama's remark about making technology harder for terrorists to exploit, French proposals to block Tor and public Wi-Fi, a Kazakhstan notice requiring a government root certificate (later withdrawn), and an app distributed by ISIS. Mozilla ended its Tiles advertising experiment and released the Focus content blocker for iOS, and a combined Pony and Angler campaign delivers CryptoWall 4.0. Miscellaneous items include libsodium 1.0.6 and Phillip Rogaway's paper on the moral role of cryptography.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday: 12 bundles, eight critical remote-code-execution items; Flash update closed 78 vulnerabilities; Steve recommends click-to-play for Flash and notes a Flash-free Speedtest beta.
- Windows 10 push: Microsoft has staged upgrade files on older systems, is moving the upgrade into optional and later recommended updates, and reportedly resets users' decline settings. GWX Control Panel (free, can run continuously) blocks this.
- Dell: after the certificate fiasco, Dell Foundation Services still ran a local HTTP/SOAP service on port 7779 exposing system enumeration; Shodan found exposed hosts. Steve reminds listeners to keep UPnP off and rely on NAT routers.
- Lenovo Solution Center: a service running with full rights listening on a local port accepted HTTP requests that could execute programs, and web pages could reach it via localhost; Lenovo urged users to disable it.
- AOL Desktop: its FDO form-definition bytecode protocol (dating from 1993) is unauthenticated and sent in plaintext, so an on-path attacker can make the client read or write files and run programs; advice is to uninstall.
- Wired suggested an Australian researcher might be Satoshi Nakamoto; Leo relays the evidence, Steve calls it fun speculation.
- Let's Encrypt public beta is live and automated domain validation via ACME is being standardized by the IETF; third-party clients, including ones that avoid running as root, already exist.
- Obama's Oval Office address asked tech and law-enforcement leaders to make it harder for terrorists to use technology; Steve is concerned the earlier no-backdoor stance is eroding.
- French draft proposals would ban Tor and temporarily public Wi-Fi and require VoIP services to hand over keys.
- Kazakhstan announced mandatory installation of a national security certificate to decrypt outbound TLS (notice later removed); Steve links this to corporate TLS interception and fears ISP versions.
- ISIS-linked Android app distributed via Telegram links rather than Google Play.
- Mozilla cancelled its advertising Tiles experiment and released Focus, a Disconnect-list-based Safari content blocker.
- Pony data stealer plus the Angler exploit kit installing CryptoWall 4.0 from compromised low-traffic sites; the FBI estimated CryptoWall victims lose tens of millions of dollars annually (Steve cited 18 million).
- Armed drones: a video of a pistol on a quadcopter prompts Steve to worry about weaponized consumer drones.
- libsodium 1.0.6 now includes authenticated encryption and wide language bindings; Steve uses it for SQRL.
- Rogaway's Asiacrypt talk argues cryptographers must respond to mass surveillance; Steve plans to read the paper.
- Number of primes below 2^1024 is about 2.5 times 10^305, and primes are densely distributed.

# Other segments

- SpinRite recovering a DVR hard drive (one needed an hdparm standby unlock first) with Walking Dead recordings.
- Wells Fargo password-length claim not reproduced by others; Moriturimax's tip that police could lift a fingerprint from the home button to learn the real unlock finger.
- Sci-fi and games: Childhood's End miniseries, The Expanse launch, Steve's sci-fi reading list, Monument Valley free, iOS Mail "no content" workaround using Outlook.
- Bitcoin mining profitability anecdote and Trump's comment about shutting parts of the Internet.
