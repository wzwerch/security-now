# Summary

A busy news week leads into a first look at iOS 9 content blockers. Steve covers XcodeGhost, in which Chinese developers who obtained a tampered Xcode toolchain unwittingly shipped infected iOS apps, including WeChat; iOS 9's very large list of security fixes; a critical Flash update; AVG's new policy to sell search and browsing data to advertisers; FireEye's discovery of the SYNful Knock implant in Cisco routers, living in the ROM monitor and activated by crafted TCP sequence values; the realization that Ashley Madison also stored weak MD5-based hashes for about 15 million accounts, allowing many passwords to be cracked; and the Volkswagen emissions defeat device, which leads Leo to note that DMCA rules deter researchers from inspecting vehicle software. India published and then withdrew a draft national encryption policy requiring plaintext for 90 days. Steve also corrects lithium-ion battery folklore, notes Stagefright patches continuing to roll out, and compares the early iOS blockers (Purify, 1Blocker, Blockr, Crystal and others) while he and Leo debate whether sites will start blocking users who block ads and whether ad-driven incentives swell the web with junk.

# Key points

- **XcodeGhost:** a developer noticed an unexpected iCloud password prompt and network traffic from a trivial app built with a tampered Xcode obtained from a local mirror; many apps were infected (Palo Alto Networks identified dozens, including WeChat); the malicious code could phish credentials, hijack URL schemes and read the clipboard; Apple removed the apps.
- **iOS 9:** an unusually large number of security fixes across WebKit, networking and OpenSSL; Steve urges updating for security, not just features.
- **Flash 19.0.0.185:** critical update; Chrome and IE auto-update, Firefox users must check and keep "ask to activate."
- **Forbes malvertising:** FireEye found Forbes serving ads that redirected to Neutrino and Angler exploit kits for about a week, with a single Flash file carrying seven exploits; real-time-bidding platforms let attackers target selectively.
- **AVG policy:** from October 15 users permit sale of browsing and search data; Steve doubts such data can be truly anonymous and notes AVG installs its own root certificate for traffic inspection.
- **SYNful Knock (FireEye):** modified Cisco IOS images in about 14 routers across four countries accept commands when sent TCP SYN packets with matching sequence and acknowledgment values; reflashing IOS alone would not remove it because it lives in the ROM monitor; default credentials and supply-chain implanting are suspected.
- **Ashley Madison:** bcrypt with 4,096 iterations protected the main hashes, but about 15 million accounts also kept an older lowercase MD5 construction, so about 11 million passwords were recovered; password reuse makes this worse.
- **Volkswagen / Audi:** EPA says software detects emissions testing and reduces pollution controls otherwise, with NOx up to 40 times the standard on about half a million diesels; DMCA limits on reverse engineering reduced outside scrutiny.
- **India's draft encryption policy:** would have mandated approved algorithms, vendor registration, and storage of plaintext for 90 days on demand; withdrawn within hours of Steve noting it after criticism over messaging apps like WhatsApp; Telegram's founder defended private communication.
- **Stagefright:** fixes keep reaching devices; the Zimperium tester remains the reference for the last bug.
- **Lithium-ion advice:** avoid deep discharge; modern chargers prevent overcharging; occasional full cycles recalibrate the fuel gauge.
- **iOS blockers:** Purify (simple, whitelist, from the uBlock maintainer), 1Blocker (power-user, fastest in a Lifehacker benchmark, paid features), Blockr (middle ground, one dollar), Crystal (no whitelist at launch); holding the reload button reloads without filtering. Peace was withdrawn because it blocked small Mac blogs' unobtrusive ads.
- **Ad economics:** Dave Winer argues for commerce services instead of ads; Steve asks whether ad incentives inflate the number of junk pages; Leo recalls demand-media content farms and Google demoting them; both worry blunt blockers harm good small sites.
