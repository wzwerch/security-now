# Summary

In a Q&A episode Steve first advises gradually migrating from TrueCrypt to VeraCrypt after a Google Project Zero researcher found privilege-escalation flaws in TrueCrypt's kernel driver, which are fixed only in the VeraCrypt fork; stored data is not at risk of decryption, but malware could use the bug to gain admin rights. He reports that Crystal, the top iOS content blocker, will adopt the Adblock Plus "acceptable ads" program for a fee, quotes the advertising trade body calling blocking robbery while also admitting it is a wake-up call, and shows a deliberately absurd rental-car site that defeats blockers by serving everything first-party. A computer-magazine columnist shows that Lenovo's ThinkPad line also sends software inventory and usage data to a third party, ending the idea that Think-branded machines are exempt. Tavis Ormandy's analysis of antivirus products (Kaspersky and others) finding easy remote code execution bugs leads to a discussion of AV as a large attack surface and the black market for AV exploits seen in the Hacking Team leak. Leo raises Apple's repeated password prompts on setup. Listener questions cover browser-based peer-to-peer VPNs, steganography, Google Contributor privacy, whether to run antivirus, password length limits, Windows 10 WiFi Sense and the "_optout" SSID suffix, iOS 9 content blocker installation and a podcast app regression.

# Key points

- **TrueCrypt:** two kernel driver privilege-escalation bugs (found by Google Project Zero) affect TrueCrypt and are fixed in VeraCrypt 1.15; the earlier cryptographic audit did not cover the driver; VeraCrypt can convert non-system TrueCrypt volumes in place, but a system partition must be decrypted first. Steve says there is no hurry but migration is advisable.
- **Crystal blocker and acceptable ads:** the author will enable Eyeo's paid acceptable-ads list by default with a switch to turn it off; critics call it a tollbooth; an Interactive Advertising Bureau executive calls commercial blockers robbery while acknowledging abusive ads provoke consumers.
- **LingsCars.com:** a deliberately chaotic first-party site cited as a blocker-proof example.
- **iOS blockers ranking:** five of the top ten apps were blockers in week one; Purify stays near the top; sites begin to detect blockers and ask users to disable them.
- **Lenovo ThinkPad:** Lenovo's own support page says ThinkCentre, ThinkStation and ThinkPad software may contact Internet servers; Think products collect application usage and preloaded application inventory data, sent to a third-party analytics company; Nirsoft's task scheduler view was used to find it.
- **Antivirus as attack surface:** Project Zero found easily exploitable remote bugs in Kaspersky; FireEye, Sophos and ESET products also had problems; weaponized ESET exploits were among Hacking Team's offerings; Steve and Leo run no third-party AV (Steve uses Microsoft's built-in tool).
- **Apple password prompts:** repeated entry on fresh setup is annoying with long random passwords, but reauthentication after power cycles guards against hardware attacks; Android asks less.
- **Hola versus a client VPN:** a peer-to-peer VPN decrypts your traffic on a stranger's machine, so use HTTPS inside it; a centralized provider concentrates traffic but is less likely to snoop.
- **Steganography:** hiding data in least significant bits of uncompressed images; low bandwidth and hurt by compression; encryption makes it look like noise; Simon Singh and David Kahn books recommended.
- **Google Contributor:** requires being logged into Google so it adds little extra exposure if you already are; Google need not sell data because it owns the ad system.
- **Password length:** 20 random characters give far more entropy than needed; sites limiting to 12 are annoying; Steve's SQRL demo site will use 256-character passwords.
- **WiFi Sense:** sharing is off by default and needs explicit per-network enabling; the "_optout" SSID suffix is likely for captive-portal and public hotspot operators.
- **iOS 9 install problems:** content blockers need iOS 9, must be launched once before they appear in settings; the stock podcast app lost full-screen video, so use alternatives; some apps broke on iOS 9 (an NNTP reader updated, SQRL iOS client needed porting).
