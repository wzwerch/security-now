# Summary

This planned Q&A was replaced by a news-heavy show, with questions deferred to episode 415. Leo opens with Ed Bott's remarks on installers from download sites and Oracle's Java that bundle the Ask toolbar and change search settings, which Steve says is driven by large payments. Microsoft's July update broke video playback for some users (the top half of video stayed black). The main story is the latest Guardian documents claiming Microsoft helped the NSA and FBI get around encryption on Outlook.com chats, gave pre-encryption access to Outlook and Hotmail mail, eased PRISM access to SkyDrive, and helped triple Skype video collection. Microsoft denied giving any government direct or blanket access but admitted that updates must preserve its ability to comply with lawful demands, and Microsoft's general counsel asked the Attorney General to let it publish more, which Steve reads as consistent with upstream taps followed by targeted decryption requests. Clapper explained his false March testimony by saying he forgot Section 215. The DEF CON organizers told federal agents to stay away, Russia reportedly buys typewriters, and an Adblock Plus developer explains the acceptable-ads program, which charges only large companies. Steve examines Lavabit (encrypted at rest, but plaintext when read or sent) and notes Snowden used PGP. He also covers iOS 7 beta breaking Google Authenticator, BitTorrent Sync Android beta, Hemlis funding, and a SpinRite SSD recovery. In the closing segment he argues for a middle path on privacy: avoid trusting big providers that are on the PRISM list, always pre-encrypt cloud data, and favor services with open protocols and honest intent (BitTorrent Sync, Hemlis, Threema, Cryptocat one-to-one chat, LastPass), plus GPG for email. The NSA now admits analysts look up to three hops from a suspect.

# Key points

- Bundled "download wrappers" and the Ask toolbar for Java are monetized heavily; Steve refuses such offers. PrivacyFix and Ghostery help show tracking.
- Microsoft patch MS13-057 (media codec fix) caused video playback glitches; fix pending.
- Guardian documents: Microsoft was first on the PRISM timeline; NSA worried about Outlook.com chat encryption and obtained a workaround with the FBI within five months; PRISM collects Hotmail, Live and Outlook.com email before encryption; FBI worked with Microsoft to ease access to SkyDrive and to understand Outlook.com aliases; Skype video collection tripled after a July 2012 capability.
- Microsoft statement: it provides data only for specific accounts under legal process and gives no direct access, but when upgrading products it may need to maintain the ability to respond to lawful requests; general counsel Brad Smith asked the Attorney General for permission to disclose more. Steve notes only Google offers SMTP encryption among major providers, so mail is exposed upstream.
- Clapper's letter said his testimony was erroneous because he did not think of Section 215 of the Patriot Act.
- DEF CON organizers asked federal agents to stay away; NSA director Keith Alexander still speaks at Black Hat.
- Report that Russian Federal Guard Service is buying electric typewriters; Steve and Leo are skeptical of its value.
- Adblock Plus "acceptable ads": only unobtrusive text ads are allowed, a community of over 27,000 reviews them, small sites are free, large companies pay, and users can turn the feature off in options.
- Lavabit encrypts stored incoming mail with the user's public key and decrypts with a key derived from the login password, so it protects against compelled disclosure of stored mail but not mail in transit or outgoing; Snowden reportedly pressed Greenwald to use PGP.
- iOS 7 beta breaks Google Authenticator, which has not been updated since 2011; HDE OTP and Authy are compatible alternatives.
- BitTorrent Sync reached beta with Android support; BitTorrent's blog shows a personal cloud using a Raspberry Pi with ownCloud. Steve still wants the protocol published.
- Hemlis raised over 150 percent of its goal in under two days; Network Solutions suffered a DDoS outage.
- Steve's tinfoil model: tap upstream to see which providers someone uses, then demand decryption from them; so avoid default big providers, treat anything not pre-encrypted as exposed, and trust open protocols and open intent rather than only open source. GRC's books are encrypted with Jungle Disk before going to Amazon S3.
- GPG makes end-to-end email encryption practical (Leo uses a 4096-bit key and a key server).
- NSA deputy director Chris Inglis told Congress analysts look two or three hops from a suspect, which covers most people given roughly 4.7 degrees of separation; Alexander's approach in Iraq was to collect the whole haystack.
- Members of Congress, including the Patriot Act's author, are critical and the metadata provisions are up for renewal in 2015; Steve recommends following the EFF.

# Other segments

- Two crowdfunded projects (SmushBox progress and "Algorithm" hacker film on Indiegogo).
- SpinRite listener rescue of an unbootable laptop with an SSD; a listener praising transcriber Elaine Farris.
- Asiana crash prank-names news blunder as an example of media unreliability.
