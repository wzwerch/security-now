# Summary

The Drupal remote-code-execution flaw (Drupalgeddon2) landed the day after last week's show, covering Drupal 6, 7 and 8; a measurement by SiteLock suggested only 18% of sites were patched, so Steve expects cryptominer-driven exploitation. Cloudflare launched its public resolver at 1.1.1.1 (with 1.0.0.1 as a slower backup), promising to drop logs within 24 hours, supporting DNSSEC validation, aggressive negative caching and DNS over TLS/HTTPS; GRC's DNS Benchmark showed it the fastest non-ISP resolver on Steve's connection, and he invites listeners to add it and report results. Microsoft's January Meltdown patch for Windows 7 x64 had flipped a page-table permission so all kernel memory was accessible to user-mode programs until a late-March fix (KB4100480). The iOS camera QR code spoof (displayed domain differs from the destination) stayed unpatched through iOS 11.3, which did remove iPhone throttling unless needed and added a battery health view. A researcher audited 83 VPN apps and found 17 leaking IPs through WebRTC. The US Department of Defense launched a bug bounty via HackerOne. Steve criticizes BIMI, an industry effort to show verified brand logos in email without a solution to logo spoofing. Also covered: dorm-room mining, Google ending new mining extensions in the Chrome Web Store, and the MyFitnessPal breach of 150 million accounts. The main section reviews ProtonMail: Swiss, open-source, browser/mobile client-side encryption with 4096-bit RSA keys protected by a mailbox password, PGP interoperability, and password-protected messages for outside recipients.

# Key points

- Drupalgeddon2: input-validation flaw tied to special handling of values beginning with a hash; Drupal 6 got a patch despite end of life; Drupal is the number two CMS; patch immediately.
- Cloudflare 1.1.1.1: privacy-first logging policy, DNSSEC-aware with negative trust anchors for misconfigured zones, DNS over TLS on port 853; encryption of the last mile needs client support (Firefox 60 pilot).
- DNS Benchmark: Add/Remove nameservers button, left-click bars for timings; Steve may update it to include 1.1.1.1.
- Windows 7 x64 kernel memory exposure: found by Ulf Frisk while building PCILeech; not remotely exploitable but trivial for local programs; check for KB4100480.
- iOS QR code URL spoofing disclosed after 90 days without a fix; iOS 11.3 fixed 43 vulnerabilities about half in WebKit; devices may need a manual update nudge.
- WebRTC leak test at ip.voidsec.com; HideMyAss was among leakers, Hotspot Shield was not.
- DoD bounty: scope covers a public-facing travel system; eligibility restricted to US taxpayers and several allied nationals; earlier programs found thousands of valid bugs.
- BIMI spec itself says verification and reputation mechanisms are out of scope, so logos in email do not stop phishing.
- Google Chrome Web Store: new mining extensions banned, existing ones delisted by late June since about 90% hid their mining.
- MyFitnessPal breach: usernames, emails and hashed passwords of about 150 million users; change reused passwords.
- ProtonMail design: keys generated in the browser, private key encrypted under the mailbox password so the provider cannot recover mail, TLS with strong configuration, optional two-password login, encrypted contacts, PGP export, self-destructing password-protected messages; Leo prefers PGP and FastMail.
