# Summary

The episode opens with Microsoft's full-screen Windows 7 end-of-support notice and a reminder that Google's Sensorvault location archive was used for geofence warrants in a Milwaukee arson investigation, returning almost 1,500 device identifiers. Steve explains Google's Verified SMS for Android Messages, a metadata-based authentication layer using key agreement and HMACs that proves sender identity without encrypting the SMS. He covers the Senate Judiciary hearing where Apple and Facebook were told to find a way to give law enforcement access, and Facebook's open letter refusing backdoors, adding his own view that lawful-intercept designs should be carefully specified. iOS 13.3 adds FIDO2 hardware key support in Safari, and December's Patch Tuesday fixed a Win32k privilege-escalation bug used with a Chrome flaw. The technical centerpiece is Keyfactor's finding that one in 172 RSA certificates share a prime factor with another, almost entirely from entropy-starved IoT devices, making them factorable via GCD. After quick bits, Steve announces he is turning SQRL over to the community and returning to SpinRite. He closes with Plundervolt, which uses Intel's undocumented voltage-control interface to induce faults inside SGX enclaves.

# Key points

- Windows 7: a full-screen end-of-support notice recommends Windows 10 on a new PC, with a "Don't remind me again" option.
- Sensorvault: geofence warrants covering about 29,000 square meters across four arson incidents yielded 1,494 device IDs; Google wipes stored location history if Location History is turned off. Leo criticises overbroad warrants.
- Verified SMS: businesses register with Google and publish keys; Messages devices hold keys; sender and receiver derive a shared key (Diffie-Hellman style), compute an HMAC over the SMS and Google compares them, showing a verified-sender notice. Needs data connectivity; supports few businesses initially.
- Encryption war: Senator Graham told Apple and Facebook to find a way or Congress will; Facebook (WhatsApp/Messenger heads) refused to build backdoors; AG Barr cited child abuse and terrorism; Steve notes group-chat designs already make silent added listeners technically possible and wants any mandate carefully written.
- iOS 13.3 Safari supports FIDO2 keys over NFC, USB and Lightning (YubiKey and similar).
- December Patch Tuesday: 36 flaws; a Win32k elevation bug exploited with a Chrome use-after-free sandbox escape (Chrome fixed it in November after Kaspersky's report).
- Keyfactor "Factoring RSA Keys in the IoT Era": of 75 million certificates, 1 in 172 shared a prime factor with another (versus 5 in 100 million from CT logs); computing the GCD of two moduli reveals the shared prime and both private keys. Fix: devices need proper entropy or should obtain keys from a trustworthy source.
- Plundervolt: a privileged attacker writes an undocumented voltage-offset register on Intel Core 6th-10th generation and some Xeon parts to cause faults in SGX enclave computation, enabling key recovery from AES-NI and memory-safety bugs; Intel shipped microcode that locks voltage to defaults. Requires ring 0 access. Steve notes BIOS/microcode updates from motherboard vendors now matter.
- Quick bits: New Orleans shut down computers and went offline after a Friday-13th attack (likely a phishing barrage); Mozilla will require 2FA for add-on developers; Microsoft Security Essentials updates end for everyone in January including ESU customers; Chrome 79 removes the option to show the www prefix and re-enabled code-integrity protection that crashes with some AV products (workaround switch exists).
- Clarification on the VPN-geddon story: unsolicited packets can reach the virtual interface unencrypted over the LAN, which is how the probing worked.
- SQRL handoff: Steve declares SQRL finished and community-maintained (SQRL Explained PDF downloaded nearly 41,000 times); a community OAuth 2 provider for SQRL using the Ask feature is announced; Leo plans to try it on the TWiT Discourse forum.

# Other segments

- Steve will move to Windows 10 early next year and is switching focus to SpinRite 6.1, with pre-release builds for owners.
- Schedule: Christmas week show moves to Monday, a decade-of-hacking retrospective follows, then a best-of.
