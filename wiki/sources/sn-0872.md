# Summary

Steve follows up on the May 2022 Microsoft domain controller breakage: Microsoft shipped an out-of-band fix within about a week, distributed via the Update Catalog, WSUS and Configuration Manager rather than Windows Update, so CISA can re-list the actively exploited flaw. He then covers Clearview AI: an Illinois BIPA settlement restricting sales to private parties, a UK fine of about 7.5M pounds, and its use by Ukraine to identify the dead and support war-crimes cases, with a long interview of the CEO. Pwn2Own Vancouver 2022 awarded about $1.155M, with Microsoft Teams, Windows 11 and Ubuntu Desktop the main losers. The US DOJ revised its Computer Fraud and Abuse Act charging policy to exempt good-faith security research. NCC Group showed a Bluetooth Low Energy relay attack on Kwikset Kevo smart locks that bypasses the crypto entirely. Listener segments include Safari ad-blocking, Voyager 1's odd telemetry (Steve jokes about a simulation boundary) and a Forbes piece arguing quantum annealing could bring "Q-Day" sooner. The main segment relays Advanced Intel's analysis that the Conti brand was deliberately dissolved into a decentralized network, with the Costa Rica attack serving as a publicity diversion.

# Key points

- Microsoft out-of-band update fixes the May 2022 domain-controller authentication failures; distributed only through the Update Catalog/WSUS/MECM. Steve's motto: patching is everything, better to patch and regret than not patch.
- Clearview AI: aims for 100 billion images (about 20 billion now); BIPA settlement bars sales to US private companies and Illinois law enforcement for five years; UK ICO fine of roughly 7.5M pounds and deletion order; Australia and Canada earlier ordered deletion.
- Clearview in Ukraine: six agencies, about 400 investigators, used to identify dead soldiers and for war-crimes evidence; vendor claims vetting, 2FA, audit logs and revocable cloud access. Steve questions whether public photos imply consent for global searchable face databases.
- Pwn2Own Vancouver 2022: 21 attempts, 3 failed, 17 contestants; targets included Teams, Windows 11, Ubuntu Desktop, VirtualBox, Firefox, Safari and the Tesla Model 3 infotainment system.
- DOJ policy: federal CFAA prosecutions need prior consultation for good-faith security research; state laws and civil suits remain a risk. Follows the Supreme Court ruling that authorized users misusing access do not violate CFAA.
- Kwikset Kevo BLE relay attack (NCC Group): relaying discovery pings lets an attacker unlock from far away; BLE lacks proximity assurance, and fixes (GPS checks, motion checks, time-of-flight radios) are imperfect.
- Safari has no good uBlock Origin equivalent; Leo suggests Firefox or DNS-level blocking.
- Voyager 1 AACS telemetry returning invalid data while the craft operates normally; RTG power dropping about 4 W per year.
- Forbes "Q-Day" article: claims annealing-based factoring with noisy qubits could shorten the timeline; Steve cannot judge but finds it worth heeding and urges quantum readiness.
- Conti dissolution (Advanced Intel): the leak site and negotiation infrastructure went dark on May 19, 2022. Causes: toxic branding after backing the Russian government, FSB pressure, internal Ukrainian-sympathizer leaks, and OFAC sanction risk that stopped victims paying. Members regrouped as autonomous brands (KaraKurt, BlackBasta, BlackByte), affiliates inside other gangs (BlackCat, Hive, HelloKitty, AvosLocker) and acquisitions.
- The Costa Rica attack was reportedly a publicity operation with a ransom demand far under $1M; the new model favors pure data exfiltration over encryption lockers.
