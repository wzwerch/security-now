# Summary

Steve reports that NASA restored contact with Voyager 2 on 4 August by "shouting" a reorientation command, 37 hours round trip. He then presents Tenable CEO Amit Yoran's public LinkedIn criticism of Microsoft for taking over 120 days to partially fix a cross-tenant Azure flaw that exposed application secrets (Microsoft declared it fully fixed within days of the post), and Bruce Schneier's analysis of the Chinese theft of an Azure signing key used to forge tokens for government email, which Schneier traces back to SolarWinds-era source code theft. A UK paper showing a deep-learning classifier recovering laptop keystrokes from audio at 95 percent accuracy (93 percent over Zoom) illustrates that attacks always get better. Listener questions cover TOTP clock tolerance, browser choice and how to protect accounts when email recovery is the weak link. The main topic revisits Global Privacy Control (GPC): Steve's Firefox GPC signal triggered a Yahoo/TechCrunch pop-up asking him to allow sale and sharing of personal data, which led him to explain Yahoo's "technical identifiers", how GPC covers only sale and sharing under California law, and which states and browsers support it.

# Key points

- Voyager 2 was recovered after a command sent from the Canberra Deep Space Network antenna turned its antenna back toward Earth; the carrier signal had been detected on 1 August.
- Amit Yoran (Tenable): Microsoft took more than 90 days for a partial Azure fix, a complete fix was promised for 28 September, and only after his public post did Microsoft say the issue was fully addressed; he described a "culture of toxic obfuscation". Google Project Zero data cited 42.5 percent of zero-days since 2014 as Microsoft products.
- Senator Ron Wyden asked CISA, DOJ and the FTC to hold Microsoft accountable over the Chinese theft of an Azure signing key used to forge authentication tokens; detection was possible only because an agency paid for extra logging, a policy Microsoft later reversed.
- Schneier: Azure accepted an expired key and the key should have stayed in a hardware security module; he suspects the compromise traces back to SolarWinds and stolen source code, and that attackers increasingly target infrastructure providers.
- UK researchers' acoustic side-channel attack on keyboards reached 95 percent accuracy with a nearby phone and 93 percent via Zoom; Steve expects deep learning to boost many older side-channel attacks.
- TOTP verifiers commonly tolerate one adjacent time step, which explains why two slightly different codes can both work.
- Steve's advice on authentication: use a password manager, an authenticator app and passkeys where available, but recognize that email-based recovery is the "total authentication bypass" so securing the email account is paramount.
- GPC is a browser-sent HTTP/DOM signal asserting a person's legal right to opt out of sale and sharing of personal data; California, Colorado and Connecticut require sites to honor it, Virginia does not.
- Yahoo (owner of TechCrunch) responded to the signal with a pop-up and a second "limit use of sensitive data" switch; its "technical identifiers" definition covers cookies, device IDs, hashed emails, IP addresses and fingerprints, cross-device linking and more.
- GPC is supported natively by Firefox, Brave and DuckDuckGo's browsers, and via extensions (Abine, Disconnect, OptMeowt, Privacy Badger) in Chrome; globalprivacycontrol.org shows whether the signal is detected.
- GPC differs from the earlier Do Not Track flag because it carries legal weight in some states; it covers sale and sharing, not all use.
