# Summary

Steve opens with the first "Video of the Week", a clip of a card-reader-controlled gate that can be opened from the inside by simply reaching through the wire and pushing the handle. The main news is a pair of Microsoft failures. WithSecure found that Office 365 Message Encryption (OME) uses AES in Electronic Codebook (ECB) mode, which leaks structure and repeated patterns without any need for the key; Microsoft paid the bounty but declined to fix it. Ars Technica then reported that the Windows blocklist of known-vulnerable drivers, which Microsoft promoted as automatically updated through Windows Update, had in practice not been updated for almost three years, leaving "Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver" attacks open even with HVCI enabled. Smaller items: an RSS feed for Microsoft security updates, a new passkeys.dev site, Cloudflare mitigating a 2.5 Tbps Mirai-variant DDoS, Signal dropping SMS/MMS support on Android, new local Administrator account lockout policy in Windows, an unauthorised early disclosure of a Fortinet authentication bypass, a planned White House IoT security label, and a $29.2 million FinCEN fine on Bittrex. After SpinRite and ZimaBoard updates and listener feedback, the main topic is a new W3C draft for a well-known URL (`/.well-known/change-password`) that sends users straight to a site's password-change page, which Steve calls a useful baby step rather than true automation.

# Key points

- OME encrypts with AES-ECB, so identical plaintext blocks give identical ciphertext blocks; the classic encrypted-penguin image shows how patterns survive, and WithSecure showed the word "Fail" remained legible in an encrypted image. Attackers holding multiple messages (archives, backups, a breached mailbox) can infer content offline without keys; bring-your-own-key does not help. Microsoft was told in January 2022 and chose not to fix; WithSecure advises not relying on OME for confidentiality.
- Steve explains that chaining modes such as CBC fix the problem with a non-secret, unique initialization vector, so ECB is simply a design failure he says indicates incompetence and complacency from Microsoft's dominant market position.
- BYOVD: attackers with admin rights install a legitimately signed driver that has a known flaw, then exploit it for kernel access. Cited uses include Lazarus (a Dell driver), BlackByte (MSI Afterburner), a Genshin Impact anti-cheat driver and AvosLocker (an Avast driver).
- Researchers Will Dormann and Peter Kalnai (ESET) showed that Windows 10 with HVCI/memory integrity still loaded known-bad drivers, and that the blocklist had not been refreshed since 2019; Microsoft acknowledged a servicing-pipeline "synchronization gap" and released a tool to apply the list manually, while a senior Microsoft executive reacted defensively.
- Microsoft finally added an RSS feed for its security update guide.
- Passkeys.dev launched as the community site for the passkey standard, listing only a few demo sites (WebAuthn.io, passkeys.io, Yubico's demo); Leo notes support in iOS 16.
- Cloudflare's Q3 2022 report: a 2.5 Tbps DDoS from a Mirai variant aimed at the Wynncraft Minecraft service.
- Signal removes its legacy SMS/MMS fallback from Android, a remnant of its TextSecure origins.
- October 2022 Windows updates add a policy to lock out local Administrator accounts after failed logins (baseline recommendation 10 attempts / 10 minutes / 10 minutes), enabled by default on new Windows 11 22H2 installs, plus password-complexity enforcement for new local admin accounts; an SMB equivalent is reportedly coming.
- Fortinet quietly patched an authentication bypass in its enterprise appliances, told customers privately, then saw details leak publicly while attacks were already underway.
- White House plans a cybersecurity seal (like a UL label) for IoT devices, starting with routers and home cameras; requirements and enforcement are unknown.
- FinCEN fined crypto exchange Bittrex $29.2 million for processing over 116,000 transactions (about $260 million) involving sanctioned parties and criminal markets, with two lightly trained staff monitoring 20,000 daily transactions.
- Well-known URIs: after robots.txt, the W3C/IETF `/.well-known/` directory (RFC 8615, 46 registered names) holds site metadata such as security.txt; Apple engineers Ricky Mondello and Theresa O'Connor proposed a `change-password` entry returning a redirect to the password-change page. Browsers and password managers could probe for it and enable a "change site password" button; a 404 means unsupported. It does not automate the change itself.
- Passkeys would make usernames and passwords obsolete; Leo raises recovery if a phone is lost, and Steve recalls SQRL's single-QR identity and old-plus-new identity rollover.

# Other segments

- Video of the week: a wire gate with proximity-card lock that opens by reaching through to the inside handle (shortcut grc.sc/gate); Leo mentions the poster's other security-fail humour.
- SpinRite 6.1: testers report 6 TB scanned in about 7.6 hours over SATA and 4 TB in 5.1 hours over USB (speed depends on the BIOS); 378 registered development testers; a success story about cloning a failing laptop drive; UEFI boot support prioritised before native USB/NVMe.
- ZimaBoard: its 14-pad header is a PC front panel (power, reset, LEDs), found via the maker's Discord; Steve uses a cheap external power/reset switch puck.
- Listener feedback: whether disabling cookies stops already-stored cookies from being sent (browsers differ); the wish for SpinRite to boot over UEFI.
- Books from Leo: "Hacker's Delight" and "xchg rax,rax", plus xorpd's x86 assembly video series.
