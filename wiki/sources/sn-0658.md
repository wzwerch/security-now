# Summary

Steve opens with Intel's final April 2018 microcode guidance for Spectre variant 2, which adds a "Stopped" category: some older chips (mostly more than five years old, often in closed systems) will never receive a fix. He then covers Russia's court push to block Telegram after it refused to hand encryption keys to the FSB, and a new US Senate attempt at mandated "lawful decryption". Oregon becomes the second state with its own net neutrality law, using a state-purchasing approach designed to survive legal challenge. Steve discusses a CyberScoop piece with Katie Moussouris on the flaws of large bug bounties and adds his own worry that huge rewards could motivate a planted, later "discovered" flaw. Follow-up on last week's 1.1.1.1 discussion: the DNS Benchmark was updated for the first time in a decade, and Quad9 and Google DNS support DNS over TLS while Cloudflare offers DNS over HTTPS. The main topic is the industry deprecation of TLS 1.0 and 1.1 ahead of the PCI June 30, 2018 deadline.

# Key points

- Intel's April 2 microcode guidance adds a "Stopped" status: no Spectre variant 2 microcode for certain older, mostly closed-system CPUs; most others are in production. Steve will update his InSpectre tool to show whether microcode exists for a chip.
- Steve praises Dell for patching even older laptops, and notes the OEM supply chain for desktop patches is as poor as for old Android phones.
- Russia's telecom regulator asked a Moscow court to block Telegram after the Supreme Court ordered key handover to the FSB; Telegram refused and sued at the European Court of Human Rights. A national block list entry is expected.
- Feinstein and Grassley, with Deputy AG Rosenstein, prepare another US attempt at legislation forcing lawful-access capability in consumer encryption; Steve expects something to eventually pass.
- Oregon signs a state net neutrality law (after Washington) conditioning state ISP purchases on neutrality, with an exception where an ISP is the sole provider.
- Windows 10 Spring Creators Update adds "Free Up Space Now" as a replacement for Disk Cleanup; the rollout was delayed.
- Windows 7 / Server 2008 R2 April Patch Tuesday updates have known issues (SMB memory leak, blue screen on CPUs without SSE2).
- Bug bounties are heavily concentrated (HackerOne data: 5% of hunters found 23% of flaws); Moussouris argues big bounties can distort incentives away from prevention. Steve adds the scenario of an insider-planted subtle flaw collected by a confederate.
- UK NCSC announces a government vulnerability-coordination pilot with Moussouris's firm.
- DNS Benchmark updated with 1.1.1.1, 1.0.0.1 and 9.9.9.9; Steve now says his slow 1.0.0.1 result was an outlier. Quad9 filters malicious domains; DNS over TLS (RFC 7858, port 853) is supported by Cloudflare, Quad9 and Google; Cloudflare provides open-source DoH proxy clients; pfSense supports DNS over TLS.
- Firefox 60 includes DoH support (needs configuration at first).
- European grid clocks lost six minutes because of a Serbia-Kosovo grid dispute lowering the mains frequency; operators corrected it by running slightly above 50 Hz for a month.
- TLS deprecation: SSL Pulse shows 88% of 150k sites still support TLS 1.0 and 85% 1.1; Cloudflare sees about 11% of traffic on 1.0. PCI requires dropping 1.0 by June 30, 2018; DigiCert, KeyCDN, Fastly, Cloudflare (June 4), GitHub and Office 365 (Oct 31) are retiring 1.0/1.1. Old tools such as some cURL builds broke when GitHub switched.
- Android before version 5 (about 18% of devices) only supports TLS 1.0. Steve: the gap between "nearly dead" and "dead" is why deadlines matter.
