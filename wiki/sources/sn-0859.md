# Summary

WordPress took the rare step of force-updating more than three million sites running UpdraftPlus after a missing permission check (CVE-2022-0633) let any logged-in user download site backups. ThreatFabric reported Xenomorph, a new Android banking trojan spread through a Google Play dropper called Fast Cleaner (Gymdrop family) that uses accessibility-service overlays and SMS/notification interception against customers of 56 European banks; Steve's advice is to let new apps age and install fewer of them. South Korean researchers published a method to decrypt files hit by early Hive ransomware because its homemade cipher reused slices of one 10 MB XOR keystream. Dan Petro of Bishop Fox showed that pixelated text can be recovered by guess-and-compare, so only solid black bars should be used for redaction. A French father's illegal phone jammer, meant to stop his teenagers using phones at night, knocked out a town's mobile service. Adobe's Magento fix from the previous week was incomplete and a second CVE (CVE-2022-24087) followed. The DDoS on Ukrainian ministries and banks used mostly HTTPS requests, which cannot be spoofed but exploit the high compute cost per dynamic web page. The main story is the 3 February 2022 BGP hijack of Kakao-hosted JavaScript that let attackers steal about 1.9 million dollars from KLAYswap users, explained via longest-prefix matching and the trust-based design of BGP.

# Key points

- UpdraftPlus backup plugin (versions 1.16.7 to 1.22.2, from March 2019): missing permissions check let any authenticated user trigger sending of backups; WordPress force-updated about 783,000 sites on 16 February and 1.7 million the next day; fixed in 1.22.3 (then 1.22.4).
- Xenomorph: related to the Alien trojan; abuses accessibility privileges for overlay injection, requests a list of installed packages, targets banks in Spain, Portugal, Italy and Belgium plus crypto wallets; the Play Store carries about 3.48 million apps with roughly 3,700 added daily; the dropper app had 50,000 installs.
- Hive ransomware-as-a-service (80/20 affiliate split, hundreds of victims by late 2021): a 10 MB master keystream with per-file offsets stored in file names allowed recovery of over 95 percent of the key material without the attacker's private key; the lesson is that homemade crypto and reused XOR keystreams fail, and new versions will fix it.
- Redaction: pixelation and blur are reversible by brute-force matching of rendered guesses (Depix, Bishop Fox search); use opaque black bars.
- Illegal RF jammer used by a parent disrupted a French town from midnight to 3 a.m.; penalty up to 30,000 euros and six months in France; also illegal in the US.
- Magento / Adobe Commerce: CVE-2022-24086 patch was bypassed (reproduced by Positive Technologies), leading to CVE-2022-24087, another pre-authentication RCE rated 9.8.
- Ukraine DDoS: traffic about three orders of magnitude above normal, 99 percent HTTPS (per CrowdStrike); TCP handshake prevents spoofing, so a large botnet is needed, but modern dynamic sites are expensive per request, making request floods effective.
- KLAYswap incident: attackers announced a more specific /24 route inside Kakao's /23 (AS38099) via a spoofed AS9457 (Dreamline) announcement, served malicious kakao.min.js from a lookalike server with a freshly issued TLS certificate for two hours on 3 February, and diverted 407 transactions across 325 wallets (about 2.2 billion won) to Orbit Bridge and FixedFloat.
- BGP background: network prefixes, longest-prefix match, autonomous systems and route advertisement; trust in announcements remains the weakness and real attacks are far rarer than accidental leaks.
