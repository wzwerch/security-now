# Summary

The EU's mandatory "Chat Control 2.0" scanning proposal is off the table after the withdrawn October vote, leaving the existing voluntary regime to become permanent, although a misleading blurb about "endorsement without debate" required Steve to read Council records to untangle it. Microsoft will include Sysinternals Sysmon natively in Windows 11 in 2026, and Chrome Canary gains native vertical tabs. Steve notes Pentagon spending on AI-driven offensive cyber startup Twenty, a GAO warning about military personnel's digital footprints, and HiddenLayer's EchoGram technique, where appending a string such as "=coffee" flips AI guardrail verdicts. Researchers enumerated roughly 3.5 billion WhatsApp accounts because Meta applied no rate limiting; Cloudflare's 18 November outage is explained as an oversized bot-management feature file. A UK parliamentary committee argues software vendors should be liable for the economic damage of insecure products after the Jaguar Land Rover incident. The main topic is Wisconsin and Michigan bills that would require adult sites to block all VPN users, with the EFF's critique and UK calls to age-gate VPNs; Steve argues the real answer is location-independent, privacy-preserving age verification and that VPN bans cannot work.

# Key points

- Chat Control: Denmark withdrew the 14 October Council vote; a leaked 5 November meeting record shows member states accepting voluntary scanning only (the interim regulation expires April 2026), while the Commission may revisit mandatory scanning later. France complained of being a hostage to data-protection constraints; Leo notes GrapheneOS plans to leave France over police demands.
- Mark Russinovich announced native Sysmon (kernel-driver based logging of process creation, network connections, DNS queries and more) as an optional Windows 11 feature enabled via Windows features and installed with a single command, bringing official support. Steve hopes attackers cannot abuse it.
- Chrome Canary can show tabs to the side (vertical tabs), as Firefox has natively.
- Forbes: startup Twenty (XX) has a Cyber Command contract of up to 12.6 million dollars for AI agents doing automated offensive cyber operations, backed by In-Q-Tel; Steve sees AI as a generic accelerant for both attack and defence.
- GAO report: public data on US military personnel creates operational and family risk; the slogan is updated to "Loose tweets sink fleets".
- HiddenLayer EchoGram: guardrails built as text classifiers or "LLM-as-a-judge" share training-data weaknesses; flip tokens (for example "=coffee") appended to a prompt injection make the guardrail return a benign verdict while the target LLM still follows the payload, and can also cause false alarms. Steve calls LLMs inherently exploitable and doubts guardrails can ever be complete.
- WhatsApp enumeration: Austrian researchers queried contact discovery at about 100 million numbers an hour, collecting 3.5 billion phone numbers, profile photos for about 57% and text for about 29%; nearly half the numbers from the 2021 Facebook leak were still active, and reused X25519 keys were observed. A 2017 warning had been ignored; Meta has now added rate limiting.
- Cloudflare 18 November outage (about 11:20 UTC to 14:30 for core traffic, fully normal by 17:06): a database permission change made the Bot Management feature file double in size, exceeding a software limit and crashing traffic routing; initially suspected to be a huge DDoS. Steve praises Cloudflare's transparency and notes growing infrastructure complexity and brittleness.
- UK Business and Trade Committee: recommends software developer liability, incentives for cyber resilience and mandatory incident reporting after Jaguar Land Rover and retailer ransomware; the BSA lobby opposes liability, the EU Cyber Resilience Act allows fines up to 2.5% of global revenue, and the NCSC's software security code of practice is still voluntary. NCSC CTO frames insecurity as a negative externality.
- Wisconsin AB 105 / SB 130 (passed the Assembly) and a Michigan bill ("Anticorruption of Public Morals Act", not advancing) would force sites with substantial adult content to block VPN and proxy IP ranges, and the Michigan text would also oblige ISPs to detect and block VPNs and ban their sale (fines up to 500,000 dollars). EFF points out harms to businesses, students, journalists and abuse survivors, the over-broad "harmful to minors" definition, and that workarounds (own servers, open proxies, Tor) remain. Texas residents already bypass the earlier law via VPNs.
- UK: VPN apps topped download charts after the Online Safety Act's age checks; the Children's Commissioner called VPNs a loophole, although the government has no plan to ban them. Steve warns of a flood of shady VPN apps and wants age verification independent of location (device-level attestation, W3C standards).
