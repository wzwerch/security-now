# Summary

The show opens with the supply-chain "picture of the week": a researcher bought the expired personal email domain of the sole maintainer of the npm package "foreach" and thereby gained control of a package with millions of weekly downloads. Steve then reviews a bad May 2022 Patch Tuesday, where CISA advised against installing the update on domain controllers because it broke authentication, even though it addressed an actively exploited PetitPotam-style NTLM relay flaw (CVE-2022-26925). Apple patched an actively exploited out-of-bounds write in the AppleAVD media decoder, and Google announced an Open Source Maintenance Crew within the OpenSSF effort. The Conti ransomware gang escalated against Costa Rica, doubling its demand and urging citizens to pressure their government. Trend Micro found 200+ "Facestealer" apps on Google Play, and F5 BIG-IP (CVE-2022-1388) came under mass exploitation as predicted. Steve corrected himself on post-quantum crypto (elliptic-curve is as vulnerable to Shor's algorithm as RSA; symmetric crypto is fine) and noted SpinRite's new back-end drivers are finished. The main segment covers the EU's 135-page proposed regulation that would require scanning private messages, including text for "grooming", which Steve considers dangerous, impractical and incompatible with end-to-end encryption.

# Key points

- npm "foreach" takeover: a maintainer let a personal email domain lapse; someone registered it and gained control of a package with ~6M weekly downloads and tens of thousands of dependents. Illustrates fragility of the open source supply chain.
- Patch Tuesday (May 2022): 75 flaws, 3 zero-days (Microsoft counts publicly disclosed as zero-day), 8 critical, 26 RCE. Update caused authentication failures on domain controllers; CISA temporarily removed CVE-2022-26925 from its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Researcher says the fix closes only one PetitPotam vector; others remain.
- Updates are now cumulative, no longer individually selectable; Steve is skeptical-but-curious about Microsoft Autopatch.
- Apple updated watchOS 8.6, tvOS 15.5 and macOS Big Sur for an actively exploited AppleAVD out-of-bounds write giving kernel code execution.
- Google's Open Source Maintenance Crew: dedicated engineers to help upstream maintainers harden critical projects; Google pledged $10B over five years for cybersecurity, $100M for foundations like OpenSSF. Steve is bullish on OpenSSF; its Package Analysis project found 200+ malicious PyPI/npm packages in a month.
- Conti ransomware vs Costa Rica: ministries crippled, state of emergency, ransom raised from $10M to $20M, 97% of 670GB leaked; gang called for overthrowing the government; US State Dept had offered a $10M bounty. Taxes had to be paid by hand at banks.
- Google Play: Trend Micro found 200+ Facestealer variants (embedded-browser Facebook credential theft) and 40 fake crypto-mining apps; Google removed over one million malicious apps in 2021. Steve calls the open-store model an intractable problem.
- F5 BIG-IP CVE-2022-1388 (CVSS 9.8, iControl REST unauthenticated command execution): added to CISA KEV, exploited for web shells and destructive file wiping; federal deadline May 31.
- Erratum: elliptic-curve crypto is not quantum-safe; Shor's algorithm threatens RSA, finite-field and elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman. Symmetric ciphers/hashes only need larger keys (Grover). Largest honest Shor demonstrations are tiny (15, 21; 35 failed in 2019).
- Listener points: "store now, decrypt later" justifies early planning, crypto agility is hard (DES still in SIM and payment cards), and NIST should not be rushed (a candidate signature scheme, Rainbow, was just broken).
- Listener on TSA pipeline security directives: operators must patch KEV-listed flaws on a deadline, yet SD02 is marked Sensitive Security Information; Steve and Leo dislike secret security standards.
- Cookie banners predate GDPR (ePrivacy directive); GDPR gave them teeth.
- EU proposal to prevent and combat child sexual abuse: would require detection of known CSAM, new CSAM and text "grooming" even in encrypted communications. Matthew Green called it the most terrifying document he had seen. Steve argues it is technically unworkable, mass-surveillance by design, and effectively ends meaningful E2E encryption; it is only a proposal, so he hopes it dies.

# Other segments

- SpinRite: the back-end hardware driver work is finished; two very old systems needed Ultra DMA disabled in BIOS; next step is the front-end rewrite on the new I/O abstraction (ready for future USB and NVMe drivers).
- Leo's cheap tethered selfie drone as a chat opener.
