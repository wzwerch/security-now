# Summary

Steve opens with Let's Encrypt, which passed two million certificates in under eight weeks after the first million, and which the EFF says mostly secures sites that were not on HTTPS before. He reacts to DNI Clapper's claim that the Snowden leaks pushed encryption seven years ahead, and to FBI Director Comey's hint that unlocking the San Bernardino iPhone cost more than his remaining salary (about $1.3 million), noting the FBI lost both court fights and dropped the New York appeal. A researcher found that Windows' signed regsvr32 utility can fetch and run remote scripts, sidestepping AppLocker. Opera's announced "free VPN" is really a browser proxy with gaps such as WebRTC. TeslaCrypt 4.1a is more evasive and appears in mass spam, while fake "Armada Collective" extortion emails demanding 10 bitcoin collected over $100,000 without any attack happening. Steve also covers US "cyberbombs" against ISIS, DNSSEC registrar support, the need to update Windows Update on fresh Windows 7 installs, PiVPN, and then answers listener questions (Firefox vs Chrome, hardware firewall vs NAT router, spam control, antivirus).

# Key points

- Let's Encrypt: second million certificates issued in under eight weeks; WordPress.com adopted it and uses shared multi-domain certificates.
- Clapper claimed Snowden accelerated encryption by roughly seven years; hosts are skeptical and note metadata remains valuable to intelligence agencies.
- FBI paid an unnamed party for an iPhone 5c unlock method (reported about $1.3 million); both legal precedents it wanted failed and the New York appeal was dropped after the passcode was reportedly found.
- AppLocker bypass: regsvr32 accepts a URL argument and is trusted and firewall-permitted by default, so remotely hosted script content can run despite whitelisting; interim mitigation is to remove outbound firewall permission for regsvr32/regsvr64; a fix could break legitimate use.
- Opera's browser "VPN" is an encrypted proxy; WebRTC and plugins are not routed and DNS may leak, so it is not a VPN in the system-wide sense.
- TeslaCrypt 4.1a: stronger obfuscation, anti-analysis, antivirus evasion, now in high-volume spam with lower ransom demands; advice is to remind colleagues not to open attachments.
- Armada Collective extortion emails reused one bitcoin address and amount; Cloudflare and Chainalysis saw over $100,000 paid with no actual attacks.
- NYT reported US offensive cyber operations against ISIS, publicly acknowledged as "cyberbombs".
- DNSSEC needs registrar support for DS records; Network Solutions lacked it, Hover offered it; Steve decided to move GRC's domain.
- Fresh Windows 7 SP1 installs need the Windows Update client itself updated manually before updates work (Steve's shortcut named wupup).
- PiVPN: a script that turns a Raspberry Pi into an OpenVPN server with simple client-certificate management.
- Q&A: Firefox vs Chrome (Steve stays on Firefox for add-ons but would put browsers in VMs; Chrome suits most users); hardware firewall and NAT router are effectively the same thing, while expensive appliances add extra security functions; spam is reduced by rotating an email address yearly and using aliases for trusted services, and Leo layers Gmail, FastMail with SpamAssassin and filters; free built-in antivirus is enough on Windows, none needed on Mac or phones, and consumer AV can weaken TLS trust.
- Marc Goodman's UPDATE acronym was discussed; Steve prefers "train your family" over "turn off your computer."
