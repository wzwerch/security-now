# Summary

Leo returns after a week away. Steve first discusses the Google Titan key and Yubico situation after talking with Yubico's founder. The news begins with a campaign that compromised over 200,000 MikroTik routers, initially in Brazil, through an authentication bypass patched back in April, and now injects Coinhive mining script into unencrypted error pages seen by users behind the routers. Reddit disclosed that an attacker intercepted SMS two-factor codes of employees and read backups and source code. WannaCry, whose kill-switch domain check evidently failed or was removed, stopped production at Taiwan's TSMC. A CSIS study found law enforcement underuses available digital evidence, undermining the push for encryption backdoors. Oracle's Dyn team reported BGP hijacks of authoritative DNS servers of payment processors, with long TTL values keeping forged answers cached after the hijack ended, which only DNSSEC would stop. Other items cover dissatisfaction with Windows 10 updates, Google's state-sponsored attack alerts for G Suite admins, Google's censored China search project Dragonfly, and SamSam ransomware's manual, high-ransom campaigns. The main topic is Jens Steube's (Hashcat author) PMKID attack, which lets an attacker crack a WPA2 password from a single captured frame without a client handshake.

# Key points

- Yubico/Google Titan: Google reported zero successful phishing of 85,000 staff using hardware keys; Steve still recommends Yubico; he and Leo note Titan was built in China.
- MikroTik: authentication bypass fixed a day after notification in April, yet hundreds of thousands remain unpatched; routers inject Coinhive into HTTP error pages (HTTPS cannot be altered), all tied to one site key. Moral: consumer and ISP routers must self-update; Leo notes some vendors charge subscriptions that include patching.
- Reddit incident (June 14-18): employees' cloud and code-hosting accounts were breached via SMS interception; read-only access to backups and logs; Reddit urged token-based 2FA.
- SQRL can send flags asking sites to disable recovery and non-SQRL authentication.
- WannaCry via EternalBlue hit TSMC after an infected tool was connected to the network; revenue impact estimated around 256 million dollars; Marcus Hutchins' kill-switch domain history recalled.
- CSIS report "Low-Hanging Fruit": most trouble for police is getting data from providers, training averaged 10-16 hours a year; FBI director Wray threatens legislation; Schneier endorsed the view.
- Oracle: BGP hijack of Amazon's authoritative DNS (April) and later of three US payment processors' DNS; forged long-TTL records poisoned recursive resolvers, DNSSEC is the only real defense.
- Windows 10 update survey by MVP Susan Bradley: low satisfaction, most want feature updates at most yearly; Microsoft's reply was boilerplate.
- G Suite admins can enable government-backed attack alerts, with optional account lock and password reset.
- Project Dragonfly: leaked plans for a censored Google search in China; Steve sees compliance with local law as inevitable.
- SamSam: manual intrusions via brute-forced exposed RDP, lateral spread, encryption at night including application files; over 5.9 million dollars earned since 2015, largest single payment 64,000 dollars, 74 percent of victims in the US.
- PMKID attack: the PMKID in a roaming-capable access point's RSN information element is an HMAC-SHA1 over the AP name and MAC addresses keyed with the PMK; capturing one frame enables offline GPU cracking, so only a long random WiFi password protects. WPA3 SAE should resist it.
