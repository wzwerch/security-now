# Summary

Steve calls Regin, disclosed by Symantec, the most sophisticated malware the show has covered: a modular, multi-stage Windows espionage framework with its own encrypted virtual file system, active since at least 2008, withdrawn in 2011 and reappearing in 2013 as a second version. Victims were mostly in Russia and Saudi Arabia and none in the US or UK, which suggests a Western intelligence origin; the dropper has never been recovered and the infection vector is unknown, although one machine pointed to an unknown Yahoo Messenger flaw. Components hide in the registry, NTFS extended attributes and the unused space after the last partition, all encrypted with a custom 64-bit-block RC5 variant, and later stages load through kernel rootkit hooks. Capabilities include keylogging, screen capture, mouse control, undeleted-file recovery, traffic sniffing, GSM base-station controller monitoring, hotel and airline records, Exchange parsing and covert exfiltration over ICMP and encrypted HTTP cookie values. The second topic is Let's Encrypt, the EFF, Mozilla, Cisco, Akamai, IdenTrust and University of Michigan project to launch a free, automated certificate authority in 2015. Steve read the 36-page ACME specification and explains how clients prove domain control, bind a key pair to a domain with a recovery code, obtain certificates and cheaply renew short-lived ones. He stresses it offers only domain validation, so it competes with cheap CAs but not with organisation or EV certificates, and he still plans to pay for EV. Other items include a correction that Apache or nginx on Windows were also exposed to the Schannel bug, his reaction to the Snowden documentary Citizenfour and a Utah bill threatening the NSA's water supply.

# Key points

- Regin: a modular framework built like an OS inside the machine; stage 0 dropper never captured, then kernel-driver stages decrypt successive stages from hidden stores; payloads were tailored to targets and included GSM base-station controller traffic monitoring.
- Hiding places: registry blobs, NTFS alternate data streams and extended attributes, and slack space past the last partition; SpinRite is aware of that slack area.
- Timeline: earliest samples from 2008, removal across the Internet in 2011, return in 2013 as v2; reports described it as likely a Western intelligence product, with the code authored in English, according to the co-host's research.
- Symantec distributed registry-key indicators; Kaspersky also confirmed it. Steve notes it is Windows-specific and says the level of resources implies a state actor comparable to Stuxnet but general-purpose.
- Leo and Steve note that once such malware is installed, encryption of email or disks does not help, because the attacker sees what the user sees.
- Correction to SN 481: Apache and nginx are only safe from MS14-066 if they run on non-Windows systems, since on Windows they use Schannel.
- Let's Encrypt: free and automatic domain-validated certificates through a new CA planned for the second quarter of 2015, with an agent that configures the web server; Steve expects servers like Apache to build it in.
- ACME protocol: the client generates an account key pair, names the domain, receives a challenge with a nonce and chooses a method; one method has the client publish CA-provided random data under a well-known acme-challenge path on the domain, the other uses a TLS connection with a self-signed certificate selected through SNI. Once proven, all later requests are signed with the account key, later binding attempts face higher proof requirements, and a recovery code allows re-proving control after key loss.
- The CA strips disallowed certificate fields (such as signing rights) and returns a renewal URL, enabling very short-lived certificates without revocation.
- DV vs OV vs EV: browsers visibly distinguish only EV and non-EV; Let's Encrypt cannot validate organisations because that needs human work, so EV certificates remain valuable and may become more so.
- Steve explains why GRC uses HTTPS (ShieldsUP needed the user's true IP past caching proxies, then e-commerce, then HTTPS-only pinning).
- Citizenfour: the Snowden documentary led Steve to a stronger respect for privacy as a right even for people who have nothing to hide; Leo mentions government dossiers on figures such as Martin Luther King.
- A Utah bill would bar state cooperation with federal data collection, which could end the NSA Bluffdale data center's water supply; reports cited heavy water and power use and repeated electrical failures.
- SpinRite warning about invalid partition for drive size appears after moving a drive between BIOSes; SpinRite 6.1 will no longer depend on the BIOS.
