# Summary

Steve starts with the 2023 Pwn2Own contest in Vancouver, where Synacktiv won more than half a million dollars plus a Tesla Model 3 by chaining bugs in Bluetooth, Tesla's gateway, VirtualBox, macOS, Ubuntu and Windows 11. GitHub rotated its RSA SSH host key after the private key was briefly exposed in a public repository. The UK National Crime Agency ran fake DDoS-for-hire sites and then unmasked one as a deterrent. Snyk counts more than 144,000 malicious packages found in open source registries, and the Kremlin ordered presidential staff off iPhones. Steve praises Zoom's maturing bug bounty program, explains the NUIT near-ultrasound inaudible voice attack through heterodyning, and notes Microsoft testing a built-in crypto wallet in Edge. The episode ends with a rare rant: Microsoft will throttle and then block mail from end-of-support or unpatched on-premises Exchange servers to Exchange Online, which Steve calls a revenue play built on a false security premise. Leo is about to leave on holiday, so guest hosts follow.

# Key points

- Pwn2Own Vancouver (CanSecWest): Synacktiv earned $530,000 and a Tesla Model 3; Tesla infotainment root via a Bluetooth chip bug earned a first-ever Tier 2 award; a TOCTOU race condition in Tesla's gateway; a three-bug VirtualBox escape; macOS, Ubuntu and Windows 11 privilege escalations. Star Labs placed second. Chinese teams no longer attend.
- GitHub replaced its RSA SSH host key on 24 March 2023 after exposure in a public repo. No compromise of systems or customer data; ECDSA/Ed25519 users unaffected. Steve: mistakes happen, rapid response matters.
- UK NCA (Operation PowerOFF) created fake DDoS-for-hire services to gather data on users and, after unmasking one, to chill the market.
- Snyk: over 6,800 malicious npm/PyPI libraries in the first quarter of 2023, 144,000+ identified in total across registries.
- Kremlin told presidential staff to drop iPhones by 1 April for Chinese Android or Rostelecom's Aurora OS, claiming iPhones are easier for Western services to hack; Steve notes the logic only makes sense as distrust of foreign closed tech.
- Zoom paid $3.9 million in bounties in FY2023 and over $7 million total via HackerOne, including about $480,000 at one live hacking event; adds a Vulnerability Impact Scoring System (VISS) beside CVSS.
- NUIT (Near-Ultrasound Inaudible Trojan), USENIX Security 2023: 16 to 20 kHz commands exploit microphone nonlinearity, effectively heterodyning two inaudible tones into a voice command. Tested on 17 devices; commands must be shorter than 0.77 s; Siri needed replayed user voice.
- Microsoft is testing a non-custodial crypto wallet in Edge, one of many shopping/finance features Steve and Ars Technica see as bloat.
- Microsoft's "Transport-based Enforcement System" will report, throttle (SMTP 450) and then block (550) mail from unsupported or unpatched Exchange servers (starting with Exchange 2007) into Exchange Online and Outlook.com. Steve argues the "inbound mail cannot be trusted" rationale is false and that it effectively revokes perpetual licences; Leo notes Exchange's long history of breaches (ProxyNotShell, Hafnium).
