# Summary

Steve first covers Entrust's reaction to Chrome's distrust decision: a letter from CEO Todd Wilkinson promising organisational changes and an FAQ stressing that existing certificates stay valid and private PKI products are unaffected. Steve notes Entrust could try to ride another CA's intermediate certificate, and compares how rival CAs marketed the opportunity; Sectigo overstated the urgency, while DigiCert described the facts accurately. He dismisses a Dark Reading story about a "Passkey Redaction Attack" as ordinary phishing in which a fake site simply omits the Passkey option, and answers listener questions on multiple Passkeys per site. Responding to listeners, he agrees that port knocking and Fail2Ban would blunt last week's OpenSSH flaw and draws the lesson that layered, dissimilar defences beat any monoculture. The main story is the Polyfill.io attack: a Chinese company, Funnull, bought the polyfill.io domain and GitHub account in February 2024 and served malicious JavaScript to mobile visitors of more than 380,000 sites that embedded it. Steve uses it to argue against loading third-party scripts into first-party pages and to call it another dodged bullet.

# Key points

- Entrust CEO letter and FAQ: acknowledges mis-issuance and unauthorised revocation delays, announces a cross-functional change control board and moves the CA compliance team into the broader compliance organisation; says roots after 31 October 2024 are distrusted by default in Chrome but certificates issued before then stay valid. Steve questions whether partner roots could help and whether Chrome would then block intermediates too.
- Sectigo (the renamed Comodo) wrongly implied immediate urgency; DigiCert's note was accurate. GRC's own DigiCert certificate was about to be replaced.
- "Passkey Redaction Attack" (eSentire): an adversary-in-the-middle phishing page hides the Passkey option and falls back to passwords, which is simply phishing and does not weaken Passkeys; Steve also grumbles about the "adversary in the middle" renaming of man-in-the-middle.
- Passkey multiplicity: the spec expects many Passkeys per account, but some sites allow only one; platform sync (Apple, Google, Microsoft) reduces pressure on sites to improve; Steve's practical advice remains a cross-platform password manager such as Bitwarden or 1Password.
- nuevoMailer correction: pronounced "nuevo"; GRC reply-to for list mail now set to securitynow@grc.com; 5,782 subscribers received this week's email.
- Port knocking would hide sshd from scanners and defeat the regreSSHion attack; Steve generalises to layered security, noting fixed-IP filtering between sites as the strongest layer.
- Fail2Ban (free, open source) scans logs such as auth.log and firewall-bans IPs after repeated failures; Steve says it would hinder timing-critical attacks like regreSSHion, though not widely distributed botnets. Leo uses similar blocking, including country blocks, on his Synology.
- Polyfill.io: polyfills fill in missing browser APIs; the service began with the Financial Times and Fastly. Funnull bought the domain and GitHub repo in February 2024; Sansec reported malware injection on 25 June (a redirect to a betting site via a fake analytics domain, targeting specific mobile devices and hours, skipping admins). Sansec first estimated 100,000 affected sites; Censys counted 384,773 hosts still embedding it, including .gov and major brands, plus 1.6 million hosts referencing related domains (bootcss.com, bootcdn.net, staticfile.net, staticfile.org). Namecheap suspended the domain on 27 June; Cloudflare and Fastly offered safe replacements, and Google blocked ads for affected sites.
- The original author, Andrew Betts, urged removal of polyfill.io in February and noted no modern site needs it. A developer (Amy Blankenship) traced odd Okta-library login errors in February to the polyfill change.
- Steve's lessons: loading third-party scripts gives them full first-party power; self-host libraries; block the domains locally; and treat such incidents like small earthquakes that warn of larger events.
