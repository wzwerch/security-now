# Summary

Steve traces the "no permission to shut down" Windows bug to Adobe Creative Cloud background services, which Adobe rolled back for Windows 7, and covers further February Windows 10 problems: the One Button PC Reset break and a rollup (KB4532693) that logs some users into a blank temporary profile, with the originals still on disk and restored by uninstalling the update. A critical flaw in the popular GDPR Cookie Consent WordPress plugin (over 700,000 sites) was fixed in version 1.83. A Nominet survey shows 88 percent of CISOs under heavy stress with a 26-month average tenure. Microsoft's ElectionGuard SDK, using homomorphic encryption and open source, had its first real pilot in Fulton, Wisconsin, with machines from VotingWorks. SQRL and SpinRite progress get brief mentions. The main topics are Check Point's attack that uses a malicious Philips Hue bulb to compromise the Zigbee bridge and the home network, and the SweynTooth family of Bluetooth Low Energy SoC bugs across at least seven chip vendors, many unpatchable in deployed products.

# Key points

- Windows shutdown-permission error: Adobe Genuine Monitor and related Creative Cloud services were the cause; Adobe rolled back for Windows 7; Windows 10 users can disable those services or sign out first.
- Windows 10 February updates: One Button PC Reset fails after the rollup (KB4524244) and Microsoft pulled that change; KB4532693 can leave users in a temporary profile because the updater forgets to remove it; uninstalling the update restores the profile. Steve advises not rushing monthly rollups.
- WordPress GDPR Cookie Consent plugin: improper access control on an AJAX endpoint let any visitor modify page content; fixed in 1.83 after the plugin was briefly pulled from the directory.
- Nominet CISO study (800 respondents): 88 percent moderately to extremely stressed, average tenure 26 months, many work 10 unpaid hours extra weekly, a quarter say boards do not accept breaches are inevitable, and most would take a pay cut for lower stress; Steve urges CISOs to keep perspective and educate executives.
- ElectionGuard: open SDK with homomorphic tallying (Josh Benaloh), paper ballots, tracking codes for voters, and third-party verifiers; pilot in Fulton, Wisconsin using VotingWorks machines; Smartmatic and Clear Ballot are partners and Dominion is exploring it.
- Philips Hue (CVE-2020-6007): a heap overflow in Zigbee handling on the bridge, reachable by a hijacked bulb with malicious firmware from over 100 meters; the user's re-pairing of a "glitching" bulb gives the attacker access to the bridge and then the LAN; fixed in bridge firmware 1935144040 via automatic updates.
- SweynTooth: 12 disclosed (more pending) BLE stack flaws across SoC SDKs from TI, NXP, Cypress, Dialog, Microchip, STMicroelectronics and Telink, in more than 480 products, enabling crashes, deadlocks or security-bypass of Secure Connections pairing; SoC vendors released patches but many end products lack any update path.
- Steve's concern: cheap Bluetooth products such as alarms will remain vulnerable forever, creating a market for hack-for-hire.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a wall-mounted rack appliance hung from a shallow rack.
- SQRL: a pure PHP SQRL implementation for the Laravel framework appears on GitHub.
- SpinRite: Steve rebuilt the FreeDOS kernel with a skipinit option and now maintains a fork of the Gravity newsreader broken by a year-2020 date check.
