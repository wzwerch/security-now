# Summary

Mozilla took its Firefox Send file-sharing service offline because criminals used it to host malware payloads; it will return with abuse reporting and mandatory Firefox Accounts, which leads Steve to predict that content-generating services may eventually need verified identity. Zoom fixed a Windows 7 and older remote code execution bug within a day after 0patch micropatched it; Steve objects to calling it a zero-day because no exploitation was known. The EARN IT Act was revised to hand power to individual states, which the EFF says threatens encryption and Section 230. Google will ban ads for stalkerware from August 2020. Two researchers published hardcoded telnet backdoors in C-Data fiber-to-the-home OLT equipment without notifying the vendor, which Steve calls irresponsible even though the backdoors look deliberate. Steve also covers Ben-Gurion research locating drone operators from flight paths, the Fraunhofer home-router study (less alarming than the press suggested, because the flaws are LAN-side), a joking SpinRite "bad sector" terminology item, and Google's open-sourced Tsunami vulnerability scanner.

# Key points

- Firefox Send (up to 1 GB anonymous, 2.5 GB signed in, end-to-end encrypted, expiring links) was used by FIN7, REvil, Ursnif and ZLoader operators; relaunch needs an abuse button and a Firefox Account.
- Zoom Windows client bug affected Windows 7 and older; found by an anonymous researcher who gave it to Acros/0patch, which wrote a micropatch for clients 5.0.0-5.1.2 and reported it; Zoom shipped a fixed client in a day. Many users were found on old Zoom versions despite auto-update.
- Steve defines zero-day as a flaw first seen through active exploitation and argues the press is drifting from that.
- EARN IT amendment replaces the federal commission with state-level liability for platforms (the "earn" is dropped), exposing services offering end-to-end encryption to suits and prosecutions.
- Stalkerware: Kaspersky counted 40,386 affected Android devices in 2018 and over 67,500 in 2019; Google Ads will prohibit ads promoting covert partner-tracking tools starting 11 August 2020, with warnings before suspension.
- C-Data OLT firmware (29 models, white-labelled under other brands) has hardcoded WAN-side telnet accounts, credential disclosure, root command execution, a crashable telnet server, cleartext management protocols and XOR-"encrypted" passwords. Steve stresses disclosure should protect users, with a private deadline for the vendor first.
- Ben-Gurion University: neural networks predicted a drone operator's location from the drone path with 78% accuracy in simulation.
- Fraunhofer analyzed 127 routers from seven vendors: 46 got no update in a year, many known vulnerabilities, rare use of exploit mitigations, embedded private keys and hardcoded credentials; AVM scored best. Steve notes the issues need LAN access, and suggests restricting router management to one LAN IP, a separate subnet, or VLANs; he prefers pfSense.
- Tsunami (Google, open source): a reconnaissance module (port fingerprinting derived from Nmap) plus a plugin-based vulnerability verification module using benign checks, tuned for very large networks and few false positives; initial plugins cover exposed Jenkins, Jupyter and Hadoop YARN interfaces and weak credentials via ncrack.
