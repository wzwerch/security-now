# Summary

Steve opens with Albania severing diplomatic relations with Iran after a July 2022 wiper and ransomware attack on government networks, the first time a cyber incident escalated to that level; Microsoft tied the attack to four Iranian groups under the Ministry of Intelligence and Security, and a second attack hit Albania's border management system days later. He then notes crypto heists are so frequent he rarely covers them and gives three typical examples. A White House "tech platform accountability" session produced six reform principles (competition, privacy, children, Section 230, algorithm transparency, anti-discrimination), which Leo agrees with in principle but distrusts in implementation. Dutch regulator Bert Hubert resigned from the interception-warrant board over weakened oversight in a new intelligence law. DeadBolt ransomware surged against QNAP NAS devices and MooBot is exploiting old and new D-Link router flaws. The main topic is EvilProxy, a turnkey phishing-as-a-service using reverse proxies to steal session cookies and bypass SMS, app-token and OAuth multifactor authentication; Steve's advice is extreme caution about links in email and a note that passkeys will eventually address this class of attack.

# Key points

- Albania expelled Iranian diplomats after concluding Iranian state hackers caused the 15 July 2022 attack; NATO, the US and UK backed the attribution; Microsoft named DEV-0842, DEV-0861, DEV-0166 and DEV-0133 as cooperating groups; a follow-on attack hit the border information system (TIMS) and disrupted six crossing points. Motive was linked to Albania hosting the exiled MEK opposition group.
- Crypto heist examples: New Free DAO flash-loan theft (~$1.5M), Gera token key leak (~$1.5M minted), Romanian police arrested a gang that re-defrauded fraud victims as fake recovery services; Leo cites Molly White's "Web3 Is Going Just Great" tracker and the Algorand/Hodlnaut exposure.
- White House listening session raised competition, privacy, youth mental health, mis/disinformation, illegal conduct and algorithmic discrimination; Leo and Steve worry about implementation, citing California's new laws (age-appropriate design, algorithm transparency) as examples of misguided regulation and the importance of Section 230.
- Bert Hubert resigned from the Dutch TIB board because the new intelligence law removes up-front (ex ante) review in favor of after-the-fact oversight and allows algorithmic analysis of bulk intercepts; he called it democratic backtracking.
- DeadBolt: asked 0.03 BTC per NAS; Censys counted infections rising from 2,144 to 19,029 between July and September 2022 via a new Photo Station zero-day; about 132 victims in an earlier batch paid ~$188,000; a QNAP forced firmware update broke decryption for some who paid, and Emsisoft built a repair tool; QNAP blamed outdated firmware.
- Steve's QNAP advice: do not expose NAS devices to the internet and consider alternative firmware.
- Censys State of the Internet report: misconfigurations account for about 60% of exposure problems versus 12% for software vulnerabilities.
- MooBot (Mirai variant) is recruiting D-Link routers using four vulnerabilities (CVE-2015-2051, CVE-2018-6530 and two 2022 flaws, all patched); users must keep firmware current.
- EvilProxy (also called Moloch), found by Resecurity, sells prepaid campaigns (about $150 for 10 days to $400 for 31 days) via a Tor portal, vetted by an administrator, with payment on Telegram.
- It reverse-proxies the real login page so the victim supplies password and one-time code while the proxy captures the resulting session cookie; targets include Google, Microsoft, Apple, Facebook, GitHub, GoDaddy, Dropbox, PyPI and npm, and it was linked to the August 2022 PyPI maintainer phishing that used JuiceStealer.
- Steve's analysis: ease of use matters more than possibility (compare Log4j); competition among such services will make them cheaper; SQRL's client-provided session solved proxy phishing and passkeys/WebAuthn are the eventual fix; meanwhile do not click email links.
