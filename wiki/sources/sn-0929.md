# Summary

Steve Gibson opens with a catch-up for Leo, who missed the previous week, including Ben-Gurion University research showing that power-LED flicker filmed with a phone camera can leak cryptographic secrets, and the MOVEit Transfer SQL injection mass-exploitation. News items cover DuckDuckGo's new Windows browser beta, Tor Browser 12.5, Opera One with a built-in AI assistant, the KasperskyOS phone operating system, a Russian law forcing foreign hosting providers to open local entities, SEC Wells notices for SolarWinds executives, a proposed U.S. Cyber Force, passkey support at Apple, and EU member states seeking a spyware exemption for journalists. A Dutch consumer study found that 2D photos fool face unlock on 26 phone models, with only Apple and Samsung largely holding up. Two class-action suits against Progress Software over MOVEit are discussed skeptically. After listener feedback (Sync.com recommendation, Syncthing, SpinRite recovery story), the main topic is Kaspersky's technical report on the TriangleDB implant used in the four-year zero-click iMessage campaign, and the Apple kernel fix CVE-2023-32434. Steve's conclusion is that Apple's opacity helps defenders and hides attackers alike, and that government demand for spyware is the real driver.

# Key points

- Ben-Gurion University researchers showed that fluctuations of a device's power LED, captured with a phone camera exploiting rolling-shutter scan rates, can reveal secret keys during crypto operations.
- MOVEit Transfer (Progress Software) SQL injection hit thousands of organizations; Steve argues that feeding user text into SQL queries is a policy error, not just a coding mistake.
- DuckDuckGo released a Windows browser beta with tracker blocking, cookie pop-up handling, a "Fire" data-clearing button and Duck Player for YouTube; it renders through the Windows WebView2 (Blink) API rather than forking Chromium.
- Tor Browser 12.5 redesigned the circuit display; Opera One launched with the Aria AI assistant and a new modular UI, which met a negative reception from existing users.
- Kaspersky previewed KasperskyOS, a minimal "hack-resistant" mobile operating system for the Russian market; Steve notes that security comes from limiting features.
- Roskomnadzor listed 12 foreign hosting providers (AWS, DigitalOcean, GoDaddy, Hetzner and others) that must set up a Russian entity under Federal Law 236-FZ or risk blocking.
- SolarWinds executives received SEC Wells notices over the 2020 breach, signalling personal accountability.
- The U.S. Senate Armed Services Committee will assess creating a dedicated Cyber Force military branch via the 2024 NDAA.
- Apple added passkey login for apple.com (needs iOS 17 / macOS Sonoma betas or later).
- France, Germany and the Netherlands push for an exemption in the EU Media Freedom Act allowing spyware use against journalists for "national security", against the PEGA committee's advice and a letter from over 60 organizations.
- Dutch consumer-group testing bypassed face unlock with a photo on 26 phones (14 Xiaomi); Apple and Samsung (apart from one low-end model) resisted.
- Google committed over $20 million to cybersecurity clinics at 20 U.S. universities.
- Two class-action suits against Progress Software (Louisiana OMV data, about six million records) were filed; Steve doubts their strength, noting Cl0p said it would delete government data.
- Operation Triangulation: a zero-click iMessage attachment exploit chain delivers TriangleDB, an in-memory implant (lost on reboot, self-removes after 30 days) that talks to command-and-control over HTTPS with protobuf messages and has 24 commands (file, process, keychain, geolocation, module loading); Kaspersky published indicator domains for DNS-log hunting.
- Apple fixed the kernel integer overflow CVE-2023-32434, credited to Kaspersky researchers; the name comes from a canvas-fingerprinting step that draws a yellow triangle.
- Eugene Kaspersky argues iOS is a closed black box that lets spyware hide for years; Steve agrees that Apple's hardening also blocks researchers' visibility.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: Apple Park as a literal walled garden; recap of the previous week's bicycle-lock picture.
- Steve's unexplained low-battery chirp in his studio; Leo suggests a "Chirp Finder" tool and mentions the TV series Avenue 5.
- Listener feedback: Jungle Disk no longer recommended (now corporate); Steve recommends Sync.com (zero-knowledge cloud storage, no Linux client), also uses Synology NAS pairs with @MAX SyncUp; Leo and Steve praise Syncthing; SQRL iOS client still being updated.
- SpinRite: a listener recovered photos with SpinRite 6.0; version 6.1 near completion, with v7 planned next.
- Leo's Disneyland trip and a short roller-coaster chat.
