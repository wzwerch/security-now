# Summary

The show begins with Chrome 103.0.5060.114, an emergency fix for the year's fourth exploited zero-day (CVE-2022-2294, a WebRTC heap overflow reported by Avast), and Firefox 102's new tracking-parameter stripping, which Steve likes in principle but finds error-prone because it must recognize specific parameter names; private-window stripping needs an about:config toggle. HackerOne disclosed that a recently hired employee used internal access to vulnerability reports and a sockpuppet researcher account to claim duplicate bounties; logging let them identify him within 24 hours. Listener feedback leads Steve to argue that putting operational technology on IP is the root of the exposure and that a low IP TTL could keep local-only gear from being routable across the internet, and to welcome the Matter smart-home standard. Light items include the 9-inch versus two 5-inch pizza area puzzle. The main story is Black Lotus Labs' report on ZuoRAT, a MIPS router implant, probably Chinese state-linked, that spent about two years hijacking DNS and HTTPS traffic on SOHO routers of remote workers and deploying Windows and Linux agents to reach corporate LANs.

# Key points

- Chrome zero-day (fourth of 2022): high-severity heap buffer overflow in WebRTC, CVE-2022-2294, exploited in the wild; Google disclosed little.
- Firefox 102 Query Parameter Stripping removes known tracking parameters (for example fbclid, mc_eid, mkt_tok) from URLs; the pbmode variant for private windows is off by default and can be enabled in about:config. Brave strips more thoroughly. Risk of site breakage.
- Insider threat at HackerOne: employee (April to June 2022) resubmitted customers' reported bugs under a sockpuppet account "rzlr" and sent threatening messages; a customer's suspicion triggered the probe, logs and payment tracing identified him, seven customers were contacted, he was fired and criminal referral considered. Steve stresses access logging and telling staff about monitoring.
- OT security debate: OT protocols such as Modbus and BACnet are old and unauthenticated; a Danish developer favors isolation behind VPN and HTTPS supervisory servers. Steve counters that moving OT onto routable IP is the core problem and recommends emitting packets with a very low TTL (2 or 3) for local-only equipment as a fail-safe.
- Smart homes: Steve keeps IoT devices on isolated guest Wi-Fi; Matter (royalty-free, backed by Amazon, Apple, Google and others, from the Connectivity Standards Alliance) should reduce fragmentation and enable hub-based security. Possible future episode "What's the Matter?"
- Passkeys: one listener asks why keep them on a PC; Steve says cross-device QR-based authentication with the phone may be enough and sensitive passkeys might stay on the phone.
- Pizza math: two 5-inch pizzas have far less area than one 9-inch pizza.
- ZuoRAT (Black Lotus Labs, Lumen): campaign active since at least late 2020, at least 80 targets seen, in North America and Europe; infects Asus, Cisco, DrayTek, NETGEAR and other SOHO routers (MIPS binary, a modified Mirai derivative), enumerates the LAN, captures traffic and can perform DNS and HTTPS hijacking.
- Follow-on agents: CBeacon (C++ for Windows), GoBeacon (Go, cross-platform) and Cobalt Strike; Chinese-language artifacts and Chinese cloud hosting for command and control; router-to-router proxying via UPnP hides infrastructure. Steve notes DNS hijack plus an issued certificate defeats TLS warnings.
- Advice: keep router firmware current, reflash with good firmware to clear implants, never expose UPnP on the WAN side, and disable remote management.
