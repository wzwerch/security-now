# Summary

The Spectre-NG leak from two weeks earlier resolves into two disclosed problems: Variant 3a (Rogue System Register Read, a Meltdown relative) and Variant 4 (Speculative Store Bypass, CVE-2018-3639, found independently by Google's Jann Horn and Microsoft's Ken Johnson), affecting AMD, ARM, IBM and Intel parts. Intel's microcode mitigation for variant 4 ships off by default because it costs roughly 2-8% performance; software can instead use speculation barriers (LFENCE) in sensitive code and reduced timer precision. Steve is unsure what happened to the other six rumored flaws. Earlier items: a Signal desktop XSS regression, DrayTek-router DNS hijacking by Roaming Mantis, a researchers' "voice squatting" attack on Alexa and Google Assistant, Chrome's coming "Not Secure" HTTP labeling, more GPON router botnet fighting, a Facebook Android permission mistake and BMW's 14 flaws.

# Key points

- Signal desktop (Windows/Linux): Juliano Rizzo's team found by accident that a shared link could inject HTML/JavaScript into the recipient's chat without interaction; the April 10 release had regressed an earlier fix; patched in 1.10.1 and the 1.11.0 beta. A macOS bug also let disappearing messages be recovered. The protocol itself was not at fault.
- Routers are a main target: Roaming Mantis (Kaspersky) used a DrayTek zero-day to change router DNS settings and push malware to Android, iOS and desktops; users should check router and DHCP DNS settings and update firmware.
- Windows 10 1803 received Intel microcode updates a day after the previous episode (KB4100347).
- Voice squatting: researchers registered sound-alike or longer-phrase skill names ("rap game" for "rat game"; "intraMatic opener") that Alexa and Google Assistant preferred; vendors said they would investigate. Leo argues the practical harm is limited; Steve sees an integrity problem with soft matching. Fix idea: block similar-sounding registrations by phoneme.
- Chrome 68 (July 2018) will label HTTP sites "Not Secure", Chrome 69 drops the green "Secure" word, and a later release will turn the warning red when entering data into HTTP forms.
- GPON routers: 17,000 infected by Satori, which scans port 3333 for Claymore Ethereum miners and redirects them to the attacker's wallet; a sixth botnet, TheMoon, uses a previously unknown flaw.
- Facebook's Android beta briefly asked rooted users for superuser access due to a coding error in an anti-fraud SDK; fixed.
- Keen Lab (Tencent) found 14 flaws in BMW vehicles (i, X, 3, 5, 7 series back to 2012), reachable via a fake GSM cell or USB; BMW is preparing firmware updates; infotainment-to-driving separation is by CAN gateways.
- Speculation background: any processor history that affects later timing leaks across process boundaries; the 90-day disclosure deadline for variant 4 was extended; Microsoft rates the risk low for now and keeps its speculative-execution bounty open until the end of 2018.

# Other segments

- Star Trek Discovery renewed; The Expanse may continue with Amazon; the "EM drive" thrust was traced to interaction with Earth's magnetic field in a German test.
- SpinRite on an SSD with contiguous bad regions: Steve explains SSD charge leakage and why read-only data can degrade; SMART may not reveal it. Optical media suggested for long-term archives.
- Steve confirms he still stores old PDAs in his empty refrigerator.
