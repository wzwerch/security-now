# Summary

Steve reviews Kaspersky's take on the July 2023 Patch Tuesday: 132 fixes, six exploited zero-days and three more Internet Explorer MSHTML bugs. Two issues were left with mitigation advice only, notably CVE-2023-36884, abused by the Russia-based Storm-0978 (RomCom) group against government and defense targets via Word documents; Steve traces the underlying cross-protocol file:// behavior back to a 2011 IE 9.0.2 fix and criticizes Microsoft for not closing it. He then covers WormGPT, a criminal GPT-J-based chatbot with no safeguards that can write polished business-email-compromise lures, and Microsoft's revocation of over 100 malicious but WHCP-signed drivers, which he attributes to the Windows NT driver trust model. Further items: Shutterfly and Discovery Channel added to Clop's MOVEit leak site, Google Play requiring D-U-N-S numbers for organization developer accounts, Meta blocking VPN workarounds for Threads in the EU, a UK insider jailed for swapping a ransom bitcoin address, and Twitter DM setting changes. Listener topics include why not to keep TOTP in the password manager, Tailscale on Synology, pagefile on SSD, and a SpinRite result restoring SSD read speed. The main segment starts a two-part series on satellite security, covering the Viasat KA-SAT attack, Hack-A-Sat 4 and U.S. military satellite history, setting up next week's IEEE paper "Space Odyssey".

# Key points

- July 2023 Patch Tuesday: 132 flaws, 9 critical, six exploited zero-days, including MSHTML bug CVE-2023-32046, Mark-of-the-Web and zone bypasses (CVE-2023-35308, 35336), SmartScreen bypass 32049, Windows Error Reporting privilege escalation 36874 and Outlook preview bypass 35311.
- CVE-2023-36884 (CVSS 8.3) exploited by Storm-0978/RomCom with Ukrainian World Congress lures; Microsoft offered a registry-based mitigation (FEATURE_BLOCK_CROSS_PROTOCOL_FILE_NAVIGATION) and Defender / Microsoft 365 Apps protections rather than a patch, fearing compatibility breakage.
- Storm-0978 also runs ransomware ("Underground") and credential-theft operations; Steve calls the unpatched legacy behavior a case of keeping a dangerous feature for compatibility.
- WormGPT, reported by SlashNext, is an uncensored GPT-J chatbot sold on cybercrime forums that produces convincing BEC and phishing emails; expect spam and phishing grammar tells to vanish.
- Microsoft added more than 100 WHCP-certified malicious drivers to the Driver.STL revocation list; the previous update was December 2022; Steve says the NT-era model of trusting drivers in ring 0 is unfixable.
- Clop's MOVEit leak list passed 200 victims, with Shutterfly and the Discovery Channel the newest.
- Google Play will require a D-U-N-S number from organizations creating developer accounts starting 31 August, expanding to existing accounts later.
- Meta's Threads stayed unavailable in the EU and Meta closed the VPN workaround for EU users.
- A UK security analyst (Ashley Liles) got three years for trying to redirect his employer's ransom payment to his own bitcoin address and was traced via his home IP.
- Twitter began routing DMs from non-followed verified accounts to message requests, prompting Steve to reopen his DM settings.
- Listener Q&A: Steve argues against storing TOTP secrets in the same password manager as passwords (single point of failure); Tailscale has a native Synology app; Steve disables Windows paging on high-RAM machines, while Leo disagrees and notes swapfile.sys uses.
- SpinRite Level 2 rewrite restored a Samsung 840 SSD from 138/445/56 MB/s to 564 MB/s across the drive, which Steve attributes to "read disturb"; SpinRite 7 will add targeted rewriting.
- Satellites: the Viasat KA-SAT hack at the start of the Ukraine invasion disabled tens of thousands of terminals in at least 13 countries, including German wind-turbine monitoring; Hack-A-Sat 4 at DEF CON would let teams attack a real orbiting satellite; background on U.S. military satellites (WGS, DSCS) and threats from anti-satellite weapons, robotic servicer satellites and cyberattacks.
