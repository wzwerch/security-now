# Summary

Steve opens with the Apollo 11 sine/cosine polynomial approximation as the picture of the week, then covers Brave's mistake of silently adding an affiliate code when users typed binance.us; Brave removed it. June 2020 Patch Tuesday set a new record with 129 CVE fixes, 11 of them critical remote code execution bugs, and the updates (plus the Windows 10 2004 feature update) caused printing failures, odd USB printer port loss, repeated SSD defragmentation and pointless TRIM commands on hard drives, and a clash with Avast/AVG hooks. SMBleed, a new SMBv3.1.1 compression information leak related to SMBGhost, shows why SMB must never be exposed to the internet. A "Breachstortion" spam campaign threatens site owners with fake breaches, while genuine database-ransom attacks have collected real money. Intel SGX was hit again by SGAxe and CrossTalk, but Intel finally ships hardware control-flow enforcement (CET) in Tiger Lake. The main story is Lamphone, Ben-Gurion University and Weizmann Institute research recovering speech from the tiny vibrations of a hanging light bulb, observed through a telescope and photodiode.

# Key points

- Brave autocompleted a typed Binance domain with an affiliate referral code; the CEO called it a judgment error, said it treated the domain like a search query, and removed it. No extra user data was sent.
- Patch Tuesday: 129 CVEs, 11 critical RCEs (Windows shell/LNK, OLE, GDI, VBScript in IE, ChakraCore, SharePoint, cabinet files); Steve notes the CVE numbers suggest long delays before fixes shipped.
- Updates KB4560960 and KB4557957 broke printing (even to PDF) for many vendors; workarounds were PCL6 drivers or uninstalling the update.
- Windows 10 2004 bugs: USB printer port missing if the printer is off at boot, SSD re-defragmentation each boot, TRIM sent to spinning disks; Steve advises waiting before adopting 2004.
- Avast/AVG hooking via the registry debugger mechanism made programs fail to launch until they shipped a fix.
- SMBleed: unauthenticated information leak in SMBv3.1.1 compression (since Windows 10 1903); ZecOps published proofs of concept chaining it with SMBGhost, drawing criticism. Advice: put SMB behind a VPN with MFA; changing ports does not help because scanners like Shodan identify services.
- "Breachstortion" email scam claims a site was hacked and demands bitcoin; WebARX found at least five payers. Real database ransom attacks on exposed SQL servers collected about 5.8 BTC from 100+ victims. Advice: never expose databases; use IP filtering or VPN.
- SGAxe and CrossTalk are new side-channel attacks against Intel SGX; Intel responded with more microcode updates.
- Intel CET (shadow stack for return addresses) arrives with Tiger Lake mobile CPUs; glibc and Windows Insider builds ("Hardware-Enforced Stack Protection") already support it, and it defeats ROP-style attacks.
- Lamphone: a hanging bulb vibrates by only 5-60 millidegrees under speech; with a telescope and photodiode sensor the team recovered speech and songs from about 25 m away, recognized by Google speech and Shazam. It is passive and needs no compromised device.
