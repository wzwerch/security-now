# Summary

Steve follows up on the Intel L1D eviction sampling disclosure, now named CacheOut, a speculative-execution style attack that lets an attacker choose which data to leak across process, VM and SGX boundaries; microcode updates exist for Windows 10 as separate KB downloads, but he does not plan to install them on personal machines. He laments that Windows 10 has become a moving target, relays the Free Software Foundation petition asking Microsoft to open-source Windows 7, and notes Microsoft will fix a wallpaper-stretch bug for all Windows 7 users after the final January update broke it. A working exploit for the Remote Desktop Gateway flaw, named BlueGate, was demonstrated. Further items cover Google paying 6.5 million dollars in bounties in 2019, Russia's weak fines against Facebook and Twitter and its block of ProtonMail, Facebook's 550 million dollar BIPA settlement, Clearview AI's scraped face database, Avast closing Jumpshot, antivirus vendors' Windows 7 support, and WireGuard entering Linux 5.6. The closing story is an artist who spoofed Google Maps into reporting a traffic jam by pulling 99 phones in a wagon through Berlin.

# Key points

- CacheOut (University of Adelaide and Michigan): lets an attacker target data evicted from the L1 cache, recovering AES keys, defeating kernel ASLR and canaries, crossing VM and SGX boundaries; Intel issued microcode; AMD not affected as far as known; no in-the-wild exploitation known; hyperthreading disablement is the strong mitigation.
- Windows 10 microcode updates per version (KB4497165, KB4494174, etc.) were released out of band for manual install; Steve says home users should wait for the normal rollup.
- Windows 10 now varies by release, making online help unreliable; example of the Control Panel icon view removal and Steve's SMBv1 and DOS file-sharing struggle.
- FSF petition to "upcycle" Windows 7 by releasing it as free software passed its 7,777-signature goal; Microsoft did not respond; Steve notes Windows 10 shares much code.
- KB4534310 broke Stretch wallpaper (black desktop) on Windows 7; Microsoft first said only ESU customers would get a fix, then said all customers would.
- BlueGate: Luca Marcelli (InfoGuard) demonstrated remote code execution on Remote Desktop Gateway servers (Server 2012-2019) beyond the earlier denial of service; nearly 20,000 unpatched gateways were exposed, about 6,800 in the US.
- Google doubled bounty payouts to 6.5 million dollars in 2019 (21 million total over ten years), raised Chrome top rewards, launched a Developer Data Protection Reward Program, and offers up to 1.5 million for Titan M exploits on preview builds.
- Roskomnadzor started proceedings against Facebook and Twitter over data localisation, with fines of only 1 to 6 million rubles; ProtonMail and ProtonVPN were blocked in Russia after refusing to register.
- Facebook settled the Illinois BIPA class action over face tagging for 550 million dollars; 23andMe laid off staff amid privacy worries and notes it complies with lawful requests.
- Clearview AI scraped over three billion images and sold face search to 600-plus law enforcement agencies; a suit was filed in Illinois; the New York Times warned it could end public anonymity.
- Avast shut down Jumpshot after reports (Motherboard and PCMag) that browsing data was linked to individuals; antivirus vendors say they will support Windows 7 through 2022; Leo and Steve recommend relying on Windows Defender and good behaviour.
- WireGuard merged for Linux 5.6: about 4,000 lines of code versus roughly 100,000 for OpenVPN, using ChaCha20-Poly1305, Curve25519, BLAKE2, SipHash24 and HKDF, with perfect forward secrecy and optional pre-shared key for post-quantum resistance; providers include Mullvad.
- Little red wagon hack: artist Simon Weckert walked 99 phones through Berlin streets, making Google Maps show congestion; movement was required and a passing car reset the street to green; Google responded in good humour.
