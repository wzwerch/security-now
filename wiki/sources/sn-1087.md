# Summary

Steve frames the episode around nation-states waking up to AI as a strategic asset. The European Central Bank tells the euro area's largest banks to plan against frontier-AI attacks by the end of October, and the European Systemic Risk Board raises systemic cyber risk to "severe". The EU publishes its Action Plan on Cybersecurity and Artificial Intelligence, China discusses restricting overseas access to its top models, and the UK's NCSC and GCHQ lay out "Cyber Shield", a national agentic red/blue defense that reminds Steve of *Colossus: The Forbin Project*. CISA's Attack Surface Evaluation team is quietly using Claude Mythos to audit government code.

Microsoft warns that AI-powered discovery (including its MDASH harness) will bring much larger Patch Tuesdays; this month again breaks the record. Defender's RoguePlanet flaw from Nightmare Eclipse is patched on the fly. A WIRED tip shows iOS Assistive Access as a far better kids' "dumb phone" than Screen Time. Steve retracts his call to block pasting browser clipboard content as unworkable. The main topic covers three new AI attacks: HalluSquatting, GhostApproval and GitLost.

# Key points

- ECB supervisory chair Claudia Buch asked big banks for anti-AI-attack plans by end of October (no fines); the ESRB rated systemic cyber risk "severe" and flagged Europe's dependence on non-EU AI providers. Mistral is pitching a flaw-hunting tool to banks.
- The EU Action Plan aims to evaluate models before market entry, build an ENISA blueprint and testing platform for critical sectors, enforce NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act, and invest in sovereign AI.
- Reuters: Beijing met Alibaba, ByteDance and Z.ai about limiting overseas access to frontier Chinese models and proposed tiered controls; China fears Mythos being used against it. Fable 5's export controls were lifted after new safeguards, while Mythos remains limited to trusted US organizations.
- UK Cyber Shield (NCSC with DSIT): federated autonomous "red" and "blue" agents for vulnerability discovery, automated mitigation, national-scale scanning and blocking. Steve thinks it is inevitable but too big for a bureaucracy to build alone.
- Microsoft's blog "Evolving Windows vulnerability management to meet the speed of AI-powered discovery" describes running MDASH (multi-model debate plus a prove pipeline) at Windows scale and warns customers to expect higher update volumes. Steve wants AI to sanitize new code before release, not just patch old bugs.
- RoguePlanet (Defender elevation of privilege) was fixed by an out-of-band Defender update. Nightmare Eclipse has now dropped eight zero-days; Steve notes the earlier BitLocker "PIN bypass" claim was knowingly false.
- Assistive Access (iOS 17+): a grid of large app tiles, no browser unless added, links in Messages shown as plain text, contact allow-lists, exit by triple-click and a separate passcode. Shortcut: grc.sc/dumb.
- Listener feedback: blocking browser-to-clipboard copying would never fly; heuristic clipboard inspection (as Brave does) or a local AI guardian is the realistic path for ClickFix. Steve expects AI to eventually end the business of Pwn2Own, bug bounties, Zerodium and NSO Group.
- HalluSquatting (paper co-authored by Ben Nassi): LLMs hallucinate repository and skill locations predictably (often `repo-name/repo-name`; 92.4% for 2025 repos versus 0.9% for pre-2019 ones). Attackers register those names and plant reverse-shell instructions; affected agents include Cursor, Gemini CLI, Windsurf, GitHub Copilot, Cline and OpenClaw.
- GhostApproval (Wiz): symlinks in a malicious repo let six coding assistants (Amazon Q Developer, Claude Code, Augment, Cursor, Google Antigravity, Windsurf) write outside the workspace while the approval prompt hides the real target. AWS, Cursor and Google fixed it quickly; one vendor called it out of scope.
- GitLost (Noma Security): a public GitHub issue carries plain-English instructions that GitHub Agentic Workflows follow, leaking private repositories via a comment. Steve's lesson: mixing command and data in one channel is the root flaw; "the agent's context window is also its attack surface".

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a staircase at an Australian IBM site that runs into a ceiling, with a "No Access Beyond This Point" sign.
- A listener used Claude to rename hundreds of GUID-named church photos by content for 0.3 cents each; another pointed to isaiprofitable.com.
- Leo notes data-center power fights (eminent domain, gas turbines) as a US handicap versus China.
