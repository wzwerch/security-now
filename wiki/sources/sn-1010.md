# Summary

Steve opens with errata: eM Client can be bought outright with a lifetime license, which he did. The first news item is a KrebsOnSecurity story about Mastercard, whose DNS delegation listed one Akamai nameserver as "akam.ne" instead of "akam.net" from June 2020 until January 2025. Researcher Philippe Caturegli of Seralys registered the unclaimed Niger domain for about $300 and saw hundreds of thousands of DNS queries per day; Mastercard played down the risk. Next, CloudSEK reported a trojanized XWorm RAT builder that infected 18,459 would-be hackers who downloaded it as a "cracked" tool. Cloudflare's 20th quarterly DDoS report described a record 5.6 Tbps attack by a roughly 13,000-device Mirai variant and 21.3 million attacks in 2024. Let's Encrypt announced optional six-day certificates and IP address certificates for 2025. A SpinRite user found a bad brand-new 8TB drive. After listener feedback on TOTP brute forcing, Syncthing/STUN, spam avoidance, ValiDrive timing, AI "neural" networks, DJI geofencing and Outlook ads, the main topic is what Steve learned adding DNS over TLS (port 853) to GRC's DNS Benchmark: encryption forces TCP and TLS state onto servers built for stateless UDP, so servers drop idle connections, which is acceptable because browser DNS use is bursty.

# Key points

- Mastercard DNS typo: one of five nameservers pointed at akam.ne (Niger TLD) rather than akam.net; unregistered for nearly five years. An attacker with a long-TTL answer could have redirected far more than one fifth of lookups because resolvers cache; possible impacts include mail interception, certificate issuance and credential capture. Mastercard fixed it and said no risk to its systems; the researcher paid for the domain himself and was asked to remove his LinkedIn post. Steve wonders how many other DNS typos exist and recalls the Kaminsky cache-poisoning panic.
- Trojanized XWorm RAT builder: spread via GitHub, mega.nz, upload.ee, Telegram channels and hacker forums; command and control over Telegram bot APIs; over 1 GB of browser credentials exfiltrated from 18,459 devices (Russia 2,478, USA 1,540). Researchers used the malware's own uninstall/kill-switch command to disrupt it, which Steve calls legally shaky. Lesson: no free lunch.
- Cloudflare DDoS report: 5.6 Tbps UDP attack on 29 October 2024 from about 13,000 Mirai-variant IoT devices, lasting 80 seconds and mitigated autonomously; 21.3 million attacks blocked in 2024 (+53%); attacks over 1 Tbps grew 1,885% quarter over quarter; about 4,870 attacks blocked per hour.
- Let's Encrypt 2025 plan: opt-in six-day certificates and IP address certificates via an ACME profile mechanism; first short-lived certificates issued internally in February, early adopters around April, general availability hoped for by end of 2025; 90-day certificates remain. Steve doubts the need but is fine if it is optional. A listener warned that certbot recently defaulted to elliptic-curve keys, which broke mail connectivity with servers lacking ECC support.
- SpinRite: one of four new WD Red Plus 8TB drives showed 216 bad sectors and thousands of command timeouts during a Level 3 pass; SMART health bars (ECC corrected, relocated sectors, relocation events) dropped. Steve's advice: drives must be tested under read/write load, and pre-deployment testing remains a valid use of SpinRite.
- TOTP feedback: a listener noted attackers could distribute guesses across many cloud instances; Steve restated that the previous week's analysis was about recovering a secret key, where each matching code sample makes the right 80-bit key a million times more certain, and that servers must throttle attempts (Microsoft's MFA recently failed at this).
- STUN / UDP hole punching: Syncthing keeps working without Tailscale because it uses QUIC with STUN for NAT traversal; Steve agrees STUN and TURN would make a good future deep dive.
- Spam avoidance: Steve rotates his email address yearly while forwarding older ones and gets essentially no spam without filtering; Leo is skeptical and suggests Steve simply exposes his address little.
- ValiDrive: a 10-pack of PNY 16GB drives were really genuine 32GB; the test spent 3.6 s reading versus about 1,308 s writing due to NAND read-to-write switching. ValiDrive 2 will batch reads and writes (two switches instead of 2,304) and is a test bed for the planned "Beyond Recall" drive-wiping tool.
- AI "neural" networks: Steve stresses artificial neural networks share only a name with biological brains; they are multiply-add-threshold structures.
- DJI geofencing: a commercial drone pilot says DJI's new warning zones align with FAA requirements, and no other manufacturer restricted flight; Steve revises the earlier negative impression.
- New Outlook: Windows Mail, Calendar and People support ended 31 December 2024 and the new Outlook shows ads; Steve is glad to have eM Client.
- DNS over TLS in the DNS Benchmark: the benchmark was rewritten to be protocol agnostic (IPv6 added first, then TLS through the Windows Schannel API). DoT uses TCP port 853; encrypted DNS needs certificates and handshakes, and busy public resolvers drop idle connections to save memory. This works in practice because a page load triggers 50 to 150 lookups in a burst, handshake cost is amortized, and TLS session resumption helps. DoH support is next; Steve expects plain UDP DNS to eventually fade like unencrypted HTTP.
