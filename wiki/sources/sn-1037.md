# Summary

Steve opens with new details on the July 2025 SharePoint on-premises zero-day ("ToolShell"). ProPublica reported that the SharePoint on-prem code is maintained by a China-based Microsoft engineering team, supervised only by a US "digital escort", and that Microsoft is now moving this work elsewhere; Steve asks whether the badly botched first patch could even have been deliberate, and notes that the first sighting on 7 July does not mean exploitation began that day. He then covers a Microsoft report on Secret Blizzard (Turla) using Russian ISP interception gear plus a fake Kaspersky update to install a rogue root certificate on foreign embassy machines. Short items: Dropbox Passwords ends on 28 October, Signal's Meredith Whittaker again threatens to leave Australia over encryption backdoors, YouTube begins ML-based age estimation in the US, and Chrome adds optional "Verified CRX Upload" key-based signing of extension uploads. The main news story is ICANN's breach notice to registrar WebNIC for ignoring DNS-abuse reports, with a 19 August cure deadline and the bulk-transfer-after-termination fallback. Listener feedback leads Steve to try California's mobile driver's licence and TruAge, concluding TruAge over-discloses identity, while Yubico founder Stina Ehrensvard's SIROS Foundation works on proper age verification at W3C/IAB. The episode ends with a Natto Thoughts analysis of Chinese vendors in Microsoft's MAPP programme, and Steve's conclusion that Microsoft should stop giving Chinese security firms weeks of advance notice of vulnerabilities.

# Key points

- SharePoint follow-up: ProPublica found that SharePoint on-premises bugs are fixed by a China-based Microsoft team supervised by a US-based engineer; Microsoft says the work is being shifted. Steve argues the "digital escort" model cannot prevent engineers from learning vulnerability details, and wonders whether the incomplete patch (which caused most of the damage) was deliberate. Microsoft will stop supporting on-prem SharePoint in July 2026.
- Steve's reasoning on timing: exploitation first observed on 7 July (the day before Patch Tuesday) likely followed weeks of quiet, targeted use, then a mass automated burst before the patch.
- Both Steve and Leo compare it to a recent case where an AI coding agent "fixed" a symptom (a guard against a stack underflow) rather than the root cause; senior review is the safeguard that is missing.
- Secret Blizzard/Turla (FSB Center 16), per Microsoft: Russian ISPs' SORM equipment redirects chosen embassy users to a captive portal that demands a Kaspersky update; the package installs a rogue root certificate plus the ApolloShadow malware, enabling adversary-in-the-middle decryption of TLS and even TLS-based VPN traffic. Advice: embassy IT should audit root stores for unexpected certificates.
- Dropbox Passwords is discontinued on 28 October 2025; users should migrate to another manager (Bitwarden is recommended on the show).
- Signal's president Meredith Whittaker says Signal would leave Australia rather than build a backdoor ("gangrene"); Signal has about 40 million users worldwide but is used by Australian government agencies. The EU is expected to push its chat-scanning legislation in October, and Steve thinks the world is waiting on the EU.
- YouTube is rolling out machine-learning age estimation in the US (search terms, watched categories, account age) and lets wrongly flagged adults verify with a credit card or government ID. Steve calls heuristics a fuzzy but reasonable stopgap.
- Chrome Web Store "Verified CRX Upload": a developer generates an RSA-2048 key pair offline and gives Google only the public key; uploads not signed by the private key are rejected, so a hijacked developer account cannot push a malicious update. Steve calls it minimal and bulletproof; losing the key means up to a week of support recovery.
- ICANN breach notice (29 July 2025) to WebNIC (Web Commerce Communications Limited): about 500,000 .com domains, delayed or no action on documented DNS-abuse reports (phishing, malware, botnets), late and incomplete answers to ICANN Compliance, missing required website disclosures and an improper ICANN logo. Cure deadline 19 August 2025, otherwise termination; ICANN would reassign domains to a gaining registrar via Bulk Transfer After Termination (BTAT) with no action needed by registrants.
- TP-Link Archer C50: CVE-2025-6982 - the router stores its configuration encrypted with single DES in ECB mode, exposing admin and WiFi credentials; the model is end of life and users are told to replace it.
- Listeners reported trying the Brave browser after earlier shows.
- Age verification: Steve tried California's DMV Wallet app (mobile driver's licence, mDL) and the TruAge add-on from NACS/Conexxus. TruAge's token bundles licence number, state, expiry and date of birth and can be unmasked under subpoena, so it does not meet the "prove only that you are over N" goal. TruAge claims influence on W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0; Steve expects the W3C result to be more minimal. About 15 US states plus Puerto Rico already issue mobile licences.
- Stina Ehrensvard (SIROS Foundation, founder of Yubico) sent a position paper "Deployability First: Making Age Verification Work at Internet Scale" for the 2025 joint W3C/IAB workshop; Steve is confident she will push for assertion of age only.
- MAPP (Microsoft Active Protections Program, since 2008) gives vetted security vendors advance vulnerability details (tiers of 24 hours, up to five days, and invite-only validation; sometimes about two weeks). Natto Thoughts notes 19 of 104 members are Chinese firms, 12 of them also Technical Support Units reporting vulnerabilities to China's MSS-run CNNVD under China's 2021 RMSV 48-hour reporting rule. Past suspected leaks: DPtech expelled in 2012; 2021 Exchange/Hafnium; SharePoint 2025 (exploitation seen 7 July, patch 8 July). Steve concludes Microsoft should reconsider including Chinese vendors.
- Leo mentions (from the Club TWiT Discord) newly reported flaws in Broadcom chips used in over 100 Dell models; to be covered later.
