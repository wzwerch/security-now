# Summary

Steve retitled the episode "Authenticity on the Internet" after reading OpenAI's announcement of GPT-2, a text generator whose full model was withheld because of misuse concerns. Before that, he covers a very large February 2019 Patch Tuesday (74 Microsoft CVEs, 20 critical, plus 71 Adobe fixes, 44 critical, including a Windows DHCP Server remote-code-execution bug). Microsoft announced that Windows 7 SP1 and Server 2008 R2 will need SHA-2 code-signing support, delivered as standalone updates in March and April, because updates will be signed only with SHA-2 from August 2019. Symantec found eight Monero-mining apps in the Microsoft Store that fetched their miner via Google Tag Manager. Google's Trusted Types proposal for Chrome aims to curb DOM cross-site scripting, Xiaomi M365 scooters lack authentication on their app link, and Italian researchers showed that Android apps can be identified inside Tor traffic. CNBC reported that the Equifax data never surfaced on the dark web, suggesting a state actor, while a GAO report urged federal privacy legislation with the FTC as enforcer. Steve closes with the GPT-2 sample about unicorns and his worry that synthetic text will flood the Internet, making human authorship impossible to assume.

# Key points

- February 2019 Patch Tuesday: 74 CVEs plus 3 advisories (20 critical), one exploited in the wild and four publicly known; CVE-2019-0626 in the Windows DHCP Server is a remote code execution flaw. Adobe fixed 71 bugs (44 critical) across Acrobat Reader, Flash, ColdFusion and Creative Cloud.
- Microsoft SHA-2 requirement: Windows 7 SP1 and Server 2008 R2 need SHA-2 signing support (standalone updates March 12 and April 9, 2019); from August 2019 updates are SHA-2 only, and devices without support stop receiving updates. Steve was unsure whether delivery would be automatic.
- Symantec reported eight potentially unwanted apps from three developer names in the Microsoft Store that mined Monero; they were progressive web apps (wwahost.exe) that loaded the miner through Google Tag Manager, deferring the payload until after store review. Microsoft pulled the apps and Google removed the script.
- Google's Trusted Types: a Content-Security-Policy extension (Chrome 73 onward) that makes DOM injection sinks reject plain strings unless a policy produces them, aimed at eliminating client-side XSS.
- Xiaomi M365 scooter (found by Zimperium): the app-to-scooter protocol has no authentication, so nearby attackers can stop or accelerate it; Xiaomi blamed third parties and no patch existed; the model is used by rental firms.
- "Peel the Onion" research (Sapienza University): traffic analysis identified Android apps behind Tor with about 97% accuracy; Leo stresses Tor hides origin, not that traffic exists.
- Equifax: roughly 148 million people affected in 2017, yet no data has appeared for sale; CNBC sources and Troy Hunt suggest a nation-state holding the data for targeted use.
- GAO report "Internet Privacy: Additional Federal Authority Could Enhance Consumer Protection": recommends comprehensive legislation and a stronger FTC (only 101 privacy cases historically); House Energy and Commerce hearing set for February 26.
- GPT-2 (OpenAI): 1.5 billion parameters trained on 8 million web pages to predict the next word; OpenAI released only a small model and paper, listing risks such as fake news, impersonation, spam and phishing. Steve argues an Internet where text is assumed human is ending; Leo is skeptical, citing the Washington Post's formulaic sports bot.
- Windows 10 LTSC (formerly LTSB) offers a feature-stripped Windows 10 with ten years of patches for enterprises (listener feedback).

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: a bar chart with a truncated y-axis that exaggerates a 99% to 1% poll result; Steve's pet peeve about unanchored charts.
- Reminiscence about the PDP-8 and PDP-11 and Oscar Vermeulen's PiDP-11 kit, which Leo received.
- Listener notes on MiB versus MB, satellite Internet (Starlink) as a route around national firewalls, and DuckDuckGo as a Safari search option.
