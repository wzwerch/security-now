# Summary

Jason Howell hosts alongside Steve while Leo Laporte is away. Steve first reports that ValiDrive is delayed because a listener's suspect drive wrongly passed (read caching in the USB chain was producing false greens). The UK government appeared to back down on scanning encrypted messages: an amendment to the Online Safety Bill limits Ofcom's powers to cases where scanning is "technically feasible" and accredited, while ministers insist nothing changed, and Signal's Meredith Whittaker calls it a victory that halts international momentum. Next, Microsoft's post-mortem explains how the Storm-0558 actor obtained a consumer signing key: a 2021 crash dump contained the key through a race condition, escaped detection, was moved to an internet-connected debugging environment, and was probably stolen from an engineer's compromised account; separate validation gaps let enterprise mail accept consumer-signed tokens. Steve's lesson is that signing keys must never sit in RAM and belong in hardware security modules. Mozilla's research found all 25 car brands collect too much data, and most share or sell it. After listener feedback on browser extensions, VirusTotal, email domains and DNS filtering, the main story is Krebs's report that more than 150 crypto thefts worth over $35 million link back to victims who stored seed phrases in LastPass, suggesting the 2022 vault theft is being cracked, especially for old accounts with low PBKDF2 iteration counts.

# Key points

- ValiDrive samples 576 evenly spaced random locations; caching somewhere between the code and a USB stick produced a false pass on a drive Steve later confirmed fake, so the release was pushed back.
- UK Online Safety Bill: after the Financial Times and other outlets reported a retreat, ministers (Michelle Donelan) denied changing the bill, but an added "technically feasible" and accredited-accuracy clause effectively neutralises the encryption-breaking power for now. Steve sees this as a major precedent and hopes the EU adds similar wording.
- Microsoft's Storm-0558 explanation lists five fixed flaws: a race condition that put the key in a crash dump, no detection of key material in the dump, credential scanning that missed it when moved to the corporate debugging network, libraries that did not enforce key scope, and mail code that wrongly trusted the libraries. Steve notes none would matter had the key never been in RAM, as HSMs ensure.
- Mozilla's "Privacy Not Included" review: all 25 car brands earned privacy warnings, 84 percent share or sell data, 76 percent say they can sell it, 56 percent can share with authorities on informal request, 92 percent give drivers little control, and none could be confirmed to meet minimum security standards; Tesla received all privacy "dings" including an untrustworthy-AI one. Steve cautions that broad policy wording does not prove those practices occur.
- Making form fields write-only to extensions would break password managers and other extensions; suggested mitigations include limiting extensions in private windows, using a separate browser with only a password manager, or OS-level AutoFill on Apple platforms. A listener reports that content-security-policy reports revealed adware extensions injecting scripts into bank apps, and that his employer disabled extensions entirely.
- Malicious servers can recognise VirusTotal's IP addresses and serve it clean files, so downloading first is safer; the VirusTotal SHA-256 lets you confirm a file is the one analyzed, and Windows' "certutil -hashfile <file> SHA256" computes it locally.
- iCloud custom-domain mail and plus-addressing are other options for email aliases; OpenDNS Family Shield (208.67.222.123 and 208.67.220.123) is suggested for child-safe DNS; Steve expects his InControl utility to keep working with Windows 12.
- Krebs reported (with MetaMask's Taylor Monahan and Unciphered's Nick Bax) at least 150 victims, over $35 million, two to five heists a month since December 2022, all security-conscious crypto users who stored seed phrases in LastPass; the thefts share laundering patterns and destination wallets, and one victim with an eight-character master password and an old low iteration count lost about $3.4 million.
- Nicholas Weaver says an Nvidia 3090 can try roughly 4 million guesses per second at 1,000 iterations but only about 8,000 at 500,000, so iteration count matters; LastPass moved some users to 600,000 only recently and never proactively fixed old accounts. LastPass declined to comment citing investigation and litigation.
- Steve's advice: vaults with 100,100+ iterations and strong passphrases are probably safe because attacks are targeted at likely rich accounts; anyone who kept crypto seed phrases or wallet keys in LastPass at breach time should move funds to a new wallet and change affected passwords, starting with email.
