# Summary

June Patch Tuesday brought dozens of fixes while Adobe delayed its update because of an actively exploited Flash zero-day. A federal appeals court upheld the net neutrality rules and AT&T promised a Supreme Court appeal. Symantec announced it would buy Blue Coat for $4.65 billion; since Blue Coat makes high-end TLS interception gear and Symantec (via VeriSign) is a top certificate authority, Steve argues that connection encryption has become something any well-resourced actor can intercept and that endpoints must rely on pinning and their own end-to-end crypto. Chrome quietly fixed a JPEG 2000 flaw in its PDF viewer. The hacker "Peace" was interviewed by Wired about reselling old breach data from LinkedIn, MySpace, Tumblr, Twitter, VK and the iMesh service, explaining how such data is used privately for spam and targeted account takeovers. Let's Encrypt apologised for a mass mailing bug that leaked earlier recipients' addresses in later messages, and a Visual Studio 2015 telemetry function in compiled binaries drew complaints. Steve previews Intel CET for next week and addresses SQRL deployment questions. The Q&A covers six-digit PINs, BitTorrent Sync's lack of documentation, whether Steve holds SQRL to a different standard, scanner stories, Shodan, password managers, biometrics, SQRL denial-of-service worries and a Windows driver backup tool.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday (June 2016): over 36 vulnerabilities fixed; Adobe postponed its release due to an in-the-wild Flash zero-day.
- Net neutrality rule upheld by an appeals court; AT&T plans to appeal.
- Symantec to acquire Blue Coat ($4.65B, up from Bain Capital's earlier purchase price); Steve notes certificate authorities can issue interception intermediates, so he advises pinning and TNO-style encryption.
- Chrome's integrated PDF viewer had a remotely exploitable JPEG 2000 flaw found by Cisco Talos and patched without user action; Steve praises the find-report-patch-push model.
- Peace sold or offered data from LinkedIn (167M), MySpace (360M), Tumblr (68M), Twitter (71M), VK (100M) and iMesh (51M, salted MD5); stated uses include spam and targeted takeover through password reuse; breaches kept private are more valuable.
- Let's Encrypt's agreement-update email bug cumulatively appended earlier recipients' addresses; 7,618 of about 383,000 emails went out before it was stopped; the team disclosed it and promised a post-mortem.
- Visual Studio 2015 inserted telemetry code into compiled binaries; Microsoft called it an undocumented, benign oversight.
- SQRL: needs site support but only a signature verification and minimal storage server-side, so sites need not protect secrets; it remains safe even over unencrypted connections, though encryption is required in practice.
- Steve obtained a GRC extended-validation certificate setup via DigiCert and a domain-validated Let's Encrypt certificate within minutes for a new FreeBSD/Apache/MariaDB/PHP7 server.
- Q&A: a six-digit random PIN can be adequate if the system enforces lockout; BitTorrent Sync lacks a published spec so Steve will not endorse it; Steve says SQRL is small enough for individuals to understand whereas Windows is not; Shodan indexes devices such as unauthenticated RTSP cameras; LastPass was chosen because its design was fully disclosed while Keeper's crypto was not; biometrics are convenience features that can be spoofed (photos, gummy fingers) and should fall back to something you know; SQRL's disable function cannot be abused by outsiders because it requires the user's client; a driver backup utility (Double Driver) was mirrored on GRC after verifying identical copies elsewhere.
- Self-driving cars have the advantage of constant vigilance.
- Reader story: a scanner overheard a family's analog baby monitor conversation revealing diamond storage location, illustrating radio monitor leakage.

# Other segments

- Intel's Control-flow Enforcement Technology (CET) announced as next week's topic; recap of DEP, ASLR and return-oriented programming.
- Richard Phillips' Rho Agenda science-fiction books recommended after a retracted snark.
- SpinRite: listener asks whether it masks impending failure; Steve points to the SMART screen and backups.
- Never10 passed 1.15 million downloads; Mark Thompson bitcoin-price banter.
