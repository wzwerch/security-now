# Summary

Steve notes that May 2020 Patch Tuesday fixed 111 vulnerabilities, making March, April and May the three largest patch rounds in Microsoft history (339 in 90 days), and advises limiting exposure of Windows to untrusted input. Windows 10 Insider builds gained native DNS over HTTPS for Cloudflare, Google or Quad9 resolvers, and a little-known built-in packet monitor, pktmon, can verify DNS behavior. The DOJ and FBI criticized Apple again after finally unlocking the Pensacola shooter's phones. Check Point showed Microsoft's fix for the reverse RDP path traversal flaw was incomplete because the path canonicalization function ignores forward slashes. Face masks are defeating London's live facial recognition trials, which had high false positive rates, and Utah launched its own location-based "Healthy Together" tracing app. The main topic is a walkthrough from Bob Metcalfe's 1973 Ethernet memo through the 802.11 generations to WiFi 6, its OFDMA and MU-MIMO features, and the new 6 GHz band.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday: 111 CVEs, 16 critical, none zero-day; every item was a genuine vulnerability. Steve advises external email filtering, a well-curated browser and using disposable VMs for risky browsing.
- Windows 10 Insider build 19628 adds DoH: set registry value EnableAutoDoh=2 so queries to Cloudflare, Google or Quad9 DNS are upgraded to DoH; additional servers can be registered with netsh dns add encryption; a DHCP extension for advertising DoH is proposed.
- pktmon: undocumented packet monitor added in the October 2018 feature update, with filters, counters and ETW capture; Windows 10 2004 adds live display. Steve used it to confirm no plain UDP/53 DNS was sent.
- FBI/DOJ press conference: after four months the FBI accessed two iPhones; Director Wray criticized Apple both for not helping and for designing phones that cannot be unlocked; EFF noted that the access argues against law changes. Steve speculates about future penalty legislation.
- RDP client path traversal (Check Point): July 2019 and February 2020 patches were bypassed because PathCchCanonicalize does not treat forward slashes as separators; Microsoft's workaround protects only its own client, not third-party RDP clients.
- London live facial recognition trials showed false positive rates up to 90 percent (5 of 6 stops wrong); masks further defeat recognition (including Steve's phone). Leo notes malls and some sidewalks are not public places for photography.
- Utah's Healthy Together app (by Twenty) uses GPS, Wi-Fi, cell and Bluetooth data, opt-in, location data deleted after 30 days; Steve would use such an app for his own state.
- SpinRite: Steve wrote his own FAT12/16/32 formatter and boot installer; remaining AHCI chipset quirks are being fixed; testing with newsgroup members.
- WiFi history: Ethernet (CSMA/CD) 1973; 10Base-5/2/T, 100Base-T, gigabit; 802.11 (1997) to 802.11a/b/g, 802.11n (WiFi 4, MIMO), 802.11ac (WiFi 5, wider channels, 256-QAM, MU-MIMO, beamforming), and 802.11ax (WiFi 6) with OFDMA subcarriers, target wake time, about 30 percent single-client gain and large gains in crowded settings. The FCC opened 1,200 MHz at 6 GHz (WiFi 6E). Steve and Leo prefer wired Ethernet and Steve will wait for 6E hardware.
