# Summary

CrowdStrike's president personally accepted the Pwnies "Most Epic Fail" award at DEF CON, which Steve praises as a classy gesture. He then covers a deliberate hardware backdoor discovered by Quarkslab's Philippe Teuwen in Shanghai Fudan Microelectronics RFID key cards (MIFARE Classic compatible chips from FM11RF08 and FM11RF08S families, backdoor reaching back to about 2007), which makes cloning cards trivial with brief physical access and illustrates the danger of unauditable imported silicon. A related story: Onur Aksoy was indicted for selling over $100 million of counterfeit Cisco gear to hospitals, schools, government and military buyers. A listener reports SpinRite level 3 restoring a degraded 1 TB SSD from a few MB/s to about 564 MB/s. After errata and listener mail about the National Public Data breach (a second check site, npdbreach.com, which hashes SSN or phone input locally), the main segment explains Bloom filters and how Mozilla's CRLite uses a cascade of them to deliver exact, local, private revocation checking from a compact data set updated several times a day.

# Key points

- Pwnies at DEF CON: CrowdStrike's president Michael Sentonas accepted "Most Epic Fail" in person; two class actions and a possible Delta suit were pending.
- Fudan RFID backdoor: undocumented authentication commands with a universal key per product line allow card cloning in seconds with brief physical access; affects hotels, offices, factories; older MIFARE Classic was already weak by design, this one is deliberate and shows up in licensed cards from other vendors.
- Steve's conclusion: imported opaque silicon cannot be audited; Apple's strict inspection of server hardware looks prudent; the whole supply chain must be secure.
- Counterfeit Cisco case: remarked older low-end devices with fake labels, pirated software and counterfeit packaging, sold through Amazon and eBay storefronts across 19 firms; about 180 customs seizures; Cisco cease-and-desist letters were answered with forged documents.
- Counterfeit-equipment risk generalises: a similar channel could deliver genuine-looking but backdoored networking gear.
- SpinRite listener story: 1 TB WD Blue SSD at five years of age, level 1 changed nothing, level 3 rewrite restored read speeds (front/middle/end 2.5, 17, 11.6 MB/s before; about 564 MB/s after); the cause is read disturb, and drive-internal re-reading slows the first sectors.
- Errata: Steve had been calling Microsoft Edge "Bing"; NPD Pentester lookup requires a US state, so non-US listeners were confused; an independent analysis suggests the NPD data is mainly US addresses with junk mixed in.
- npdbreach.com offers lookups by name, SSN or phone, hashed in the browser; checking both lookup sites is advised, especially for common names. Credit freeze remains the advice; TransUnion login is described as painful.
- Listener letter from a new fan in Australia working through episodes 1-100 (tutorial series on how the Internet and PKI work) is shared as evidence old episodes stay relevant.
- Bloom filter basics: a bit array plus several hash-derived bit positions; "maybe present" or "definitely absent"; no false negatives, tunable false positives; extremely small and fast (example: 100,000 revoked certificate thumbprints in 128 KB versus 3.2 MB as a list).
- Cascading: level one is trained on revoked certificates; all valid unexpired certificates (from Certificate Transparency logs) are run through to find false positives, which train level two; a third level catches the rest; the result is an exact classifier with no errors.
- Mozilla CRLite (announced January 2020) combines Certificate Transparency data, internet scans and cascading Bloom filters; filters rebuilt several times per day and fetched by Firefox every six hours; avoids the roughly 130 ms and 7% timeout cost of OCSP and removes the CA privacy leak.
- Mozilla also raised the concern that CAs could use revocation to censor sites, and that honouring only some revocations undermines owners' intentions; Firefox bypasses revocation checks for very short-lived certificates.
- Other Bloom filter uses: Akamai cache admission, Bigtable, HBase, Cassandra, PostgreSQL lookups, old Chrome malicious-URL check, Bing's BitFunnel, Bitcoin wallet sync, Medium, Ethereum logs.
