# Summary

The show opens with Fuxnet, malware attributed to a Ukraine-linked group called Blackjack, which allegedly attacked Moscow's Moscollector sensor network; Claroty's analysis suggests it bricked about 500 sensor gateways (wiping files, wearing out NAND flash and flooding serial buses) rather than destroying 87,000 sensors, and Steve and Leo discuss the ethics of civilian infrastructure attacks. Voyager 1 resumed sending engineering data after engineers relocated corrupted flight-data software to other memory. Other items: Android 15 may quarantine apps that turn malicious after passing Play Store review, Thunderbird will add Exchange support written in Rust, China had Apple remove Signal, Telegram, WhatsApp and Threads from its App Store, and a Gentoo developer proposed banning AI-generated contributions. Listener feedback includes a Hyundai owner who found a Verisk driving report despite never opting in, credit-freeze procedure questions, the AT&T passcode encryption weakness, GhostRace follow-ups on atomic instructions across cores and the Triton lesson on human workarounds, a phishing email imitating an AT&T breach notice, free S/MIME certificates from Actalis, and SpinRite and Mac boot tips plus Windows Defender false positives and Microsoft ending EV code-signing benefits. Leo reports Google delayed Chrome's third-party cookie removal again, to 2025. The main topic is the April 2024 revision of the EU Child Sexual Abuse Regulation ("Chat Control"), expected to pass by early June, which would require scanning of encrypted messages including text grooming detection.

# Key points

- Fuxnet: Ukraine-aligned group Blackjack claimed to wipe Moscollector infrastructure; Claroty found the malware deletes files and routing data, rewrites flash and tries to physically wear out NAND, and sends random data over the serial Meter-Bus; real damage appears limited to about 500 gateways. Steve worries about civilian impact and escalating cyber conflict; Leo raises the idea of a Geneva-style cyber convention.
- Voyager 1: on 18 April the team moved engineering-data-packaging code to new memory locations, confirmed on 20 April; science-data code will be relocated over coming weeks.
- Android 15 (expected) quarantines apps after suspicious behavior: activity stopped, windows hidden, notifications and API services blocked.
- Thunderbird gains Microsoft Exchange email this summer, with the mail code being rewritten in Rust for memory safety.
- China ordered Apple to remove Threads, Signal, Telegram and WhatsApp from the Chinese App Store; Steve sees technology yielding to national legislation.
- Gentoo proposal to ban AI-assisted contributions over copyright, quality and ethics concerns; Leo notes a study finding GPT-4 could exploit 87 percent of CVEs from their descriptions.
- Vehicle data: a listener's Verisk report listed 242 trips, 24 hard-braking and 26 rapid-acceleration events although he had refused the driver-score programme; Hyundai then said driver scoring and data collection were disabled.
- Credit freeze: authentication to unfreeze relies on a PIN; automatic refreeze timers exist; FTC IdentityTheft.gov lists bureaus; Steve and Leo expect weak support-desk social engineering; proposed idea of per-merchant temporary access.
- AT&T passcode analysis: crypto did not need to be reversed because identical plaintext mapped to identical ciphertext, so patterns in other record fields revealed each code.
- GhostRace and synchronisation: atomic exchange is safe across threads on one core; multicore systems use the LOCK prefix and the x86 exchange instruction implies it; processors' speculation state lacks compartmentalisation, similar to shared third-party cookie jars.
- Triton (2017 plant attack) lesson: engineers left a maintenance key in place; physical-button controls should be edge triggered, but even bypassable secure-by-design is better than insecure-by-design.
- Phishing email copying AT&T's breach notice with a sign-in link caught a listener; never click links in email.
- Actalis (Italy) issues free S/MIME certificates; Mac and Linux users can boot SpinRite or GRC's Bootable via an image zip and tools like Etcher; Windows Defender flags SpinRite 6.1 as a trojan (false positive), and Microsoft is dropping special trust for EV code-signing certificates because it will sign kernel drivers itself.
- Google again delayed removing third-party cookies in Chrome (to 2025), pending the UK CMA's review of the Privacy Sandbox.
- Chat Control: 203-page draft dated 9 April 2024 (leaked) adds Section 12a exempting non-public communications services such as government, police and military internal systems; it keeps detection of known and new CSAM and grooming text; 78 percent of polled EU citizens oppose it; Member of the European Parliament Patrick Breyer (Pirate Party) calls the exemptions an admission that the algorithms are unreliable; adoption targeted for early June. Unlike the UK Online Safety Bill, there is no "technically feasible" escape clause.
- Steve: some problems are intractable; society must choose between true encryption privacy and diluted privacy; activist groups could test services by simulating grooming chats.
