# Summary

The headline story is CVE-2025-55182, a maximum-severity unauthenticated remote code execution bug in React Server Components (the Flight protocol) caused by unsafe deserialization; Next.js and several other frameworks inherit it, a single crafted HTTP request works with near-100% reliability, public exploit code appeared within days, and two China-linked groups attacked within hours of disclosure. A Cloudflare firewall update meant to protect customers caused a brief outage. Earlier items include a 750,000 euro CNIL fine against Vanity Fair France for cookie violations, GrapheneOS removing its infrastructure from France over law-enforcement pressure, and the eve of Australia's under-16 social media ban with a European Parliament resolution calling for a 16-year minimum and an EU age-verification app. India ordered a state tracking app to be preinstalled on all phones, Apple refused, India withdrew the mandate two days later but still requires SIM binding for messaging apps with six-hourly web logouts. Other items: the abbreviation SLH for Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters and a reported shift towards Zendesk, a RAM price crisis driven by AI demand (Micron leaving the consumer market), listener feedback on Cisco support-contract paywalls, and Chrome's Local Network Access permission. Steve also announces GRC's new DNS Benchmark v2 as a one-time 9.95 dollar purchase.

# Key points

- CVE-2025-55182 (React Server Components; Next.js tracked as CVE-2025-66478): affects React 19.0.0 to 19.2.0 line (fixed in 19.0.1, 19.1.2 and 19.2.1 per the show) and frameworks such as Vite RSC plugin, Parcel RSC, React Router RSC preview, RedwoodSDK, Waku and Next.js; React is on about 6% of websites and 39% of cloud environments; roughly 570,000 public servers using React components and 440,000 using Next.js were visible on Shodan. Fix is to upgrade React and dependants; there is no auto-update. Researcher Lachlan Davidson found the bug.
- AWS attributed early exploitation to Earth Lamia and Jackpot Panda (China-nexus), using anonymizing proxies and their own exploit code; CDN and WAF protections arrived about 48 hours after disclosure, and Steve argues major CDNs should get advance notice. Cloudflare's WAF change caused a roughly 25-minute outage on 5 December; Network World notes monoculture risk.
- Vanity Fair France (Conde Nast) fined 750,000 euros by CNIL after a 2019 NOYB complaint and a 2021 compliance order were not honored; the EU/California move to browser-sent preference signals is coming.
- GrapheneOS left French hosting (OVH), is rotating TLS, Let's Encrypt and DNSSEC keys, and says French authorities harassed the project; it states that secure-element throttling and signed-firmware rules mean it cannot help brute-force locked phones, and avoids Chat Control-supporting countries. Experimental Pixel 10 support may slip.
- Social media age limits: Australia starts 10 December with fines up to 50 million Australian dollars; teens already bypass with photos of parents or VPNs; the European Parliament voted 483 to 92 for a non-binding call for a 16 minimum and backs an EU age-verification app and eID wallet; Malaysia, Denmark and Norway may follow. Steve wants device-based, privacy-preserving "older than X" assertions.
- India: ordered Sanchar Saathi (a lost-phone tracing and blocking app, over 4.2 million devices blocked) to be preinstalled and pushed to existing phones within 90 days; Apple planned to refuse; the mandate was withdrawn after criticism. A separate directive requires messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal and others) to bind accounts to the registering SIM within 90 days and log out web sessions every six hours. Steve and Leo see governments feeling empowered to control devices.
- SLH (Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters) is moving from Salesforce toward Zendesk-related SaaS attacks.
- RAM shortage: Micron exits the consumer Crucial brand; retailers sell memory at daily market prices (a 32 GB kit tripled in three months); AI data centers absorb DRAM output. Steve doubts blanket data-center bans but suggests smaller, distributed ones.
- DNS Benchmark v2: adds IPv6, DNS over TLS and DNS over HTTPS, ranks resolvers on cached, uncached and dotcom lookups combined (v1 was dominated by cached speed, which now mostly measures network proximity and LAN gateway caches), samples multiple rounds for statistical stability, sidelines hopeless resolvers; about 200 KB of assembly; runs under Wine on ARM Macs; sold once with all future updates and a DNS-based update check. ValiDrive 2 is next.
- Listener feedback: network engineer complains that Cisco gates firmware patches behind support contracts, undermining Cisco's own patching message.
- Chrome 142 Local Network Access (W3C spec): a public site now needs user permission before its scripts may contact devices on the local network or loopback, protecting routers and printers; Steve notes users must still judge prompts, and direct visits to your own LAN addresses are not affected.
