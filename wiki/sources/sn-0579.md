# Summary

With Fr. Robert Ballecer again filling in, Steve covers a week he calls very busy; the planned Q&A is bumped. Brian Krebs's site was hit by a huge sustained DDoS that led Akamai to drop him, after which Google's Project Shield took him on; Steve explains anycast and why consumer bandwidth growth plus insecure IoT devices make terabit attacks plausible and mitigation hard. Yahoo disclosed a breach of about 500 million accounts, with Steve unhappy about the delay and the vague account of which passwords used bcrypt, and advising a password change plus emptying old mailboxes. ElcomSoft reported that iOS 10 added a second, far weaker password check for local backups, making cracking roughly 2,500 times faster; Apple acknowledged it. A teenage researcher reported jailbreaking the iPhone 7 within a day. Microsoft released a Get Windows 10 remover update. A fixed OpenSSL bug allowed a remote memory-exhaustion DoS using an oversized OCSP status request plus renegotiation. Mozilla proposed distrusting newly issued WoSign and StartCom certificates after evidence of back-dating SHA-1 certificates. BitTorrent Sync, now Resilio, still lacks a protocol specification. A listener ran SpinRite against a phone via VirtualBox.

# Key points

- DDoS: brute bandwidth concentration is the issue; CDNs such as Akamai use anycast so attack traffic is absorbed near its source; layer 3/4 floods versus layer 7 valid-looking requests; Akamai cut off Krebs because of the cost, Google's Project Shield offered free protection to news and dissident sites.
- Why DDoS persists: it is overload with legitimate-looking requests; compromised IoT devices stay owned by sending only a trickle each; faster home broadband makes each device more dangerous. Steve suggests holding ISPs responsible for infected customers as the only real lever, with caveats.
- Yahoo: roughly 500 million accounts exposed, disclosure reportedly years late; passwords mostly protected with bcrypt but not all, and Steve explains why hash upgrades only happen when users log in; advice is to change reused passwords everywhere, clear old mail that exposes other accounts, and consider abandoning the service. Fr. Robert describes how forgotten throwaway accounts are used to reach active ones.
- iOS 10 backup flaw (ElcomSoft): a new verification mechanism exists beside the old PBKDF path and skips checks; CPU-only cracking jumped from about 2,400 to about 6 million guesses per second; a cracked backup also exposes the Keychain; Steve advises deleting local backups made on early iOS 10 builds and updating. The researcher disclosed publicly before telling Apple.
- Teenage hacker Luca Todesco says he jailbroke an iPhone 7 within 24 hours for research; no details published.
- Microsoft KB 3184143 removes the Get Windows 10 app and related files from Windows 7 and 8.1; Never10 nears two million downloads. Fr. Robert abandoned Windows 10 after forced updates ignored active hours.
- OpenSSL: 1.1.0a, 1.0.2i and 1.0.1u fix a memory leak triggered by a large OCSP status request extension with repeated renegotiation, affecting default servers; Steve explains OCSP stapling and argues OpenSSL is too big for production versus small TLS stacks.
- WoSign/StartCom: Mozilla's CA team published evidence of purposeful back-dating of SHA-1 certificates and proposes to stop trusting newly issued certificates from both brands while leaving existing ones valid; Fr. Robert asks about scaling this process and points to DANE as an eventual way out.
- BitTorrent Sync is now Resilio Sync; the new security brief is not a protocol specification; Steve points to the open Syncthing as an alternative; the reverse-engineered 2013 protocol spec is mentioned.
- Correction: Ubiquiti's EdgeRouter runs Debian, not FreeBSD.
- SpinRite on a phone: a listener exposed the phone as a raw disk to a VirtualBox VM running SpinRite and saw performance improve; Fr. Robert plans to try it.
