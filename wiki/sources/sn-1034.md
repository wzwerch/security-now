# Summary

Steve begins with Peter Gutmann and Steven Galbraith's paper (published March 2025) that matches every published quantum factorization "record" using a Commodore VIC-20, an abacus and a dog, arguing the records rely on contrived numbers, preprocessing on classical computers, or compiled forms of Shor's algorithm that already know the answer; no real factoring beyond 15 and 21 has been shown. He then covers the Notepad++ 8.8.3 decision to sign releases with a self-created root certificate that users are asked to install, which he considers unhelpful and a bad habit, in the context of expensive code-signing certificates and aggressive antivirus. Listener feedback explains that Signal encrypts its on-device database while WhatsApp keeps messages decrypted at rest for portability, raises the question of how WhatsApp fixed a PDF rendering flaw "server-side" under end-to-end encryption, notes New Zealand's plan to ban crypto ATMs, and prompts a walk through the EFF's Cover Your Tracks fingerprinting metrics. Steve also answers whether rewriting interpreters in memory-safe languages would help. The main topic is an introduction to zero-knowledge proofs, motivated by Google's open-sourced ZK age-verification code, using three thought experiments and the properties of completeness, soundness and zero knowledge.

# Key points

- Quantum factoring skepticism: Shor-based demonstrations are limited to 15 (2001) and 21 (2012); an attempt at 35 failed. "Stunt" factorizations use numbers with hidden structure (factors a few bits apart so a square root finds them, mostly-zero binary patterns, the "Callas Normal Form"), preprocessing that converts the problem, or compiled circuits that use prior knowledge of the answer; the 2024 D-Wave RSA-2048 claim used factors only 2 or 6 apart. The paper proposes criteria for honest future claims. Leo says he repeated the headlines uncritically.
- Notepad++ 8.8.3 ships signed by a self-signed CA whose root users are told to install into Trusted Root Certification Authorities. Steve: AV tools will not trust that root, malware authors can copy the trick, and training users to install roots is dangerous; the underlying problem is that code-signing reputation requires paid certificates (roughly $250 to $400 per year for non-EV). He notes Microsoft dropped special treatment for EV code-signing certificates and that Defender may delete unsigned developer builds.
- Let's Encrypt is introducing IP address certificates; a cert must contain the IP when a dotted quad is used in a URL.
- WhatsApp vs Signal: Signal encrypts its SQLite database with a key in the keychain so backups and spyware do not see plaintext; WhatsApp stores messages decrypted so they are portable. WhatsApp offers an optional, off-by-default end-to-end encrypted backup. Steve concedes his earlier claim that WhatsApp could filter malicious PDFs server side cannot be right under end-to-end encryption; either rendering is delegated to Meta or a downloadable module was fixed.
- New Zealand announced a bill to ban crypto ATMs; the final Bobiverse book (Book 6, "Revelations") is in manuscript.
- Cover Your Tracks (coveryourtracks.eff.org): Steve's Firefox with uBlock Origin and Privacy Badger blocked ads and trackers but still had a unique fingerprint among 244,246 browsers; individual attributes (user agent 4.41 bits, screen size 11.65 bits, time zone, fonts, WebGL) add up. Fingerprinting regenerates deleted cookies; blocking third-party scripts breaks sites.
- Memory safety: interpreters are bug magnets because their behavior depends on data; rewriting them in memory-safe languages gives outsized security benefit.
- Zero-knowledge proofs (cast: Peggy the prover, Victor the verifier): Where's Wally with an oversized masking sheet; two competitors verifying equal purchase quantities with four keyed ballot boxes; Ali Baba's ring cave with repeated 50/50 challenges (12 rounds gives a 1 in 4,096 cheat chance) and plausible deniability. Three properties: completeness, soundness, zero knowledge. For age verification, ZKPs are only one part and need a trusted link between identity and an unspoofable biometric.
