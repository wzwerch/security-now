# Summary

Steve resolves the previous week's mystery: the "unauthenticated Linux RCE" is a chain of four CUPS printing-system flaws (CVE-2024-47176, -47076, -47175, -47177) in cups-browsed, exploitable remotely only where UDP port 631 is reachable and the service runs. Red Hat and others judged it important rather than catastrophic, but proof-of-concept code released before patches brought mass scanning, with 75,000 to 107,000 exposed hosts reported. Researcher Simone Margaritelli's account of a 22-day disclosure fight sparks a discussion of open-source triage and disclosure etiquette. Steve then warns about what attackers can do with even a brief domain takeover (Ether.fi lost control of its Gandi registration for about three hours), reports Russia moving to block Discord, a VLC update, the merger of the Tor Project and Tails, and Telegram's new willingness to hand data to police. Listener feedback includes Experian account mishaps, Windows setup nags and authors' publishing economics. The main segment reviews Microsoft's new Recall architecture, which Steve finds impressively redesigned though he will not run it.

# Key points

- CUPS chain: a remote attacker can announce a fake printer to cups-browsed, get a malicious printer definition installed, and cause code to run as the printing user when a job is printed; not vulnerable in default Red Hat configurations; mitigation is to disable or remove cups-browsed, update, and block UDP 631. Critics (Tenable, watchTowr, Risky Business) said the 9.9 score was overstated and it is nowhere near Heartbleed or Log4Shell.
- Margaritelli's complaint: roughly two days of research versus 22 days of argument with maintainers; he used social-media pressure to force prioritization, which Steve finds understandable given volunteer-staffed projects, while noting early PoC publication let attackers start scanning.
- Domain registrar takeover: attackers abused Gandi account recovery to switch Ether.fi's nameservers for about three hours; in that window they could obtain valid TLS certificates (revocation being unreliable) and sign email that passes SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Steve says multi-factor authentication at the domain registrar is the highest-value place to apply it.
- Russia's Roskomnadzor added Discord to its registry, the first step toward blocking it.
- VLC fix for an integer overflow reachable through a crafted MMS stream (update to 3.0.21 or later).
- Tor Project and Tails announced a merger: Tails (amnesic live USB OS routing through Tor) had outgrown its structure and gains the Tor Project's administrative and fundraising capacity. Onion routing is recapped.
- Telegram changed its privacy policy to share phone numbers and IP addresses with authorities under court order for criminal investigations, after Pavel Durov's arrest in France; reports say abusers are leaving.
- Enshittification: a listener received an offer to "rent" his Google Play developer account to publish third-party apps; Steve sees it as platform decay and a likely scam.
- Windows tip: disable "Suggest ways I can finish setting up my device" under Settings > System > Notifications; the idea may be added to GRC's InControl.
- Experian problems: an unverified signup let someone register another person's account using a stranger's email address, so the email owner received that person's credit alerts; Steve notes email is the real root of account security.
- Apple's new Passwords app mainly consolidates existing features; third-party managers keep their cross-platform value.
- GRC's SpinRite 6.1 announcement run finished; Microsoft's mail systems sent it to junk even without a block; the Security Now mailing list nears 10,000 subscribers; nuevoMailer again recommended.
- Recall redesign (David Weston's post, September 2024): opt-in at Copilot+ PC setup and removable via Optional Features; snapshots and the semantic index are encrypted with keys sealed to the TPM and bound to Windows Hello Enhanced Sign-in Security; processing runs inside a Virtualization-Based Security (VBS) Enclave isolated from kernel and administrators, with anti-hammering, timeouts and secure-by-default settings; requires secured-core features such as BitLocker or Device Encryption, TPM 2.0, Secure Boot-measured boot and Kernel DMA Protection; reviewed by Microsoft's MORSE team and an independent firm. A Recall User Activity API lets apps support "resume where you left off".
- Steve's verdict: a seriously rethought architecture that answers the original criticism ("message received"), though only time will show whether it holds; he personally stays on Windows 10 or Server 2022.
