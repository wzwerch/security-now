# Summary

Recorded a day early for Apple's event, the episode opens with ransomware fallout: all Flagstaff, Arizona schools closed for two days, New Bedford's refusal of a $5.3 million Ryuk demand in favor of restoring from backup, and the Texas Sodinokibi attack being declined too, with new detail that a shared IT provider's persistent OpenVPN links to the 22 towns spread the infection. Steve reports Apple's rebuttal to Google's iOS watering-hole report (fewer than a dozen sites, about two months of activity), which he and Leo find unconvincing given that the exploit code keyed on visitors in China; Alex Stamos is cited as saying the tone was a legal/PR reaction. Other items are Zerodium raising Android zero-click payouts to $2.5 million, public release of a working BlueKeep Metasploit module, and critical PHP updates. The closing topic is deepfakes: a voice-cloning fraud costing a UK energy firm 220,000 euros, and the Facebook/Microsoft-backed Deepfake Detection Challenge, with a discussion of how detection will be a cat-and-mouse game.

# Key points

- Flagstaff Unified School District shut all schools Sept 5-6 after a cyberattack; no public details on ransom or recovery.
- New Bedford, MA: Ryuk encrypted 158 of about 4,000 PCs (July 5); demand of $5.3 million; the city stalled, offered $400,000 and was refused, then restored from backups.
- Texas: the $2.5 million Sodinokibi demand was declined; over half of the 22 entities back online in three weeks. Incident responders advised limiting remote-access authentication, 2FA on remote admin tools/VPNs, blocking Tor exit nodes and Pastebin, and EDR for odd PowerShell activity. A listener reported the IT provider had always-on OpenVPN links to each town, which would explain lateral spread.
- Apple said the attack hit fewer than a dozen sites aimed at the Uighur community and lasted about two months; Steve and Leo note RiskIQ-provided script geo-checked for China and conclude it was geographically broad even if population-targeted. Volexity also described separate Android-targeted activity. Project Zero has reported 200+ bugs to Apple.
- Zerodium: top Android payout raised from $200,000 to $2.5 million (above $2 million for iOS), plus payouts for WhatsApp and iMessage and persistence techniques; Steve reasons it reflects buyer demand and Android's long unpatched tail.
- BlueKeep: a public Metasploit module for remote code execution appeared; Marcus Hutchins published a write-up; Steve expects less skilled attackers to use it against unpatched RDP hosts.
- PHP: critical fixes across the 7.1-7.3 branches, including use-after-free issues in a bundled regular expression library; PHP has no auto-update, so administrators must patch manually.
- Deepfakes: the fraud case used synthesized speech of a parent-company CEO; Steve foresees needing face-to-face confirmation. Facebook, Microsoft and universities launched the Deepfake Detection Challenge with a $10 million+ commitment and a paid-actor dataset; Leo notes subtle one-word edits are the real risk.

# Other segments

- SQRL documentation set (four PDFs, 79 pages) completed.
- Firefox memory tip: UnloadTabs add-on to release tab memory.
- SpinRite listener question on whether to buy it for a drive with bad sectors; Steve explains SMART needs load to be informative.
- Upcoming Boston authentication event and rescheduled recordings, including a file-sync episode.
