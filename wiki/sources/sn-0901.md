# Summary

Steve begins with Chrome 108, which adds native passkey support on Windows 11, macOS and Android; the catch is that Windows 10, still about 70% of Windows installs, is excluded and syncing between ecosystems is missing, so he expects password managers to take over passkey handling. He reports on a multi-day Sync.com outage that also revealed his old desktop clients had not auto-updated, and Leo mentions Syncthing as a local alternative. Medibank took its whole IT estate offline for a weekend to rebuild and harden, and Steve reads a note from an Australian law-enforcement cybersecurity analyst explaining that even uninterpretable CSV dumps can expose officers who signed up with government email addresses. Group-IB's CryptosLabs operation ran more than 300 fake trading sites and took about 480 million euros, Telegram is becoming the cybercrime marketplace of choice and now lets users auction short usernames on the TON blockchain, and more US states ban TikTok on government devices while Steve judges the arguments weak because other social platforms collect similar data. A class action was filed against LastPass by a customer that needlessly rotated all its passwords, Rackspace's hosted Exchange business was wiped by ransomware with at least three class actions following, and Japan announced a plan for preemptive cyber defence. The main segment dissects Apple's announcement of Advanced Data Protection, Security Keys for Apple ID and iMessage Contact Key Verification, concluding that ADP genuinely removes Apple's access to most iCloud data if the user accepts recovery responsibility and avoids iCloud.com web access.

# Key points

- Chrome 108 passkeys: stored in the platform keystore (iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, Windows Hello), Bluetooth only used for proximity check; Windows 10 unsupported; Steve doubts platform lock-in will last once password managers support passkeys.
- Sync.com suffered a roughly week-long outage after maintenance went wrong; Steve had to manually update clients and keeps Sync.com for true end-to-end encryption; Syncthing mentioned for NAS-to-NAS and laptop sync.
- Medibank shut down apps, stores and call centres for a weekend (Operation Safeguard) with Microsoft experts helping; the extortion demand was reported at 15 million dollars, costs at least 35 million; two-factor authentication introduced only after the breach.
- CryptosLabs: 300+ cloned fintech and crypto sites in French-speaking Europe, deployed with an automated kit, mimicking about 40 brands; victims are lured by search and social ads.
- Positive Technologies reports Telegram has replaced hacking forums for selling RATs, credentials and cash-out services; Telegram added sign-up without SIM via anonymous numbers and an auction market (Fragment) for usernames paid in TON, a coin originally from Telegram's abandoned Gram project.
- TikTok: Texas became the fourth state (after Maryland, South Carolina, South Dakota) to ban it on government devices; Indiana's attorney general filed two lawsuits; Steve cites Kaspersky and FBI statements and calls much of it protectionist posturing.
- Class action against LastPass (filed by DebtCleanse Group) after two breaches; Steve points out the plaintiff ignored that vault data was reported as not exposed.
- Rackspace hosted Exchange ransomware: outage over a week, stock down about a third, customers migrated to Microsoft 365, possible loss of "legacy data", at least three class actions; Steve uses it to question blind trust in cloud hosting.
- Japan plans a legal framework permitting preemptive cyber operations against potential attackers.
- Zero-width space (U+200B) abuse: a listener found it prepended to a sender name to force sort order.
- Apple Advanced Data Protection: raises end-to-end-encrypted iCloud categories from 14 to 23 (iCloud Backup, Photos, Notes); mail, contacts and calendar excluded for interoperability; keys held in HSMs are deleted and rotated; iCloud.com web access disabled by default and re-enabled with per-session authorization from a trusted device; shared links, iWork collaboration and Shared Albums stay under standard protection; requires two-factor authentication, updated OS versions and a recovery contact or key, since Apple cannot recover data otherwise.
- Security Keys for Apple ID add hardware keys as a second factor (anti-phishing), targeted at high-risk users; Steve and Leo ask about the fallback method.
- iMessage Contact Key Verification will warn if a new device is silently added to a conversation and allow comparing a verification code; Steve notes this addresses the theoretical risk of a compelled hidden key but only for users who opt in.

# Other segments

- SpinRite 6.1 alpha 5 with about 500 registered testers; command-line model names with spaces need quoting; a warm-boot lockup on HP all-in-one PCs led Steve to buy one on eBay for testing.
