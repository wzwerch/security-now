# Summary

The raw file's frontmatter title is garbled ("EPISODE: #1085"); the episode title in the body is "A SOTA State-Sponsored Campaign". Steve opens with Microsoft's second one-year extension of Windows 10 Extended Security Updates (to 12 October 2027), arguing it gives Microsoft's MDASH AI time to clean up Windows 10 and predicting a stripped-down "Junior 11". He follows with CISA's three-calendar-day deadlines: three Ubiquiti UniFi OS flaws (CVE-2026-34908/09/10) and a Cisco Unified Communications Manager SSRF (CVE-2026-20230), the latter with a Friday-to-Sunday window. A discussion on zero-reboot patching follows.

ASIO's 2026 threat assessment describes a state-sponsored group that compromised an Australian critical infrastructure provider and stole active user credentials, which Steve links to the main topic. OpenAI's "Patch the Planet", built with Trail of Bits on its Daybreak/GPT-5.5 Cyber work, is presented as the open-source counterpart to Anthropic's Project Glasswing. Meta's tracking of employee keystrokes and screens for AI training exposed data across 45,000 Hive tables and was paused. A GitHub repo of 23 AI-fuzzed proofs of concept shows low-skill actors skipping responsible disclosure. Andrew Ng's "loops" letter frames AI iteration. After a Kevin Mitnick story, the main topic is the full FortiBleed campaign: a Russian-speaking initial access broker using credential stuffing and sniffing, with 86,644 firewalls listed. Leo ends with news that export controls on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are being lifted.

# Key points

- Windows 10 ESU extended again to 12 October 2027 amid high PC prices and a RAM/chip shortage expected into 2028; Steve predicts a "Junior 11" that runs on Windows 10-class hardware.
- CISA gave agencies three days for UniFi OS flaws (access control bypass, path traversal, command injection; patches had been out since May) and then until Sunday night for CVE-2026-20230 in Cisco Unified CM, patched on 3 June and exploited three weeks later. Steve argues for automatic updates and no-reboot patching (similar to 0patch).
- ASIO's director-general described a nation-state group mapping an Australian infrastructure network for sabotage and stealing admin credentials; the activity is "difficult to overstate" across the region.
- Patch the Planet: Trail of Bits works with maintainers of cURL, NATS Server, pyca/cryptography, Sigstore, aiohttp, Go, freenginx, Python and python.org; partners include HackerOne and Calif. Humans triage every finding before it reaches a maintainer.
- Reported findings: a fuzzing lab built in under a day, variant-analysis pipelines, differential testing, plus Linux kernel leaks and local privilege escalations, a 23-year-old OpenBSD semaphore use-after-free, 34 FreeBSD vulnerabilities, dnsmasq patterns, Chrome V8, Safari and a Firefox WebAssembly bug fixed before Pwn2Own Berlin (CVE-2026-8390), after which five of six Firefox entrants withdrew.
- Steve is annoyed that Calif, which published HTTP/2 Bomb without coordination, takes part in the initiative.
- Meta's Model Capability Initiative collected keystrokes, clicks and screen content from US employees; misconfigured ACLs exposed it internally, 1,600+ staff had signed a protest petition, and collection was paused. Steve acknowledges the privacy problem but notes that training an AI on one workforce's inputs is an unexplored experiment.
- A GitHub repo ("exploitarium") posted 23 AI-fuzzed proofs of concept; its author says a frontier model is not needed with a good harness. Steve sees script kiddies weaponizing AI and discarding responsible disclosure.
- Looping: Andrew Ng's three loops (agentic coding, developer feedback, external feedback). Steve explains that non-deterministic neural nets benefit from repeated passes, which also explains why Mythos missed Firefox bugs on the first run.
- Kevin Mitnick left a bequest to former Novell administrator Shawn Nunley, whose voicemail recording was the key evidence in Mitnick's case; they became friends after Mitnick apologized.
- FortiBleed in full: it began in February 2026 as mass scanning and password spraying against RDWeb, Sophos and Citrix VPNs, RDP and MSSQL before moving to Fortinet FortiGate; an attacker script ("FortiGate Sniffer") intercepted credentials on 24 protocols from May; hashes were cracked on a GPU cluster, validated, and access was sold on a Russian forum (16 June) by an initial access broker Unit 42 named SantaAd.
- SOCRadar counted 86,644 compromised firewalls in 194 countries and 260 attacker servers; the campaign is mainly credential reuse, not a Fortinet code flaw. Steve says brute-force lockout and MFA would have prevented it and that Fortinet should enforce stricter defaults.
- Breaking news from Leo: the Commerce Department is lifting export restrictions on Fable 5 and Mythos 5.
