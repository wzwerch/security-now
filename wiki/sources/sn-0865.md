# Summary

Apple shipped its fourth and fifth zero-day fixes of 2022 (an Intel graphics driver out-of-bounds write and an AVD media decoder out-of-bounds read, iOS/iPadOS 15.4.1 and macOS 12.3.1), already pointing to a faster pace than 2021. A Java Spring Core remote code execution bypass of a 2010 fix, dubbed Spring4Shell, appeared with a public proof of concept and received a CVSS 9.8 rating; VMware's Spring.io issued patches, and Steve expects exploitation to follow though it is less trivial than Log4j. QNAP NAS devices were affected by the OpenSSL infinite-loop bug and by Dirty Pipe, adding to a year of ransomware and brute-force trouble. Sophos Firewall CVE-2022-1040, an authentication bypass, was exploited against South Asian targets and fixed through automatic hotfixes, even for end-of-life versions. CISA ordered agencies to patch eight exploited bugs, including very old GPON router and Kerberos flaws. The Browser-in-the-Browser phishing technique discussed two weeks earlier was already used by the Belarusian group Ghostwriter. Checkmarx linked nearly 800 malicious npm modules to RED-LILI, who automated one-account-per-package creation. FinFisher went insolvent, and two teenagers were charged in the Lapsus$ investigation. Steve criticized Wyze for ignoring Bitdefender's disclosure for three years and described a trivial camera login bypass. The main topic is port knocking and its modern form, single-packet authorization with fwknop, as a way to hide services from the public Internet.

# Key points

- Apple zero-days four and five of 2022: Intel graphics driver out-of-bounds write (kernel memory read) and AVD out-of-bounds read (code execution with kernel privileges); Google patched 16 Chrome zero-days and Apple 12 in all of 2021.
- Spring4Shell: bypass of a 2010 Spring Core fix (CVE-2010-1622) affecting JDK 9 and later, Spring MVC and WebFlux; exploitability depends on configuration and use of deserialization utilities, but Will Dormann saw it working against Spring's stock form-handling sample; CVSS 9.8; patches from VMware published on 4 April.
- Deserialization explained: turning stored byte streams back into objects is inherently an interpreter and naive code assumes valid input.
- QNAP: some NAS models hit by the OpenSSL BN_mod_sqrt hang (only a denial of service, and only if TLS is exposed publicly), no patch yet for Dirty Pipe (local privilege escalation), plus DeadBolt ransomware waves and a forced January update; Steve notes the pattern of exposed ports.
- Sophos Firewall CVE-2022-1040 (CVSS 9.8, versions 18.5 MR3 and earlier): authentication bypass in the user portal and web admin leading to code execution; exploited against a small set of South Asian organizations; automatic hotfix and patches even for end-of-life versions; advice to disable WAN access to the user portal and web admin.
- CISA ordered fixes by 21 April for eight exploited bugs: Sophos, Trend Micro Apex Central file upload, QNAP HBS 3 improper authorization, two 2018 Dasan GPON router flaws and the 2014 Kerberos checksum bug (CVE-2014-6324).
- Ghostwriter (UNC1151) used the Browser-in-the-Browser technique within about ten days of its public demonstration, hosting phishing pages on compromised sites; Google TAG noted many actors using Ukraine war lures, including extortion by impersonating soldiers.
- RED-LILI npm campaign: nearly 800 malicious packages using name collision with scoped packages (Azure, Uber, Airbnb), one fresh account per package created by automation (Python and Selenium) with disposable email one-time passwords, which defeats single-stroke takedowns; Steve sees npm's weak account controls finally becoming a problem.
- FinFisher (maker of FinSpy) declared insolvency after a German investigation into selling spyware to Turkey without an export permit; customers had included several authoritarian governments.
- Lapsus$: two minors aged 16 and 17 charged in London and seven people aged 16 to 21 arrested; the group claimed Microsoft, NVIDIA, Samsung, Okta and Ubisoft breaches and 70 GB of Globant data.
- Wyze cameras (Bitdefender report published 29 March 2022 after three years of silence): a 128-bit pre-shared-key handshake could be bypassed by skipping the first step because the stored nonce stayed all zeroes; a stack buffer overflow in a debug server-selection command (ID 2776, 64-byte buffer) and unauthenticated web access to SD card contents plus weakly obfuscated logs that held the shared key; v1 cameras were abandoned unpatched; the camera must be reachable (for example via a forwarded port) to be attacked.
- Port knocking: a daemon such as knockd watches packets below the TCP/IP stack and opens a firewall rule for the knocker's IP after a secret sequence of port hits (each random port is about 16 bits of secret); weaknesses are replay and sniffing; ports look stealthy otherwise.
- Single-packet authorization (fwknop from Cipherdyne): one encrypted and HMAC-authenticated packet containing the client IP, with replay protection by remembering hashes of used packets; clients exist for many platforms, and it can also trigger actions such as Wake-on-LAN.
- Steve's framing: port knocking is another layer, comparable to a password in being a secret, not a replacement for service authentication.
