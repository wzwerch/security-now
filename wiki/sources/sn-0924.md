# Summary

The episode begins with AirTag follow-up: a hack removes the tag's speaker, which Steve says shows an irreconcilable tension between finding your own lost item (beeping helps) and recovering a stolen one (beeping reveals the tracker), and that the draft standard's 8 to 24 hour delay is a compromise. On auto-updating, Steve's old ASUS RT-AC68U gained an automatic-update feature, but HP's firmware push that bricked OfficeJet Pro 902x printers worldwide, apparently tied to its "Dynamic Security" anti-third-party-ink measures, shows how autonomous updating can fail catastrophically without a fallback image. The Supreme Court ruled 9-0 in the Twitter terrorism case and declined to touch Section 230 in the YouTube case. The KeePass "master password dumper" is a local-memory attack, which Steve says applies to any password manager. Other news covers Apple's internal ChatGPT ban, Chrome 115 shipping Privacy Sandbox with third-party cookie removal planned for 2024, FBI misuse of FISA searches, and PyPI suspending new registrations after a malware wave. Steve announces a patch for a decades-old SpinRite 6.0 division-overflow bug on drives over 549GB, and the episode closes on Recorded Future's report on voice cloning as a service.

# Key points

- AirTag speaker removal: stealthy theft recovery and anti-stalking are inherently in conflict; remote owner control of the beeper cannot be trusted because a stalker is also an "owner".
- ASUS RT-AC68U received automatic firmware updating (2 a.m.) in a recent update; TP-Link AX21 had it on by default; regulations increasingly call for autonomous updates.
- HP OfficeJet Pro 9020e/9022e/9025e series were bricked (blue screen, error 83C0000B) by a May 2023 firmware update reported worldwide; HP gave no public explanation for 12 days. Steve says a fail-safe design (backup flash bank plus watchdog timer) would have prevented total failure; Ars Technica linked such updates to HP's Dynamic Security ink blocking.
- Supreme Court: unanimous Twitter v. Taamneh ruling (aiding and abetting under the Anti-Terrorism Act not shown) and a short order in Gonzalez v. Google that sidestepped Section 230, which therefore stands; Leo adds the opinion arguably supports 230's rationale, including algorithmic recommendations.
- KeePass 2.x leaves per-character leftover strings in memory (custom .NET text box); the proof of concept needs a RAM dump, pagefile, hiberfil or process dump; fix due in 2.54 in July; KeePassXC is not affected. Steve: password managers do not defend against local malware, and headlines overstated it.
- Apple joins Samsung, Amazon, Verizon and others in restricting employee use of ChatGPT and similar services; it is reportedly building its own LLM.
- Google Privacy Sandbox APIs ship with Chrome 115 (Q3 2023); third-party cookie removal starts for 1% of Chrome users in Q1 2024 and reaches 100% by Q3 2024.
- Declassified documents: the FBI improperly searched FISA-collected communications more than 300,000 times (2020 to early 2021), including queries tied to the George Floyd protests, January 6, and political donors; FISA was up for renewal.
- PyPI paused new user registrations and new package uploads after a wave of malicious packages overwhelmed a short-staffed team; existing users could still update.
- SpinRite 6.0 bug: a division overflow (error B04E) in DynaStat when handling linear-to-CHS conversion on drives above 549GB, present since roughly SpinRite 3.1; independent coder Paul Farrer produced a CONFIG.SYS driver and a TSR patch (MDFYSR60) and a separate patch for buggy AMI BIOSes with USB drives above 137GB; SpinRite 6.1 is not affected.
- Recorded Future's report "I Have No Mouth, and I Must Do Crime": voice cloning (e.g. ElevenLabs, often under $5 per month) is already used in extortion, impersonation and vishing, can defeat voice-based authentication, and has started to be sold as a service on Telegram. Steve and Leo conclude that voice no longer proves identity.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: Road Runner and Wile E. Coyote illustrating test-at-top versus test-at-bottom loops.
- Leo describes voice banking for ALS patients (Acapela, Apple's upcoming feature) and AI-generated audiobooks.
- Reminder that GRC's newsgroups held extensive AirTag discussion.
