# Summary

GoDaddy told the SEC that three incidents between 2020 and 2022 were part of one multi-year campaign by an unidentified, sophisticated actor who stole source code and credentials and planted malware that redirected customer sites; Steve concludes that clean-up after the first breach was inadequate, as with LastPass. The Supreme Court began hearing cases (Gonzalez v. Google and a Twitter case) that could narrow Section 230 for algorithmic recommendations; Leo argues weakening it would harm small forums most. Twitter will drop SMS two-factor authentication for non-paying users after 20 March. Argon2 is now available in Bitwarden but only usable once all clients are updated. Meta launched the paid Meta Verified. Further items include fake Emsisoft code-signing certificates, a record 71 million requests-per-second DDoS stopped by Cloudflare, the V3G4 Mirai botnet exploiting old IoT flaws, malicious npm packages, a bumpy Patch Tuesday, Samsung's image sandbox, and the TikTok-spread Hyundai/Kia theft method. Steve closes with his view that ChatGPT is a clever regurgitator that simulates intelligence, supported by a test showing it fails at analysing complex unseen malware.

# Key points

- GoDaddy (about 21 million customers, nearly 4 billion dollars revenue) disclosed in an SEC filing a multi-year campaign: March 2020 credential theft affecting about 28,000 hosting accounts; September-November 2021 intrusion exposing source code and credentials for about 2.1 million Managed WordPress customers; December 2022 malware on cPanel hosting servers that intermittently redirected customer sites, which GoDaddy only learned of from customers. Earlier lapses include a 2019 DNS hijack and spam-site abuse. Steve stresses that after a breach nothing the intruder could have obtained should remain valuable.
- Section 230 ("26 words"): the Court heard whether algorithmic promotion makes platforms publishers; Leo, citing amicus briefs and Jeff Kosseff's book, fears collateral damage to forums and moderation rights; a decision was not expected soon.
- Twitter stopped enrolling non-Blue users in SMS 2FA and will disable it for existing users after 20 March 2023, citing abuse by premium-number fraudsters (reported losses around 60 million dollars a year); only 2.6% of users had 2FA and 74% of those used SMS. Steve fears users will simply turn 2FA off rather than move to an authenticator app or security key.
- Bitwarden's Argon2 KDF option is rolling out (version 2023.2); the setting is account-wide so every client must support it first, and unsupported clients are locked out; app-store review delays the rollout.
- Meta Verified: paid subscription (12 dollars/month web, 15 on iOS) with government-ID badge, impersonation protection and support access; Steve finds the recurring cost steep.
- Emsisoft warned that attackers sign malware with fake certificates using its name; Steve argues code signing will become necessary but never sufficient.
- Cloudflare mitigated a record HTTPS DDoS of 71 million requests per second (previous record 46 million at Google Cloud); mitigating at the "mouth of the funnel" requires very large global networks, and Leo says TWiT uses AWS Shield.
- Unit 42 tracked Mirai variant V3G4 (July-December 2022) exploiting 13 vulnerabilities, several nearly a decade old, in IoT devices and Linux servers plus telnet/SSH default credentials, offering TCP/UDP/SYN/HTTP floods; Steve's longstanding worry about unpatchable IoT gadgets and vanishing vendors is materialising.
- Check Point found 16 malicious npm packages that installed cryptominers; Steve notes a constant flow of malicious packages into npm, PyPI and similar registries.
- Patch Tuesday: Microsoft fixed 80 flaws including three zero-days; Apple patched a WebKit zero-day; KB5022842 reportedly stopped some Secure Boot Windows Server 2022 VMs on ESXi from booting; ESXiArgs still produced 500 more victims.
- Samsung Message Guard (Galaxy S23) is an image-rendering sandbox akin to Apple's BlastDoor, aimed at zero-click image exploits.
- Hyundai and Kia: about 3.8 million Hyundai and 4.5 million Kia vehicles without engine immobilizers can be started with a USB cable, a method spread by a TikTok "challenge"; thefts rose sharply (85% in Los Angeles, ninefold in Chicago) with at least 14 crashes and eight deaths. Hyundai is rolling out a free software update that requires the key fob to unlock first, plus window stickers and steering-wheel locks for models that cannot be updated; Kia's update is pending.
- ChatGPT: Steve calls it a "clever regurgitator" that mimics intelligence without understanding; ANY.RUN found it explained toy malware well but collapsed on complex samples, a "complexity cliff". He suggests much human work is also derivative recombination and jokes that it should not get launch codes.
