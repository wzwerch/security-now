# Summary

Steve opens with Google's formal Passkeys launch, framing WebAuthn as the end of the 50-year-old shared-secret login model: servers keep only public keys and verify signed challenges. He stresses that real adoption depends on every other site, that password fallbacks will linger and remain the weak link, and that users without their own device cannot hold passkeys; he contrasts this with his SQRL design. Listener feedback confirms TP-Link AX21 routers offer auto-update in setup, so Steve advises buying only auto-updating routers. News items: a ransomware breach has kept a US Marshals Service surveillance system offline for over ten weeks, T-Mobile suffered its second breach of 2023 (including account PINs), Chrome 117 replaces the padlock with a neutral "tune" icon, and Apple shipped its first non-beta Rapid Security Response patch. NewsGuard found 49 apparently AI-written news content farms, and Steve debunks Russian claims of replacing TCP/IP with a better homegrown protocol. Vint Cerf's three admitted mistakes lead into the main topic: the Apple/Google IETF draft "Detecting Unwanted Location Trackers", whose proposed pairing registry (owner identity recorded, partly visible to nearby users, available to law enforcement) alarms both hosts. The full spec walk-through is deferred to the next episode.

# Key points

- Google launched Passkeys support; WebAuthn moves logins from server-held secrets to public-key signatures. Adoption will be slow, like TOTP, and sites must offer a way to register additional devices; passkeys are not exportable between ecosystems.
- Passkeys need per-site key pair storage on a personal device, so shared library or caf PCs cannot use them; password fallback persists and keeps breach exposure alive. Dedicated FIDO dongles likely fade now that phones qualify as authenticators.
- TP-Link AX21 routers offer auto-update in the setup wizard; advice is to buy only routers (and IoT devices) with autonomous updates because routers are exposed, bridging, and neglected.
- US Marshals Service Technical Operations Group: a February 2023 breach plus ransomware (no ransom paid) took down a system for court-approved pen register/trap and trace data collection; staff phones were wiped; the Justice Department called it a major incident. Backups apparently were missing or also destroyed.
- T-Mobile disclosed a second 2023 breach (Feb 24 to Mar 30), exposing account data including PINs, its ninth breach since 2018.
- Chrome 117 (September) removes the padlock for a settings-style "tune" icon; Google's research found only 11% understood the lock's meaning and nearly all phishing sites use HTTPS. Extended Validation indicators were already gone.
- Apple deployed Rapid Security Response for the first time on non-beta systems (a Safari fix); it can be disabled under Automatic Updates but should not be.
- Automattic ended Jetpack Social auto-sharing to Twitter after failed API terms talks and plans Mastodon and Instagram support; Mastodon now onboards new users on mastodon.social by default.
- NewsGuard identified 49 sites in seven languages written largely by AI, betrayed by leftover chatbot refusal text, fake author photos and unfilled template pages; Steve expects these to improve and flood search engines.
- Russian officials described an "Internet from Russia" project to replace TCP/IP, including a claim that TCP needs six minutes to recover from loss versus 5.7 seconds; Steve calls this nonsense and notes TCP/IP is an open standard, so a split would isolate Russia.
- Vint Cerf's three regrets: 32-bit addresses, too little security attention (key distribution was impractical), and not foreseeing the web's content avalanche and the need for search engines.
- Apple and Google, with Samsung, Tile, Chipolo, eufy and Pebblebee support, submitted an IETF draft for detecting unwanted Bluetooth LE trackers across iOS and Android.
- Draft Section 3.15 would require a pairing registry linking each tracker serial to verified owner identity, expose partial owner info (last four phone digits or masked email) to nearby users, keep data at least 25 days after unpairing, and make it available to law enforcement; Leo and Steve find this alarming.
- Context: NYC's mayor urged residents to tag cars with AirTags amid a surge of Hyundai and Kia thefts; AirTag stalking lawsuits exist.
