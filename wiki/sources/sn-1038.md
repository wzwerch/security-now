# Summary

The show opens with CISA's rare emergency directive (issued 7 August 2025, deadline 11 August) ordering all federal agencies to fix a design flaw in hybrid on-prem/cloud Microsoft Exchange configurations that lets an attacker with admin rights on the on-prem server move into Exchange Online. NVIDIA published a formal "No Backdoors. No Kill Switches. No Spyware." statement, citing the Clipper Chip failure, in response to US proposals for chip location tracking or remote disable. Steve then covers Dashlane ending its 25-password free tier, malicious npm packages with a destructive "kill switch", uBlock Origin Lite arriving for Safari, SonicWall Gen 7 firewalls being breached (traced to old CVE-2024-40766 and unreset migrated passwords rather than a zero-day), and the Cisco Talos "ReVault" flaws in Dell's ControlVault3 security chip. A listener's detailed account explains why enterprises struggle to patch on-prem SharePoint and Exchange. After feedback and a progress report on the DNS Benchmark 2.0, the main topic is Cloudflare's report that Perplexity uses undeclared crawlers, rotating IPs and a Chrome-impersonating user agent to bypass robots.txt and WAF blocks. Leo pushes back strongly, arguing user-initiated AI fetches are different from training crawls and that Perplexity disputes the findings (it blames Browserbase traffic); Steve stays on the side that a site owner's no-crawl wish should be honoured.

# Key points

- CISA Emergency Directive (authority under 44 U.S.C. 3553(h)): hybrid Exchange configurations (2016, 2019 and Subscription Edition) historically shared a Service Principal with Exchange Online, so a compromised on-prem server could forge cloud tokens with little audit trail. Fix is the April 2025 hotfix plus migration to a dedicated Entra hybrid app and rotation of old certificates.
- NVIDIA states its GPUs have no backdoors, kill switches or spyware, arguing hardware kill switches are single points of failure and invoking the 1990s Clipper Chip debacle; Steve hopes it defuses government "special access" ideas and notes the EU chat-scanning decision due in October.
- Dashlane ends its free tier on 16 September 2025 (it only allowed 25 passwords); Steve says 1Password and Bitwarden are better deals and Leo notes Bitwarden's open-source licence means its free tier cannot be withdrawn.
- Socket found npm packages naya-flore and nvlore-hsc posing as WhatsApp libraries; with a phone number not on a remote whitelist they run a recursive forced delete in the working directory (about 1,110 downloads in a month). Steve's advice: keep versioned backups that do not propagate deletions (he uses Syncthing and Sync.com; Leo uses copy-only sync to a NAS). Also noted: 11 malicious Go libraries and five malicious PyPI packages aimed at Bittensor wallet developers.
- uBlock Origin Lite (by Raymond Hill, "gorhill") released for Safari on iOS, iPadOS and macOS; it follows the Manifest V3 approach.
- SonicWall: attacks on Gen 7 firewalls with SSL VPN were reported by Arctic Wolf, Google Mandiant, Huntress and Field Effect; SonicWall says it is not a zero-day but linked to CVE-2024-40766 with local passwords carried over from Gen 6 and not reset (fewer than 40 incidents). Guidance: upgrade to SonicOS 7.3.0, reset local accounts, enable MFA, Botnet Protection and Geo-IP filtering, remove unused accounts. Steve praises Geo-IP filtering because scanners outside allowed countries see nothing at all.
- Cisco Talos "ReVault": five vulnerabilities (out-of-bounds, arbitrary free, stack overflow, unsafe deserialization) in Broadcom-based Dell ControlVault3 firmware and its Windows API, affecting 100+ Latitude/Precision models. A non-admin Windows app can reach the firmware; the flaws allow persistence surviving Windows reinstalls, and physical attackers can bypass login or make the fingerprint reader accept any finger. Mitigation: install Dell's firmware updates (also via Windows Update), disable ControlVault services/devices if unused, enable chassis-intrusion detection, disable fingerprint login when travelling, use Windows Enhanced Sign-in Security.
- Steve laments that researchers must reverse engineer firmware that vendors already hold the source for.
- Listener "Rosco" explains the dependency cascade (Windows Server, Active Directory, Exchange, SQL, Dynamics CRM, third-party tooling) that makes on-prem SharePoint patching take weeks, and why Online SharePoint loses third-party workflow features; Steve notes the resulting lock-in drives subscription revenue.
- Perspective Image Correction (free Windows tool on SourceForge) recommended for keystone/perspective fixes.
- DNS Benchmark 2.0: base edition nearly done; runs on demand for up to 500 resolvers over IPv4, IPv6, DoH and DoT, uses 150 queries per resolver against the top 50 domains, now computes statistical significance; the Pro edition will run as a Windows service collecting long-term data. Results vary by location and time of day (DNS4EU is slow in the US but fast in Europe).
- SpinRite for flash storage (Kindle/Android tablet): must expose internal storage as a USB drive; run Level 3; DOS boot via BootAble freeware until SpinRite 7 becomes a Windows app.
- InControl (GRC freeware, sets Windows Update target-version policy) turned out to block KB5001716, the Windows 10 update that nags about end of support and unsupported hardware; briefly disabling InControl lets it install.
- Cloudflare vs Perplexity: customers who blocked PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User via robots.txt and WAF rules still saw access; Cloudflare's test with fresh private domains showed Perplexity answering with their content, using an impersonated Chrome-on-macOS agent and rotating IPs and ASNs (tens of thousands of domains, millions of requests/day). Cloudflare delisted Perplexity as a verified bot. Perplexity replies that the requests are user-driven, that Cloudflare misattributed 3-6 million daily Browserbase requests, and that it does not train on the content. Steve explains why search bots (which drive traffic) differ from AI bots (which reduce it); Leo argues for the open web and "right to read" and notes the dispute is nuanced. robots.txt is voluntary, not enforcement (RFC 9309).
