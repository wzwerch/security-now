# Summary

Ant Pruitt hosts with Steve while Leo Laporte is away. Steve explains why GRC's ValiDrive is still unpublished (Windows' USB stack interfered more than expected, plus accessibility polish), then covers Apple's removal of PostScript support in macOS Sonoma after interpreter vulnerabilities. China's Ministry of State Security publicly accused the NSA of hacking Huawei since 2009, a story already known from the Snowden documents, and Wiz Research found a Microsoft AI-research GitHub repository whose Azure Shared Access Signature token exposed 38 TB of internal data including employee backups. Signal announced PQXDH, adding the Kyber post-quantum key agreement to its existing elliptic-curve exchange to counter "harvest now, decrypt later". Listener feedback ranges from an essay arguing that no new web browser can be built, how public-key crypto works in both directions, a claimed "non-deterministic" random algorithm, wiping drives by encrypting them, the Y2038 fix, LastPass vault cracking, and Steve's Twitter plans. The title refers to the closing story: Citizen Lab and Google TAG found that an Egyptian opposition figure was infected with Cytrox Predator spyware through a middlebox that redirected his plain-HTTP browsing to an exploit chain, vindicating listeners who said every site should use HTTPS.

# Key points

- ValiDrive needed substantial new code to cope with Windows' USB chain; remaining work is report saving at non-100 percent display scaling and high-contrast support, and the code will be reused for GRC's future secure-erase tool.
- Apple deprecated PostScript in WebKit (macOS 12.3), removed PostScript and EPS conversion from Preview (Ventura) and removed the pstopdf tool and CGPSConverter in Sonoma; Steve views removing a rarely used, dangerous interpreter as the right call. Zscaler had found flaws in Adobe's and Apple's PostScript engines in 2022. PDF itself stays.
- China's MSS accused the NSA of long-running access to Huawei's servers (the SHOTGIANT program reported in 2014); Steve sees a shift to publicly acknowledging US intrusions, while noting Chinese intrusions into Microsoft involved damage.
- Wiz found robust-models-transfer in Microsoft's GitHub: an Azure SAS token granted full control of an entire storage account for an unlimited period, exposing 38 TB including personal-computer backups, secrets and over 30,000 Teams messages from 359 employees; since model files used Python's pickle format, an attacker could have tampered with models. Microsoft revoked the token and described the issue as an overly permissive token.
- Signal PQXDH (formerly X3DH) combines X25519 Diffie-Hellman with CRYSTALS-Kyber so both must be broken; it is already active in current clients and Signal will later disable the old-only mode. Steve explains "harvest now, decrypt later" and notes quantum computers had only factored 35 so far.
- Drew DeVault's essay counts 114 million words in 1,217 W3C specifications, arguing that building a new browser is infeasible and that the browser duopoly lets vendors serve their own interests; Steve agrees the situation is probably irreversible.
- Public-key operations work in either direction (encrypt with public key for confidentiality, sign with private key for authentication); RSA's public key hides a prime in a product of two primes, which is what quantum factoring would threaten.
- Steve disputes a listener's claim of a "non-deterministic mathematical algorithm" producing true randomness and invites proof.
- Encrypting a drive with VeraCrypt and discarding the key is a valid wipe alternative; random-noise wiping can raise suspicion of a hidden volume, so an extra zero pass may help; VeraCrypt blobs on SSDs stay encrypted even in wear-leveled leftovers.
- The Y2038 problem was fixed in the Linux kernel in 2018 (over 80 source files), leaving embedded and legacy systems as the risk.
- LastPass: attackers appear to crack only a couple of vaults a week at high cost, so most of the 25 million vaults are unlikely to be opened; Steve advises not storing SSH keys and recovery keys in a web-facing password manager; he explains the breach path (a developer's unpatched home Plex server) and remains wary of private-equity ownership. Ant says he rotated all passwords and stayed with LastPass.
- Steve will keep tweeting but create an email list for listeners; he is paid up on Twitter for a year. A maintained HashCheck fork supporting SHA-256 and BLAKE3 exists on GitHub (grc.sc/hashcheck). The librarian behind the previous episode's question turned it into a "Stochastic Pseudonymizer" for roughly 400,000 patrons.
- Apple patched three iOS zero-days (CVE-2023-41991, -41992, -41993) found by Citizen Lab and Google TAG after Ahmed Eltantawy, a former Egyptian MP planning to run for president, suspected his phone. When he visited plain-HTTP sites over Vodafone Egypt mobile data, a Sandvine PacketLogic middlebox on the Telecom Egypt/Vodafone Egypt interconnect matched on the Host and User-Agent headers and issued a 307 redirect to a Predator delivery site; earlier SMS lures posing as WhatsApp login alerts were also sent. Citizen Lab concludes a single non-HTTPS visit can lead to infection.
- Steve concedes HTTPS would have blocked the injection and floats restricting scripting and iframes on plain-HTTP pages rather than removing HTTP.
