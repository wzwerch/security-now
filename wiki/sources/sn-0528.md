# Summary

Steve and Leo spend the episode on a week of heavy news. Patreon lost a snapshot of its production database after a publicly reachable development server ran a Python debugger; Experian lost credit applications of about 15 million T-Mobile customers; and Scottrade disclosed a breach affecting 4.6 million customers. A second raft of critical StageFright media-library flaws surfaced in Android, with no real mitigation beyond waiting for patches. Symantec's write-up of Linux.Wifatch, a "vigilante" IoT worm that infects routers with default credentials and then hardens them, prompted a discussion of the ethics of immunizing worms. Steve also relayed early bugs in VeraCrypt 1.15, VeriSign's new free public DNS, the economics of ad blocking and anti-adblock notices, and a roundup of Android Marshmallow security improvements. He closed with Nadim Kobeissi's point that the Volkswagen emissions cheat shows how closed-source security code could behave differently depending on context without anyone noticing.

# Key points

- Picture of the week: an analysis claimed mobile carriers earn far more from the data used by ads than publishers earn from them (one cited figure: consumers pay about 16.6 times more in data costs than the top 50 news sites earn in ad revenue); a news-site load went from roughly 1.7 MB to 6.2 MB without an ad blocker, mostly due to scripts.
- Patreon breach: attackers reached a development server exposed to the internet that ran the Werkzeug debugger bound to all interfaces instead of localhost; the server held a recent copy of the production database. About 15 GB of data (passwords hashed with bcrypt, donation records, source code) was published; Troy Hunt added roughly 2.3 million emails to Have I Been Pwned. Detectify had reportedly warned the company days earlier.
- Shodan is a scanner-based search engine indexing server banners, which is how exposed debuggers, webcams and Telnet routers are found by both researchers and attackers.
- Experian/T-Mobile: credit-application data (names, birthdates, addresses, SSNs or ID numbers) for about 15 million applicants from Sept 2013 to Sept 2015 was taken; Experian offered two years of credit monitoring although the data is useful for life. Steve again recommends freezing credit at all three bureaus, noting it costs a fee.
- Scottrade: 4.6 million customers with accounts before Feb 2014 were affected; contact and identity data taken, login and trading platform data reportedly not.
- StageFright round two: critical remote-code-execution flaws in Android's media handling reachable via MMS, email or web media; Google said no known exploitation and patched Nexus devices first. Steve frames it as an attack-surface problem and notes Google's trend toward taking direct responsibility for updates.
- Linux.Wifatch: peer-to-peer router worm with no malicious payload that kills Telnet and other exposed services, removes other malware and spreads using weak or default credentials; tens of thousands of infected devices, mostly in China and Brazil. Its author said the goal was learning and security. Steve says such immunizing worms are illegal but this one's outcome seems benign.
- HOLA distributed VPN: a listener-driven follow-up says it contains remote-code-execution flaws and was being abused, adding to Steve's earlier warnings about exposing your IP as a peer.
- VeraCrypt 1.15 (the fix for the TrueCrypt driver flaw from the prior week) reportedly had usability bugs such as failing to delete directories on mounted volumes; Steve advises watching for fixes before migrating.
- F-Secure launched an iOS ad blocker without whitelisting; Steve says iOS blockers are trivial to build because of the content-blocker API.
- VeriSign launched a free public DNS service promising privacy and stability; Steve notes plain DNS is unencrypted and his own ISP's resolvers benchmarked fastest.
- Anti-adblock notices are spreading, with a third-party service selling "ad-block busting" to sites; Steve measured only about a 20 percent page-weight increase on one such site.
- Android 6.0 Marshmallow: boot verification warnings (yellow, orange, red), runtime per-permission control, a Google password store (Smart Lock), hardware-backed full-disk encryption on by default, a VPN settings page, a native fingerprint API (Nexus Imprint), app-level backup, voice control from the lock screen, visible security patch level, and encrypted adoptable SD storage.
- Nadim Kobeissi's "what the VW scandal means for encryption software": Steve extends it into "context-aware security", where unaudited closed-source crypto could weaken itself (for example a poor TLS client random) only in certain network contexts.

# Other segments

- SpinRite: a listener photo of DynaStat in action; Steve worries people use it only for emergency recovery and urges running it as maintenance; SpinRite 6.1 promised as a free update.
- Snowden's Twitter following and Steve's reaching 50,000 followers.
- Leo and a crew member joke about downloading every episode of the show with a loop of curl calls; Steve asks listeners to use full-bandwidth versions from the TWiT mirror.
