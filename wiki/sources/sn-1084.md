# Summary

The episode begins with the claim, reported by The Economist from Senate testimony, that the NSA found Mythos and Fable breached nearly all its classified systems within hours; Leo and Steve conclude that this shows buggy systems rather than superhuman AI. Steve then summarizes The Atlantic's "Assume You Will Be Hacked", which cites a fourfold rise in daily attacks (Palo Alto Networks), exploitation times falling from over 700 days in 2020 to 44 in 2025 (Moody's), and warnings from Alex Stamos, Mozilla's CTO and others. Steve's point is that finding bugs is necessary but not sufficient; fixes must reach deployed systems.

Next is Firefox 152, which fixed 40 flaws (13 high) credited to many human researchers even though Mythos had helped fix 400+ bugs in April; Steve attributes the misses to LLM non-determinism, differing prompts and models. Nightmare Eclipse struck another Patch Tuesday with RoguePlanet, a Microsoft Defender race-condition privilege escalation (CVE-2026-50656). "FortiBleed" is a trove of valid credentials for about 74,000 Fortinet firewalls gathered by a brute-force campaign. F5 issued emergency NGINX fixes. A new "AI Potpourri" section shows a listener's Agent Smith persona prompt. The main topic is residential proxy networks, drawn from a Wall Street Journal piece, followed by a Canadian court allowing CSIS to disrupt infected devices.

# Key points

- NSA claim: according to The Economist, the NSA director told Senator Warner Mythos broke into nearly all classified systems in hours; Steve and Leo see it as evidence of poorly secured systems, not of AI doing what skilled humans could not.
- The Atlantic piece: warns of AI-driven attack deluges, notes Meta's support AI handed over about 30,000 Instagram accounts (Meta said internal checks failed, not the AI), the Canvas breach by ShinyHunters, and Mozilla's estimate that the internet needs Y2K-scale upgrades. Leo notes the Instagram failure was a policy gap, not AI. Chatbot use now exceeds 50% of adults.
- Firefox 152 (16 June) fixed 13 high, 18 moderate and 9 low issues, including use-after-free and sandbox-escape bugs, also in ESR 140.12/115.37 and Thunderbird; updates apply automatically.
- Steve thinks Microsoft's MDASH system may be genuinely ahead of general-purpose models.
- RoguePlanet: a Defender race-condition SYSTEM-privilege exploit released on June Patch Tuesday with a proof of concept in a self-hosted Git repo; Microsoft assigned CVE-2026-50656 and acknowledged it without crediting the finder. Earlier Nightmare Eclipse leaks include BlueHammer, RedSun, GreenPlasma, MiniPlasma, YellowKey and UnDefend; Microsoft had hinted at legal action.
- FortiBleed: Bob Diachenko found an open server; Hudson Rock counted 73,932 firewall URLs in 194 countries and 21,632 domains. The attackers reportedly made 1.16 billion login attempts against 320,777 FortiGate devices and cracked captured hashes on a 45-GPU cluster. Kevin Beaumont verified the data and said many devices exposed management to the internet. Advice: rotate credentials, add MFA, hide management interfaces, per CISA also use PBKDF2 hashing.
- F5 emergency NGINX updates fixed two critical flaws (one mitigable by disabling HTTP/3) plus two NGINX Gateway Fabric issues; CISA lists seven exploited F5 bugs.
- AI Potpourri: a listener (Will) made Claude adopt an Agent Smith persona through the account "Instructions for Claude" field; Steve was surprised how thoroughly a personalization prompt reshapes behaviour. Leo mentions SOUL.md-style files and custom voices.
- Residential proxies: according to the WSJ, Comcast investigated six IPs given by Microsoft after the Midnight Blizzard breach and found a network of about 750,000 addresses run by Chinese provider Ipidea, installed through pre-loaded streaming boxes, picture frames, apps and pirated games. An advocacy group estimates 20 million such backdoors in the US.
- Nation-state actors use these US home IPs to defeat geofencing, for example Midnight Blizzard using fake Teams meetings to steal Microsoft 365 credentials (Volexity). Google's January takedown of Ipidea was reversed within two weeks.
- Steve's advice: put IoT devices on an isolated network or guest WiFi with isolation on, test that they still work, or block their outbound traffic with static IPs.
- Canada's CSIS obtained a Federal Court warrant (spring 2024, 120 days, renewed once) to disrupt two botnets by removing compromised devices from Canada; the ruling was released only this month.
