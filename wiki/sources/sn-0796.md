# Summary

Chrome 87.0.4280.88 fixed eight issues. Ransomware news includes the DoppelPaymer attack on Foxconn's Mexico facility (about 1,200 to 1,400 servers encrypted, 20 to 30 TB of backups deleted, a roughly 35 million dollar demand), Huntsville schools, Vancouver's transit system and Kmart (Egregor), and K12 paying a Ryuk ransom. Steve describes Google Project Zero researcher Ian Beer's six-month effort producing a wormable zero-click iPhone exploit over Wi-Fi via Apple's AWDL protocol, patched in iOS 13.5. Oblivious DoH, a Cloudflare/Apple/Fastly design adding a proxy so resolvers cannot link queries to client IPs, is introduced. Check Point found popular Android apps still bundling a vulnerable Google Play Core Library months after the fix. Steve explains why the Apple M1 runs x86 code well, gives SpinRite/ReadSpeed progress and examples of SSD slowdowns, and then analyzes Amazon Sidewalk: a low-bandwidth neighborhood network on 900 MHz (LoRa) and Bluetooth Low Energy using layered encryption, which he judges well designed despite alarmist press coverage of its default opt-in.

# Key points

- Foxconn (about 172 billion dollars revenue, 800,000 staff) suffered a DoppelPaymer attack over Thanksgiving; 100 GB of unencrypted data was also taken.
- Egregor printed ransom notes on Vancouver transit printers; Huntsville City Schools told staff to keep district devices off; K12 Inc. paid Ryuk on insurance and promises from the criminals.
- Ian Beer's AWDL exploit: a buffer overflow in C++ kernel code parsing untrusted Wi-Fi frames, fully weaponized against an iPhone 11 Pro with a single bug; macOS shared the AWDL code but had unstripped symbols, which helped. Steve notes it was wormable but fixed in May 2020 and argues big vendors devote few resources to code audits.
- ODoH: client encrypts queries for the DoH resolver and sends them through an independent proxy, so the proxy sees who but not what, and the resolver sees what but not who; spec moving through the IETF, with Firefox interested.
- Google Play Core Library: bug let a malicious app inject code into apps using the library; fixed in April 2020 but Check Point found 14 apps (nearly 850 million downloads, including Viber, Booking, Edge, Grindr, OkCupid) still vulnerable; 13% of Play apps use the library and 8% of those were outdated. Steve says developers must update bundled libraries.
- Tesla key fob researcher quote: the design had what it needed for security but small mistakes allowed circumvention.
- Apple M1: runs translated x86 well partly because Apple added an Intel-style memory-ordering mode to the CPU, plus JavaScript-specific instructions and larger caches.
- ReadSpeed examples: a stable Samsung SSD vs. an SSD with dramatic slow spots; verbose mode warns when a 6 Gb drive is on a 3 Gb link; InitDisk 2 fixes a Windows 10 permissions issue and 4K cluster sizing. Throughput near 544 MB/s means a half-terabyte drive scans in about 15 minutes.
- Amazon Sidewalk: gateways (such as Echo and Ring devices), endpoints (sensors, locks, pet trackers), Amazon's Sidewalk Network Server and manufacturers' application servers exchange small messages over 900 MHz (LoRa or FSK) or BLE. Bandwidth is capped at 80 kbps and 500 MB per month per customer. Three encryption layers (application end-to-end, Sidewalk layer, and a gateway Flex layer) blind each party to the others; transmission IDs rotate every 15 minutes; routing data is kept about 24 hours. Existing devices are opted in by over-the-air update after an email notice, new devices ask during setup. Steve sees no reason to disable it but notes real-world implementation still needs scrutiny.
