# Summary

A grab-bag episode. Firefox and Chrome are starting to distrust Symantec-chained certificates (Firefox 63 and Chrome 70 stable in October), and several large sites had not yet replaced theirs; DigiCert took over Symantec's CA business. Research called BygoneSSL shows that a valid certificate issued to a domain's previous owner can still exist after the domain changes hands, a weakness amplified by broken revocation. Epic Games' sideloaded Fortnite installer for Android was vulnerable to the man-in-the-disk attack; Google disclosed it quickly and Epic complained. A new critical Apache Struts remote-code-execution flaw demanded immediate upgrades, the Sora Mirai variant gained cross-compilation reach, and a researcher published an unpatched Windows ALPC local privilege-escalation zero day. Turla malware was found using PDF attachments in email as a command channel, antivirus vendors reacted to Chrome's anti-injection stance, Tavis Ormandy found more critical Ghostscript bugs, a single-trace EM attack recovered RSA keys from OpenSSL, OpenSSH leaked valid usernames, and a big USENIX study showed thousands of undocumented AT commands in Android firmware.

# Key points

- Symantec distrust schedule: Firefox nightly already; Chrome 70 stable October 16 and Firefox 63 stable October 23; sites cited by Mozilla included a game console store and several banks. DigiCert's acquisition moved it to third by market share.
- BygoneSSL: of 1.5 million certificates predating a domain's current registration, about 25 percent had not expired; new owners cannot rely on revocation, so they should check Certificate Transparency.
- Fortnite for Android: installer staged the APK in shared external storage; any app with storage write permission could swap it, and on Samsung devices install was silent; Epic released v2.1 and criticized Google's short disclosure window.
- Apache Struts: new remote-code-execution flaw in the core (fixed in 2.3.35 and 2.5.17), exploitation expected quickly, echoing the Equifax breach.
- Mirai/Sora uses the Aboriginal Linux cross-compiler toolchain to try many architecture builds, jumping to Android and Debian; infections rose sharply from June to August.
- SandboxEscaper published an ALPC local privilege-escalation zero day working on fully patched 64-bit Windows 10; CERT confirmed it; the fix was expected on the September Patch Tuesday.
- Turla (ESET report): an Outlook and The Bat! backdoor DLL reads weaponized PDF attachments as commands and forwards outgoing mail to the attackers.
- Bitdefender, Avast/AVG and others dropped in-browser exploit monitoring in Chrome rather than trigger crash warnings; Steve suggests Google offer sanctioned hooks.
- Ghostscript: Ormandy reported multiple remote-code-execution bugs, with no patch yet, and advised disabling PS/EPS/PDF/XPS coders in ImageMagick's policy.xml.
- "One and Done" (Georgia State): electromagnetic emanations from a phone or embedded board recover OpenSSL RSA exponent bits from one decryption, fixed in OpenSSL by grouping exponent bits.
- OpenSSH username enumeration: malformed authentication packets get different replies for valid users, present since 2000; key-based login avoids password guessing.
- AT commands: researchers extracted about 3,500 AT commands from over 2,000 Android firmware images; many can rewrite firmware, unlock screens and inject touch over USB, even in charge-only mode; Pixel 2 not vulnerable.
