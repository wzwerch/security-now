# Summary

Steve starts with complaints that the restored Fable 5 falls back to Opus 4.8 too often because Anthropic added keyword-style cyber safeguards with a large safety margin after the US export-control episode. He argues that control of powerful LLMs is the unsolved next problem and should not come at the cost of capability. Opera introduces Paste Protect against ClickFix attacks, which Huntress said drove over half of malware loader activity in 2025; Steve wants Windows to block pasting browser-originated clipboard content instead. CISA confirms ransomware use of the Defender BlueHammer privilege escalation, patched on 14 April. Vint Cerf retires from Google and predicts agentic AI will force formal interoperability standards.

Chrome 150 shipped with 433 security fixes, 20 of them critical, which Steve reads as AI-assisted code audits spreading, and he encourages listeners to try bug bounty hunting with AI while it is still easy. News items cover the EU court upholding Google's Android antitrust fine, the looming Chat Control vote, and research that found six bugs in AirDrop and Android Quick Share. DeepKeep's InkJect shows hidden image instructions bypassing OpenAI and Anthropic guardrails. Steve describes SpinRite restoring a slow laptop SSD. The closing topic is runZero's seven FatFs vulnerabilities found with GitHub Copilot, with HD Moore calling the era that of the "apex agentic adversary".

# Key points

- Restored Fable 5 is available on subscription at reduced cost until 12 July, but users report frequent fallback to Opus 4.8 on security-adjacent words; BleepingComputer says the model is not weakened, only the safeguards are stricter. Leo estimates one month of his use would cost about $5,000 at Fable API prices versus about $117 on DeepSeek.
- Steve's thesis: current LLMs resist control; commercial providers must now invest in accurate usage control while capability continues to grow. Foreign open models lack such guardrails, which can also expose users to indirect attacks.
- Opera Paste Protect (first browser to ship it, default on) warns when sites place suspicious commands on the clipboard; Opera has about 2% share. ClickFix caused over 53% of malware loader activity in 2025 per Huntress.
- BlueHammer (Nightmare Eclipse leak, early April) is a Defender local privilege escalation to SYSTEM via the SAM database; patched 14 April, added to CISA KEV on 22 April and now flagged as used by ransomware gangs. Steve is baffled that unpatched systems remain.
- Vint Cerf (83) retires from Google; he expects agent ecosystems to demand precise standards rather than natural language. Leo cites MCP as an example.
- Chrome 150 fixed 433 security issues, mostly use-after-free and memory bugs; one ANGLE issue earned a $250,000 bounty. Steve sees many vendors (such as Synology) shipping unusually large fix batches, an AI-driven Y2K-style remediation wave.
- Chrome 150 also adds ML-DSA post-quantum signatures for TLS, FIDO credential exchange on Android and an "Always use secure connections" mode.
- The EU's top court upheld Google's roughly 4.1 billion euro Android fine; Steve learned that fines are held in escrow during appeals.
- Chat Control 2.0 (mandatory scanning) faces another vote; Steve sees the privacy contradiction as irresolvable, and Leo expects Signal to leave the EU if it passes.
- "Protocol Prying" research found six bugs: three AirDrop pre-authentication denial-of-service issues, two Samsung Quick Share flaws including a D2D encryption bypass, and a use-after-free in Quick Share for Windows (bounty paid); vendors acknowledged the reports.
- InkJect (DeepKeep): instructions hidden in an image as white-on-white or skewed text evade OCR defenses and made GPT-5.2, GPT-5.4 Mini, Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.5 create an attacker admin account during a repository deployment. Steve's advice: be vigilant about what an AI retrieves autonomously.
- SpinRite 6.1 on a Dell laptop SSD: the start of the drive read at 13 MB/s versus 570 MB/s elsewhere; after a level 3 pass the boot became fast. Steve then ran Windows' Optimize to re-TRIM the SSD.
- FatFs: runZero (HD Moore, Tod Beardsley) published seven CVEs (CVSS up to 7.6) in the ubiquitous embedded FAT/exFAT library, used by ESP-IDF, STM32Cube, Zephyr, MicroPython, ArduPilot and others; triggerable by a crafted SD card, USB stick or update image. The maintainer did not respond, so they disclosed and warned downstream users.
- HD Moore argued that keeping such bugs quiet in the "age of the apex agentic adversary" would be security theater.
