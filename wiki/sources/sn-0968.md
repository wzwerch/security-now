# Summary

The show leads with a Linux kernel local privilege escalation (versions 5.14 to 6.6.14, CVSS 7.8, public proof of concept after two months of patches), urging anyone who has not updated since February to do so. The AT&T leak is revisited: a dataset of about 73 million records (names, addresses, phone numbers, SSNs, birth dates) circulated for years, AT&T finally reset about 7.6 million account passcodes after TechCrunch showed the four-digit passcodes were encrypted with a fixed, unsalted scheme so only 10,000 distinct ciphertexts existed and could be mapped to plaintext. Short items cover Signal's planned optional cloud backups, Telegram letting users in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus restrict who can message them, HP leaving Russia early, Ghostery's survey of ad-blocker use, Google deleting billions of Incognito browsing records under a class-action settlement, and more malicious packages on npm, PyPI and Canonical's app store. Steve recounts running SpinRite on his wife's slow SSD laptop and getting 15-second boots, explained by cell voltage drift and heavy error correction. The main topic is the XZ backdoor: a persona "Jia Tan" gained maintainer trust over about two years, hid a payload in test binaries, and targeted sshd via the systemd-linked liblzma, found only when Andres Freund investigated a small SSH latency regression. Steve calls it a near-catastrophe and expects more such attempts.

# Key points

- Linux kernel local root exploit (affects Debian, Ubuntu, Red Hat, Fedora and others) with a reported 99.4 percent success rate; patches had been available since late January.
- AT&T: about 7.6 million current and 65.4 million former account holders affected; Troy Hunt verified the data (49 million unique emails, 44 million SSNs); AT&T still cannot say how it leaked. Fixed unsalted encryption of 4-digit passcodes made recovery trivial by correlating with other fields.
- Signal is developing end-to-end encrypted optional cloud backups (free tier with 30 days of media, paid tier up to 1 TB).
- Telegram adds the ability to restrict who may message you after a wave of terror-spam messages.
- HP completed its Russia withdrawal two months early.
- Ghostery/Censuswide survey: ad-blocker use is 52 percent of Americans, 66 percent of experienced advertisers, 72 percent of programmers and 76 percent of cybersecurity professionals; privacy is the top motive. Steve views Chrome's Privacy Sandbox as a major shift that will take time to be accepted.
- Google's Incognito settlement: delete billions of records, strip identifying fields, remove the X-Client-Data header, block third-party cookies in Incognito for five years; internal staff had called the mode misleading.
- Repositories: eight new malicious npm packages, PyPI temporarily halted sign-ups and uploads after a malware wave, Canonical began manual review of Ubuntu store apps after fake crypto-wallet submissions.
- SpinRite on SSDs: slow reads come from degraded cell voltage requiring error correction, a rewrite refreshes cells; SpinRite 7 aims to target slow spots; one refresh per year is reasonable.
- XZ Utils 5.6.0 and 5.6.1 (February 2024) contained an obfuscated backdoor reaching sshd via the distro-patched systemd to liblzma link, allowing a holder of a specific key to run code pre-authentication. Jia Tan (JiaT75) built trust from 2021 to 2023, helped by sock-puppet accounts pressuring burned-out maintainer Lasse Collin, and asked oss-fuzz to disable a check. The payload was hidden in compression test files and build scripts.
- Reached Fedora Rawhide/41, Debian testing and unstable, openSUSE Tumbleweed, Kali, Arch and others, plus Homebrew and OpenWrt; not yet in stable production releases.
- Discovered by Microsoft engineer Andres Freund who traced extra SSH CPU time and valgrind errors; experts (Valsorda, Zalewski, Beaumont) call it a likely state-sponsored, professional operation and a near-miss comparable to SolarWinds.
- Steve's lesson: asymmetry favours attackers, and with one malicious maintainer anything can be subverted; more near misses are coming.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: wire wound around plug pins as a makeshift power strip.
- Listener-style anecdote of Steve's wife's EEG laptop (QEEG biofeedback practice) restored by SpinRite.
- Chrome left idle consumed half of Steve's CPU after a translation page.
