# Summary

Mikah Sargent hosts while Leo is on vacation. Steve first follows up on the listener who reported an outdated nginx on a huge company's site: CERT declined the case because it avoids live-website reports and the vendor was cooperating, so Steve now suggests the listener take the bounty and move on. Broadcom/VMware patched four serious USB-controller flaws allowing guest-to-hypervisor escape, Microsoft disclosed on a Friday afternoon that Russian group Midnight Blizzard had reached internal systems and source code and used secrets found in stolen email, and China's secret "Document 79" orders state firms to replace foreign IT by 2027. Signal 7 hides phone numbers by default and adds optional usernames, while Meta explains how it will meet the EU Digital Markets Act with Signal-protocol-based interoperability. The Change Healthcare attack is reviewed: an affiliate of ALPHV/BlackCat, roughly 6 TB of data, a reported $22 million payment and a probable exit scam by BlackCat. After SpinRite news and feedback (Telegram's non-default E2E, email-only login as liability shifting, address-masking services being blocked, a tool to detect Chrome extension ownership changes), the main topic explains that passkeys, being public-key based and holding no server-side secrets, beat password plus MFA, provided old password logins are disabled.

# Key points

- CERT/CC declined the nginx-vulnerability report because it normally avoids live websites; Steve notes researchers lack the legal backing that Google has for 90-day disclosure clocks.
- VMware advisory VMSA-2024-0006: use-after-free bugs in UHCI and XHCI USB virtual controllers (CVSS up to 9.3) plus an information leak; patches issued even for out-of-support versions; removing the virtual USB controller is a stopgap.
- Microsoft: Midnight Blizzard (Nobelium, SVR) accessed internal systems and source repositories and password-spray volume grew tenfold in February; Steve criticises the pattern of Friday-afternoon disclosures.
- China's "Document 79" ("Delete A") directs state-owned firms to replace US and other foreign hardware and software by 2027, affecting Cisco, IBM, Dell, Microsoft and Oracle.
- Signal 7: phone number hidden by default, optional unique usernames, optional setting to prevent discovery by phone number; a phone number is still required to register.
- EU DMA interoperability: Meta will let third-party messengers connect to WhatsApp and Messenger if they use the Signal protocol or demonstrate equivalent security; year one covers one-to-one text and files.
- Change Healthcare (UnitedHealth): attack began 21 February 2024; processes about 15 billion transactions yearly and touches one in three patient records; BlackCat affiliate "Notchy" claimed Optum paid 350 BTC (about $22 million) and was cheated of its share as BlackCat staged a fake law-enforcement takedown; lawsuits followed.
- BlackCat lineage: DarkSide, BlackMatter, then BlackCat; exit scams damage affiliate recruitment.
- SpinRite: forthcoming downloadable boot images so Linux users need no Windows; the Windows USB-detection method does not work under WINE.
- Telegram: default chats use only transport encryption, end-to-end "secret chats" are per chat, one-to-one only, so Steve now agrees with Apple's Level 0 label.
- KeePassXC added passkey support; password managers are becoming cross-platform passkey stores.
- Email-only login may be motivated by liability avoidance; only 34% of people use a password manager (21% in 2022), and the 23andMe breach showed credential stuffing; ad-industry pressure to collect confirmed emails after third-party cookie deprecation worsens this.
- Sites increasingly reject masked-email services such as duck.com and strip plus-addressing.
- "Under New Management" extension flags Chrome extension developer changes; the Hover Zoom+ author has logged more than 165 purchase offers, illustrating why extensions get sold to abusers (Avast was accused by the FTC of harvesting browsing data).
- Passkeys vs 2FA: TOTP and password hashes are shared secrets stored by the site and can be phished through a man-in-the-middle proxy or stolen in a breach; passkeys store only a public key that merely verifies a signed unique challenge. Advice: use passkeys when offered, disable or neutralise password fallback where possible, add MFA to remaining passwords, and replace the stored password with a bogus value.
- FIDO hardware keys do the same public-key operation as passkeys (passkeys are FIDO2).
