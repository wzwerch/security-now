# Summary

The last episode of year 15 starts with the Check Point "Achilles" research: over 400 vulnerabilities tied to Qualcomm's Hexagon DSP SDK affect roughly a billion Android phones, with details withheld. August 2020 Patch Tuesday fixed 120 flaws, the third-largest ever, including two zero-days. Mozilla laid off 250 more staff, though Leo reports the Google search deal was renewed; Canon's data was leaked by the Maze gang, suggesting no ransom was paid. California's COVID reporting glitch was an expired TLS certificate. Microsoft added Control Flow Guard support to Rust and LLVM, Threema added end-to-end encrypted video calls, and Steve explains why SpinRite cannot rely on the read-verify command. The main story is Microsoft's delays: a privilege escalation flaw sat for six months until ZDI published it and was chained in Operation PowerFall with an IE flaw, and the "GlueBall" signature bypass, reported two years earlier and dismissed, was finally fixed after being exploited.

# Key points

- Achilles: Check Point examined Snapdragon's compute DSP; code signed by Qualcomm can be bypassed and flaws in the Hexagon SDK propagate to hundreds of libraries; media files such as MP3 or JPEG processed by the DSP could trigger them. Details withheld; older phones will not be patched. Steve advises treating a phone as having about a three-year security horizon (Samsung and Google now promise three years of updates).
- August Patch Tuesday: 120 fixes, 17 critical; exploited flaws were an IE scripting-engine memory corruption (reached via Office documents) and the signature spoofing bug; also Media Foundation and .NET RCE fixes. Mood: June 129, July 123, August 120.
- Mozilla: 250 additional layoffs (Servo developers and the threat management team); the Google default-search contract had been 90% of revenue; Leo reports the deal was extended to 2023. Firefox Send remains offline.
- Canon: Maze published a 2.2 GB archive of stolen material, implying Canon did not pay; Steve notes double extortion (encrypt plus leak).
- California COVID testing backlog: the upload server's certificate had expired. Steve renews his revoked.grc.com test certificate before the 1 September one-year-plus-a-month limit.
- Microsoft's Control Flow Guard now exists in Rust and LLVM front ends: a per-process bitmap of valid indirect-call targets stops jumps into the middle of functions, hindering ROP-style attacks; the Windows kernel has used it since the 1703 release.
- Threema: WebRTC-based end-to-end encrypted video calls with metadata fixes contributed upstream; no phone number is required; Leo questions Signal's phone-number requirement.
- SpinRite: GRC's newsgroup server is free to use and isolated from global Usenet; the SpinRite 6.1 beta will be open to owners. Tests showed many drives mishandle read-verify and "mark unreadable", so SpinRite will not use verify and will run at SATA III speed.
- Operation PowerFall (Kaspersky): a South Korean target was attacked with an IE11 RCE chained to a print spooler privilege escalation Microsoft sat on for six months after ZDI's report; attackers acted the day after ZDI went public. Microsoft patched the escalation in June and the IE flaw in August.
- GlueBall (CVE-2020-1464): appending data, such as a JAR, to a signed MSI keeps the Authenticode signature valid, so the file passes signature checks and Java still runs it. VirusTotal's founder reported it on 18 August 2018; Microsoft declined to fix; it was patched in 2020 only after exploitation. Leo guesses MSI appending existed for localization data.
