# Summary

Steve frames the episode around the pandemic's effect on everything. The long-time vulnerability publisher SandboxEscaper has been hired by Microsoft. Zoom keeps responding impressively: it hired Luta Security (Katie Moussouris), added advisors including Matthew Green and Lea Kissner plus audit firms, added stronger password rules, regional datacenter selection and abuse reporting, and promised to move from AES-ECB to AES-GCM. Meanwhile Zoom meeting credentials are sold cheaply and two alleged Zoom zero-days were reported on the exploit market. Chrome fixed a critical speech-recognition use-after-free, and Google restored FTP support temporarily. April's Patch Tuesday fixed 113 flaws including three exploited zero-days that Windows 7 users will not receive. Microsoft and Google postponed Basic Authentication shutdowns. Signal's spokesman criticized the EARN IT bill, the Tor Project laid off staff, and Cloudflare replaced reCAPTCHA with hCaptcha. Steve praises VirtualBox and reports first working AHCI "identify device" results for the next SpinRite.

# Key points

- SandboxEscaper, who had published many Windows zero-days, joined Microsoft.
- Zoom: Luta Security engaged to rebuild vulnerability disclosure; additions to the advisory group (Alex Stamos, Matthew Green, Lea Kissner; Bishop Fox, NCC Group, Trail of Bits); weekly CEO webinars; stronger meeting password options; region choice for data routing; report-abuser feature; plan to adopt AES-256-GCM and later a new crypto design.
- About 500,000 Zoom meeting IDs and passwords were reported for sale at a tiny price each; two Zoom client zero-days (Windows, macOS) were reportedly offered at an asking price of $500,000, with brokers and experts doubting the value. Zoom said it found no evidence.
- Chrome 81.0.4044.113 fixed a critical speech recognizer use-after-free reported by Qihoo 360's Alpha Lab.
- Chrome reinstated default FTP support because government sites such as NIH still use FTP; Google had wanted it removed for years.
- April 2020 Patch Tuesday: 113 fixes, 19 critical, including three exploited zero-days (two in the Adobe Type library, one kernel privilege escalation found by Project Zero) and a scripting engine bug in Internet Explorer. Windows 7 without extended support stays unpatched; the 0patch micropatch service is an option. The SMBGhost bug was demonstrated as a remote code execution exploit by Ricerca Security.
- Basic Authentication: Microsoft postponed turning it off for Exchange Online until at least the second half of 2021; Google deferred disabling "less secure apps"; both push OAuth 2.0.
- Signal's position on EARN IT: making Section 230 protection depend on "best practices" overseen by the Attorney General would effectively target end-to-end encryption; users are urged to contact legislators.
- Tor Project cut staff from 35 to 22 due to falling donations.
- Cloudflare moved from Google reCAPTCHA to hCaptcha, citing privacy, China reachability and the cost Google planned to charge; Cloudflare pays hCaptcha and offloads load via Workers.
- SpinRite 6.1 progress: SpinTest successfully ran an AHCI identify-device query on a VirtualBox VM and a physical laptop; SpinRite will bypass the BIOS for storage access. Next week: Cloudflare's RPKI routing test.
- Steve switched from VMware to VirtualBox, praising VBoxManage (raw disk access, image creation).
- Listener Q&A: hiding Firefox's top tab bar when using Tree Style Tabs via a stylesheet; concern that third-party contact tracing apps seeing diagnosis keys could correlate location, which Steve says is why he prefers the platform-provided apps.
