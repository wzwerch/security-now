# Summary

The episode opens with the Bishop Fox DEF CON talk "You're Doing IoT RNG": IoT firmware pervasively fails to check error returns from hardware random number generators, so devices that run short of entropy silently get zeros or stale values, undermining keys and TLS. Steve then covers VPN trouble, namely a bypass of an earlier Ivanti Pulse Connect Secure fix and two serious Cisco small-business router web interface flaws, followed by the 14 INFRA:HALT vulnerabilities in the NicheStack TCP/IP library used by 200+ operational technology vendors. Microsoft's experimental "Super Duper Secure Mode" in Edge, which disables the JIT compiler, is presented as a promising trade of a little performance for a large cut in browser exploit surface. After listener feedback (Tailscale enthusiasm, a 2016 episode about the same hosts-file workaround), the main segment explains the two separate Apple child-safety features: CSAM Detection for iCloud Photos using NeuralHash, and opt-in Communication Safety in Messages for child accounts. Steve and Leo discuss the privacy-line crossing that on-device scanning represents, the EFF's slippery-slope objection, and the possibility of government pressure behind Apple's move.

# Key points

- IoT RNG failure: hardware RNG peripherals produce entropy slowly; code calling the HAL (MediaTek, FreeRTOS examples with thousands of GitHub hits) rarely checks for failure, so keys may be generated from zeros, partial entropy or uninitialised memory. The researchers claim around 35 billion devices are affected.
- Steve explains why an entropy pool plus a CSPRNG is the proper design and why Diffie-Hellman key exchange needs unpredictable private values.
- Ivanti Pulse Connect Secure: a patch bypass of CVE-2020-8260 (archive extraction leading to root RCE) after six new flaws were fixed on August 2, 2021; update to 9.1R12 or later and the follow-up fix.
- Cisco RV340/345 (CVSS 9.8) and RV160/260 (8.2) router web-management flaws; remote WAN management is off by default, but the LAN interface cannot be disabled. Steve: never expose admin web interfaces to the Internet.
- INFRA:HALT (Forescout and JFrog): 14 flaws in NicheStack (InterNiche, 1996, now HCC Embedded) including missing DNS length and compression-pointer checks and an HTTP POST heap overflow; fixed in v4.3; over 6,400 devices were visible on Shodan, and millions more sit inside industrial networks. Steve introduces "operational technology" (OT) as the term to use.
- Edge Super Duper Secure Mode: about 45% of V8 CVEs relate to the JIT, and over half of exploited Chrome bugs abuse it; Microsoft found disabling JIT rarely hurt performance on modern CPUs. Available only in pre-release builds at the time.
- Apple CSAM Detection: NeuralHash of images bound for iCloud Photos is matched on the device against NCMEC-provided hashes; a threshold of matches is required before Apple can review, and Apple then reports to NCMEC. It stops if iCloud Photos is off.
- Communication Safety in Messages: separate, opt-in for child accounts in a family group, on-device image classifier that blurs explicit images and warns the child; parents are notified only for children 12 and under.
- Debate: EFF and Matthew Green call it a backdoor and fear scope creep; Leo notes other providers scan cloud uploads (Facebook reported about 20 million NCMEC reports versus Apple's 265), but scanning on the device crosses a line. Steve suspects behind-the-scenes government pressure.
