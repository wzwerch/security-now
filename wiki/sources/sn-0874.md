# Summary

Steve opens with ServiceNSW's official reply to the criticism of its Digital Driver's Licence, which calls the tampering known, harmless to backend data, illegal and independently assessed, while missing the point that the on-screen licence cannot be trusted. ExpressVPN removed its India servers ahead of a CERT-In directive requiring VPN providers to log customer data for five years; several countries (Syria and others) cut off national internet during school exams. Follina (CVE-2022-30190) is now exploited by a likely state-aligned actor, and a second Office-triggered handler, search-ms:, can be abused. Other items: a UNISOC cellular modem denial-of-service flaw Steve rates as overblown, Mandiant's finding that Evil Corp affiliates moved to LockBit to dodge sanctions, and leaked Conti chats showing research into Intel Management Engine and UEFI implants. After errata and feedback, the main topic is Steve's reaction to Apple's WWDC passkey announcement: FIDO2 passkeys use random per-site key pairs that must be synchronized through a vendor cloud, risking platform lock-in, unlike SQRL's single master key. His advice: wait for cross-platform support, preferably via a third-party password manager.

# Key points

- ServiceNSW response: says tampering only affects the user's own device, police use a real-time check against the DRIVES back end, and altering a licence is illegal. Steve notes the offline display is therefore untrustworthy; listeners say the app has animated effects that defeat screenshots, but that is not security.
- India's CERT-In "Cyber Security Directions": VPN providers must keep names, contacts and IPs for at least five years and report incidents within six hours, effective June 27, 2022; ExpressVPN responded with virtual India servers physically in Singapore and the UK. Steve sees it as part of a broader government push against private communications.
- Exam-time shutdowns: Syria (via BGP withdrawal, four 3.5-hour outages), Iraq, India, Algeria, Jordan and Sudan cut internet to stop cheating.
- Follina follow-up: Proofpoint blocked 1,000+ phishing messages with an RTF lure aimed at European and US government entities; payload was a PowerShell downloader with VM checks and credential theft. 0patch issued an unofficial micropatch; Microsoft still advised disabling the protocol.
- search-ms: handler: with an Office OLE flaw (Matthew Hickey) a Word document can open an Explorer search window listing attacker-hosted files, suppressing the usual confirmation. Microsoft's reply was to advise safe computing habits, which Steve calls missing the point of zero-click handler abuse.
- UNISOC modem chipset buffer overflow (CVSS 9.4) found by Check Point: a malformed packet can crash the radio but no code execution was shown; UNISOC holds about 11% of SoC market. Steve argues important proprietary components should be open to review.
- Mandiant "To Hades and Back": UNC2165, overlapping with Evil Corp, switched from custom ransomware families to LockBit RaaS to hinder attribution and evade OFAC sanctions; sanctions make victims unable to pay named groups.
- Leaked Conti chats (June 2021): member researched Intel Management Engine/AMT, SPI flash writing and SMM implants, reaching proof-of-concept UEFI persistence beyond OS reinstall or disk replacement. Firmware is rarely updated, and an updatable BIOS can be modified by malware.
- Triple encryption with three 256-bit keys yields 2^768 keys, an enormous increase though real strength is limited by key entropy.
- Passkeys critique: FIDO2 creates random key pairs per site, needs cloud sync, risks vendor lock-in (Apple, Google, Microsoft not yet interoperable); SQRL derived per-site keys from one master key via keyed hash of the domain name. Per-site keys preserve unlinkability. Advice: wait for cross-platform support or a third-party password manager. Apple demoed a QR-code flow for signing in on another device.
