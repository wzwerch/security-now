# Summary

Recorded on the show's 20th birthday, the episode begins by extending the previous week's Perplexity discussion: AI answer engines and Google AI Overviews are producing "zero-click" behaviour that undermines the ad-funded web, and nobody yet knows what replaces it. News follows: Plex tells users to update Media Server to fix a bug-bounty-reported flaw; the Allianz Life Salesforce data (about 2.8 million records including Social Security numbers) was leaked by the ShinyHunters/Scattered Spider/Lapsus$ collective, which gained access by tricking staff into authorising a malicious OAuth app. Chrome 140 (due 2 September) adds script blocking in Incognito for domains on the Masked Domain List, new Base64/hex helpers for JavaScript and a permission prompt for requests to the local network. Further items: data brokers hiding opt-out pages from search engines, Russia restricting WhatsApp and Telegram calls while pushing the state messenger Max, NIST finalising the Ascon-based lightweight cryptography standard (SP 800-232), and Syncthing 2.0. The main topic, Scriptcase, is a low-code PHP generator whose "production console" had an unauthenticated admin-password reset chained with a command injection (CVE-2025-47227 and CVE-2025-47228). Steve's conclusion: bugs and attackers are permanent, so anything not designed to be publicly reachable should never be exposed to the Internet, regardless of its login page.

# Key points

- AI and web economics: Forbes-cited figures say about 60% of searches end without a click and AI Overviews can cut organic traffic 15-64%; a Contrary Research piece argues consumers will decide the outcome. Leo notes AI needs creators to be paid and discusses the TWiT Club as a response to falling ad support.
- Plex Media Server versions 1.41.7.x to 1.42.0.x have an undisclosed flaw found through the bug-bounty programme; fixed in 1.42.1.10060 and Plex emailed affected users. Reminder that an unpatched home Plex server was the entry point for the 2022 LastPass breach.
- Allianz Life: ShinyHunters leaked the Salesforce Accounts/Contacts tables (about 2.8 million records with names, addresses, birth dates and tax IDs). The campaign relied on social engineering employees into linking a malicious OAuth app; the gang told BleepingComputer that ShinyHunters and Scattered Spider are the same crew, with Telegram channel "ScatteredLapsuSp1d3rHunters" claiming other breaches (Internet Archive, Pearson, Coinbase). Steve's advice: design networks that assume some insiders will be fooled; Leo points to zero trust and canaries.
- Chrome 140: Incognito will block scripts from domains on the Masked Domain List (maintained by Disconnect, hosted on GitHub), which already routes those domains' requests through Google privacy proxies to hide the user's IP; Steve contrasts this list-based approach with Brave's fingerprint randomisation. Also adds Uint8Array Base64/hex conversion and a Local Network Access permission prompt to protect routers from drive-by requests; DNS rebinding protection has largely vanished from public resolvers (observed by DNS Benchmark testers).
- The Markup/CalMatters: 35 of 499 registered California data brokers used no-index code on their deletion pages; several removed it after being contacted, two kept it claiming spam avoidance.
- Russia: Roskomnadzor started restricting voice/video calls on WhatsApp and Telegram, reportedly after a petition from the four largest telcos; officials must set up channels on the state messenger Max.
- NIST SP 800-232 finalises Ascon: Ascon-128 AEAD, Ascon-Hash256, Ascon-XOF128 and Ascon-CXOF128, designed by Graz, Infineon and Radboud researchers in 2014 and chosen in 2023; aimed at IoT, RFID tags, implants and toll transponders. Steve explains AEAD (authenticated encryption with associated data) and the value of "right-sized" crypto.
- Syncthing 2.0: database moves from LevelDB to SQLite, structured logging, deleted-item records expire after 15 months by default, double-dash command-line options, three connections between v2 devices, no default folder, several platforms lose prebuilt binaries (including Windows on ARM). Steve and Leo stay on 1.30 for now ("sense of adventure" warning).
- Scriptcase (Synacktiv disclosure, 4 July 2025): unauthenticated reset of the production-console administrator password because a failed login still sets the session flag, combined with a shell command injection in the connection test feature. The vendor was slow to respond over about five months. VulnCheck (14 August) found roughly 2,800 exposed instances, 57% on vulnerable versions, and GreyNoise saw dozens of malicious IPs scanning for it.
- Steve's lessons: Scriptcase's changelog shows near-daily releases that train users to ignore updates; authentication cannot be trusted as the only protection; software not meant to serve the public (admin consoles, low-code tools) must sit behind VPNs/overlay networks rather than on the open Internet.
