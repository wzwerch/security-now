# Summary

The first episode of 2025 begins with a catalogue of US state laws demanding age verification and parental consent for social media (Connecticut, Louisiana, Texas, Maryland, Utah, Tennessee, Florida, Georgia and others), with an adult-content site going dark in Florida and industry groups suing on First Amendment grounds; Steve stresses that cyberspace has always been anonymous and the privacy question of proving age remains unsolved. The next story is a phishing attack on a Cyberhaven employee on Christmas Eve that let attackers publish a malicious version of the company's Chrome extension to steal Facebook sessions, part of a campaign that hit at least 35 extensions (over a million users). Steve then covers the persistence of the SonicWall SonicOS flaw CVE-2024-40766: about 48,900 devices were still unpatched in late December and the Akira and Fog ransomware groups appear to be exploiting it against more than 100 organizations. The Shadowserver Foundation reported about 3.3 million POP3 and IMAP servers (898,700 in the US) running without TLS, and Steve explains why Let's Encrypt style automation does not exist for mail servers and why shortening certificate lifetimes will make that worse. Shorter items: AT&T, Verizon and Lumen say they have evicted Salt Typhoon, HHS proposed HIPAA cybersecurity requirements, the EU mandated USB-C charging, and a DOOM-based CAPTCHA appeared. The main segment is Steve's summary of three weeks of AI study: neural-network pre-training, the 2017 Transformer and attention paper, and test-time inference chains behind OpenAI o1 and the announced o3, with the lesson that nothing true about AI yesterday stays true tomorrow and that he retracts his "Wizard of Oz" skepticism.

# Key points

- **Age-appropriate internet laws**: timeline of state laws (Connecticut SB 3, Louisiana Act 456, Texas HB 18, Maryland Kids Code, Utah, Tennessee HB 1891, Florida HB 3 with 50,000 dollar penalties, Georgia SB 351, Minnesota); NetChoice and CCIA sued Florida; Leo suggests some legislators actually want to push adult sites out and mentions camera-based age estimation (Yoti), which Steve finds spooky.
- **Cyberhaven extension compromise**: phishing email mimicking a Chrome Web Store policy warning led an employee, despite MFA and Advanced Protection, to authorize a malicious OAuth app; malicious version 24.10.4 ran for about 25 hours, stealing Facebook access tokens, ad and business account data; fixed in 24.10.5. Secure Annex linked it to a campaign since May 2024 against at least 35 extensions (examples include Reader Mode, YesCaptcha assistant and several AI helper extensions). Steve's advice: keep extensions to a minimum; he regards a password manager and uBlock Origin as the must-haves. Relevant to Manifest V3 debates.
- **SonicWall CVE-2024-40766**: improper access control in SonicOS (CVSS 9.8) disclosed August 2024; about 390,000 devices worldwide, 238,678 in the US; 48,933 still unpatched, 29,107 of them in the US; a Japanese researcher found 46 percent of Akira and Fog victims run SonicWall versus 5 percent for other groups. Steve urges monitored vendor notification mailboxes and calls for vendors to push patches to enterprise gear.
- **Unencrypted mail servers**: Shadowserver notified owners of about 3.3 million POP3 hosts (and similar IMAP) without TLS; US 898,700, Germany 560,900, Poland 388,000, Japan 294,000, Netherlands 137,300. ACME and Let's Encrypt do not cover mail servers; browsers forced HTTPS but nothing forces mail encryption; Steve is considering refusing unencrypted inbound mail at GRC and notes spammers favor port 25.
- **Salt Typhoon**: AT&T, Verizon and Lumen say they evicted the group; CISA's advice to use end-to-end encrypted apps such as Signal stands.
- **HIPAA update**: HHS proposed rulemaking adding mandatory encryption, MFA, network segmentation and vulnerability scanning.
- **EU USB-C**: common charger rule for phones and many devices from 28 December 2024 and laptops from 28 April 2026, with unbundled chargers to cut e-waste.
- **DOOM CAPTCHA**: a WebAssembly demo that asks users to kill three enemies; Leo notes that bots would do better.
- **AI pre-training vs test-time compute**: pre-training stores knowledge in billions of parameters via back-propagation (GPT-3 had 175 billion); Google's 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" Transformer paper made large-scale training practical; test-time computation (many queries, comparing results, chains of thought) boosts reliability and underlies o1 and the benchmarked o3; "temperature" noise explains varying answers.
- **Steve's revised view**: AI is moving faster than any field he has seen, o3 benchmarks show reasoning beyond most humans on unseen problems, corporate secrecy is increasing (even model sizes are no longer published), and neural networks are inefficient compared with what a future design might achieve; he recommends the 3Blue1Brown video series. Leo cites Rich Sutton's "bitter lesson" and Musk's lawsuit against OpenAI.

# Other segments

- Steve's DNS Benchmark rewrite: 32-bit IP assumptions removed, IPv6 working, DNS over TLS under way (DoH and DoQ planned).
- First Security Now! caption contest for the Picture of the Week (a locked gate in the middle of a field); GRC mailing list at 15,060 subscribers.
- Leo plans to rename This Week in Google to a show about intelligent machines.
- Steve reports he stays off sponsors topics; hardware aside that he works long coding stretches.
