# Summary

The episode is named after the "Something went wrong" page that Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Oculus showed during their roughly six-hour outage on 4 October 2021. Before that Steve covers Chrome's 13th and 14th exploited zero-days of 2021 (CVE-2021-37975 and CVE-2021-37976), the official Windows 11 release with a known File Explorer memory leak, a planned 30-country meeting on ransomware and crypto abuse, and an FCC notice of proposed rulemaking on SIM swapping and port-out fraud. He then describes Zimperium's GriftHorse campaign, in which hundreds of Play Store apps tricked about 10 million people into premium SMS subscriptions, and a University of Birmingham and Surrey paper showing that Apple Pay Express Transit mode with a Visa card allows unauthorised contactless payments from a locked iPhone, a flaw Apple and Visa each decline to fix. A SpinRite 6.1 update explains why the old benchmark timing code is being replaced. In the main segment Steve walks through how BGP route withdrawal, DNS with a five-minute TTL, and Facebook's own statements add up to a self-inflicted backbone outage that also locked engineers out of their tools and buildings.

# Key points

- Chrome 94.0.4606.71: CVE-2021-37975 (use-after-free in V8) and CVE-2021-37976 (information leak) were exploited; this brings the 2021 count to 14. Steve could not get Chrome to self-update from .61 during the Facebook outage.
- Windows 11 became downloadable (grc.sc/11 shortcut); Steve is unimpressed and highlights a File Explorer memory leak in build 22000.194 that does not free RAM after windows are closed; Paul Thurrott also thought it was too early.
- White House to convene 30 countries on ransomware, cybercrime and cryptocurrency abuse after the Colonial Pipeline, JBS and Kaseya attacks; the FBI saw no sign that Russia cracked down on the gangs.
- FCC proposed rules for carriers on SIM swaps and number porting. Steve is sceptical because rulemaking is slow and the comment system has been unreliable; criminals bribe carrier staff or exploit back-end systems.
- GriftHorse (Zimperium zLabs): around 139 trojan apps, up to roughly 10 million victims in 70 plus countries, geolocation-specific pages, repeated "you won a prize" pop-ups leading to premium SMS charges of about 36 euros per month. Google removed the apps from Google Play but they persisted on third-party stores.
- Apple Pay and Visa: in Express Transit mode, relay of NFC messages lets an attacker bypass the lock screen and the contactless limit (a test payment of 1,000 euros raised no fraud alarms); Mastercard on Apple Pay and Visa on Samsung are unaffected, and researchers advise disabling transit mode on Visa cards.
- Facebook outage at about 15:39 UTC on 4 October: BGP routes to Facebook networks, including the name servers a-d.ns.facebook.com, were withdrawn so DNS queries returned SERVFAIL and traffic fell to zero within minutes. Steve notes the DNS TTL is only 300 seconds with a seven-day SOA expiry, so routing, not DNS caching alone, caused the abrupt cliff.
- Facebook's post-mortem: a command during maintenance meant to assess backbone capacity unintentionally disconnected the backbone, a bug in the audit tool failed to block it, and DNS servers then withdrew their own BGP advertisements because they judged the network unhealthy.
- Recovery was slow because internal tools depended on the same network; engineers had to go on site, badges reportedly failed, and registrars briefly listed facebook.com as available. Steve stresses designing out-of-band management paths.
- Steve's reflections: GRC's own remote management crosses the border it controls, so lockouts have required a physical visit; Facebook has built its network software stack in-house.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a map of the world's submarine cable network.
- "Foundation" and "Invasion" TV commentary; a tip to disable Dolby Atmos to fix muddy dialogue.
- SpinRite 6.1: benchmark timing to move from the old ripple-counter timer to RDTSC; extended sector addressing to 64 bits; next pre-release then data recovery code. Discussion of the economics of retail software distribution in the 1990s.
- Leo describes a full-body MRI screening service; Steve mentions periodic carotid ultrasounds. Steve to join a Club TWiT AMA.
