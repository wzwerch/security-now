# Summary

During Black Hat and DefCon week the hosts keep to news and then devote the main segment to Windows 10's privacy model. Steve first returns to Stagefright: exploits already appear in exploit kits, so he advises updating firmware where possible and turning off automatic MMS retrieval in the Hangouts and Messages apps. A newly disclosed BIND flaw lets a malformed transaction-key packet crash public DNS servers, which Steve uses to argue that sprawling legacy platforms like BIND and OpenSSL are giving way to smaller rewrites. PagerDuty's breach is noted as a model of password storage (bcrypt or iterated SHA-1 with per-user salt and a site-wide pepper) even though the intrusion itself occurred. A Mac privilege-escalation zero-day is briefly covered. Steve then admits he is experimenting with abandoning NoScript for Sandboxie because script use has exploded. The main discussion treats Windows 10 as a philosophical shift to a cloud-connected, account-based, advertising-aware OS, with Steve recommending a custom (non-Express) install, walking through the 13 privacy settings pages, and explaining WiFi Sense, advertising ID, BitLocker key backup and Cortana's data use. He says he will stay on Windows 7, while Leo leaves most settings enabled.

# Key points

- **Stagefright mitigation:** devices from Android 2.2 are exposed; about 950 million phones; Google and CyanogenMod have fixes but carriers lag; disable auto-retrieve of MMS in Hangouts and the Messages app; proof-of-concept code promised after the conference; Steve imagines a safe verification service.
- **BIND DoS:** a flaw related to TKEY record handling allows remote crashing of exposed servers; fixed versions exist; Steve notes his own master is hidden behind provider-hosted slaves, and that BIND is a "kitchen sink" like OpenSSL.
- **PagerDuty breach:** attacker got into an administrative panel at an infrastructure partner and a replica database; leaked names, emails, calendar feed URLs and hashed passwords; users forced to reset; hashing used bcrypt (work factor 10) or iterated SHA-1, with per-user salt and a secret 40-character site-wide pepper.
- **Mac zero-day:** a password-bypass privilege escalation affecting OS X 10.10.4 and the 10.10.5 beta, exposed publicly by a different researcher than the finder.
- **NoScript fatigue:** Steve starts running Firefox inside Sandboxie instead of NoScript because modern sites pull scripts from many domains; Leo recommends Alan Kay's talk "Normal Considered Harmful" and notes browsers should behave like OS kernels with sandboxed processes.
- **Windows 10 philosophy:** Express settings turn almost everything on except location; Steve advises custom setup. Privacy settings span 13 pages with global switches and per-app granularity: advertising ID, typing and handwriting data collection, language list, camera, microphone, contacts, calendar, messaging, radios, background apps, feedback frequency.
- **BitLocker:** recovery keys backed up to the user's Microsoft account on supported editions; Steve sees it as a convenience trade-off, not true trust-no-one encryption.
- **WiFi Sense:** sharing is per network and must be enabled explicitly, but contacts cannot be chosen individually.
- **Cortana:** needs broad access (location, contacts, calendar, search and browsing history, messages); Microsoft's privacy statement lists these. Steve says Microsoft is transparent, but he does not want it. Local versus Microsoft account choice affects synchronization.
- **Advertising opt-outs:** Microsoft's personalization settings page and the AdChoices program are mentioned; reports suggested the opt-out might not persist.

# Other segments

- PodCall.io phone-based podcast access discussed; Leo notes it would not count downloads.
- SpinRite testimonial about maintaining media-server drives.
- Steve explains why he still uses Windows XP (stable working tool) and plans Windows 7 and later 64-bit.
- Windows 10 launch numbers: Leo cites tens of millions of upgrades within days.
- Ten-year anniversary approaching; Steve will join The New Screen Savers on Saturday.
