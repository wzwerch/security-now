# Summary

Steve opens with the continuing silence from HP three weeks after its firmware update bricked OfficeJet Pro 9020e printers. He then covers Google's launch of eight new top-level domains, focusing on .zip and .mov, whose names collide with common file extensions; researchers promptly showed how to fake a file archiver in the browser and craft lookalike URLs using the "@" character and Unicode slash lookalikes, and many experts urged blocking them. The Python Software Foundation will require 2FA for maintainers and is hashing IP addresses with a secret salt after a Department of Justice subpoena for download data. A popular Android VPN app that promised no logs exposed 360 million records. Shorter items: Twitter leaving the EU disinformation code, Bitwarden's passkey-related announcements, Meta's record 1.2 billion euro GDPR fine, WhatsApp message editing, and Google's Mobile VRP bug bounty. The main topic is Brave's "Request Off the Record" feature, which lets sensitive sites ask the browser to forget a visit, aimed at victims of intimate partner violence, and which Steve hopes becomes a standard.

# Key points

- HP printers: still no fix 22 days after the May 8 firmware failure; Steve notes the update may have been meant to tighten third-party ink blocking.
- Google Registry launched .dad, .phd, .prof, .esq, .foo, .zip, .mov and .nexus. Security vendors and researchers warned that .zip and .mov invite phishing (fake archiver pages, File Explorer search launching a .zip domain, URLs like github.com plus lookalike slash characters followed by "@" that actually point to a .zip domain). Advice from a Citizen Lab researcher: block .zip and .mov now. Leo notes Firefox warned about the forged slashes, and suggests disabling "open safe files" on Safari.
- PyPI will require 2FA for all maintainers by end of 2023 (free security keys were earlier given to top-1% project maintainers) and is replacing logged IPs with hashes using a single secret salt, because IPv4 is too small for ordinary salted hashes. This followed a DOJ subpoena for data on five accounts and the IPs of everyone who downloaded their packages. Other registries are also moving to mandatory 2FA.
- "Super VPN" (SuperSoftTech, over 100 million Android downloads) left a database open with about 360 million records, including emails, real IPs and server connection data, contradicting its "no logs" claim; found by Jeremiah Fowler. Lesson: a no-logs claim only matters if the provider is trustworthy.
- Twitter withdrew from the EU's voluntary disinformation code of practice; the Commission said obligations under the coming Digital Services Act remain.
- Bitwarden: the news was backend Passkey/WebAuthn APIs and libraries from Passwordless.dev for enterprises, plus passkey login to Bitwarden clients; client-side passkey storage was promised for later in summer.
- The Irish DPC fined Meta 1.2 billion euros (the largest GDPR fine) over EU-US data transfers and ordered transfers halted; Meta said it would appeal and seek a stay.
- WhatsApp added message editing within 15 minutes.
- Google Mobile VRP covers first-party Android apps, with rewards from about $750 to $30,000 for classes such as arbitrary code execution and sensitive data theft.
- SpinRite: Alpha-28 released after the backend rewrite and testing.
- Brave Request-OTR (version 1.53 desktop, 1.54 Android): a site sends a Request-OTR header (or is on a preloaded partner list); the browser asks the user, then visits in temporary storage and leaves no history, cookies, cache or permissions behind, while other sites are logged normally so no suspicious gaps appear. Limits: extensions, malware, network monitoring and OS logs. Brave wants to standardize it.
