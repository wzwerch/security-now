# Summary

Leo Laporte is away on a retreat, so Ant Pruitt co-hosts. Steve found that Adobe's Flash Player Updater was still running hourly on his PC years after Flash's end of life and urges listeners to check Programs and Features, criticising Adobe for never pushing a final self-removal update. He answers a desperate reader who lost a VeraCrypt password (no recovery by design; keep a backup of the password) and walks through Firefox 120's new privacy features, including a German court ruling that LinkedIn must honour Do Not Track signals. Security news covers the mass exploitation of ownCloud's graphapi flaw (CVE-2023-49103, CVSS 10), a CrushFTP unauthenticated flaw with about 10,000 exposed servers, fingerprint-sensor bypasses in Goodix, Synaptics and ELAN sensors, continuing exploitation of Apache ActiveMQ, and fresh breaches at TransUnion and Experian with a reminder to freeze credit files. Listener questions cover keeping passkeys in a password manager, parental controls, Leica's content-credentials camera, removing EU root certificates, a domain registrar recommendation and Google's pressure on Apple over RCS. The episode title is a birthday nod to Leo.

# Key points

- Adobe Flash Player Updater and its scheduled task kept running after the January 2021 end of life; Steve found it by chance while using the free API Monitor tool and removed it via Programs and Features. Risk: a lapsed update domain or unpinned update signature could turn millions of leftover updaters into a delivery channel. (The API Monitor download page was plain HTTP, noted by a listener.)
- VeraCrypt has no recovery path for a lost password; advice is to use a strong password and keep a physical or trusted offline copy (Schneier: people manage paper well), considering the threat model and inheritance.
- Firefox 120: "Copy Link Without Site Tracking", Global Privacy Control setting (on in private windows), canvas fingerprinting protection, cookie-banner blocking and URL tracking protection initially for German users, and import of third-party root certificates from the OS store (aimed at enterprise TLS-inspection proxies; can be disabled).
- Berlin Regional Court (30 Oct 2023) ruled that LinkedIn must respect browsers' Do Not Track signals under the GDPR; LinkedIn plans to appeal. DNT was proposed in 2009 and abandoned as a standard in 2019, while Global Privacy Control (2020) gained legal footing, for example against Sephora in California.
- ownCloud: CVE-2023-49103 (graphapi app exposes phpinfo output including admin password, mail credentials and license keys), plus CVE-2023-49105 (WebDAV pre-signed URL authentication bypass, 9.8) and CVE-2023-49104 (OAuth2 subdomain validation bypass, 9.0). GreyNoise saw mass exploitation within four days; mitigation is to remove the file, disable phpinfo, update, and rotate all credentials.
- CrushFTP enterprise suite: unauthenticated flaw in AS2 header parsing leads to file read/delete and full compromise; fixed in 10.5.2; patch reverse-engineered with public proofs of concept.
- Fingerprint sensors (Goodix, Synaptics, ELAN) in Dell Inspiron 15, Lenovo ThinkPad T14 and Surface Pro X could be bypassed by adversary-in-the-middle and downgrade tricks; Microsoft's Secure Device Connection Protocol (SDCP) was off by default or missing. Steve advises not over-relying on biometrics.
- Apache ActiveMQ CVE-2023-46604 remains under heavy exploitation, with cryptominers installed on unpatched servers.
- TransUnion and Experian reportedly hacked by group N4ughtySecTU demanding USD 30 million each; Steve urges everyone to keep credit-bureau locks on permanently, with temporary lifts when needed.
- Passkeys can live in the same vault as passwords (they are just better passwords), but one-time MFA codes should stay separate from the autofilling password manager.
- Parental controls: Steve argues the answer for a tech-savvy child is honest conversation and open communication rather than ever-stricter technical blocks; Ant agrees from his own experience.
- Leica M11-P signs photos with C2PA content credentials; Steve is sceptical because a camera must hold a secret key in the field and extracted keys would produce validly signed deepfakes. Canon, Nikon and Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 are also adopting the scheme.
- Removing EU eIDAS 2.0 root certificates yourself might be technically possible if they are distinctly marked, but most of the world would not do it.
- Domain registrar advice: Steve recommends Hover over Network Solutions and GoDaddy (relevant because Google Domains was moving to Squarespace).
- Google lobbied EU regulators to force iMessage open under the Digital Markets Act; Apple's RCS announcement is read as a countermeasure.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a London Euston station escalator sign saying it is "refusing to escalate".
- Steve is still working on unattended server-side Authenticode code signing with a PIN-protected hardware token.
