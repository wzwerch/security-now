# Summary

Steve reviews January 2025's record Patch Tuesday (159 CVEs, eight zero-days, 36 percent remote code execution, with three 9.8-rated flaws in PGM multicast, OLE and NTLMv1) and argues that anything other than web and email servers should never be directly exposed to the Internet; extra access control in front of Windows services makes such bugs largely moot. Microsoft will force-install the new Outlook for Windows on Windows 10 (late January optional update, mandatory with the February update) and offers only a removal command plus a registry value to prevent reinstallation; Steve mentions he has switched from Thunderbird to eM Client. The FTC is requiring GoDaddy to overhaul its hosting security after breaches between 2019 and 2022, a lesson in vetting acquisitions. The UK's Ofcom will require strong age checks for porn sites by July, and the US Supreme Court seemed ready to uphold a Texas law, raising privacy and scam concerns. Arctic Wolf reported attacks via publicly exposed FortiGate management interfaces using a zero-day, and DJI replaced no-fly zones with FAA-based warning zones in the US firmware. CISA's scanning programme cut critical infrastructure remediation times from about 60 to 30 days. Listener feedback covers digit-repeat statistics in TOTP codes, the lack of salted hashes in Active Directory under a police CJIS audit, finding TWiT sponsors, and Syncthing update frequency and self-hosted relays. The main topic computes the effort to brute-force an 80-bit TOTP secret key, concluding it is adequate but that 128 bits or more would give a comfortable margin.

# Key points

- **January 2025 Patch Tuesday**: CrowdStrike counted 159 CVEs, 10 critical and eight zero-days; 36 percent remote code execution and 25 percent privilege escalation; Windows 132 fixes, ESU older Windows 95 fixes, Office 19. The three 9.8 bugs: Windows Reliable Multicast Transport Driver (PGM) RCE, an OLE RCE triggerable by merely previewing an email in Outlook (workaround: plain-text email), and an NTLMv1 privilege escalation (set LmCompatibilityLevel to 5 or filter by IP). Two Remote Desktop Gateway race-condition RCEs (8.1) exist since Windows Server 2012. Steve and Leo discuss why the legacy-compatible Windows attack surface is large and that newer Windows received more fixes than frozen older versions.
- **Layered access control advice**: never expose SMB, NTLM or RDP gateways directly; use IP allow-listing, overlay networks or a front authentication layer.
- **New Outlook force install**: Microsoft admin message MC976059; new Outlook is a web-based client without PST or COM support and limited third-party mail support; remove with Remove-AppxProvisionedPackage plus a BlockedOobeUpdaters registry value to stop reinstall.
- **eM Client**: Steve reports he moved from Thunderbird; it threads and tags IMAP mail well, costs about 40 dollars per year, and a listener notes a lifetime-upgrade license exists.
- **GoDaddy and the FTC**: proposed order requires a comprehensive security programme, outside assessments every two years and bans exaggerated security claims, over failures in update tracking, logging and shared-hosting segregation affecting about 5 million hosting customers; compared with Marriott/Starwood due-diligence failures.
- **Age verification**: Ofcom's rules for UK porn sites by July (photo ID matching or credit-card checks; self-declaration and debit cards not accepted; fines up to 18 million pounds or 10 percent of revenue); Open Rights Group warns about scam verification sites and leaks; the US Supreme Court appeared likely to allow a Texas law targeting sites with more than a third adult content; Leo notes phone-based verification proposals and Apple's reluctance.
- **FortiGate**: Arctic Wolf saw unauthorized admin logins, new accounts and SSL VPN set-up on firewalls with management interfaces reachable from the Internet; a zero-day was confirmed and CISA set a very short patching deadline. Steve: never rely on a vendor's own login for public management access.
- **DJI geofencing**: DJI's US apps moved to FAA-sourced "Enhanced Warning Zones" from 13 January, putting responsibility on pilots, and published a "Get the facts" post denying political motives; Steve finds the timing odd, given a pending US ban.
- **CISA vulnerability scanning**: 7,791 critical infrastructure organizations enrolled by August 2024; remediation time fell from 60 to 30 days; 1,200 vulnerabilities added to the KEV catalogue in the period.
- **Statistics of six-digit codes**: only 151,200 of a million codes have six unique digits; about 41 percent have at least two adjacent equal digits, a birthday-paradox style effect.
- **Salting in Active Directory**: a police IT admin must meet the FBI CJIS policy requirement that secrets be hashed and salted, but Active Directory stores unsalted NT hashes; Microsoft's answer is moving to cloud (Entra ID), which GCC tenants find costly and feature-poor. Salting dates back to Unix V7 (1979).
- **Syncthing errata**: Steve was wrong that Syncthing rarely updates; a listener runs it on 25+ devices with his own relay and discovery servers.
- **Attacking TOTP**: with an 80-bit secret (16 base32 characters, common at Google and others although RFC 4226 requires at least 128 bits and recommends 160), a single six-digit sample cannot reveal the key; each sample cuts the candidate key space by a factor of one million, so about four samples with known timestamps are needed; a 50 percent success chance requires roughly 6.9 x 10^23 trials (57 percent of the space), which even at a million billion tests per second would take about 22 years. Symmetric crypto is not threatened by quantum computers; Steve recommends 128-bit secrets as a safe margin.
