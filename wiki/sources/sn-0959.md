# Summary

The episode begins with Apple's announced changes for iOS 17.4 in the EU: because of the Digital Markets Act, alternative browser engines (Blink, Gecko) are allowed for authorised developers and EU users get a randomised browser-choice screen with 12 popular browsers. Steve doubts real engine ports will come quickly and worries about the EU's eIDAS 2.0 forced-root-certificate plans. Short news items follow: China's ICBC bank survived ransomware because a key system ran Novell NetWare, HP is bricking printers with third-party ink while blaming "viruses" in cartridges, the "MOAB" aggregation of 26 billion leaked records (12TB), DOJ/FTC warnings that ephemeral chat must be preserved during investigations, a Mercedes-Benz employee's GitHub token left public for months, and Coveware data showing only 29 percent of ransomware victims paid in Q4 2023. Listener feedback covers camera-signed images, the flashlight app that was sold to ZipoApps, app ownership changes, TOTP codes in Bitwarden, and Windows 7 update tips. Steve announces SpinRite 6.1 is finished after three years. The main segment is Alex Stamos's LinkedIn critique that Microsoft downplayed the Midnight Blizzard breach, which reached other tenants through OAuth application abuse in Entra ID, and used the incident to upsell security products.

# Key points

- Apple iOS 17.4 (EU only): third-party browser engines allowed after Apple authorisation, plus a Safari default-browser choice screen showing 12 locally popular browsers in random order. Steve expects mostly skins over WebKit at first, thinks Google (which has worked on a non-WebKit Chrome for a year) may port because of Privacy Sandbox needs, and doubts Mozilla will.
- Steve links the DMA precedent to his worry about eIDAS 2.0 forcing browsers to trust EU-mandated root certificates.
- ICBC ransomware: the US-arm trading network reportedly ran Novell NetWare, which the ransomware could not infect; modern workstations were cleaned and the bank carried on.
- HP pushed firmware that disables printers with third-party cartridges; CEO claimed cartridges could carry viruses, security experts called it implausible; HP faces a class action.
- MOAB: roughly 12TB and 26 billion records compiled from past breaches (Tencent QQ 1.4B, Weibo, MySpace, Twitter, LinkedIn and others), found by Bob Diachenko, attributed to Leak-Lookup, exposed by a firewall misconfiguration. Danger is the aggregation for credential stuffing and phishing; much data is old.
- DOJ and FTC updated preservation letters to cover Slack, Teams and Signal; Google accused of training staff to use history-off chats, Amazon accused of deleting Signal chats.
- Mercedes-Benz: an employee's GitHub token in a public repository gave access to the internal GitHub Enterprise Server (source, cloud keys, credentials); found by RedHunt Labs after about four months, revoked after TechCrunch disclosure; Mercedes would not say whether others accessed it.
- Coveware: only 29 percent of ransomware victims paid in Q4 2023, down from 85 percent in Q1 2019, thanks to better backups.
- Camera content authentication (Leica M11-P, Content Authenticity Initiative): Steve argues a camera cannot reliably keep a private signing key secret; Leo notes the chain-of-custody value.
- Simple Flashlight mystery solved: developer sold the Simple Mobile Tools suite to ZipoApps, known for adding costly subscriptions; the open-source suite was forked as Fossify. Steve proposes that app stores notify users when an app's owner changes (another listener saw this with the Network Toolbox iOS app).
- Microsoft's OneNote web clipper page says it works best in "a modern browser like Edge" on Firefox.
- TOTP in Bitwarden: Steve and Leo agree that convenience wins; auto-filled one-time codes are better than none.
- Windows 7 tip: install the SHA-256 update, Servicing Stack update and KB3102810 first to fix HTTPS/updater problems.
- Stamos on Microsoft: the Midnight Blizzard techniques only work on Microsoft-hosted identity, so other tenants were also hit (Washington Post sources: at least 10 companies). The attackers used a legacy test OAuth app with elevated rights, created malicious OAuth apps and a consent user, and obtained the Exchange Online full_access_as_app role. Stamos calls "legacy" a dodge, criticises upselling Entra ID Protection and Purview Audit Premium, and urges secure-by-default products with all security features for all customers.
- Steve adds that Microsoft similarly withholds patches for out-of-support Windows from consumers while selling extended support to enterprises.

# Other segments

- SpinRite 6.1: declared complete after more than three years, tested by 818+ GitLab testers, runs on Intel Macs; next come online documentation, GRC email facility and a Security Now mailing list, then notification of past buyers. Upgrades from earlier versions will end after release.
- LearnDMARC site (SPF/DKIM/DMARC checker) praised; Gmail requires authenticated bulk mail from 1 February.
- iPhone flashlight has four brightness levels via long-press in Control Center; discoverability complaint about modern UIs.
- Credit Karma iOS app storing over 1GB question; app bloat rant.
- Sci-fi: Steve gave up on Aeon 14 and Expeditionary Force, recommends Ryk Brown's Frontiers Saga.
- Picture of the Week: "Danger - no Wi-Fi beyond this point" sign.
