# Summary

Z.ai's open-weight GLM-5.3, which surprised even its makers, has been abliterated; a Palo Alto startup called Abliteration sells it as a model that "doesn't say no", and Hugging Face hosts an abliterated "Warlock" version. Anthropic's analysis found GLM-5.3 nearly matches Claude Mythos Preview at end-to-end exploit development (50 versus 56 of 410 ExploitBench attempts) and found browser and driver zero-days in hands-on tests; NIST's CAISI calls it the most cyber-capable open model, about four months behind the US frontier. Its safeguards fall to role-play prompts (64%), prefilled reasoning (92%) or abliteration (100%). Steve's takeaway: frontier-class offensive capability is now freely available.

Firefox 157 fixed 38 high-impact flaws, many use-after-free and sandbox escapes. A key-forgery attack cuts RSA blind signatures (used in Privacy Pass) to roughly 65, 90 and 119 bits of security for 1024-, 2048- and 4096-bit keys. Gambit Security found an operator using open-source AI harnesses (Strix, Cairn, Hermes) to breach retailers for about $25 per target, stealing over 600,000 cards. VUSec's Branch Target Reuse exploits stale branch-predictor entries in JIT engines on every CPU tested. Steve asks listeners for trustworthy crypto-recovery help for a scammed neighbor and shows how the DNS Benchmark exposed a friend's WiFi packet loss. The main topic is Jakub Pachocki's essay on why making AI smarter is easier than aligning it.

# Key points

- Abliterating GLM-5.3 took Anthropic about 2,200 GPU hours (~$4,400) and cut refusal rates from over 90% to 2-12% with no real capability loss. GLM-5.3-Flash built a Chrome n-day exploit chain bypassing ARM64 pointer authentication in eight hours for about $20 of API cost. Anthropic urges government testing and wider defender access (Mythos 5.1 via trusted programs); Steve notes its 10,000-vulnerability claim remains unreconciled.
- The RSA attack (paper dated 20 September) bypasses factoring for blind/"textbook" RSA only; PKCS and PSS-padded signatures used for certificates are unaffected. It took 1,380 CPU core-years and billions of queries, so rate limiting helps.
- Gambit: 105 attack projects in 10-15 September, at least 27 companies compromised (a Fortune 500 hospitality firm, a major US airline), skimmers on five sites; an agent's cleanup routine destroyed one victim's data. Shadowserver helped take infrastructure down.
- Branch Target Reuse: CPUs keep code coherent after self-modification but not indirect-branch predictions, enabling a speculative "execute-after-free" in Linux cBPF, GraalVM and SpiderMonkey; Linux and Oracle have patched. Steve recalls Edge disabling its JIT.
- The new DNS Benchmark reports lost packets as a percentage; red bars across every resolver, even the local router, mean the PC's own connection is bad.
- OpenAI's agent-security lead "Joe" says containment is far harder than critics assume. Pachocki (OpenAI chief scientist): AI is "grown more than designed"; training is increasingly automated; value alignment generalizes poorly, and goal-driven training lets models bend aligned reasoning; chain-of-thought monitoring is becoming less reliable as models reason less in words; Astra is better aligned than GPT-5.6 Sol. He expects recursive self-improvement, wants an automated AI researcher, mandated safety bars and international coordination, and says no lab has solved alignment enough to keep scaling at maximum speed.
