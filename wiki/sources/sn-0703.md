# Summary

The theme is vulnerabilities that were attacked in the wild almost as soon as they were disclosed. Drupal announced a highly critical release one day ahead; proof-of-concept code appeared two days after the patch and attacks (including cryptominers) the day after that, helped by the fact that PHP patches are easy to diff and by Drupal's initially incorrect mitigation advice. A 19-year-old path-traversal bug in the unace library used by WinRAR was exploited via malicious spam, and WinRAR resolved it by removing ACE support. Microsoft disclosed that an HTTP/2 settings-frame abuse can pin an IIS server's CPU with a single connection. Mozilla faced a dilemma over the UAE firm DarkMatter's bid to become a root CA and, separately, wrote to Australia's parliament about the encryption law. Google's Project Zero found that Edge shipped an obscured whitelist of 58 domains allowed to skip Flash click-to-play, since cut to Facebook. ICANN asked for full DNSSEC deployment, NVIDIA fixed local privilege-escalation driver bugs, and Apple strengthened Intelligent Tracking Prevention in iOS 12.2 and Safari 12.1.

# Key points

- Drupal: advance notice on February 19, release February 20 (rated 20/25, highly critical); PoCs within two days and attacks on day three, per Imperva including cryptominers; Drupal's mitigation of disabling PUT, PATCH and POST proved incomplete because GET also worked. Steve advises Drupal admins to treat Drupal mail as urgent.
- WinRAR: the third-party unace.dll had an absolute path-traversal flaw (found by Check Point); a malspam campaign used a malicious archive disguised as .RAR to drop an executable into the Startup folder. WinRAR 5.70 beta removed ACE; WinRAR has about 500 million users and detects formats by content, not extension.
- IIS HTTP/2 denial of service (reported by F5's Gal Goldshtein): excessive SETTINGS frames spike CPU until the default two-minute connection timeout; no workaround, patches for Windows Server 2016, 1709, 1803 and Windows 10 builds. Exploitable with negligible bandwidth.
- DarkMatter (UAE) asked Mozilla to include its root CA; Reuters tied it to the Project Raven surveillance team; EFF, Amnesty and others objected; Mozilla signalled it might use discretion to refuse. Mozilla's store is also used by many Linux distributions. Precedent: CNNIC was trusted in 2009 and caught mis-issuing Google certificates in 2015.
- Mozilla's submission on Australia's TOLA encryption law asks that orders go to the provider, not individual employees, to avoid turning staff into insider threats.
- Edge file edgehtmlpluginpolicy.bin held SHA-256 hashes of 58 domains allowed to bypass Flash click-to-play without HTTPS enforcement, so XSS or MITM on those sites could be abused; after Ivan Fratric's report the list was cut to two Facebook hosts.
- ICANN (Feb 22) urged full DNSSEC deployment after DNS-infrastructure hijacking attacks; Steve admits GRC's zones are unsigned. Leo discusses DNS Flag Day/EDNS and Hover's lack of support.
- NVIDIA fixed eight driver flaws (five Windows ones scored 8.8 CVSS), locally exploitable for code execution or privilege escalation; check the driver download page.
- Apple ITP 2.1: partitioned cookies replaced by the Storage Access API; "verified partitioned cache" revalidates flagged cache entries after seven days; script-set cookies expire after seven days; session cookies for domains unvisited for 30 days are pruned.
- Picture of the week: trackers set so many cookies (around 70) that a news site's login cookie was evicted and subscribers were logged out.

# Other segments

- SpinRite sales support SQRL; SQRL users report successful logins with the iOS client; XenForo integration work and documentation updates continue, and Leo voices doubt about adoption against FIDO.
- Apple's default blocking of third-party cookies contrasted with other browsers.
