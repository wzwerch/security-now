# Summary

The lead story is the French arrest of Telegram founder and CEO Pavel Durov, released on EUR 5 million bail and barred from leaving France, over alleged complicity in illegal activity on an unmoderated platform and refusal to cooperate with a judicial request. Steve contrasts Telegram's distributed-jurisdiction boast and cloud-stored chats with Signal's cooperative-within-limits stance, and notes that mobile operating systems always see decrypted content. Smaller items follow: a year after Microsoft made security event logging free, CISA credits it with better visibility; CrowdStrike expects to lose about $60 million in new recurring revenue and Microsoft is convening an endpoint-security summit; Yelp sues Google; Telegram hosts a thriving DDoS-for-hire market (140+ channels, from $10 a month); Google raises Chrome vulnerability rewards up to $250,000; Cox Media Group is again pitching "Active Listening" ad targeting; and a listener received a solicitation to front for a foreign developer, echoing the North Korean fake-worker problem. The closing segment walks through Matthew Green's post: Telegram only offers end-to-end encryption through opt-in one-to-one "Secret Chats" using the homegrown MTProto, never for groups, so most traffic is readable by Telegram.

# Key points

- Durov arrest: charges include complicity in distribution of child abuse material, drug trafficking and fraud, and non-cooperation with authorities; a trigger cited was Telegram's failure to identify a suspect after a judicial request.
- Telegram claims data is split across jurisdictions so several court orders are needed and says it has disclosed zero bytes of user data; groups reach 200,000 members, versus 32 for iMessage and about 1,000 for Signal and WhatsApp.
- The EU is investigating whether Telegram understated its EU user count (41 million claimed against a 45 million Digital Services Act threshold).
- Steve's point: iOS and Android control apps tightly and the OS can see all plaintext, so platform vendors are the other place pressure may land.
- Microsoft logging: after the 2023 State Department Exchange Online intrusion and the Cyber Safety Review Board report, broader free logging is now used by agencies and critical infrastructure; CISA and partners issued an event-logging best-practices guide referencing Volt Typhoon style living-off-the-land activity.
- CrowdStrike: about $60M expected net new ARR loss; SentinelOne and Palo Alto claim inquiries from customers; Microsoft's Windows Endpoint Security Ecosystem Summit on 10 September 2024 to discuss moving security tooling out of kernel drivers; Leo notes this is hard because EDR relies on deep hooks.
- Yelp antitrust lawsuit against Google alleges search monopoly used to favour Google's own reviews.
- DDoS-as-a-service on Telegram (Falconfeeds report): 3,529 DDoS incidents in Europe in the first half of 2024, over 140 sales channels, payment mostly in cryptocurrency; Leo argues the web offers the same, Steve stresses the low barrier of entry.
- Chrome VRP overhaul: tiered rewards by report quality; top reward $250,000 for demonstrated remote code execution in a non-sandboxed process; Google openly admits new features keep introducing bugs.
- Cox Media Group "Active Listening": leaked pitch deck claims ad targeting from conversations picked up by smart devices; Google removed CMG from its partner program; Leo thinks the claims are exaggerated sales talk.
- Follow-up on Bloom filter cascades: real CRLite cascades have variable depth and continue until no false positives remain; the three-layer description was an illustration.
- Listener solicitation: a supposed Malaysian developer proposes using the listener's identity, accounts and a secondary laptop (remote desktop) to land US jobs and split the pay 75-85/15-25; Steve sees parallels with North Korean IT worker schemes.
- Matthew Green's critique: Telegram is "encrypted" only in the sense that connections use TLS-like transport; end-to-end Secret Chats are off by default, hidden several menus deep, need both parties online, and never cover groups; the MTProto 2.0 design with its IGE mode is unusual and non-standard; Telegram's UX has hardly changed since 2016 while Durov criticises Signal and WhatsApp.
- Metadata (who talks to whom and when) remains exposed in nearly all messengers regardless of end-to-end encryption.
- Steve's conclusion: Telegram is a social media platform with an undeserved security reputation, benefiting from the real encryption of Signal, WhatsApp and iMessage.
