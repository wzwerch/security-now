# Summary

With Jason Howell filling in for Leo, Steve covers a court order obtained by Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit, Fortra and Health-ISAC to disrupt infrastructure using cracked copies of Cobalt Strike, contrasting it with Sony's misguided demand to a DNS resolver. Samsung semiconductor staff leaked proprietary code to ChatGPT, which suggests demand for private, in-house AI. Apple patched two actively exploited zero-days (a WebKit use-after-free and an IOSurfaceAccelerator kernel flaw) and backported fixes to old devices. Sucuri names a six-year-old WordPress malware campaign "Balada Injector". Shorter items include Mozilla's Firefox Monitor breach list, Canada's probe of OpenAI, and Android policy changes. A SpinRite user story about read-disturb slowdowns on an SSD leads into the main topic: the H26FORGE research paper, which builds a tool to craft valid but spec-violating H.264 files and found kernel and memory bugs in iOS, Firefox, VLC and Android hardware decoders. Steve fears the open-sourced tool will help spyware vendors as much as defenders.

# Key points

- Microsoft DCU, Fortra and Health-ISAC obtained a US court order (EDNY, 31 March 2023) to take down servers used by cracked Cobalt Strike copies, which were linked to 68+ ransomware attacks on healthcare in 19+ countries. Defender saw about 1.5 million infected machines contacting such servers over two years.
- Sony's earlier legal demand that Quad9 (9.9.9.9) block a domain was ineffective; Steve says the right target is the hosting provider.
- Samsung engineers pasted chip-defect code into ChatGPT on three occasions; Samsung capped prompt length at 1 KB and is building an internal assistant. Steve predicts sealed on-premises AI appliances.
- Apple fixed two exploited zero-days (WebKit use-after-free, IOSurfaceAccelerator out-of-bounds write) reported by Google TAG and Amnesty's Security Lab, in iOS/iPadOS 16.4.1 and macOS 13.3.1; fixes were also backported to iPhone 6s/7, iPad Air 2 and others, indicating known in-the-wild targeting, likely by commercial spyware.
- Sucuri named the Balada Injector WordPress campaign (active since 2017, over a million sites infected, 141,000 detections in 2022), exploiting many theme and plugin flaws and pushing scam redirects. WordPress runs 43% of websites.
- Mozilla's Firefox Monitor now has a public list of all breaches since 2007; Steve recommends registering up to five addresses.
- Canada's privacy commissioner opened an investigation of ChatGPT, following Italy.
- Samsung Galaxy S10 series reached end of life with the March 2023 patch (which includes the Exynos modem fix from episode 915). Google restricted loan apps from reading photos and contacts, and will require in-app and web account deletion by early 2024.
- A SpinRite 6.1 alpha tester's Samsung 860 EVO SSD was slow at the start and caused blue screens; a Level 3 rewrite flattened ReadSpeed results and removed the crashes. Steve ties this to SSD "read disturb" and plans targeted rewriting in SpinRite 7.
- H26FORGE (USENIX Security 2023, UT Austin and Oberlin): about 30,000 lines of Rust with Python scripting, generates syntactically valid but semantically non-compliant H.264 streams. Found Apple AppleD5500 kernel bugs (including CVE-2022-42850), a Firefox GPU-process out-of-bounds read (fixed in v105), a VLC/FFmpeg use-after-free, and Android hardware-decoder kernel bugs. Thumbnail generation can be a zero-click path.
- The paper argues video decoding violates Chrome's "Rule of 2" and recommends deprivileging decoder components, memory-safe rewrites and simpler hardware interfaces; fragmentation across 25 vendors makes auditing hard.
