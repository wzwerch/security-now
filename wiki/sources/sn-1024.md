# Summary

Steve celebrates episode 1024 and covers Microsoft's completed rollout making all new consumer accounts passwordless by default, using an emailed one-time code and then a passkey, with existing users able to delete their password. Meta changed Ray-Ban glasses terms so voice recordings can no longer be opted out of cloud storage. Satya Nadella said 20 to 30 percent of Microsoft's code is written by AI, while Meta expects about half within a year. In Google's antitrust remedies trial a Google security executive argued that divesting Chrome would endanger its security. Sansec found backdoors planted in 2019 in license files of 21 Magento plugins from three vendors that were activated in late April 2025 to hijack nearly 1,000 stores. Listener feedback covers honeypots and SSH hygiene (non-standard ports, Fail2Ban), the real meaning of "trust this computer", Cloudflare's speed test, Rufus, 0patch for out-of-support Windows, a programmer's loss of craft joy, phone-store identity fraud, and a simple story-based AI jailbreak. The main topic is the news that the Signal-like app seen on a U.S. official's phone was TeleMessage's modified archiving clone "TM SGNL", which was hacked in 15 to 20 minutes, exposing plaintext messages and contact data; Steve argues Signal itself should not be blamed.

# Key points

- Passwordless by default: new Microsoft accounts verify an email address with a one-time code and are then prompted to enroll a passkey; Microsoft says tests cut password use by over 20% and it plans to eventually remove password support; existing users can delete their password in account settings. Steve is delighted.
- Meta Ray-Ban glasses: "Meta AI with camera" stays enabled unless the voice trigger is disabled; voice recordings stored up to a year with no opt-out (90 days if accidental).
- AI coding: Nadella says 20 to 30% of Microsoft code is AI-written; Zuckerberg predicts half of Meta development by AI within a year; Google over 25% (October 2024); Shopify and Duolingo are shifting work to AI. Steve thinks a dedicated code model, not a generalist, would be better.
- Google antitrust trial: Heather Adkins testified a divested Chrome might lose Google's security infrastructure, citing Operation Aurora where the one employee using Internet Explorer got compromised; the DOJ noted AT&T and Microsoft made similar national-security arguments; expert testimony suggested remedies could reduce Google's search share toward 51%.
- Magento supply-chain attack: License.php or LicenseAPI.php in plugins from Magesolution, Meetanshi and Tigren were backdoored in 2019, dormant until late April 2025, allowing remote command execution via a secret key; a $40 billion multinational was among victims; vendors were slow or denying. Lesson: compromised developers or machines are never fully trustworthy afterward.
- eM Client 10.3 finally fixes Steve's IMAP real-time mail retrieval problem seen in Thunderbird.
- Honeypots (OpenCanary, T-Pot) show constant 24/7 scanning; Steve runs SSH on a non-standard port not for security but to avoid noise, and says standard ports are only needed for services that solicit anonymous connections.
- "Trust this computer" semantics vary: it may mean persistent login, skipping 2FA on a known device, or both; Steve proposes explicit wording like "Always log me out when the browser closes".
- Cloudflare's speed.cloudflare.com is recommended as a detailed speed test; Rufus (rufus.ie) can build Windows install media that skips Microsoft-account and TPM requirements.
- 0patch.com offers micropatches for out-of-support Windows; Windows 10 had 52.94% share versus 43.72% for Windows 11 that month, with about 2.4% on Windows 7.
- Identity fraud: a listener with frozen credit reports still had a fake-ID phone account opened in his name at a carrier store.
- A story-based jailbreak (a character reconstructing a damaged Base64 manual) got ChatGPT and Grok to produce deepfake instructions; Steve suggests a separate supervisor AI that sees only the output.
- TeleMessage TM SGNL: a modified Signal client (and clones of WhatsApp, Telegram, WeChat) that forwards copies of messages to an archive for compliance, used by Mike Waltz as seen in a Reuters photo; it uses Signal's servers and protocol, so other participants may use normal Signal. 404 Media reported a hacker took about 15 to 20 minutes to breach TeleMessage's AWS-hosted backend, obtaining plaintext message fragments, backend credentials and contact data for CBP, Coinbase and others. Signal says it cannot guarantee unofficial versions. Steve's conclusion: adding a message tap destroys end-to-end security; the fault lies with TeleMessage, not Signal.
