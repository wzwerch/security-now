# Summary

Trail of Bits' Dan Guido independently confirmed that the AMD flaws reported by CTS Labs are real, and AMD acknowledged four problem classes and promised firmware patches; the flaws appear to need administrative access, which Steve notes is weak comfort given Pwn2Own privilege-escalation results. CTS's CTO published a letter arguing the existing 30/45/90-day responsible disclosure model leaves customers uninformed and that researchers should announce impact on day zero while withholding technical details until a fix. Steve disagrees: AMD would have acted quickly if given a private notice, and the flawed ASMedia chips (ASM1042, ASM1142, ASM1143) also appear in other motherboards. Intel's CEO said new silicon in the second half of 2018 will add hardware partitioning against Spectre variants 2 and 3, and Microsoft's KB4090007 microcode list grew to eight CPU IDs while March Patch Tuesday brought Skylake microcode and x86 coverage. Intel and Microsoft both offer speculative-execution bounties up to $250,000 through year end. Windows feature updates will shorten offline time to about 30 minutes by moving work online. Researchers found significant leaks in Hotspot Shield (fixed), PureVPN and ZenMate. Steve comments on the first pedestrian death from a self-driving Uber and gives a SQRL development update. The main section recounts Pwn2Own 2018: no Chinese teams, only about $267,000 of $2 million awarded, Richard Zhu named Master of Pwn for Edge and Firefox exploits chained with Windows kernel bugs, plus Safari and VirtualBox successes.

# Key points

- CTS's argument versus the standard disclosure norm; Steve's position that firmware can now be patched through OS loaders so vendors should get time first. A stock-shorting motive was alleged by critics.
- Intel: hardware fixes for Spectre variants 2 and 3 in Cascade Lake Xeons and eighth-generation Core later in 2018; variant 1 stays a software mitigation.
- Microsoft microcode updates now cover Skylake, Kaby Lake and Coffee Lake in KB4090007; some builds already ship in cumulative updates. Steve will update InSpectre accordingly.
- Bug bounties: Intel up to $250,000 for severe side-channel bugs; Microsoft's four-tier program (new class up to $250,000, mitigation bypasses up to $200,000, known-issue instance $25,000); both expire 31 December.
- Windows 10 feature updates now take roughly 82 minutes (Creators) to 51 minutes (Fall Creators) offline and target about 30.
- VPN audit by vpnMentor: Hotspot Shield issues included a proxy autoconfig parameter allowing traffic hijack, DNS leakage and an overly broad bypass whitelist; recommended checking DNS leaks with a leak test site.
- Self-driving fatality in Tempe, Arizona: preliminary reports suggest a pedestrian stepped out mid-block; Leo and Steve argue fear of autonomy should not outweigh safety statistics.
- SQRL: a live "sensitive data monitor" showed leftover keys and led to more aggressive wiping; high-DPI scaling and web forums are next.
- Pwn2Own: targets included VirtualBox, VMware, Hyper-V, major browsers, Office 365, Outlook, servers; Chinese absence cut payouts from $833,000 the prior year; some competitors withdrew after Patch Tuesday fixed their bugs; entries are bought by Trend Micro's Zero Day Initiative and disclosed to vendors.

# Other segments

- Expedia/Orbitz-related breach news mentioned by Leo (880,000 payment cards).
- Listener thoughts on social-engineering risks in SpinRite re-download policy; Steve defends GRC's trusting policy.
- EV certificate question: DigiCert separates account identity vetting from per-certificate issuance; "perfect forward secrecy" joke.
- DEF CON China announced for Beijing in May.
