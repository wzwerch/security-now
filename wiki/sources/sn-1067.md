# Summary

This episode was recorded early, on Sunday March 1, because Steve and Leo were travelling to the Zero Trust World conference. After a long informal preamble, Steve reveals the "no turn" sign from last week was real (Simcoe, Ontario, since removed). He argues that headlines about AI-assisted hacking (an AWS report on a Russian-speaking actor compromising 600+ Fortinet firewalls with exposed management ports and weak passwords, and Claude used against Mexican government agencies) are overblown: AI is a tool and the failures are poor configuration. Apple is rolling out age-range APIs and download blocks to comply with laws in several countries and US states, the FTC issued a COPPA enforcement-discretion statement for age-verification data, Meta's AI-driven CSAM reporting is flooding US ICAC investigators with unusable tips, Russia blocked 469 VPN services, and the UK touts its national vulnerability scanning service (with a garbled description of DNS weaknesses that Steve suspects AI wrote). Other items: the Vastaamo extortion hacker's sentence, Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters recruiting women for help-desk vishing, the Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN authentication bypass CVE-2026-20127 (CVSS 10.0, exploited), and a teaser of VulnCheck's 2025 exploitation report. Steve describes the $72 SmartCard-HSM he uses for code signing, discusses why self-signed code signing cannot replace certificate authorities, and welcomes the idea of a local small-language-model assistant that warns users about risky actions. The main topic is Huntress's CrashFix: the KongTuke actor's malicious NexShield Chrome extension (a clone of uBlock Origin Lite) crashes the browser and then shows a fake Edge repair prompt that leads the user to paste a clipboard-loaded PowerShell command into the Run dialog. Steve concludes that only Microsoft can fix this class of attack, for example by treating browser-sourced clipboard content like Mark-of-the-Web files.

# Key points

- Picture-of-the-week follow-up: the sign banning both turns at a T-intersection was genuine (Crescent Boulevard at Queensway, Simcoe, Norfolk County, Canada) and was removed after embarrassment.
- AWS reported a financially motivated Russian-speaking actor who breached over 600 FortiGate devices from about January 11, 2026 by using exposed management ports, weak passwords and no MFA, with scripts written by AI tools (DeepSeek for reconnaissance, Claude for assessments); the actor was unsophisticated and moved on from hardened targets. Steve's view: AI is just a tool, and humans are accountable.
- A hacker reportedly used Claude to help steal 150 GB from Mexican agencies including the tax authority and electoral institute (195 million records, per Bloomberg).
- Apple posted developer guidance on the Declared Age Range API: blocking 18+ app downloads in Australia, Brazil and Singapore from February 24, 2026 without adult confirmation, and age signals for new accounts in Utah (May 6) and Louisiana (July 1). A California law requiring operating systems to do age signaling takes effect January 1, 2027, which Leo notes is unenforceable against Linux.
- FTC COPPA policy statement: no enforcement against operators that collect personal data solely to verify age, if they limit use, delete promptly, share only with capable third parties, give notice, secure the data and use reasonably accurate methods; COPPA review to follow.
- Meta CSAM reporting: ICAC investigators told a New Mexico trial that AI-generated tips are often non-criminal or missing key evidence; Meta filed 13.8 million of NCMEC's 20.5 million 2024 reports, and the REPORT Act (Nov 2024) widened duties. Steve sees growing pains of AI deployment and expects machine moderation to become unavoidable.
- Roskomnadzor blocked 469 VPN services and Telegram is being blocked in stages (reportedly fully from April).
- UK government vulnerability monitoring service scans about 6,000 public bodies for about 1,000 vulnerability types and claims fix times for domain-related issues dropped from 50 to 8 days; Steve criticizes the nonsensical wording ("weak DNS records") and praises the idea of national self-scanning.
- Vastaamo: the Finnish hacker who extorted psychotherapy patients in 2020 lost an appeal and will return to prison (six years three months); Steve repeats that the clinic kept too much old data online.
- Dataminr: Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters (alliance of Lapsus$, Scattered Spider and ShinyHunters, 1.5 billion records stolen) recruit women for vishing at $500-1,000 per call with scripts; defenses are help desk training, out-of-band identity checks, FIDO2 keys instead of SMS/push MFA, and log audits after help desk actions.
- Cisco CVE-2026-20127 (SD-WAN Controller/Manager peering authentication bypass, CVSS 10.0, exploited as a zero-day): found by Australia's ASD, attackers add a rogue peer and gain root; NSA, CISA and Five Eyes partners urged patching. Steve notes IP allow-listing of peer endpoints would have blunted it.
- VulnCheck 2025 report teaser: about 1 percent of disclosed vulnerabilities were exploited; proof-of-concept exploits rose 16.5 percent with AI slop; China-nexus attributions up 52 percent; 56.4 percent of ransomware CVEs were zero-days. Full coverage planned for a later episode.
- Code signing: Steve's SmartCard-HSM 4K (open-source tooling, supports 4096-bit RSA, key replication between devices) holds both his DigiCert (expires November 2026) and new IdenTrust certificates; freeware such as ValiDrive is cosigned to build reputation. Self-signed code signing only works where the root is installed (e.g. inside enterprises), so it cannot replace CAs; Steve laments consolidation of the CA industry into resellers of DigiCert.
- Listener idea: a local small language model watching user actions ("Charlemagne") to flag lookalike sites and risky clicks; Steve is bullish and ties this to his Zero Trust World theme "overprivileged users making mistakes".
- CrashFix (Huntress, KongTuke actor tracked since early 2025): the NexShield extension, a find-and-replace clone of uBlock Origin Lite promoted through a malicious ad and available on the Chrome Web Store, intentionally crashes the browser, then shows a fake "Microsoft Edge stopped abnormally" scan prompt, copies a PowerShell command to the clipboard and instructs WIN+R, CTRL+V, ENTER. Domain-joined machines get a fuller infection chain aimed at Active Directory; home machines received only a test payload. Steve says Microsoft must quarantine browser-sourced clipboard contents, as with Mark of the Web; Leo suggests restricted Windows or Chromebook-like modes for ordinary users.
