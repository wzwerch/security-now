# Summary

Leo returns. Steve covers the first Patch Tuesday under Microsoft's new model for Windows 7 and 8.1: one security-only bundle and one monthly quality rollup instead of many individual patches, with five zero-days fixed that month. Exploit broker Zerodium raises its iOS bounty to $1.5 million and Android to $200,000, saying chains are harder to build. Signal for Android had a length-wrap bug in attachment MAC checking, fixed with little risk. A small NIST interview study found widespread "security fatigue". Firefox's Test Pilot features and Chrome 55's lower V8 memory use are noted. WoSign and StartCom met Mozilla and are being split into separate companies after further misconduct came to light, including ads in certificates. Samsung halted the Note 7 after replacement units also overheated. Steve then covers Reuters' report that Yahoo scanned all incoming email for US intelligence in 2015 using code its security team thought was a rootkit, and Yahoo's disabling of new mail forwarding to hinder users leaving. The main technical item is research showing a 1024-bit discrete-log computation made roughly 10,000 times easier by choosing a trapdoored prime, with Steve explaining that the finding is a warning about unexplained standard primes rather than proof any deployed prime is bad.

# Key points

- Windows Update change (October 2016): "security only" bundle plus "security monthly quality rollup" and an advance preview a week later; five actively exploited zero-days patched across IE, Edge, Office, GDI+ and Windows messaging; Microsoft plans to extend rollups back toward the base service pack image.
- Zerodium: $1.5 million for iOS 10 full-chain exploits, $200,000 for Android, $80,000 for Flash; the founder says price gap reflects difficulty and demand; Apple's hardware-backed security design is credited.
- Signal Android bug: attachment length stored in 32 bits, so appending exactly 4 GB could hide data from the MAC check; hard to exploit, fixed, not present in iOS or desktop; first such bug in Signal.
- Discussion of provably correct software and whether AI could close the gap between programmer intent and code.
- NIST/IEEE study (40 interviews): many participants showed resignation, loss of control, fatalism and decision avoidance - "security fatigue".
- Browser news: Firefox Test Pilot (native side tabs, screenshots, Wayback Machine for dead links, tracking protection); Chrome 55 reduces memory by up to 40 percent on heavy sites.
- WoSign, StartCom and parent met Mozilla in London; Mozilla's disclosure shows additional faults (adding unvalidated domains to an order, advertising links inside certificates); the CEO's role changed and the two CA brands are to be separated.
- Samsung Note 7: replacement units also reported overheating; production halted; Steve suspects interaction of charging and internal heating weakening cells.
- Errata: HSTS preload now accepts only base domains and covers all subdomains.
- Let's Encrypt growth chart: exponential growth earlier in 2016, still strong; short-lived certificates reduce the importance of revocation.
- Yahoo: Reuters says it ran a custom program to scan all incoming mail in 2015 under a government order, installed without the security team's knowledge and later closed after Alex Stamos escalated; EFF calls it an unprecedented expansion of surveillance; Steve links it to the "going dark" encryption trend and notes that traffic concentration points (like VPN exits) attract surveillance; Yahoo disabled setting up new automatic forwarding, which Steve condemns; Verizon sought a $1 billion price cut.
- Trapdoored primes: researchers computed a 1024-bit discrete logarithm in about two months on 2,000-3,000 cores because they had hidden a special structure in the prime; estimated at least 10,000 times harder for an honestly generated 1024-bit prime. The concern applies to fixed, unexplained primes in standards (such as Diffie-Hellman parameters), not to keys generated fresh; remedies are 2048-bit or larger sizes and demonstrable provenance (publish the seed or derive from digits of pi or e); Steve recalls the dual EC random generator and cites Bernstein's curve choice for SQRL as the right kind of transparency.
