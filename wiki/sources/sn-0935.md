# Summary

The show celebrates its 18th anniversary (episode 1 aired in August 2005, titled "As the Worm Turns") and Steve announces that, prompted by listener feedback, he will not stop at episode 999 and will keep podcasting as long as people want it. Listener feedback covers Firefox Multi-Account Containers, why an in-use SSD cannot be fully encrypted in place without a secure erase, whether to run SpinRite before copying old drives, an absurdly restrictive JPMorgan Chase password rule list, DuckDuckGo email aliases versus self-run aliases, GPL and Russian open-source restrictions, satellite crowding and the Tab Session Manager extension. The rest of the episode is the long main topic: Google's Topics API, which began rolling out in Chrome 115 as part of the Privacy Sandbox. Steve explains how the browser classifies visited sites into a roughly 350-topic taxonomy locally, keeps weekly epochs for three weeks, and discloses at most one topic per week to each calling site, with noise and a filter that stops it acting as a tracking beacon. He considers it a genuine, user-controllable alternative to tracking-based profiling and hopes Firefox adopts it, while noting that the EFF-style absolutists will object and that legislation may still be needed.

# Key points

- The podcast turned 18 (first episode recorded 19 August 2005); Steve announced he will continue beyond episode 999 and Leo said the TWiT software already supports four-digit episode numbers.
- Firefox Multi-Account Containers separates cookies and storage per tab container so one can be logged into several accounts of the same site; Steve cautions it does not hide a shared IP address or prevent correlation by determined trackers.
- An SSD in use cannot be fully encrypted in place: wear leveling, sparing and over-provisioning leave inaccessible regions that may hold old plaintext, and only a full secure erase clears them (copy off, erase, encrypt, restore). Steve plans to split early SpinRite 7 work into a product called Beyond Recall.
- For old drives, Steve advises copying files off first with retry-and-skip tools (Robocopy, XCOPY) and using SpinRite only if errors appear, since it can repair file-system metadata problems.
- JPMorgan Chase's published password rules (8-32 characters, no special characters, no repeats, not equal to the last 24 passwords, and so on) are shown as a counterproductive example: they push users toward minimal passwords and shrink the brute-force search space.
- Neal.fun's "Password Game" is mentioned as satire of such rules.
- Steve supports email aliases but prefers running his own because an address is a persistent recovery identity and a third-party alias service (such as DuckDuckGo's) could be withdrawn.
- A listener notes the GPL's give-back obligations conflict with Russia's ban on contributing to foreign open-source work; a second listener puts satellite crowding in perspective (orbital shells have hundreds of millions of square kilometres of surface).
- Voyager 2's 2-degree mispointing at about 12.3 billion miles implied the beam missed Earth by roughly 430 million miles, yet NASA could still send and hear.
- Tab Session Manager (open source, also for Chrome and Edge) is Steve's recommended Firefox session-saving add-on.
- Topics API: browser-side classifier maps hostnames to up to about three topics from a public taxonomy (349 entries in Google's version, versus about 1,500 in the IAB taxonomy); only sites that call the API contribute.
- Topics keeps week-long epochs at random offsets and retains the last three; each epoch picks the top five topics plus one random topic, and a keyed hash of a per-user secret, the week and the calling domain picks which one each site sees, with a 5 percent chance of a random wildcard.
- A caller only receives a topic if it already observed the user on a site mapping to that topic in the previous three weeks, so Topics cannot reveal more than third-party cookies could; calls via redirects do not count; incognito mode, clearing history or the Chrome setting disable it, and users can view, delete or block topics.
- Rollout plan reported by TechCrunch: Privacy Sandbox APIs run beside third-party cookies, with 1 percent cookie deprecation in early 2024 and full phase-out by the second half of 2024.
- Steve contrasts Topics with FLoC (opaque cohort hash broadcast to every site) and with Global Privacy Control from the previous episode, calls it "privacy-forward", and hopes Firefox adopts it; Leo confirms it is part of Chromium.
