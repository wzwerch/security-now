# Summary

Steve notes that the Celsius cryptocurrency platform's bankruptcy filing, a 14,532-page document, exposed names and transactions of hundreds of thousands of customers, and that Meta found more than 400 malicious mobile apps (mostly photo editors) harvesting Facebook logins. Uber's former chief security officer Joe Sullivan was convicted of obstruction and lying to authorities for hiding the 2016 breach and disguising a $100,000 payment as a bug bounty. More crypto turmoil follows: Binance's BNB Smart Chain bridge exploit (about $112 million kept by the thief) and a Zcash spam attack. The new UK digital minister wants to replace the GDPR, the NSA is recruiting 2023 interns, an IBM survey shows incident responders are highly stressed, and Microsoft is still fumbling the response to the Exchange zero-days it had observed in August. Steve reports SpinRite 6.1 now handles drives over 2.2 TB through the BIOS and recommends the fanless ZimaBoard single-board computer. The main topic is a Hebrew University finding that Linux 5.12's RFC 6056 double-hash source port selection let remote parties derive a per-device identifier and track Linux devices across browsers, VPNs and networks.

# Key points

- Celsius bankruptcy filing published customer names and transaction histories because the court only allowed redaction of addresses.
- Meta analysis: 400+ malicious Android and iOS apps stealing Facebook credentials; 42.6% photo editors, then business utilities, phone utilities and games; Steve advises restraint with downloads and checking reputation off-platform.
- Joe Sullivan convicted (obstruction and misprision-type charges around concealing a 2016 breach from the FTC); he faces up to eight years; the hackers were paid $100,000 in Bitcoin via the bug bounty program and made to sign NDAs falsely claiming no data was taken.
- Binance BSC bridge exploit minted 2 million BNB (~$560 million), roughly $112 million escaped; Leo notes cross-chain bridges are a repeated target. Zcash blockchain more than tripled in size to over 100 GB due to a spam attack with cheap shielded transactions.
- UK Secretary of State Michelle Donelan announced plans to drop the GDPR for a bespoke data protection regime inspired by Israel, Japan, South Korea, Canada and New Zealand; Steve worries about fragmentation of the single global Internet.
- NSA 2023 summer internship applications open until Halloween 2022.
- IBM survey of 1,100 incident responders: duty to help is the top draw; 67% report stress in daily life, nearly 65% sought mental health help, 81% say ransomware worsened the pressure.
- Microsoft's Exchange mitigation script was edited daily (spaces removed, typos fixed); Microsoft admitted seeing the CVE-2022-41040 and CVE-2022-41082 chain in fewer than 10 organizations in August, attributing it to a probable state-sponsored actor installing the Chopper web shell, and no patch was yet available.
- SpinRite 6.1 tested with 4 TB and 3 TB drives via BIOS USB, about 2 TB per hour on SATA; Steve induces errors using old READ LONG/WRITE LONG drive commands to test recovery; SpinRite 7 will add UEFI boot, 7.1 native USB.
- ZimaBoard (~$120, fanless, Intel, dual Realtek NIC, two SATA, PCIe x4, BIOS and UEFI) chosen as Steve's silent SpinRite test platform.
- Listener insight: Firefox's per-site cookie partitioning means Google sees no Google cookie and shows more CAPTCHAs.
- Language discussion: Steve's assembler web server code does its own reference counting; Leo says Rust in the Linux kernel is good news; Winget and Patch My PC are discussed for Windows patching; uBlock Origin annoyance lists can hide GDPR cookie banners; Fathom Analytics suggested as GDPR-friendly analytics; public WiFi caveats around non-TLS apps and using a phone hotspot; Security Now counts toward ISC2 CPE.
- Source port randomization: traditional sequential ephemeral ports were considered guessable (cf. Dan Kaminsky's DNS attack), so RFC 6056 offered algorithms; Linux 5.12 adopted double-hash selection, and researchers showed hash collisions depending on a per-device key act as a device ID that survives browser changes, private modes and some VPNs until reboot; a Linux patch landed in May 2022 and Android declined to adopt the algorithm. Steve's moral: yesterday's "secure enough" often fails when researchers look harder (Spectre, Rowhammer, port selection).
