# Summary

Chrome received its second out-of-cycle fix of 2022, CVE-2022-1096, a type confusion in the V8 JavaScript engine reported on 23 March and patched within two days. Steve relays an interview in which the CTO of Ukrtelecom explains why Russia cannot easily cut Ukraine off (dispersed infrastructure, western links, cooperation among competing ISPs) shortly before that ISP suffered a large DDoS; Starlink deliveries also continued. JFrog found 218 malicious npm packages that reused names from the @azure scope without the scope prefix to steal information. University of Massachusetts Dartmouth researchers showed that 2016 to 2020 Honda Civic key fobs send a fixed, replayable code (CVE-2022-27254), and Honda said it had no plans to update older cars. The FCC added Kaspersky and several Chinese carriers to its Covered List; Steve says the concern is Russia's government, not Kaspersky's past conduct. After listener feedback on "GC holes" in .NET, the main item is Google TAG's report on how two North Korean groups (Operation Dream Job and Operation AppleJeus) used the first Chrome zero-day (CVE-2022-0609) from at least 4 January 2022 against about 250 people at media, IT and registrar firms and about 85 crypto and fintech users, with carefully protected multi-stage exploit delivery. Steve recommends enabling Chrome's Enhanced Safe Browsing.

# Key points

- Chrome CVE-2022-1096: type confusion in V8 exploited in the wild; fixed in 99.0.4844.84 for Windows, Mac and Linux; only two Chrome zero-days so far in 2022; Chromium-based browsers need their own updates.
- Ukrtelecom (about 12,000 staff, coverage at 84 percent of pre-war levels, external capacity back to 130 percent): resilient design, remote-managed monitoring centers, ISPs cooperating, equipment blocked if seized; Russia may be sparing the networks because it wants intelligence; Russian handsets work poorly in Ukraine, so soldiers take civilian phones; the company was hit by a growing DDoS right after its CTO's interview.
- Steve's own lesson: bragging about being DDoS-resistant invites attacks.
- npm name-confusion: 218 packages mimicking @azure, @azure-rest, @azure-tests, @azure-tools and @cadl-lang names (about 50 downloads each) carried PII stealers; scoping explained; advice is to always use the scoped name and filter packages; Steve calls supply-chain trust brittle.
- Honda Civic 2016 to 2020 remote keyless entry and engine start use unchanging codes on 433 MHz FSK, so a recorded press can be replayed (record-and-replay with a software defined radio); contrasts with Samy Kamkar's 2015 RollJam against rolling codes; Honda disputed the significance.
- FCC Covered List: Kaspersky added (HackerOne also ended its bug bounty relationship; Germany's BSI warned against it); earlier additions included Huawei, ZTE, Hytera, Hikvision, Dahua, China Mobile International USA and China Telecom Americas; Kaspersky says the move is political.
- Listener feedback: "GC hole" is the .NET term for runtime garbage-collector reference mistakes; use-after-free in advisories is a catch-all.
- Google TAG on CVE-2022-0609: found 10 February, patched 14 February, exploited since 4 January; lures were fake recruiter emails (Disney, Google, Oracle) with lookalike job-site domains; AppleJeus used two compromised fintech sites and fake crypto-news sites; hidden iframes served a fingerprinting script, then a Chrome RCE and a sandbox-escape stage; safeguards included time-limited iframes, one-time links, per-session AES encryption and halting on failure; separate payload paths for Safari on macOS and Firefox; attackers kept trying after the patch.
- Google added the domains to Safe Browsing and alerted targeted Gmail and Workspace users; Enhanced Safe Browsing (Chrome Settings, Security and privacy, Security) reduces successful phishing by 35 percent, adds extension trust signals and deep download scanning, at the price of sending browsing data to Google.
