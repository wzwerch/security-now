# Summary

Steve notes that the first virtual Black Hat and Defcon produced nothing dramatic, though Leo mentions Qualcomm's Achilles flaw and unpatched older Android phones. Graz University's new paper, "Speculative Dereferencing of Registers", shows that the prefetch attribution in earlier KASLR-breaking and Meltdown/Foreshadow work was wrong and the real cause is speculative dereferencing of user-space registers in the kernel; this revives Foreshadow and affects ARM, IBM and AMD as well as Intel. Steve muses about choosing between fast and secure processors. Canon suffered a Maze ransomware outage. A researcher publicly disclosed a bypass of the vBulletin patch for a PHP remote code execution flaw without telling the vendor, and attacks followed within hours. Windows 10 will get native DNS over HTTPS, Troy Hunt will open-source Have I Been Pwned after failing to find a buyer, and Steve shares SpinRite driver progress. The main story is Geneva, a University of Maryland genetic algorithm that automatically discovers packet manipulation strategies to defeat censors such as China's block on ESNI.

# Key points

- Graz paper: the cache effects previously blamed on prefetching come from speculative dereferencing of user registers in the kernel; KAISER-style mitigations do not fully stop it; demonstrated covert channel of 10 bits per second (128 bytes), an SGX register leak in about 15 minutes, and JavaScript/WebAssembly variants. Steve: only chip vendors can fix this; users are spectators.
- Steve speculates about selling a fast but leaky processor versus a slower secure one, or safe context-switch boundaries; Leo recalls a 1995 paper warning about cache timing channels in x86.
- Canon: services down from 30 July for about a week; BleepingComputer identified Maze ransomware; Canon said it was unrelated to the image.canon storage loss, and Leo notes those uploads are gone.
- vBulletin: after the September 2019 PHP RCE and its quick patch, a researcher published a working bypass and tools on 9 August 2020 without notifying the vendor; the Defcon forums were attacked within hours; vBulletin issued patches the next day; the interim workaround is disabling PHP and ad module rendering. Steve recommends containing forum software behind a deny-by-default external firewall with its own database.
- Windows 10 Insider channels renamed (Dev, Beta, Release Preview); build 20185 brings native DoH.
- Have I Been Pwned: after a failed sale process Troy Hunt will open-source the code; he added governments and 1.7 billion breach records during the year.
- Bleeping Computer registry tips (disable Bing in search, verbose boot mode) helped Steve find a service stalling shutdown. Bloatbox again did not impress him.
- SpinRite R&D: some SSDs need transfers of 32K sectors instead of 64K; a bad SATA connector made a drive lose link, so SpinRite will distinguish link errors (encoding, CRC, framing) from media errors.
- Great Firewall: China began blocking TLS 1.3 connections using ESNI, dropping traffic for 120 to 180 seconds on any port, bidirectionally.
- Geneva: a genetic algorithm with a strategy engine that duplicates, tampers with, drops or fragments packets; deployed against censors in China, India, Iran and Kazakhstan, found dozens of evasions. For ESNI it found strategies such as sending an extra malformed SYN and splitting the first TLS bytes across segments; some also work from the server side. Steve sees promise for "smart fuzzing"; Leo worries about autonomous attack agents.
