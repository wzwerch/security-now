# Summary

With Jason Howell filling in for Leo Laporte, Steve starts with Tavis Ormandy's second LastPass extension report in two weeks, which LastPass was already handling and which Steve says reflects a fundamental design issue that will take weeks to fix; he argues that heavily scrutinized products improve and recommends KeePass for people who want no browser integration. iOS 10.3 brought a long list of security fixes, and a new bot called GiftGhostBot brute forces gift card balances. Microsoft's Docs.com exposed many users' documents because sharing defaults were public, the UK government again pressed for access to encrypted messengers after the Westminster attack, and the US Senate and House voted to repeal FCC broadband privacy rules. Nest sat on a researcher's camera denial-of-service report for four months, a professional dishwasher shipped with a directory traversal bug, and listeners asked about TOTP, DNSCrypt and SpinRite. The main topic is Google's announced plan to distrust Symantec-issued certificates after finding about 30,000 improperly validated certificates, using shrinking maximum validity periods and removing Extended Validation treatment.

# Key points

- Tavis Ormandy reported another LastPass 4.1.43 browser extension code-execution flaw; LastPass publicly acknowledged within hours and withheld details. Steve says the previous bug was fixed before Tavis's 90-day clock moved, and he continues to use LastPass while noting KeePass as a non-browser-integrated alternative.
- iOS 10.3 introduced the new Apple file system and fixed a very large batch of vulnerabilities, with the most in WebKit (17), the kernel (8, all code execution), image and font parsing and others; Steve notes even a mature, tightly curated OS still has many bugs and says new code adds new bugs.
- GiftGhostBot (Distil Networks): a distributed bot rotating over 740 user-agent profiles and running JavaScript tries gift card numbers against balance-check pages, then drains balances; advice is to use balances promptly and expect CAPTCHAs or offline balance checks. Gift cards lack the fraud protection of credit cards.
- Docs.com: documents uploaded were public by default and indexed by search engines, exposing credentials, financial and medical data; Microsoft removed the search bar and pointed users to privacy settings.
- UK Home Secretary Amber Rudd said encrypted messaging like WhatsApp is unacceptable after the Westminster attack; Steve expects legislation compelling per-user access via warrants, not a master key, and recalls suspending his CryptoLink project for regulatory risk. Vault 7 showed intelligence agencies can attack endpoints around WhatsApp and Telegram.
- US Senate voted 50 to 48 and the House 215 to 205 to repeal the October 2016 FCC rules requiring consent before ISPs sell browsing data; Steve notes ISPs mostly see DNS queries and IP addresses, suggests DNSCrypt and VPNs, and fears ISPs may one day require customers to install their root certificate.
- Nest/Dropcam cameras had two Bluetooth buffer overflows and a WiFi disassociation trick that could black out video; Bluetooth stays enabled after setup. Google's program validated the report in October 2016 but nothing was fixed until the researcher published in March, which Steve calls the worst possible response.
- A Miele professional dishwasher/disinfector had an embedded web server vulnerable to classic directory traversal, letting a proof of concept read the Linux shadow file; Steve uses it for "I Don't IoT" commentary.
- TOTP clarifications: HMAC-SHA1 collisions do not matter for authenticator codes; Google Authenticator keys cannot be exported so Steve prints setup QR codes; SMS is the weakest second factor; eBay replaced its hardware token with SMS and Instagram added SMS-only two-factor.
- DNSCrypt is the practical way to hide DNS lookups from an ISP; SQRL server-side code is finished and client work is in progress.
- Google's Symantec action (announced by Ryan Sleevi): Symantec delegated issuance to partner CAs (CrossCert, Certisign, Certsuperior, Certisur) without adequate oversight; at least 30,000 certificates were misissued, up from an initial 127. Chrome will gradually cap the accepted validity of Symantec certificates, from 33 months in Chrome 59 down to nine months by Chrome 64, and will immediately stop showing Extended Validation indicators for Symantec certificates for at least a year. Steve praises Google, recommends DigiCert, and expects Symantec to lose share.

# Other segments

- Steve recounts how ShieldsUP began from finding exposed Windows file shares near GRC's first static IP, and how Leo's invitation led to the podcast in 2005.
- Picture of the Week and quip about a computer that deliberately fails the Turing test.
- Conway's Game of Life runs in a Google search box.
- SpinRite listener who runs it quarterly and replaces drives when SMART numbers worsen; mention of The Good Fight.
