# Summary

The first episode of year 17 begins with Firefox 91's Enhanced Cookie Clearing, which builds on the per-site partitioning of Total Cookie Protection (Firefox 86): because all third-party data is stored in the jar of the site being visited, clearing a site wipes everything it caused to be stored. Facebook Messenger adds optional end-to-end encrypted voice and video calls, new disappearing-message timers, and tests for group chats and Instagram DMs. Steve reports that PrintNightmare is now used in real ransomware campaigns (Vice Society, per Cisco Talos; Magniber in South Korea, per CrowdStrike), and describes a Linux cryptomining botnet that disables CPU hardware prefetch via the MSR driver for roughly 15% better XMRig speed. Shorter items cover the NortonLifeLock and Avast merger, ASUS BIOS updates adding TPM 2.0 for Windows 11 on 207 boards, errata, and listener questions on IoT segmentation. The main segment turns a routine Patch Tuesday review (44 fixes, three zero-days) into an indictment: Microsoft disclosed another print spooler remote code execution flaw (CVE-2021-36958) that had been reported to it in December 2020, which Steve reads as deliberate cost-benefit neglect by a company with enormous cash reserves.

# Key points

- Firefox 91 Enhanced Cookie Clearing: with Strict mode and Total Cookie Protection, "forget this site" removes cookies, supercookies and other site data including embedded third-party storage. Steve wants the architecture in Chromium and wants JavaScript denied sensor-style fingerprinting data without permission.
- Facebook Messenger: opt-in E2EE for voice/video calls, disappearing-message timers from 5 seconds to 24 hours, group E2EE tests, and opt-in encryption for Instagram DMs between existing contacts.
- PrintNightmare now seen in the wild: Vice Society ransomware moving laterally (Talos) and Magniber ransomware in South Korea (CrowdStrike).
- Uptycs found a cryptomining botnet on Linux servers that loads the MSR driver to turn off hardware prefetch for faster XMRig mining, which also slows the host and increases heat and power use.
- NortonLifeLock and Avast merge in a deal around $8.1 to $8.6 billion, giving about 500 million users; dual headquarters in Prague and Tempe.
- ASUS offers updated BIOSes with TPM 2.0 enabled for 207 motherboards (Intel PTT or AMD fTPM) to ease the Windows 11 requirement.
- Microsoft's August Patch Tuesday: 44 fixes, seven critical, three zero-days; one day later a new spooler RCE (CVE-2021-36958) was acknowledged after being reported in December 2020. Will Dormann asked why anyone bothers with disclosure.
- Steve's thesis: with $130 billion in cash and a captive customer base, Microsoft's delay on Exchange and spooler bugs cannot be explained by capacity; attackers get blamed while unfixed vendor flaws are tolerated. He calls it culpable negligence, while conceding that he still depends on Windows.
- Listener points: Apple's CSAM approach may relate to future iCloud backup encryption (Steve is sceptical); the "tyranny of the default" in organ donation opt-in versus opt-out; a service to broadcast firmware update availability; running an old router as an isolated access point on a separate pfSense interface for IoT.
