# Summary

Steve covers a new wave of Salesforce data theft in which the merged Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters group abused tokens belonging to Gainsight's connected apps, affecting more than 200 Salesforce instances, and uses it to argue that today's API-linked outsourcing model is too fragile. He is delighted by a Cisco executive post announcing "Resilient Infrastructure": secure defaults, warnings about insecure options, eventual removal of legacy insecure features and virtual "shields" instead of waiting for patches. Next is Australia's under-16 social media ban starting 10 December 2025, which in practice forces all users to prove their age, and a letter from European Parliament members urging the Parliament to drop Microsoft and other US technology. A long listener-mail section answers when to use passwords, passkeys and YubiKeys, whether idle SSDs keep their data, XSLT dependencies (RSS feed styling, IIS failed-request traces), a Bitwarden extension passkey bug, VPN-blocking experience, copy-protected floppies and the limits of AI on niche assembly code. The episode ends with GreyNoise's free IP Check, which Steve shortcuts as grc.sc/botcheck, and explains why NAT makes the single public IP a useful health indicator for every device behind it.

# Key points

- Gainsight/Salesforce: Salesforce revoked all Gainsight app tokens and pulled the apps from AppExchange; Google's threat intelligence group counts over 200 affected instances. ShinyHunters says it reached Gainsight through the earlier Salesloft Drift token theft and plans an extortion site; named alleged victims include security and software firms, though some deny impact. CrowdStrike says it fired a suspicious insider. Steve's lesson: outsourced, API-linked services widen the blast radius and static credentials are a crude model.
- Scattered Lapsus$ Hunters is an English-speaking collective of ShinyHunters, Scattered Spider and Lapsus$ members using social engineering (earlier victims include MGM, Coinbase, DoorDash). Leo mentions Brian Krebs tracing a teenage administrator, a reminder that these actors are being identified.
- Cisco Resilient Infrastructure (post by Anthony Grieco, chief security and trust officer): modeled on Microsoft's Secure Future Initiative; defaults secure, explicit warnings for insecure choices, legacy methods deprecated then removed, better monitoring, planned real-time virtual patching "shields", and post-quantum readiness; Cisco cites 48% of network assets as aging or obsolete. Steve sees a real shift from an optional hardening guide to built-in responsibility.
- Australia: from 10 December 2025 platforms must take reasonable steps to keep under-16s off accounts; eSafety listed Facebook, Instagram, Kick, Reddit, Snapchat, Threads, TikTok, X, YouTube and Twitch (Pinterest judged out of scope). Because every user must prove age, Steve and Leo expect chaos; small services like Mastodon instances and forums have no verification mechanism, and VPN demand is expected.
- European Parliament: 38 MEPs urge phasing out Microsoft products, including Windows, and US-made hardware in favour of European options (Vivaldi, Qwant, Proton, Nextcloud).
- Best practice: use a cross-platform password manager (1Password and Bitwarden sync passkeys), choose passkeys wherever offered, and reserve hardware keys (YubiKey stores private keys in a hardware module, limited slot count, no fallback) for a few high-value accounts.
- SSDs: Steve believes powered-on idle SSDs do not rewrite their own cells; SpinRite Level 3 rewrites revive aging drives; store archival drives cool because charge leakage rises with heat.
- A coding-focused listener request leads Steve and Leo to discuss a possible one-off roundtable on coding, with Leo pointing to YouTube and Advent of Code.
- Bitwarden browser extension 2025.11.1 broke passkey prompts for some Firefox users; roll back to 2025.11.0 until the fix.
- XSLT: RSS feed previews depend on it; Steve discovered IIS Failed Request Tracing logs use an XSL stylesheet to render in the browser, so Microsoft will need a workaround.
- Ethan Stone describes how many sites flag VPN exit IPs and silently degrade service; Steve notes lists of VPN IPs exist (for example, IP lookup services mark them).
- Copy-protected 3.5-inch floppies: Steve explains protection tricks (data in the track slack zone, extra cylinder 81, deliberately bad sectors) that make imaging unreliable; Leo suggests patching out the check in a disassembler.
- Passkey multiple identities: authenticators should offer several passkeys per site for different accounts, though shared-account support varies.
- AI limits: a listener's ChatGPT swapped two disk enclosures; Steve's own request for 32-bit Intel multiply code produced nonsense because assembly training data is sparse. LLM knowledge is spiky; whether LLMs can go beyond pattern matching is the open trillion-dollar question.
- GreyNoise IP Check: free lookup of whether your public IP was seen scanning or in residential-proxy and botnet activity (Clean, Malicious/Suspicious, or Common Business Service), with a 90-day timeline and a JSON API. Because of NAT, a clean result covers every device behind the router.
