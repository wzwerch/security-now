# Summary

Meltdown and Spectre still dominate. Intel reversed its guidance: the new Spectre variant 2 microcode caused higher-than-expected reboots and unpredictable behavior on Broadwell, Haswell and other families, so OEMs and users were told to stop deploying it and (e.g. Dell) to offer earlier BIOS versions, which re-opens the variant 2 hole. Steve advises that people with stable systems who already updated stay put, those who have not yet updated wait, and that Meltdown, the only variant with a proof of concept, is already fixed in software. He defends Intel against Linus Torvalds' public tirade, arguing microcode can only steer existing hardware, so the realistic fix is a switch that disables the risky branch prediction, with real fixes in future chip generations. Other items: a 32-bit Windows 10 1709 update that adds Meltdown mitigation (via the Update Catalog), Net Neutrality measuring apps such as OONI, DuckDuckGo's browser extension and apps with HTTPS upgrading, Tim Cook promising a switch for iPhone throttling and battery health display, and several errata and listener Q&As. The main story is Dark Caracal: a 51-page EFF and Lookout report tracing a multi-year, multiplatform surveillance operation, run from the Lebanese General Directorate of General Security building in Beirut, that stole data from thousands of victims in 21+ countries.

# Key points

- Intel microcode: root cause found for Broadwell and Haswell reboot problems; stop-deploy guidance covers many generations; reverting microcode removes the Spectre variant 2 mitigation but not variant 1 or Meltdown. Red Hat had also pulled microcode.
- Meltdown fix does not need firmware; high-performance mitigation needs PCID plus INVPCID, available from Haswell on (Steve corrects his earlier misstatement). Spectre is harder to exploit; no in-the-wild exploit known.
- Torvalds' complaint centers on Intel's "solution" being a bit that disables branch prediction; Steve explains microcode is only a traffic cop over fixed hardware, so a true fix needs new silicon.
- KB4073291 brings Meltdown and Spectre mitigations to 32-bit Windows 10 1709, available only through the Update Catalog; Microsoft's earlier 32-bit updates covered only two of the three variants.
- Timer fuzzing idea: reducing RDTSC resolution to about a microsecond would blur the cache-timing measurements the attacks need without harming real applications.
- OONI (Open Observatory of Network Interference): open-source crowd-sourced measurement app to detect Net Neutrality violations; Steve warns that streaming behaviour can create false alarms.
- DuckDuckGo privacy extension/apps: tracker blocking and "smarter encryption" upgrading to HTTPS when available.
- Apple: iOS throttling switch and battery-health UI promised.
- Dark Caracal: spear-phishing and social-media lures to watering-hole sites (fake Flash updates, spoofed Facebook/Twitter, trojanized Signal, Threema, WhatsApp and Telegram-client apps on Android implanted with a tool Lookout named Pallas), cross-platform Java RAT "CrossRAT", Bandook, a FinFisher variant; no zero-days; infrastructure shared with an earlier campaign aimed at Kazakhstan critics; hosting at a permissive provider; 90+ indicators of compromise; victims included military, lawyers, journalists, utilities and defense firms. Steve stresses every nation-state likely runs similar programs.
- Leo mentions a BuzzFeed report alleging Russian state influence over Kaspersky's cloud network; Steve treats it as hearsay.

# Other segments

- Cartoon about a login that hints how close a wrong password is, illustrating why error messages should be uniform.
- Errata on INVPCID timing; InSpectre's yes/no indicators are confusing and may be reversed; InSpectre on WINE reads real CPU registers.
- Listener Q&A: iPhone battery care (avoid deep discharge; modern laptops hold at partial charge), WebMon cannot monitor HTTPS sites, shared hosts that do not offer free certificates, a SpinRite Level 3 success story.
- News of author Ursula K. Le Guin's death.
