# Summary

Steve and Leo chat about Apple's new iPads and iOS 7.0.3's option to replace animations with a dissolve, then discuss John McAfee's brief role as an adviser to a congressional panel on the troubled HealthCare.gov site. Steve notes Google's free Project Shield, which shields politically sensitive sites from denial of service attacks, and the shutdown of the CryptoSeal consumer VPN, whose operators wiped their keys because the US government argues it can compel cryptographic keys through pen register orders. The main news item is CryptoLocker, ransomware spread by phishing that finds its server through generated domain names, receives a per-victim RSA-2048 public key, encrypts documents with AES-256 across every reachable drive and share, destroys the symmetric key and demands about 300 dollars in Bitcoin or prepaid cards within 72 hours; Steve calls the cryptography flawless and stresses offline backups. He then covers Mozilla's Shumway Flash renderer written in JavaScript, three messaging apps (BlackBerry Messenger's global key makes it only "scrambled", Heml.is will not publish its design, and Threema is end-to-end encrypted with a verification-by-QR trust ladder), the Patent Office upholding all claims of Apple's multi-touch "Steve Jobs patent", and a SQRL update: after newsgroup objections, Steve designed an out-of-band identity unlock mechanism so a compromised identity cannot be silently replaced.

# Key points

- iOS 7.0.3 added a setting that disables the swooping animations in favor of a dissolve. Steve will test both iPad mini and Air and has begun switching from BlackBerry to an iPhone 5s.
- HealthCare.gov problems were attributed to integrating dozens of databases and loading dozens of script files; McAfee had been chosen as adviser before the government shutdown dropped him.
- Google Project Shield provides free, invitation-based DDoS absorption for at-risk news, human rights and election sites, not a commercial service.
- CryptoSeal Privacy VPN closed after zero-filling all keys, saying that US law might force keys to be handed over; Steve says wiping keys protects previously captured traffic, and suggests using non-US VPN endpoints.
- Dan Geer's argument discussed: a demand for total security implies total information awareness; metadata is often more valuable than content; IBM's Watson is being used for security analysis.
- CryptoLocker: installed from phishing links, persists via autorun, contacts one of many algorithmically generated domains, gets an RSA-2048 public key, encrypts files with AES-256 across local, mapped and shared drives (hot backups too), erases the key and shows a countdown demanding 300 dollars; law enforcement takedowns of key servers make recovery impossible. Defense: cold or offline backups, not clicking email links, and warning friends. Steve contrasts it with the 1999 CIH/Chernobyl virus that he mitigated with a free fix tool.
- A listener's SpinRite story from Bulgaria involved trading computer repair for dental work and a dinner.
- Shumway (Mozilla's AreWeFlashYet project) is an HTML5/JavaScript renderer for SWF files.
- BlackBerry Messenger launched for iOS and Android with 10 million downloads in a day; PIN-to-PIN traffic uses a global Triple-DES key and passes through BlackBerry servers, so BlackBerry itself calls it scrambled not encrypted.
- Heml.is says it uses XMPP with PGP, dismisses OTR and will not allow third-party clients; Steve says closed designs cannot be trusted.
- Threema (about 2 dollars, Swiss, iOS and Android): end-to-end elliptic-curve encryption, server cannot read messages; a three-dot trust display (red: key fetched from server, orange: matched to a contact by phone or email, green: verified in person by QR scan).
- The Patent Office reexamination upheld all 20 claims of Apple's touch-gesture patent; Steve and Leo find claims such as vertical scrolling obvious, and expect litigation.
- SQRL: identity changes proposed last week were criticised for allowing a possibly compromised key to authorize its replacement, so Steve designed a separate identity unlock key, never stored in non-volatile memory, used only to change identity associations; web servers need only some extra storage.
- SQRL.pl, a community illustrated guide, appeared; the newsgroup neared 3,000 posts.
