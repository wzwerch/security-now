# Summary

Steve starts with a password-strength chart and new Chrome features (command-style omnibox suggestions and tab search). Ransomware items cover Delaware County, Pennsylvania paying about half a million dollars after a DoppelPaymer attack, Canon's confirmation of a Maze attack, U.S. Fertility, Danish news agency Ritzau declining to pay, Baltimore County schools telling users to rely on Chromebooks, and a DoppelPaymer hit on Banijay. A critical Drupal flaw via the PEAR Archive_Tar library affects nearly a million sites. NCC Group found serious flaws in cheap no-brand video doorbells, including sending Wi-Fi credentials to servers in China. Researcher Lennert Wouters again defeated Tesla Model X key fobs, this time through the fob firmware update path (fixed in software 2020.48). DigiCert introduced multi-year plans for the new 398-day certificate limit. Leaks include about 50,000 Fortinet VPN credentials and a credential-stuffing database used against hundreds of thousands of Spotify accounts. MobileIron products received fixes for a 9.8-rated remote code execution flaw. Steve describes how the ReadSpeed benchmark reveals SSD slowdowns, suggesting SpinRite can rewrite weakened regions. The main topic is a Carnegie Mellon study showing DNS provider consolidation worsened since the 2016 Dyn attack.

# Key points

- Chrome omnibox: enabling "Omnibox suggestion button row" and "pedal suggestions" flags turns typed commands such as clearing cache into buttons; an optional tab search feature is coming. Firefox added similar commands earlier.
- DoppelPaymer (successor of BitPaymer) added multithreaded encryption; the gang advised Delaware County to guard against Mimikatz, an open source credential-harvesting tool. Canon confirmed a Maze breach that stole data; the image.canon outage was said to be unrelated. Ritzau restored encrypted servers without paying.
- Drupal: update to 9.0.9, 8.9.10, 8.8.12 or 7.75; flaw arises from the PEAR Archive_Tar library when handling uploaded .tar-type files; about 944,000 of 1.12 million known sites were on vulnerable versions. WordPress holds about 64% CMS share.
- NCC Group tested 11 off-brand smart doorbells: Victure and Ctronics models had critical flaws, one sent Wi-Fi name and password unencrypted to China, another was vulnerable to KRACK. Advice: buy major brands and isolate IoT on a separate network. The proposed US IoT Cybersecurity Improvement bill would require patchability and no hard-coded passwords for government purchases.
- Tesla Model X: attacker with a salvaged older ECU lures a victim's fob into accepting a malicious Bluetooth firmware update, extracts unlock messages, then pairs their own key via the diagnostic connector; requires brief proximity and cheap hardware. Tesla fixed it over the air.
- DigiCert allows multi-year commitments with certificates reissued at will within the 398-day maximum; Steve moved from EV to OV certificates; he would like a flag distinguishing automated from human-vetted issuance.
- About 50,000 Fortinet SSL VPN credential records (from the previously patched path traversal flaw) leaked; remediation requires resetting credentials as well as patching.
- A database of 380 million records was used to credential-stuff 300,000 to 350,000 Spotify accounts; Spotify lacked rate limiting and multifactor authentication then and reset affected passwords.
- MobileIron (reported by Orange Tsai): critical RCE across Core, Connector, Sentry and reporting components; the vendor estimated 90-95% of devices were on patched versions.
- SpinRite and SSDs: ReadSpeed measures timing at sub-200-picosecond resolution, exposing regions slowed by flash translation layer error handling or cell drift; Allyn Malventano advised rewriting slow sectors in 4K or 8K aligned chunks. A full-drive scan of a 500 GB SATA SSD at about 544 MB/s takes about 15.5 minutes.
- DNS consolidation (CMU, IMC 2020): 89.2% of sites use third-party DNS, 84.8% rely on a single provider with no backup (up 4.7% since 2016); Cloudflare (24%), AWS (12%) and GoDaddy (4%) alone serve about 38% of the top 100,000 sites. Dyn's 2016 Mirai attack took down about 175,000 sites. Steve recommends two separate DNS providers for anything mission critical.
