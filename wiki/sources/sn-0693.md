# Summary

Steve starts with Australia's Telecommunications (Assistance and Access) Bill, passing with bipartisan support, which creates three kinds of notices (voluntary request, mandatory assistance, and a capability-building notice) that can compel providers to help access communications; Apple's submission called it dangerously ambiguous. Steve argues technology could offer escrowed per-device keys within a single vendor's ecosystem, while Leo notes the multi-vendor problem. Twelve US states sued Medical Informatics Engineering over a 2015 SQL-injection breach of 3.9 million health records, citing shared test accounts, no exfiltration alarms and late notification. A scraped database of 66 million professional profiles was exposed on MongoDB and added to Have I Been Pwned. Microsoft's president called for facial-recognition regulation, a Sophos-reported click-fraud scheme spoofed iOS user agents, Marriott said it would reimburse passport replacement in fraud cases, and DHS is soliciting tools to trace Zcash and Monero. Tor's 2017 filing shows US government funding down to 51 percent with Mozilla contributing heavily. CoAP, an IoT protocol using unauthenticated UDP, has about 600,000 exposed devices mostly in China and is already used for roughly 46-fold DDoS amplification; Steve sketches a stateless permit scheme that would fix it. Errata cover Sennheiser follow-up (Chrome's pinning may not protect Google domains) and Blizzard doing the per-machine certificate correctly. The main topic: Google found and fixed a Google+ API bug itself (52.5 million users affected), illustrating the four ways bugs are discovered and why internal red teams matter.

# Key points

- Australian bill: technical assistance request, notice and capability notice; stated limit against creating systemic weaknesses is vague; Five Eyes context ("privacy is not absolute").
- Suit by 12 attorneys general against MIE and NoMoreClipboard: unencrypted data, generic "tester" accounts, no data exfiltration alerting, delayed notices; proposed consent decree with multifactor authentication, monitoring and audits; Uber's 148 million dollar 50-state settlement cited.
- Exposed MongoDB with 66,147,856 scraped professional records; owner unknown.
- Microsoft's Brad Smith lists questions regulation should answer: human oversight, accuracy minimums, notice and consent, rights of the misidentified.
- Android apps on Google Play spoofed iOS user agents to earn higher ad click payouts.
- Marriott will reimburse passport replacements after proven fraud.
- DHS pre-solicitation seeks forensics for privacy coins like Zcash and Monero; law enforcement already traced most ransomware cash-outs through the BTC-e exchange.
- Tor revenue 4.2 million dollars in 2017; US government share fell from 85 percent (2015) to 51 percent; Mozilla gave over half a million dollars.
- CoAP: unauthenticated UDP with about 46-50x amplification; attacks averaging 55 Gbps, largest 320 Gbps; DTLS option too heavy; Steve proposes a stateless encrypted-IP permit round trip.
- Sennheiser follow-up: Chrome did not warn on forged Google cert in the researchers' test; Blizzard Battle.net uses a per-machine self-signed cert, the acceptable pattern.
- Google+ API bug exposed non-public profile data to apps for six days; Google accelerated the shutdown of the consumer product and APIs.
- Four discoverers of bugs: black hat, gray hat (irresponsible disclosure), white hat, and the company itself; only internal discovery lets a firm control the outcome but it costs money; Microsoft ignored its own Windows 10 feedback data.
