# Summary

The episode is largely about Google's FLoC (Federated Learning of Cohorts). Vivaldi announced it will not support FLoC, DuckDuckGo will send a FLoC-blocking header, and WordPress proposed adding a four-line header to core; Steve argues much of the criticism is misinformed. Chrome 89 got an out-of-band fix for two exploited bugs (one shown at Pwn2Own), and Chrome 90 brings HTTPS-by-default for typed URLs, a renewed block on port 554, AV1 for WebRTC and a coming "link to highlight" feature. The US Justice Department and FBI, under a court order, removed one hacking group's web shells from hundreds of vulnerable on-premises Exchange servers without the owners' prior consent, a first of this scale. After a Humble Bundle of O'Reilly Head First books and listener feedback (TTL-limited management access, signed vaccination credentials, QNAP defaults), Steve reports SpinRite 6.1 progress. He closes with Google's paper on the "sensitivity of cohorts": FLoC cohorts are k-anonymous and must also satisfy t-closeness for sensitive categories, defending against homogeneity attacks where everyone in a cohort shares a sensitive attribute. His view: if all other tracking could truly be killed, a short-lived coarse cohort tag would be a privacy improvement, though adoption looks doubtful.

# Key points

- Vivaldi says it disables FLoC and calls it privacy-invasive profiling; Edge, Opera and Brave also do not enable it; DuckDuckGo and WordPress (proposal) would add the opt-out header so visits do not feed cohort calculation.
- Chrome 89 update fixed two flaws with exploits in the wild, including a V8 bug shown at Pwn2Own; the second went from report to patch in under a week, which Steve contrasts with Microsoft's Exchange handling.
- Chrome 90 tries HTTPS first when no scheme is typed, saving the port 80 redirect hop for most sites.
- Port 554 is blocked again in Chrome 90 (NAT Slipstreaming risk; only about 0.00003% of requests used it).
- Chrome 90 adds AV1 for WebRTC video and is rolling out "Link to a Highlight" using an extended URL fragment syntax from a W3C draft.
- DOJ/FBI court-authorized operation copied and removed web shells (identified by unique file path) from hundreds of US Exchange servers compromised via the early-2021 zero-days; it did not patch the servers or remove other malware; owners were notified by email. Steve recalls DOJ once refusing a similar vigilante idea against worms.
- FLoC facts: coarse hashed cohort ID derived from only seven days of browsing, computed in the browser, ad techs see cohort numbers instead of detailed histories; Google cites studies saying personalised ads can be 50-65% of publisher revenue.
- Google's paper "Measuring Sensitivity of Cohorts Generated by the FLoC API" requires k-anonymity plus t-closeness against sensitive categories (medical, adult, political, religious) taken from Google's ad policy, to prevent homogeneity attacks.
- Leo's counterpoints: cohort IDs still add fingerprinting bits, and Google must keep FLoC useful to advertisers; Steve compares the fate of FLoC to Do Not Track.
- Listener idea: lower TTL on management-interface replies so only nearby hosts can reach them (partial mitigation, not a solution).
- Signed QR codes could make unforgeable vaccination credentials; the technology (digital signatures) already exists.
- QNAP and Plex flaws noted last time involved features not enabled by default, per a listener.
