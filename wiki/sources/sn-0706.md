# Summary

Steve reviews March 2019 Patch Tuesday: 64 CVEs, including fixes for the win32k.sys privilege-escalation zero-days that Google had paired with a Chrome bug, and a second in-the-wild win32k flaw reported by Kaspersky that two threat actors (FruityArmor and SandCat) used, hinting at a shared exploit supplier. He confirms the Windows 7 SHA-2 update arrived and criticises Chinese vendor 360's rapid publication of a proof of concept. Hundreds of exploits now target the WinRAR ACE path-traversal flaw, since WinRAR has no auto-update and about 500 million installs. A discussion on mozilla.dev.security.policy about DarkMatter exposed that certificates from GoDaddy, Apple and Google carried 63-bit rather than 64-bit serial numbers because of an EJBCA default, forcing mass re-issuance, which Steve considers a tempest in a teapot. Further stories cover a Mirai variant that targets enterprise presentation systems and signage TVs, a Counter Strike 1.6 trojan botnet that ran 39% of game servers, privacy changes in Android Q, and Microsoft's Application Guard extensions that push Chrome and Firefox users into Edge. The main segment describes DARPA's $10 million project, led by Galois, to build open-source, verifiable voting systems.

# Key points

- March 2019 Patch Tuesday: 64 CVEs (17 critical); win32k.sys elevation-of-privilege zero-days fixed, one used with Chrome against Windows 7, another (affecting later Windows) reported by Kaspersky and used by FruityArmor and SandCat; 360 Core Security published a PoC two days after the patch, which Steve thinks is too soon.
- Windows 7 SHA-2 signing support (KB4474419) arrived in the March updates, so systems can still get updates once Microsoft stops dual-signing.
- WinRAR: McAfee counted over a hundred exploit samples; one disguised as a pirated pop album dropped a remote access trojan into Startup and was detected by only 11 antivirus engines at first; another delivered a .NET ransomware demanding 0.05 BTC.
- 63-bit serial numbers: EJBCA generated 8-byte serials with the sign bit forced to zero, giving 63 bits of entropy versus the 64 required by CA/Browser Forum rules; GoDaddy alone had issued about 1.8 million affected certificates and could not re-issue within seven days. Steve explains two's complement and calculates that the collision odds change from 1 in 2^64 to 1 in 2^63, which he considers insignificant; DigiCert certificates use longer serials. TWiT's GoDaddy certificate was affected.
- Mirai variant (Palo Alto Unit 42): 11 additional exploits plus four credential sets, now targeting wePresent WiPG-1000 presentation systems and LG Supersign TVs, signalling a shift toward enterprise networks.
- Counter Strike 1.6: Dr. Web found 1,951 (about 39%) of roughly 5,000 servers distributed the Belonard trojan via client remote-code-execution flaws and then used victims' PCs to promote other servers.
- Android Q: clipboard access limited to the foreground app (except the keyboard), MAC address randomisation on by default, restricted /proc/net, special permission for non-resettable identifiers such as IMEI, and location access while in use only. Leo cites research that randomisation can still be defeated.
- Windows Defender Application Guard extensions for Chrome and Firefox (Insider Build 18358) redirect untrusted URLs into an isolated Edge session in a Hyper-V container, driven by enterprise trusted-site lists; Steve is baffled by the approach.
- AMD stated its processors are not susceptible to SPOILER because they do not match partial addresses above bit 11 when resolving load conflicts.
- DARPA and Galois: $10 million project for open-source hardware and software voting systems; a ballot-marking device with human-readable printout and no barcodes, then an optical-scan system issuing a cryptographic receipt so voters can verify inclusion and anyone can write a tabulator; prototypes go to the DEF CON Voting Village in 2019 and 2020 and to university test teams; designs will be free for vendors to adopt. Linton Salmon of DARPA and Joe Kiniry of Galois are quoted.
- Leo reports MySpace lost uploads from 2003 to 2015 during a server migration, and Norsk Hydro was hit by LockerGoga ransomware.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: McAfee's site asking visitors to unblock social features, an irony Steve noted.
- Never10 passed 3 million downloads and DNS Benchmark has about 4.7 million; Steve plans SpinRite 6.1 first (fast direct AHCI), then USB support in 6.2, releasing interim builds to owners.
- Android Q naming banter; Leo shows a book photograph of an ARPANET Interface Message Processor.
