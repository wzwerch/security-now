# Summary

Steve notes that Windows 11 installs on unsupported hardware are receiving updates, then covers October Patch Tuesday: another round of network-printing breakage after a PrintNightmare fix, a new Windows 11 printer-installation bug, a Word/Office/SharePoint RCE (CVE-2021-40486) and one exploited Win32k zero-day (CVE-2021-40449) used by IronHusky with the MysterySnail RAT. REvil shut down a second time after its Tor portal was apparently hijacked; the new admin said this was final. A US-led meeting of 30 countries plus the EU produced a counter-ransomware statement focused on money laundering and crypto exchanges, with China and Russia absent. China's Tianfu Cup paid out $1.88 million for exploits against Windows 10, iOS 15, Chrome and others, including a zero-click iPhone 13 Pro remote compromise. Avast reported that the MyKings botnet's clipboard-hijacking module diverted about $24.7 million in cryptocurrency, and LinkedIn announced it would shrink to a jobs-only service in China. SpinRite's fourth pre-release went to testers. The main segment retells Minh Duong's senior prank in Illinois District 214: using default credentials on classroom IPTV boxes and the bell system, he made every display and bell play "Never Gonna Give You Up", then delivered a 26-page report and avoided charges.

# Key points

- Windows 11: reports indicate unsupported-hardware installs still receive updates, so the "no updates" threat looks hollow; Steve still sees little reason to upgrade.
- October Patch Tuesday (74 flaws): KB5006670 caused 0x709 network-printing errors; Point and Print is a feature Microsoft is trying to patch, creating monthly breakage; CVE-2021-36970 spooler spoofing fixed; Windows 11 had a separate HTTP/IPP printer install failure.
- CVE-2021-40486 (Word/Office/SharePoint RCE exploitable via preview) and the exploited Win32k elevation CVE-2021-40449 (Kaspersky: IronHusky, MysterySnail RAT) were fixed.
- REvil shut down again after the admin "0_neday" said someone compromised its hidden-service setup; the group was already distrusted over stolen affiliate funds and an alleged backdoor. A Recorded Future analyst suspected a government operation.
- Counter-Ransomware Initiative (30 countries plus EU): stress on cyber hygiene, information sharing and anti-money-laundering controls for virtual asset providers; US Treasury sanctioned the Suex exchange; FinCEN tied large bitcoin flows to ransomware, with REvil, Conti, DarkSide, Avaddon and Phobos most reported. Steve doubts one $5.2 billion figure's valuation basis.
- Tianfu Cup 2021 (Chengdu): 13 of 16 targets fell, Windows 10 five times, iOS 15 three times, Chrome twice; Kunlun Lab won $654,500 and a zero-click iPhone 13 Pro Safari chain earned the biggest single award. Western observers worry about state use of such exploits since a 2020 iOS exploit was later used against Uyghurs.
- MyKings (Smominru, DarkCloud) botnet: Avast analysed 6,700 samples and 1,300 wallet addresses, finding at least $24.7 million (Bitcoin, Ether, mostly Dogecoin) mainly from clipboard hijacking that swaps pasted wallet addresses; over 144,000 PCs hit since 2020 in Avast's telemetry.
- LinkedIn will replace its China service with a jobs-only app after regulatory pressure and censorship burden.
- Rickroll case: the student scanned a school district network as a freshman, found IoT devices with default credentials, and on 30 April 2021 synchronised a countdown and video on all classroom displays, then replaced the bell tones. Steve stresses it was illegal and that leniency was lucky; the key lessons are default credentials, shared student and admin networks, and segmentation.
- The prank was carefully non-disruptive (avoiding exam times), self-cleaning, and documented in a 26-page report; the district declined discipline and invited a debrief, and the student revealed himself only after graduating.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a roadside sign asking whether anyone tried unplugging the United States and plugging it back in.
- SpinRite 6.1: fourth pre-release sent to the dev newsgroup for hardware testing; Steve is fixing a regression with an Intel chipset on Lenovo laptops and planning a surprise benchmark-screen feature.
- Brief aside on a state governor who called a reporter's View Source discovery hacking (raised by listeners and Leo).
- Apple TV's "Foundation" chatter and an Apple event mention.
