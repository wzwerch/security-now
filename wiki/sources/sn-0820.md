# Summary

Firefox's Fission project, full site isolation with a separate OS process per domain, is now available (enable `fission.autostart` in about:config), bringing it level with Chrome's site isolation at the cost of more memory. The Conti ransomware crew (Wizard Spider, Trickbot/BazarLoader operators) shut down Ireland's entire Health Service Executive, demanded just under $20 million, then released a free decryptor while still threatening to leak 700 GB; Ireland's High Court issued an injunction mainly to limit republishing of leaked data. CNA Financial paid about $40 million to a ransomware group, and insurers such as AXA are retreating from ransom reimbursement while the GAO reports rising premiums and tighter coverage. Steve argues that "IoT" botnets like Simps/Gafgyt actually infect ordinary NAT routers, so no listening ports should be exposed. A Palo Alto Cortex Xpanse study coins "mean time to inventory": scanning for a newly disclosed bug can begin within 15 minutes (5 minutes for Exchange patches), RDP being the leading vector. After light segments (a Doom-based CAPTCHA, the Helios screensaver) and listener feedback on onion-site TLS certificates, auto-updating Asus routers, sci-fi picks and vaccine passports, he predicts that ransomware-as-a-service ecosystems will develop reputations, commission competition, and dark-web escrow services to pay affiliates reliably.

# Key points

- Fission: Mozilla began work in April 2018, announced February 2019, shipped opt-in in May 2021; each site gets its own process, containing browser exploits; Chrome did this in 2018; Safari's version is weaker.
- Conti: FBI counted 16 attacks; victims include Sangoma, Advantech, Broward County Public Schools, SEPA and Ireland's HSE; attackers wait for a quiet time before encrypting; the Irish prime minister refused to pay.
- Irish High Court injunction was served on the gang's Tor site; per the Irish Times it is meant to stop media and others from sharing leaked documents.
- CNA Financial paid $40M (down from a $60M demand) to a Phoenix Cryptolocker operator tied to Evil Corp lineage; US Treasury OFAC guidance warns of penalties for paying sanctioned groups.
- Cyber insurance: AXA stops reimbursing French clients' ransoms; GAO reports premiums rose 50% from 2016 to 2019 and insurers limit coverage for riskier sectors.
- Simps botnet (Keksec) is spread by Gafgyt onto Realtek and Linksys-based consumer routers; Steve repeats his ShieldsUP "stealth" advice.
- Mean time to inventory: scanning begins within minutes of disclosure; renting cloud capacity for an internet scan costs about $10; RDP is about a third of observed enterprise exposures; stolen RDP credentials are sold.
- Steve defends NAT as a protective feature and warns against mapping IPv6 addresses straight through to inside devices.
- Onion sites and TLS: CA/Browser Forum ballot (Feb 2020) allowed DV and OV certificates for version 3 onion addresses (previously EV only); Facebook has had an HTTPS onion site since 2014.
- Asus routers now support scheduled automatic firmware updates (a FragAttacks fix was included).
- Signed QR-code vaccination credentials can be verified offline with a government public key; Steve says this is only a demonstration of what cryptography allows.
- Prediction: affiliates are complaining about unpaid DarkSide commissions; expect RaaS reputation, commission competition and ransom escrow services; DarkSide had put 22 BTC on deposit with the XSS forum admin as a guarantee. Leo adds that damaging reputations may be a good law enforcement lever.
