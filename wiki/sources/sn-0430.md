# Summary

Steve first closes loose ends from the previous show (his chosen iPhone case, the iPad Air, SQRL progress), then explains why Calomel rates GRC poorly: GRC had put BEAST mitigation first in its cipher order to get an A from SSL Labs, but SSL Labs has now downgraded BEAST as browsers fixed it themselves, so GRC will reorder its cipher suites to prefer perfect forward secrecy. News: the TIFF zero-day in Microsoft Office was not patched on this Patch Tuesday and exploitation is growing, so the registry-based codec disable remains advised. The month's release fixed 19 vulnerabilities in eight bulletins, including an IE zero-day whose exploit had just appeared on Pastebin and a WordPad/GDI flaw, and Adobe shipped a Flash update. Bitcoin rose above 400 dollars while an exchange (Input.io) lost 4,100 bitcoin and a Chinese exchange vanished with millions; Steve says to hold coins offline. A Backblaze study shows about 80 percent of drives surviving four years. Q&A covers how Adobe's key stays unknown, birthday-collision odds for 256-bit identifiers, the value of SSL after the NSA revelations, Knock's poor implementation, Bluetooth proximity unlock, whether audits are required before trusting tools, Apple's iCloud Keychain and Sandboxie.

# Key points

- Calomel's grading versus SSL Labs: perfect forward secrecy (ephemeral Diffie-Hellman) now outranks BEAST mitigation; enabling PFS only requires reordering the server's cipher suite preference.
- Office TIFF zero-day: exploitation widening, trivial to trigger by displaying an image in email or a document; Microsoft's Fix it only disables the TIFF codec; no patch in November.
- November Patch Tuesday: eight bulletins, 19 flaws, 13 of them remote code execution; an IE zero-day with public exploit code was ranked among the top priorities; Steve urges prompt updating and notes Flash needed updating too.
- Bitcoin hazards: Input.io lost 4,100 bitcoin and offered partial repayment; a fraudulent Chinese exchange took roughly 4 million dollars. Advice: use exchanges only to convert, keep wallets under your own control, ideally offline, with a strong password; malware will hunt wallet files.
- Backblaze data from 25,000 drives: about 80 percent last four years and half are gone by year six, with an infant-mortality bump; Leo notes this resembles Google's study but is skewed because suspect drives are pulled early.
- Adobe follow-up: the encryption key is still unknown, so only weak, frequently reused passwords with revealing hints are exposed; likely 3DES, with 168 bits of key strength; Facebook reportedly cross-checked the dump against its users and prompted password changes.
- Collision maths: with a birthday-attack approximation, a billion users of 256-bit identifiers (Bitcoin, BitTorrent Sync, SQRL) give a collision chance near one in 4.3 times ten to the sixtieth, vastly less likely than an extinction event within any given second.
- SSL remains worthwhile despite the NSA: it prevents passive eavesdropping and session-cookie theft on open Wi-Fi (the Firesheep lesson), and basic certificates can be free.
- Knock: Steve praised its cryptographic design, but Leo found it paired with a colleague's phone twice, and bumping the phone can trigger unlock, so the implementation lacks confirmation of which device is trusted; Bluetooth LE reduces power drain compared with older proximity-lock tools such as BlueProximity on Linux.
- Audits: Steve admits auditing everything is impossible; judge products by openness and credibility (Threema looks right, OTR is well studied, BitTorrent Sync and Apple iMessage are closed and undocumented).
- iCloud Keychain: Apple claims AES-256, elliptic-curve key wrapping and device-generated keys; Steve advises a long security code and disabling keychain recovery, since the default four-digit code with server-side recovery is weak.
- Sandboxie is recommended for webmail users as well as for ransomware containment, with the caveat that downloaded files must be dragged out of the sandbox; NoScript does not sync with Firefox Sync.

# Other segments

- Steve's favourite iPhone 5 case after trying around 50 (an Incipio two-layer model), the iPad Air and mini comparison.
- SQRL status: group has settled phishing and man-in-the-middle mitigation for version 1 and the argument format; a community wiki holds a formal specification; Steve will write the client in assembly.
- SpinRite: viewport tip for checking a wipe at 50 and 99.9 percent, and a testimonial of a dead laptop drive recovered after more than a month of runtime, with the advice to run SpinRite preventively.
- Thanks to listeners for reviewing Jenny's children's book; jokes, WiFi password habits.
