# Summary

Princeton researchers (with Nvidia) built a tool that automatically synthesizes microarchitecture-specific attack programs and found two new related attacks, MeltdownPrime and SpectrePrime, using invalidation-based cache coherence. Intel's annual 10-K disclosed 30 customer and 2 securities class actions over Meltdown and Spectre, which Steve finds unfortunate since speculation was an industry-wide design choice. New York's financial cybersecurity regulation began phased enforcement, requiring senior executives to certify a security program, CISO, incident response and access control. Coinhive launched AuthedMine, an opt-in miner that adblock vendors are asked not to block; Salon switched to it. Steve stresses that JavaScript mining yields little (the BrowseAloud compromise of 4,275 sites earned about $24), while native mal-mining pays: a campaign on roughly 25,000 exposed Jenkins servers cashed out over 10,800 Monero (about $3.4 million), and a WebLogic campaign earned more than $226,000. Other stories: Mercedes allegedly also cheating emissions tests, the first crack of a Windows UWP game's DRM, an Apple Unicode (Telugu/Bengali) crash bug patched early, NCC Group's controlled "EternalGlue" test where a rebuilt NotPetya-style worm owned 107 hosts in about 45 minutes, a Brooklyn bitcoin rig jamming T-Mobile's 700 MHz service, and Chrome's built-in filtering of intrusive ads. The final topic reviews how the Russian influence operation used US servers, VPNs, stolen identities and PayPal, and agrees with Techdirt that platforms cannot simply wave away such abuse.

# Key points

- MeltdownPrime/SpectrePrime: automated synthesis shows more variants of speculative side channels exist; unclear whether current microcode fixes cover them.
- Intel 10-K: lawsuits as of 15 February 2018; Intel says it cannot estimate losses. Vendors are updating documentation for flush and sync instructions.
- NY DFS cybersecurity regulation covers 3,000+ firms: first deadline executive attestation, later monitoring or pen-testing, risk assessment, multifactor authentication for remote access, training and CISO reports.
- AuthedMine: consent token enforced server-side, valid at most 24 hours per domain; the Coinhive miner on Salon previously started mining before consent, which was fixed.
- Jenkins exploitation used an old, patched Java deserialization flaw; interpreters (deserializers) are a classic source of bugs. Other miners hit Ruby on Rails, PHP, IIS and WebLogic servers.
- EternalGlue (NCC Group, commissioned test): starting from one unprivileged machine with three unpatched hosts, it took the network and domain in minutes; one unpatched machine is enough.
- Chrome ad filtering based on the Coalition for Better Ads standards went live; sites get 30 days after notice, 42% had fixed issues; users can opt out per site.
- Russian operation: US-hosted servers, VPNs, fake personas, stolen identities for PayPal, ads, and recruiting real Americans; Steve argues there is little technology can do without crippling open platforms.
- Listener tips: 1 Gb router ports do not guarantee 1 Gb routing throughput (EdgeRouter X versus EdgeRouter 4); benchmark DNS speed versus real-world feel; BullGuard's IoT scanner checks whether Shodan lists your IP.
