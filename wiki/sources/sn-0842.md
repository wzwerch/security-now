# Summary

Steve opens with good news for early Windows 11 adopters: an optional preview update (KB5006746) bundles fixes for printing, AMD gaming performance and Bluetooth input lag that are otherwise due on the next Patch Tuesday. He then reports that the REvil ransomware gang was hacked again by a multi-country operation after its remaining members restored their servers from a backup that law enforcement already controlled; the FBI's earlier delay in releasing the Kaseya decryption key is explained by this ongoing infiltration. Microsoft launched a "Comms Hub" chat feature in its researcher portal and published a Q&A on why it expires superseded update packages. Windows 10 21H2 is nearly ready as a small feature update. Zerodium publicly solicited zero-days in ExpressVPN, NordVPN and Surfshark for Windows. Steve debunks the "Gummy Browsers" fingerprint-spoofing paper as nearly harmless, then covers the real threat of the week: the popular UAParser.js npm package, hijacked via the maintainer's account to ship a password stealer and miner. Listener feedback about Nubeva's "RansomLess" key-capture claims draws strong skepticism. The show closes with a 2001 Screen Savers clip about PatchWork and the same patching failures seen in 2021.

# Key points

- Windows 11 preview update KB5006746 (13 highlights, 64 fixes) addresses enterprise printing failures, AMD gaming slowdowns and laggy Bluetooth peripherals; it will arrive automatically on November Patch Tuesday, and Steve says installing it early is reasonable for those already on Windows 11.
- REvil was reportedly taken offline again by the FBI, Cyber Command, the Secret Service and foreign partners; the gang restored servers from backups that were already under law-enforcement control. Treating ransomware as a national-security matter gave agencies the legal basis for military and intelligence help.
- The FBI had held the universal Kaseya decryption key for a while while quietly infiltrating REvil, which accounts for the controversial delay.
- Microsoft MSRC added a Comms Hub chat to its Researcher Portal for case tracking and attachments; Steve hopes it signals deeper reform after the Exchange Server mishandling.
- Microsoft explained that it expires superseded update packages on an unspecified schedule; security-only packages for Windows 7/8.1 and Server 2008-2012 never expire because they are not cumulative, while Windows 10 monthly updates are cumulative.
- Windows 10 21H2 enters the Release Preview channel with WPA3 H2E support and GPU compute in WSL; the Windows Hello for Business "cloud trust" part slipped to a later monthly update. Support is 18 months for Home/Pro and 30 months for Enterprise/Education.
- Windows XP turned 20; roughly 0.6% of Windows machines (about 1 in 167) still run it.
- Zerodium advertised for information-disclosure, IP-leak or remote-code-execution zero-days in three VPN clients (Windows only; local privilege escalation excluded). Steve calls the business model sleazy; Leo notes one of the VPNs is a show sponsor and that the request shows no such bugs are currently known to Zerodium.
- "Gummy Browsers" (Texas A&M and University of Florida): an attacker captures a victim's browser fingerprint and replays it to see the victim's fingerprint-targeted ads. Steve judges it unimportant because fingerprints are fuzzy and the attacker cannot know what a site collects.
- UAParser.js (6-7 million weekly downloads) was compromised after the maintainer's npm account was hijacked; versions 0.7.29, 0.8.0 and 1.0.0 delivered a cryptominer and a Windows info-stealer (possibly DanaBot-related). CISA and GitHub issued alerts: treat any machine that ran it as fully compromised and rotate all secrets from another machine. Sonatype found three more malicious npm libraries the same week.
- Leo adds that other package ecosystems (PyPI, Arch User Repository, GitHub downloads) share the same trust problem; Steve's advice is to stay current and subscribe to advisories.
- Nubeva's "Session Key Intercept" and "RansomLess Decryption" claim to capture ransomware encryption keys at the moment of use. Steve is skeptical: symmetric keys have no recognisable structure, so the approach would need per-malware customisation, and an agent that sees ransomware starting could simply kill it.
- A new website annoyance: exit-intent pop-ups triggered when the mouse leaves the page; Leo adds delayed newsletter overlays with hidden close buttons as similar dark patterns.

# Other segments

- Sci-fi talk: Steve and Leo praise the film Dune (part one) and Arrival, are disappointed by the series Foundation and Invasion.
- SpinRite: Steve is heading toward the fifth pre-release; an Intel legacy IDE/ATA-mode bug was fixed and a related one is being chased, while he also improves the benchmarking feature.
- The closing clip is from The Screen Savers, 9 April 2001: Steve discusses PatchWork (a small tool built in two days for the FBI and SANS to check NT/2000 IIS servers for four known unpatched flaws), LeakTest and the never-released NetFilter. Lesson: extortion of unpatched Windows servers by Eastern European actors was already the story 20 years ago.
- Leo will be away next week (Jason fills in); clocks go back soon.
