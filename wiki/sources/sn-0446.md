# Summary

Steve devotes most of the show to Apple's updated iOS Security document dated February 2014, which he calls overwhelmingly impressive and a model of cryptographic design, with one troubling exception he saves for part two (an iCloud keychain choice involving the NIST P-256 curve, and the fact that iMessage security depends on trusting Apple's key directory). Before that come the news items: Snowden's live SXSW interview, which Steve found sane and which stressed that crypto math works and only end-to-end encryption with authentication is trustworthy; the Newsweek claim to have found the Bitcoin creator in Dorian Nakamoto, who then disavowed involvement; the Lakota tribe's MazaCoin; the final-but-one Patch Tuesday for Windows XP; the Foxit installer scandal; and Team Cymru's report on small office router exploitation. SQRL's user-interface design is finished after ten weeks and coding begins, with a crowd-translation project that attracted dozens of languages. Steve repeats that careful users can keep running XP, backing his view with an F-Secure list of eight precautions, then walks through the iOS design: encrypted flash with an instantly erasable key, boot ROM trust chain, per-device signed updates that block downgrades, and the Secure Enclave that processes fingerprints without exposing them to the main processor. He also uses F-Secure's figure that 97 percent of 2013 mobile malware families targeted Android to underline why a locked-down ecosystem matters.

# Key points

- Snowden at SXSW (via Google Hangouts and proxies): the fundamentals of cryptography work, but implementations and some NSA-influenced standards such as the Dual EC random generator are suspect; only end-to-end encryption with key authentication is truly private; he cited the Fourth Amendment as his motive. Steve says the world is better for knowing what was revealed.
- Dorian Nakamoto: Newsweek said he was the Bitcoin inventor; he told the AP he was misquoted and was no longer involved in engineering; Steve is sympathetic to his right to privacy.
- MazaCoin: a Bitcoin clone adopted by the Lakota nation, raising questions about sovereign currency.
- iOS 7.1 update improved Touch ID recognition speed according to early reports.
- The TrueCrypt audit's first phase was rumored to have results, but nothing was posted yet.
- Team Cymru published "Growing Exploitation of Small Office Routers Creating Serious Risks"; Steve flags it for a future show.
- Foxit Reader updates reportedly installed extra software (Conduit toolbar-type components); Steve points listeners to Sumatra PDF as a lighter alternative.
- XP: F-Secure's eight steps include installing the final update, using a second browser rather than Internet Explorer, patching Office, removing unneeded software, disabling plug-ins or setting click-to-play, keeping security software (Microsoft Security Essentials continues to be updated) and staying behind a NAT router.
- F-Secure H2 2013: 97 percent of mobile threats targeted Android (804 new families against 238 in 2012); the remainder hit Symbian; Android's weakness is ease of installing outside apps rather than platform vulnerabilities.
- iOS design: a hardware AES-256 engine sits in the path between flash storage and memory, file-system keys are rooted in per-device fused identifiers Apple does not know, and an "Effaceable" storage area lets a key be wiped instantly even with NAND wear leveling.
- The boot ROM contains Apple's root key and every boot stage is verified; updates are personalized with a per-device identifier and a nonce so old signed builds cannot be reinstalled (downgrade protection).
- Secure Enclave (A7): a separate processor with its own secure boot, random number generator and encrypted memory; main processor communicates only through a mailbox and cannot read keys; passcodes are "tangled" with the device key using PBKDF2 to force on-device, rate-limited brute force.
- Touch ID: fingerprint sensor data is encrypted and authenticated between sensor and Secure Enclave using a negotiated session key; the stored representation is a lossy ridge-flow map, so fingerprints cannot be reconstructed.
- Steve's caveat: iMessage and iCloud keychain are where Apple's trust tradeoffs appear; details deferred to the next episode.

# Other segments

- SQRL: UI design complete after about ten weeks (key management reorganized into parent-child relationships); Crowdin translation project already had 34 translations and 80 volunteers for some 30 languages.
- SpinRite listener tweets about recovering 350 GB of photos and using it as maintenance before recovery.
- Entertainment asides: The Good Wife NSA storyline, and Steve reading the Honor Harrington series.
