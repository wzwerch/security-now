# Summary

The week opens with the Washington DC Attorney General's suit against Athena Bitcoin, alleging 93% of deposits at its DC crypto ATMs were scam-related and that it charged hidden fees up to 26% (median victim loss $8,000, median age 71). Steve then discusses ransomware at the Uvalde school district and at Jaguar Land Rover, whose weeks-long production stoppage threatens small suppliers, and argues enterprises must assume employees will click bad links and segment networks accordingly. He reports that the Scattered LapSus Hunters group posted a goodbye note, Germany's government says it opposes breaking encryption under the EU Chat Control proposal, Russia's new Max messenger is already suffering account sales, Samsung patched an image-codec flaw used in a WhatsApp chain, UK data shows students cause most school insider breaches, and HackerOne was among those hit through Salesloft Drift. Further items include hacked smart washing machines in Amsterdam, a 1.5-billion-packet-per-second DDoS, Bluesky's conditional age verification for South Dakota and Wyoming, Ofcom enforcement, and a Colorado/California/Connecticut probe into Global Privacy Control compliance. After feedback, the main segment explains Apple's Memory Integrity Enforcement: five years of work turning Arm's debugging-oriented Memory Tagging Extension into an always-on, synchronous, hardware-enforced defence against buffer overflows and use-after-free bugs in the iPhone 17 / A19 chips, which Steve thinks may make mass-market zero-day exploitation of iPhones dramatically harder.

# Key points

- Athena Bitcoin (largest US crypto ATM operator): DC AG Brian Schwalb alleges the firm knew scams drove usage, relied on ineffective warnings, and charged undisclosed fees of up to 26% versus 0.24-3% at competitors; only about 7% of transactions were legitimate.
- Uvalde school district was shut for a week after ransomware hit phones, cameras, visitor management and thermostats. Steve's message: training cannot prevent every click, so networks must be hardened (VLAN segmentation, least privilege) so a compromised workstation can do little damage.
- Jaguar Land Rover production halted for over two weeks after ransomware; BBC reports suppliers facing bankruptcy and estimated losses of GBP 50-100 million; economists expect an effect on UK growth.
- Scattered LapSus Hunters (ShinyHunters, Scattered Spider, Lapsus$ members) posted a rambling farewell referencing Jaguar and four Google intrusions after threatening Google the week before; reasons unknown.
- Germany told its parliament's digital committee that private communication must stay private and it will not support breaking encryption in the EU CSAM regulation ("Chat Control"); the Danish presidency treats the file as high priority. EU court rulings on data retention are cited as a limit.
- Russia's Max messenger: hijacked accounts sold for up to $250 or rented hourly; officials say 67,000 accounts blocked.
- Samsung patched CVE-2025-21043 (critical, libimagecodec.quram.so) that was used in a WhatsApp exploit chain parallel to the Apple DNG one.
- UK ICO analysed 215 insider breaches in education (2022 to mid-2024): students caused 57%, and nearly all stolen-password cases (97%) involved students; the ICO blames weak school security practices.
- HackerOne confirmed a subset of its Salesforce data was accessed on 13 and 18 August through the Drift application; all Drift connectors were taken offline and credentials rotated; data segmentation limited impact. Salesloft Drift chat bots integrate deeply into customer systems, giving a large blast radius.
- Amsterdam campus laundry room replaces hacked smart washing machines with coin-operated ones; Steve recounts his own 1973 Berkeley dorm coin-box exploits.
- DDoS: FastNetMon reported a 1.5 billion packets/second UDP flood from CPE and IoT devices on 11,000+ networks; Cloudflare mitigated 11.5 Tbps. Steve explains why source-address spoofing no longer matters and why large CDNs absorb the load.
- Bluesky will apply conditional age verification (via KWS) in South Dakota and Wyoming, mirroring the UK, while staying blocked in Mississippi; Leo notes ChatGPT will estimate user age and ask for verification if unsure; Ofcom is investigating 34 plus 22 porn sites. Steve expects geolocation-based "safe haven" VPN exits and cites Stina Ehrensvard's age-verification nonprofit.
- Global Privacy Control: California, Colorado and Connecticut attorneys general announced a joint investigation of companies ignoring the GPC opt-out signal, which is legally binding in those states.
- Memory Integrity Enforcement (MIE), announced by Apple's SEAR group after about five years of work: builds on Arm's Memory Tagging Extension (2019) and Enhanced MTE (2022, co-developed with Apple). Memory is tagged with 4-bit values; accesses must present the matching tag; adjacent allocations always get different tags to stop buffer overflows; tags change on free to stop use-after-free. Apple runs it synchronously and always-on for the kernel and 70+ userland processes, extends checks to untagged memory accesses, adds Tag Confidentiality Enforcement (using Secure Page Table Monitor, resisting side-channel and speculative-execution leaks) and typed secure allocators (kalloc_type, xzone malloc); EMTE is also offered to developers in Xcode. Steve concludes it could end most memory-corruption exploit chains such as the recent DNG/WhatsApp attack and reduce emergency patch reboots.
- Feedback: Synology Drive and Hyper Backup work well (Steve's concern was bandwidth behaviour of Synology's built-in NAS sync); Cloudflare has a Certificate Transparency monitoring preview; 1.1.1.1 is an APNIC-assigned IP address, not a domain; UK users who already enabled Apple's Advanced Data Protection still have it, new users cannot; a listener points out that varying age thresholds across sites could leak a user's age through tracking.
