# Summary

Steve opens with the Brave browser: it randomizes its fingerprint (so EFF's Cover Your Tracks no longer sees it as unique) and the upcoming version 1.81 will block Microsoft Recall by default by marking every tab as private; Steve has switched from Firefox to Brave. Clorox is suing its IT provider Cognizant for $380 million after a 2023 Scattered Spider intrusion in which the help desk allegedly handed out employee passwords on request. Windows 10 ESU enrollment offers (six months, obtainable via Bing points or cloud settings backup) are beginning to appear. News items cover Ukraine's cyber operation against Crimean occupation administration systems, the Allianz Life CRM breach via social engineering (again Scattered Spider's style, part of a wave against insurers such as Aflac), a breach of the NRO-run CIA Acquisition Research Center site, and the Pentagon's claim that it was not hit by the SharePoint attack. A US woman was sentenced to 8.5 years for running a North Korean laptop farm. Expel Security retracted its report of a FIDO passkey man-in-the-middle bypass: the Okta logs actually showed later MFA challenges failing, and cross-device FIDO requires Bluetooth proximity. Steve then covers the UK's apparent retreat from its Apple ADP backdoor demand under US pressure, and Denmark reviving the EU "Chat Control" proposal. The main topic is the SharePoint "ToolShell" zero-day chain, which Microsoft failed to fix properly in the July Patch Tuesday; over 400 organizations were compromised, and Steve stresses that patching is not enough, machine keys must be rotated.

# Key points

- Brave randomizes fingerprints, supports vertical tabs, and from 1.81 disables Microsoft Recall in all tabs by default; Steve cites "the tyranny of the default" and moved from Firefox to Brave (Firefox's drag-and-drop URL restriction on Windows was a long-standing annoyance).
- Clorox sued Cognizant over the August 2023 Scattered Spider attack: $380M claimed (about $50M remediation, about $330M lost sales); transcripts allegedly show the help desk resetting passwords without verification. Steve is skeptical of Cognizant's "narrow scope" defense and critical of business process outsourcing.
- Windows 10 ESU: enrollment wizard rolling out; Steve plans to stay on Windows 10 and expects 0patch to cover gaps after the six months.
- Ukrainian military intelligence (HUR) reportedly DDoSed and then raided Crimean administration systems, taking about 100 TB of documents and wiping servers; Russia shutting cellular service because drones use it for navigation. Steve argues for air-gapping.
- Allianz Life: attacker reached a third-party cloud CRM on July 16, 2025 via social engineering; data on most of its 1.4 million customers affected; Google had warned of Scattered Spider intrusions across insurers.
- Steve's rant: "I forgot my password" and "I don't have my authenticator" fallbacks make MFA optional theater; email is the real root of trust; SQRL allowed disabling all alternative logins.
- CIA/NRO Acquisition Research Center website breached, apparently including data on a program called Digital Hammer; Steve suspects SharePoint exploitation but this is unconfirmed.
- Pentagon CIO says DoD was not affected by the SharePoint attack; Energy (incl. NNSA), DHS and Education were hit.
- Christina Chapman got 8.5 years for the North Korean laptop farm (300+ US jobs, over $17M for the regime).
- Expel retracted its PoisonSeed/passkey bypass claim; FIDO cross-device auth requires a local Bluetooth link, so the described real-time relay does not work.
- TADTS (Alcohol & Drug Testing Service) breach exposed about 750,000 people's data and disclosure took a year.
- UK reportedly backing down on its Apple Advanced Data Protection backdoor demand after US pressure (Cloud Act concerns).
- Denmark's EU Council presidency re-tables Chat Control (client-side scanning for CSAM), debate set for October 14, 2025; Germany's position decisive; Steve prefers on-device local AI over encryption backdoors if scanning must happen.
- ToolShell: Pwn2Own Berlin (May 2025) SharePoint bug chain (CVE-2025-49704 RCE, CVE-2025-49706 spoofing); July 8 patches were bypassable; large-scale exploitation observed from July 18 by Eye Security; new CVEs 53770/53771; over 400 systems compromised in four waves, 9,000+ exposed servers per Shadowserver.
- The attack steals ASP.NET MachineKey values (including ValidationKey) via a stealthy .aspx page so attackers can forge trusted payloads later; remediation is patch plus rotating machine keys and restarting IIS. Only SharePoint 2016/2019 got patches; 2010/2013 must be isolated or shut down. CISA recommends disconnecting end-of-life public-facing SharePoint.
- Suspicion (unproven) that the exploit leaked through Microsoft's MAPP early-disclosure program; Steve notes the diffing of public patches alone could explain the bypass.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: tweet about posting a wrong answer on Reddit to get a correct one.
- Listener feedback: staying on Windows 10; SpinRite 6.1 supports drives up to 144,000 TB (the 2 TB limit is an old-BIOS/USB issue); why hashing quantities does not give zero-knowledge proofs; cheap fingerprint-equipped phones (Samsung A15) make biometric age verification feasible.
- GRC: DNS Benchmark works fine on Windows 11.
- Books/films: Project Hail Mary movie news, Andy Weir's "Artemis".
