# Summary

Steve Gibson opens with research showing spearphishing shifting from email to Twitter and Facebook, where people trust links more. He covers the Chipotle card-malware breach (purchases between March 24 and April 18) and fake Android "WannaCry protection" apps that are mostly adware. Check Point's "attack by subtitle" research found parsing flaws in VLC, Kodi, Popcorn Time and Stremio, exposing roughly 200 million players. The Cloak & Dagger paper from UC Santa Barbara and Georgia Tech showed that Android's "draw on top" and accessibility permissions let an app take over the UI, yet Google marked most reports "won't fix". SambaCry, a seven-year-old Samba flaw allowing a writable-share library upload to run as root, is described as Linux's WannaCry, along with the unpatched NSA EsteemAudit RDP exploit on Windows XP and Server 2003. Steve praises 1Password's Travel Mode and discusses Google tying credit card records to ad effectiveness. Listener topics include a whitelist-based robocall blocker idea, a Let's Encrypt outage caused by base64 in OCSP GET requests, and a map analogy for open source.

# Key points

- Spearphishing is moving to social media: in an experiment, 275 of 819 people opened links sent through social networks, far above current email rates.
- Chipotle announced card-stealing malware on its payment system; cards used March 24 to April 18 may be exposed, and misuse often appears months later. Watch statements; US credit card liability is limited.
- Fake "WannaCry protection" Android apps are mainly adware; WannaCry is a Windows-only issue.
- Check Point "Hacked in Translation": crafted subtitle files can give attackers control via VLC, Kodi, Popcorn Time and Stremio; patched versions exist (VLC 2.2.6), update players.
- Cloak & Dagger: apps with the auto-granted "draw on top" permission can trick users into enabling accessibility access, enabling clickjacking, keylogging and silent unlock; a test app was approved by Google Play within hours; a user study of 20 people noticed nothing; Google called most issues intended behavior and planned only a documentation change.
- Recommendation from the researchers: audit which apps hold these permissions, e.g. via the Play Store permission details, since the device UI itself cannot be trusted.
- SambaCry: all Samba versions from 3.5.0 allow an attacker with write access to a share to get a library loaded by the server (running as root); wormable; fixed in 4.4.14, 4.5.10 and 4.6.4; workaround is setting `nt pipe support = no`, which may affect Windows clients; NAS vendors will be slow to patch. Shodan showed about a million non-Windows hosts with port 445 open.
- EsteemAudit (NSA tool leaked by Shadow Brokers) targets RDP on XP and Server 2003; about 24,700 such systems are exposed; no Microsoft patch, but EnSilo published an unofficial silent patch.
- 1Password Travel Mode removes vaults not marked "Safe for Travel" from devices before border crossings and restores them afterwards; Steve calls it exactly right.
- Google uses billions of credit card transaction records via partners (said to cover about 70 percent of US card transactions) to prove online ads drive offline sales; Google claims custom privacy-preserving encryption.
- Follow-ups: GoFCCYourself.com redirects to the FCC net neutrality comment page; DigiCert explained why Stack Exchange's wildcard cert uses an OV certificate chained to the EV root (EV certs cannot contain wildcards except for Tor .onion names).
- Robocall plague: Steve proposes a whitelisting call screener (voice modem plus Raspberry Pi Zero) that plays a "press zero if you are human" message to unknown callers; commercial device Sentry v3.1 does something similar.
- Printing 2FA QR codes is equivalent to writing down passwords, which Steve considers sound practice.
- Let's Encrypt handles about 25 billion OCSP requests a month; its May 18-19 outage stemmed from a deploy touching how OCSP GET requests carry base64 (with slashes) data.
- Advice for aspiring security researchers: learn low-level code and machine language.
- Edge versus Firefox and Chrome memory use for five tabs; Steve still respects Edge as a clean rewrite.
- Open vs closed source analogy: not publishing source is like not printing a map; attackers can still drive the route.
