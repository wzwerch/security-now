# Summary

After a quick reminder that Microsoft's June Patch Tuesday was routine (and that Microsoft published a Fix It tool to disable Java in Internet Explorer), Steve devotes the episode to the June 2013 NSA disclosures. He plays Glenn Greenwald's interview and the March 2013 exchange in which Senator Wyden asked Director of National Intelligence James Clapper whether the NSA collects data on millions of Americans and Clapper answered no. He explains why telephony metadata collected under FISA Section 215 is powerful: it lets analysts build a graph of everyone's connections and look backward in time, a point Edward Snowden also made, and mentions the NSA's huge Utah data center. His main contribution is a theory, stated as inference from public evidence, that PRISM refers to optical splitting of fiber upstream of the named companies: the 2006 sworn declaration of AT&T technician Mark Klein described a secret room at 611 Folsom Street in San Francisco fed by fiber splitters on AT&T's peering links. He reasons that this explains how companies can honestly deny giving direct server access while the NSA claims collection directly from servers, that only unencrypted traffic such as email between providers and DNS is readable, and that SSL still protects the rest. He also notes European anger, the stopwatching.us petition, and that he is quietly porting SpinRite to Macs, which would be free for existing owners.

# Key points

- Patch Tuesday was light; update promptly since attackers reverse-engineer patches. Microsoft released a Fix It to disable Java in IE.
- Apple's iOS 7 will update apps in the background, which Steve welcomes.
- Steve cautions that NFC payment cards are "broken at birth" because radio reaches farther than expected.
- Edward Snowden leaked NSA documents to Glenn Greenwald (Guardian) and the Washington Post; Steve says he is glad the programs surfaced because Congress cannot oversee what officials misstate, although he would personally feel bound by a secrecy oath.
- Clapper's March 12 testimony denying "wittingly" collecting data on millions of Americans; Clapper later explained his answer using a "least untruthful" reading, which Steve and the EFF call misleading.
- Metadata is not harmless: call records (numbers, serial numbers, towers) that carriers keep for 60-90 days are copied by the NSA under a Section 215 business-records order, creating a retroactive social graph; Steve notes burner phones would stand out as isolated clusters.
- Steve's PRISM explanation: a prism splits light, and the NSA taps fiber-optic circuits just upstream of big companies' bandwidth providers, so the companies are truthful in denying direct access to servers. This is Steve's inference rather than a confirmed fact in the episode.
- Evidence cited: the Mark Klein declaration (Room 641A, 611 Folsom Street, splitter cabinet, Sun servers, Juniper routers and "semantic analysis" gear), the EFF summary of 15-20 similar AT&T sites, and the timeline of companies being added one at a time.
- What is exposed: unencrypted email in transit between providers, DNS queries and metadata; SSL/TLS traffic is believed to withstand collection, though stored data may be broken later; Steve suggests moving beyond 128-bit keys.
- Leo recalls Carnivore and the warrantless wiretapping revelations as precedent; a survey found most Americans accept the programs if they prevent terrorism.
- Finland's communications minister said NSA collection might break Finnish law and Europe may involve Neelie Kroes; the StopWatching.us coalition launched.

# Other segments

- Steve says SpinRite now runs on his MacBook Air under Boot Camp BIOS emulation after he re-engineered its keyboard hardware access, and existing SpinRite 6 owners will get the update free.
