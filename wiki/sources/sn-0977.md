# Summary

Steve opens with a remembered DOS notes app, Tornado Notes, to argue that simple single-purpose tools often beat feature-rich successors. He reports on the first week of GRC's email system, including pitfalls for Gmail-hosted custom domains and anonymising alias services, and describes receiving a Have I Been Pwned alert for 16 GRC addresses in a 361-million-address Telegram combolist breach, none of them in use. News items include a new supply-chain lure in which attackers answer Stack Overflow questions by recommending a malicious PyPI package, Google's decision to stop trusting new certificates from Austria's GlobalTrust CA, and the announced June 2024 shutdown of ICQ. After recommending the film Deja Vu, he turns to Recall again: security researcher Kevin Beaumont, after about ten days with the feature, showed that its SQLite database can be read and exfiltrated without system rights, that it keeps deleted and disappearing messages, and that info-stealer malware could grab it in seconds. Steve estimates, from the small database size, that Recall may store a PC's whole lifetime of use, and theorises that Microsoft is building the corpus for a personal local large language model, while warning that Windows security is not good enough to guard such data.

# Key points

- Tornado Notes (Micro Logic, Jim Lewis) filtered a pile of notes as you typed; its Windows successor Info Select lost the magic through featuritis. Steve's lesson: composable, single-purpose tools beat bloated ones (Unix pipes, his own tiny freeware).
- GRC email: the sender filter looks at the real sending account, so people using a custom domain on Gmail or a one-time alias service such as SimpleLogin must register their underlying address; Steve was reconsidering the check since From headers can be spoofed anyway. The "prime directive" is no unwanted mail and no sharing of addresses.
- Have I Been Pwned: the "Telegram Combolists" collection (May 2024) had 361 million unique emails, 122 GB, built from combolists and infostealer logs; domain-wide notification is free after proving domain ownership. hMailServer's dynamic blocklist caught 473 spamming IPs in two hours.
- Malicious PyPI package "pytoileur" (part of the "Cool package" campaign) was pushed through Stack Overflow answers; its setup script hid a long Base64 blob beyond the visible margin that downloaded an executable stealing browser data, cards and documents. Advice: verify package sources and review code with word wrap enabled.
- Chrome will distrust new TLS certificates from GlobalTrust (Austria) issued after 30 June 2024, using issue-date-based distrust rather than removing the root; effectively ends its web-server certificate business.
- ICQ (created 1996, once over 100 million accounts) announced shutdown for 26 June 2024.
- Beaumont's Recall findings: data stored as plaintext-searchable SQLite under a CoreAIPlatform folder in AppData; screenshots taken every few seconds; accessible by other users and malware without SYSTEM rights; UAC is not a security boundary; edge cases exclude Edge InPrivate but not Chrome; deleted and auto-deleting messages (Signal, WhatsApp, Teams) remain; several days of activity is about 90 KB, so exfiltration takes seconds; Defender remediated an off-the-shelf infostealer too late; enabled by default on Copilot+ PCs.
- Beaumont's recommendation: Microsoft should pull and rework Recall; Steve points to Satya Nadella's "do security" email as the test of sincerity.
- Steve's inference: at that storage rate 50 GB would last centuries, so Recall likely records an entire machine lifetime, intended as training data for a personal on-device LLM; he sees the idea as powerful but dangerous given Windows' security record (61 flaws in the previous Patch Tuesday). Historical aside: his failed 2001 warning about Windows XP raw sockets.
- Listener points: Apple "Hide My Email" and Fastmail provide alias addresses; Recall could be swept into OneDrive or cloud backups; a SpinRite 6.1 user reported dramatic SSD speed recovery.
