# Summary

Steve opens with Signal's blog post "Privacy is Priceless, but Signal is Expensive": the nonprofit expects to need about USD 50 million a year by 2025, of which roughly USD 6 million goes to SMS and voice registration codes, run by only about 50 staff. He is pessimistic that donations alone can sustain it. News: the ALPHV/BlackCat group claimed a breach of MeridianLink and filed an SEC complaint about non-disclosure under rules that were not yet in force, with no proof of stolen data; ETSI finally opens the secret TETRA radio encryption algorithms after the Dutch research findings; Apple announces RCS support for green-bubble messaging in 2024; and Steve found the YubiHSM 2 FIPS for server-side EV code signing. Listener topics include defeating The Barnacle parking device, USB drive speed testing, a pro-QWAC perspective on the EU's eIDAS 2.0 and Steve's rebuttal, how many roots a browser trusts, whether companies can really refuse EU rules, Protectli hardware for pfSense, malvertising and uBlock Origin, and Manifest V3. The main segment celebrates Ethernet's 50th anniversary: Metcalfe and Boggs's 1973 design at Xerox PARC, based on ALOHAnet and CSMA/CD, and Steve's own presence at the Stanford AI Lab that summer.

# Key points

- Signal: client teams for Android, iOS and desktop, server and calling libraries, support in 60+ languages, about 100,000 requests per second; funded by donations including a loan from Brian Acton; Signal Protocol now underpins WhatsApp and Google Messages encryption.
- GRC statistics: DNS Benchmark about 8.66 million downloads, ShieldsUP over 101 million uses, ValiDrive 68,000 downloads; all financed by SpinRite sales.
- Escalating extortion: encrypt, then DDoS, then data theft, then contacting customers, now ALPHV's SEC complaint against MeridianLink (which said it found no unauthorized access to production platforms). The SEC four-day Form 8-K disclosure rule was due to take effect December 15, 2023.
- TETRA (Terrestrial Trunked Radio) had a deliberate backdoor and other flaws found by Dutch researchers; ETSI now publishes the algorithms; Matthew Green called secret ciphers a 1960s practice.
- Apple's RCS adoption adds read receipts, typing indicators and larger media for iPhone to Android chats; iMessage remains for iPhone to iPhone.
- YubiHSM 2 FIPS is suited for EV code signing with open developer support, unlike the Thales SafeNet token Steve had used.
- The Barnacle can reportedly be defeated by running the windshield defroster and sliding a card under the suction cups, and some universities stopped using it.
- USB speed: ValiDrive is not a realistic benchmark; SpinRite 6.1 Level 1 versus Level 3 timing can reveal write speed; Steve plans a ValiDrive 2.0 with larger blocks.
- QWAC debate: a listener argues QWACs are a "super-EV" identity signal that would defeat interception by CDNs; Steve notes CDNs terminate TLS with the customer's real certificate so a QWAC would give false assurance, and browsers abandoned EV UI cues for good reason. Another listener found 165 roots in Firefox (84 after manual pruning), showing how much the web relies on CA good behavior. A commenter argues the EU market is too large for vendors to refuse, unlike the UK messaging case.
- Code-signing reputation: EV signing and heuristics appear to keep uniquely personalized SpinRite binaries from triggering antivirus alerts.
- Protectli appliances are recommended for pfSense/OPNsense routers; uBlock Origin in a 25,000-user rollout helped against malvertising delivering Redline stealer; Chrome's Manifest V3 may disable uBlock Origin in Chrome 127.
- Ex-LastPass users should check card statements: one listener's closed account kept generating billing attempts.
- Ethernet history: 2.94 Mb/s coax with vampire taps in 1973, 10 Mb/s commercial version in 1980, IEEE 802 standard in 1983; design assumed failure and congestion collapse is solved by adding bandwidth.
