# Summary

Steve starts with critical browser updates: Firefox fixed a use-after-free in SCTP handling (CVE-2020-16044) and Chromium browsers fixed a V8 out-of-bounds write plus a dozen other bugs. Firefox 86 will stop using the Backspace key as a Back shortcut after years of debate, restorable through an about:config setting. Ransomware economics follow: Ryuk's tracked proceeds exceed $150 million, with a record single payment around $34 million, and REvil claims $100 million in a year. Steve dismisses Intel's CES claim that 11th-gen vPro "Threat Detection Technology" detects ransomware in silicon as marketing, because hardware counters lack context. An IoT Bluetooth chastity device with API flaws was hijacked for extortion, a new kind of ransomware. On SolarWinds, Kaspersky found code overlaps between Sunburst and the Turla-linked Kazuar backdoor, a US agency statement called the actor "likely Russian", a shareholder suit cited a weak update-server password, and SolarWinds hired the Krebs Stamos Group. The Zyxel hardcoded account from episode 800 is now being scanned for in the wild. WhatsApp's forced metadata sharing with Facebook caused a surge of Signal sign-ups. The main segment recalls DOS-era disk limits and explains why SpinRite 6.1 will drop its legacy code.

# Key points

- Firefox CVE-2020-16044: use-after-free in SCTP COOKIE-ECHO chunk handling; likely needs a malicious server (watering hole). Fixed by auto-update.
- Chromium V8 out-of-bounds write (CVE-2020-15995) and 12 more bugs patched; Edge simply mirrors Chromium advisories; bounty payouts around $20,000 each.
- Firefox 86 plans to disable Backspace-as-Back (40 million monthly users used it) because of form data loss; the browser.backspace setting in about:config reverts it.
- Ryuk operators cash out via exchanges such as Huobi and Binance after broker and laundering hops; average ransom about 48 BTC; REvil seeks new affiliates with a stated goal of $2 billion.
- Intel TDT / PMU ransomware detection: low-level counters see anonymous instructions with no context, so Steve calls it hype.
- Cellmate IoT chastity device: API flaws let anyone lock devices remotely and exposed location and plaintext passwords; the vendor ignored disclosure deadlines; extortion demands of 0.02 BTC followed.
- Kaspersky: Sunburst shares UID generation, sleep logic and FNV-1a hashing with Kazuar (linked to Turla); possible explanations include same author, shared source, or deliberate false flag. Advice: isolate network-management software on separate VLANs, restrict outbound connections, and analyse memory dumps.
- US agencies (FBI, CISA, ODNI, NSA) said the SolarWinds actor is "likely Russian"; Steve notes attribution in cyberspace is inherently hard.
- Shareholder class action against SolarWinds executives alleges, among other things, a weak update-server password.
- Krebs Stamos Group (Chris Krebs and Alex Stamos) hired by SolarWinds as a crisis consultancy.
- GreyNoise and SANS ISC saw scanning for the Zyxel "zyfwp" account on SSH within days of disclosure, from Russia-geolocated IPs; with the account public, attackers could create VPN accounts and pivot into internal networks.
- WhatsApp's new privacy policy (effective February 8, 2021) forces acceptance of data sharing with Facebook; Signal saw record sign-ups and temporary SMS verification delays. Messages stay encrypted; the concern is metadata.
- SpinRite history: DOS 3.3 capped partitions at 32 MB (16-bit sector numbers), later workarounds included CHS translation, sector-size-lying drivers and on-the-fly disk compression. SpinRite 6.1 will drop legacy code, move to 48-bit (internally 64-bit) sector addressing, transfer large blocks, and respect physical sector size, keeping the text UI.
