# Summary

Steve first revisits the UK Online Safety Bill (OSB): penalties up to 18 million pounds or 10% of global turnover plus possible prison for senior managers make defiance impractical, Wikipedia says it will not perform age checks, and the Guardian explains that the bill leaves age-verification technology unspecified. WhatsApp's head says the service would accept being blocked rather than weaken encryption, joining Signal, and Steve suggests that any mandated monitoring should be shown to users with a prominent banner. Listener feedback covers the ZimaBoard, Microsoft's Rust adoption and parental responsibility, and a new US Protecting Kids on Social Media Act is described. News items: a Service Location Protocol (SLP) reflection attack with up to 2,200x amplification, Google Authenticator adding unencrypted cloud sync, Israeli police using a reduced Pegasus variant, a Russian push for domestic operating systems, Mirai botnet operators weaponizing a TP-Link router patch within weeks, Intel having TDX security-audited before release, a new Intel EFLAGS timing side channel, a warning from curl's maintainer not to delete curl.exe, and VirusTotal Code Insight.

# Key points

- OSB penalties: fines up to 18 million pounds or 10% of global turnover, business restrictions and up to two years' prison for certain senior-manager offences. Steve sees only two real options for encryption providers: comply by adding side-channel scanning, or leave the UK.
- Wikimedia Foundation refuses UK age checks and may be blocked; UK government says only the highest-risk services need age verification. The bill does not mandate a specific technology and Ofcom will issue age-assurance codes.
- WhatsApp's Will Cathcart: the service will not lower security and has accepted being blocked elsewhere (for example Iran); he and Signal's Meredith Whittaker vow to push back together. Steve speculates Apple could fall back to SMS in the UK.
- US: the Protecting Kids on Social Media Act would bar accounts under 13 and require parental consent under 18; Utah and Arkansas have similar laws; a CSAM bill is also moving.
- Steve's idea: show a red banner "This communication is being monitored by your government" if services must comply, recalling his Aureate spyware discovery and the OptOut tool.
- SLP (port 427) abuse by Bitsight and Curesec gives up to 2,200:1 UDP amplification; roughly 70,000 instances are exposed; Cloudflare and NetScout expect attacks to rise.
- Google Authenticator added Google-account sync of one-time-code seeds, but without end-to-end encryption (reported by Mysk); alternatives noted are OTP Auth and 2FAS. Steve keeps printed QR codes as backup.
- Israeli police reportedly used a lighter Pegasus variant, Saifan, on citizens; the government formed a commission.
- Russia plans to require pre-installed Russian operating systems on new PCs.
- A Pwn2Own-demonstrated TP-Link router flaw was patched in March 2023; Mirai operators reverse engineered the patch and hijacked unpatched routers. Steve argues consumer routers should auto-update by default with rollback.
- Intel TDX (Trust Domain Extensions) went through Project Circuit Breaker, a Google Project Zero review (10 issues found) and internal red teaming before release.
- "Timing the Transient Execution": a Jcc/EFLAGS-based side channel on Intel i7/i9 CPUs; relevant mainly to shared virtualized servers.
- Microsoft is rewriting core Windows components in Rust (David Weston), since about 70% of its CVEs were memory-safety bugs.
- Daniel Stenberg warns users not to delete curl.exe from System32 after scanners flagged an inflated NVD score, as Windows Update breaks.
- VirusTotal Code Insight uses AI to summarize PowerShell samples; Steve advises treating such output skeptically.
- Shodan lost Twitter single sign-on when Twitter ended API access, a downside of third-party login.
