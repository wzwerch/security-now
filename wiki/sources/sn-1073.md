# Summary

Steve opened with fallout from Apple's iOS 26.4 age confirmation in the UK: surprised users, complaints from people without credit cards or recognized IDs, and Steve's view that platform-level anonymous age assertion is the right approach. He then traced how "tracking pixels" evolved from 1x1 images to full third-party JavaScript, as background to "BrowserGate": research by Fairlinked e.V. (confirmed technically by BleepingComputer) shows LinkedIn's roughly 2.7 MB script probing Chromium browsers for over 6,000 extensions and collecting 48 device characteristics into an RSA-protected "super-fingerprint" attached to every request; LinkedIn disputes the framing and points to the complainants' commercial dispute. Leo interrupted with Anthropic's Claude Mythos preview and Project Glasswing, which gives vetted companies access to a model that autonomously found and exploited decades-old bugs. Further items: Microsoft force-upgrading unmanaged Windows 11 24H2 to 25H2, Cisco's source code theft through the Trivy-borne TeamPCP credentials, Proton Meet, GitHub accelerating Actions security, Cloudflare's audited 1.1.1.1 privacy, and Cloudflare's AI-built WordPress alternative EmDash with sandboxed plugins. The main topic was the FCC's 23 March 2026 addition of all foreign-produced consumer routers to the Covered List, which blocks approval of new models but leaves existing ones in use; Steve, drawing on a Technology Policy Institute analysis, argues it is arbitrary industrial policy that fixes nothing and recommends OPNsense or pfSense on small hardware.

# Key points

- Apple 26.4 in the UK: age check via account age, credit card or ID; some users (debit-card only, unrecognized licences, no passport) cannot verify; Apple iterates. privacy.apple.com lets users request account data to learn account age.
- Tracking evolution: 1x1 image pixels with Referer/cookie leakage, sweepstakes sites to deanonymize, blockers (uBlock Origin, Privacy Badger, Ghostery, AdGuard, Disconnect, NoScript), then full third-party JavaScript and fingerprinting. Steve expects only legislation (likely EU-led) to rein it in.
- BrowserGate: LinkedIn "Spectroscopy" script fires up to 6,222 requests probing extension files, grew from 38 extensions (2017) to 461 (2024) to about 6,167 (Feb 2026); includes competitors' sales tools (Apollo, Lusha, ZoomInfo) and extensions hinting at religion, politics, neurodivergence or job hunting. Collects 48 attributes, JSON-serialised and encrypted with an RSA public key, sent as a header with each API request; third-party HUMAN Security element also loaded. LinkedIn says it hunts scraping extensions and does not infer sensitive data; fined 310 million euros by the Irish DPC in 2024 for other GDPR issues. Steve argues browsers should give users control over such JavaScript behavior.
- Anthropic Mythos/Glasswing (per Leo): restricted access plus $100M credits for critical-software vendors; reportedly found a 27-year-old OpenBSD bug, 16-year-old ffmpeg bug, a 17-year-old FreeBSD NFS remote root flaw, chaining up to six exploits autonomously.
- Microsoft is force-upgrading unmanaged Windows 11 24H2 Home/Pro to 25H2 (24H2 end of support 13 October 2026); GRC's freeware InControl can hold a version by making Windows appear managed.
- Cisco breach: stolen Trivy-linked credentials let attackers into Cisco's build environment, cloning 300+ GitHub repositories (including AI products and some customer code) and abusing AWS keys; TeamPCP also hit LiteLLM and Checkmarx KICS. Steve notes attackers now exploit stolen credentials immediately.
- Proton Meet launched as an end-to-end encrypted conferencing service; Steve finds Proton's eavesdropping claims about rivals dated but trusts Proton more.
- GitHub will accelerate planned Actions security features after the Trivy/Cisco/LiteLLM attacks.
- Cloudflare's independent audit again confirmed 1.1.1.1 privacy (source IPs anonymized, logs deleted within 25 hours).
- Cloudflare EmDash (TypeScript, Astro, MIT licence, v0.1.0 preview): plugins run in isolated Dynamic Workers with manifest-declared capabilities, addressing WordPress's plugin model (cited: 96% of WordPress security issues come from plugins).
- FCC router ban (23 March 2026): all consumer routers "produced in foreign countries" (any major stage of design, assembly, manufacture) added to the Covered List after an executive-branch determination three days earlier; no notice-and-comment; existing models can still be sold and used; manufacturers may seek conditional approval from DoD/DHS by disclosing supply chains and onshoring plans; waiver allows firmware updates until at least March 2027. Cited reasons include Volt, Flax and Salt Typhoon.
- Critique (Technology Policy Institute, Scott Wallsten): contrast with the deliberative 2019-2022 Huawei/ZTE process; Volt Typhoon abused end-of-life Cisco and Netgear devices, so country of origin is a poor proxy; December 2025 foreign-drone ban already being challenged by DJI. Steve adds that old UPnP/WAN exposure problems were fixed around 2013-14 and that IoT devices inside the LAN are a larger risk.
