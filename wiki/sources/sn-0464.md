# Summary

Leo returns after two guest weeks. Steve updates the Indian CA story: Microsoft added three NIC certificates to its untrusted list, India's CCA reported only four mis-issued certificates (three for Google domains plus Yahoo) but Google knows of others, so Chrome will restrict that CA to a few Indian government domains. UC Berkeley researchers published a paper on five web-based password managers (LastPass, RoboForm, My1login, PasswordBox and NeedMyPassword), where four had fixed the flaws nine months earlier and the fifth never responded; Steve will cover it next week. After last week's light-bulb flaw, three IoT standards efforts appear: the AllSeen Alliance (Qualcomm's AllJoyn, with Microsoft joining), the Open Interconnect Consortium (Intel, Samsung and others) and the Thread Group (IPv6-based, backed by Google's Nest, supporting low-power "sleepy nodes" over 802.15.4/6LoWPAN). Google announced Project Zero, a full-time team of top hackers (including George Hotz and Tavis Ormandy) disclosing flaws in any vendor's software. LibreSSL's first release neutered the call that reseeds the random number generator after a fork, a regression versus OpenSSL. Verizon and Netflix are arranging direct connections to relieve peering congestion, with debate on "balanced" peering. The Justice Department said CryptoLocker is neutralized by disrupting its servers, though it reportedly extorted over 27 million dollars. Listener questions cover SpinRite speed in virtual machines, the EFF open WiFi SSID, whether LastPass's insecure-form warning works, why Steve trusts LastPass, blocking CryptoLocker with policies, refrigerating old electronics and microphones as bugs.

# Key points

- Indian CA incident: only Windows trusted the CA; Google's pinning caught the fake certificates; Microsoft revoked three; scope of the breach unknown, so Chrome will whitelist only a few domains for that CA. Steve says it should simply be distrusted.
- Berkeley paper "The Emperor's New Password Manager" found bookmarklet and one-time-password weaknesses in web-based managers; LastPass said it fixed them within a day and saw no abuse; the unresponsive NeedMyPassword stayed vulnerable.
- IoT: AllSeen/AllJoyn, OIC and Thread aim to standardize device connectivity; mesh networks and ultra low-power nodes are central; OIC promises focus on security and authentication.
- Google Project Zero will hire elite hackers, disclose flaws responsibly across vendors, and publish them in an external database after patching.
- LibreSSL 2.0.0 and 2.0.1 were preview releases; a researcher found its PRNG was less safe than OpenSSL's because the reseed-after-fork hook was made a no-op and process IDs may repeat for grandchildren processes. Steve says PRNG design should be modernised, as in SQRL.
- Verizon said the Netflix problem arises on congested peering links and agreed to direct connections; Steve and Leo discuss why peering demands "balanced" traffic, which no longer fits content providers.
- Justice Department claimed CryptoLocker is nonfunctional because its infrastructure was disrupted; BitDefender expects clones, noting nine months of operation and over 27 million dollars extorted.
- SpinRite 6 uses BIOS I/O, so virtual machines with Ultra DMA virtual BIOSes can run it much faster than real hardware stuck in PIO; SpinRite 6.1 will use large 32 MB transfers.
- LastPass warns before filling insecure forms, but scripting-heavy sites can defeat the detection; the setting is per browser.
- Steve trusts LastPass because its protocol was documented and independently reimplemented, matching results; open source is not itself proof (OpenSSL, TrueCrypt); tradeoffs and where to start trusting are a judgment.
- BleepingComputer's CryptoLocker page lists group-policy settings that currently block it, but malware evolves.
- Cold storage slows chemical aging in film and batteries; chilled but not frozen for lithium-ion.
- Webcam and headset microphones can be used as bugs by RATs; disabling the device in software is not enough, so remove the driver or unplug the headset.
- Chip-and-PIN cards are arriving in the US (Leo's first one).
- SQRL: identity management in Windows client nearly done; a German company will support SQRL on Android and iOS clients; the EFF firmware will be announced at the HOPE conference.
