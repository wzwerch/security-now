# Summary

The last live episode of 2022 starts with a rift in the URSNIF malware group, where an ex-member extorted payment by threatening to leak leaders' identities. Pwn2Own Toronto 2022 saw DEVCORE win after hacking fully patched routers, printers, NAS boxes and phones, and Steve stresses that printers and routers remain easy persistence points. Citrix and Fortinet each patched an actively exploited zero-day (APT5 against Citrix, ransomware against FortiOS SSL-VPN). December Patch Tuesday fixed 72 flaws including a SmartScreen/Mark-of-the-Web bypass and the case of malware drivers that had obtained valid Microsoft signatures through Partner Center accounts. The Uber employee data leak came from IT provider Teqtivity, which illustrates managed-service concentration risk. Steve describes Twitter's blocking of whole mobile-carrier IP ranges in India, Russia and Indonesia, Vivaldi's native Mastodon integration, the Dutch government's vulnerability warnings to companies, Meta's report on 200 coordinated inauthentic behavior networks, and NIST's decision to retire SHA-1 by 2030. Listener feedback covers Apple ADP key handling, Eufy deleting privacy promises, the US Senate unanimously barring TikTok on government devices and zero-width space uses. The main story is Team82's generic bypass of WAFs from Palo Alto Networks, F5, AWS, Cloudflare and Imperva using JSON operators in SQL, which Steve uses to argue that SQL injection reflects a fundamentally flawed architecture and that sites without a WAF may be exposed.

# Key points

- URSNIF malware crew: a disgruntled ex-member leaked chats, source code and low-level members' names on Twitter and was paid; fourth ransomware-ecosystem group to suffer internal disputes in 2022.
- Pwn2Own Toronto 2022 (DEVCORE winners): dozens of working attacks on up-to-date Canon, HP and Lexmark printers, TP-Link, Synology, Netgear and MikroTik routers, a Synology NAS and Samsung Galaxy S22; printers are noisy network citizens and attractive for persistence.
- Fortinet FortiOS SSL-VPN zero-day (unauthenticated RCE) spotted by Olympe being used by ransomware actors; patched in three days, including old 6.0 firmware.
- Citrix zero-day found by the NSA being used by Chinese group APT5.
- Patch Tuesday (72 fixes): SmartScreen/MotW bypass for standalone JavaScript files; malicious drivers signed via Microsoft Partner Center abused by Hive and Cuba ransomware, reported by SentinelOne, Mandiant and Sophos; Apple also patched a WebKit zero-day.
- Uber breach number two: 77,000 employee records, source code and credentials leaked from contractor Teqtivity; Steve discusses the in-house versus managed-service dilemma.
- Twitter blocked IP blocks of about 30 Asian mobile carriers to stop bots, cutting off legitimate users in three countries; Elon Musk's poll about stepping down also mentioned.
- Vivaldi first browser with Mastodon in the sidebar.
- Dutch government sent over 5,200 vulnerability warnings to companies since summer 2021, 76% about internet-exposed services such as RDP, SMB and LDAP.
- Meta's Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior report: 200 networks from 68 countries disrupted; Russia 34, Iran 29, Mexico 13 originating; US most targeted (34), then Ukraine (20), UK (16); Mexican networks sometimes served rival candidates simultaneously.
- NIST retires SHA-1 for federal modules after 2030; Steve prefers removing support altogether to avoid downgrade attacks.
- Wordfence launched a free WordPress plugin vulnerability database.
- Listener feedback: Apple ADP keys are moved into the user's Keychain blob, deleted from Apple's HSMs, then rotated; The Verge reports Eufy removed ten privacy promises instead of answering; the US Senate voted unanimously to bar TikTok from government devices, which Steve calls geopolitical theatre given deep dependence on Chinese-made hardware; zero-width space has benign uses such as preventing auto-linking.
- Team82 WAF bypass: SQL engines (MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQLite, MS SQL, MariaDB) support JSON operators that WAF parsers did not understand, so JSON-wrapped SQL injection slipped past Palo Alto Networks, F5, AWS, Cloudflare and Imperva; vendors patched; Steve warns that unprotected sites and the mixing of data with commands remain a deep problem, comparing it to printf format strings.
