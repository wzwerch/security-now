# Summary

The episode opens with Citizen Lab's forensic confirmation that Israeli vendor Paragon's Graphite spyware, delivered through an iMessage zero-click (CVE-2025-43200, fixed in iOS 18.3.1) and a WhatsApp PDF/FreeType exploit (CVE-2025-27363), was used against European journalists. A long run of shorter items follows: Windows 11 cutting restore point retention to 60 days, the EU moving off Azure toward OVHcloud, the EU post-quantum roadmap (start by end 2027, high-risk systems by 2030, rest by 2035), Russia's planned national IMEI database, the UK-Canada Common Good Cyber Fund, U.S. state laws on Bitcoin ATMs, and the House banning WhatsApp on staff devices. Steve then discusses the LibXML2 maintainer's decision to treat security reports as ordinary public bugs because big companies use the library for free, a WinRAR 7.12 directory traversal fix, the haveibeenpwned.watch visualization site, Sophos data showing exploited vulnerabilities and stolen credentials cause most ransomware, Microsoft's planned non-kernel endpoint security platform after the CrowdStrike outage, and the Supreme Court upholding Texas porn age verification. Canada orders Hikvision out, Germany seeks to ban DeepSeek, and Russian ISPs reportedly cap Cloudflare pages at 16 KB. The main topic is an Atlantic Council paper on the offensive cyber supply chain, which argues the U.S. should become an open, direct buyer of zero-day exploits.

# Key points

- Paragon Graphite: Citizen Lab found it on devices of Italian journalist Ciro Pellegrino and a European journalist; Apple's Threat Notifications use the term "mercenary spyware"; seven Italians were notified; the Italian government denied ordering surveillance of journalists. The WhatsApp issue involved a malformed font in a PDF sent in groups, patched server side. Steve notes the five known commercial spyware vendors are all Israeli (NSO, Cellebrite, QuaDream, Candiru, Paragon).
- Windows 11 24H2 now keeps restore points for 60 days rather than 90.
- EU: advanced talks with OVHcloud to reduce Azure dependence after U.S. sanctions closed Microsoft accounts of International Criminal Court judges; EU post-quantum cryptography roadmap with 2027, 2030 and 2035 milestones; Steve praises the orderly transition (hybrid schemes, lattice crypto).
- Russia plans a central IMEI database, officially for fraud prevention.
- Common Good Cyber Fund: UK and Canada seed about $5.7 million for nonprofits maintaining core internet infrastructure; G7 endorsed.
- Crypto ATMs: FBI reported $247 million in losses in 2024; Illinois, Vermont, Nebraska, Arizona, Oklahoma and Rhode Island passed rules (receipts with wallet address, daily limits, licensing, refunds, warnings); Spokane banned kiosks; operator Bitcoin Depot reports about 20% operating margin.
- House Chief Administrative Officer banned WhatsApp on staff devices citing lack of transparency and on-device protection; Meta disagrees; the discussion notes backups (iCloud) often expose data unless protected.
- LibXML2 maintainer Nick Wellnhofer stepped down from LibXSLT and proposed treating security issues as normal public bugs; he argues Apple, Google and Microsoft depend on volunteer code and Project Zero deadlines pressure him; Red Hat's Michael Catanzaro suggests a 14-day disclosure deadline and corporate maintainers.
- WinRAR 7.12 fixes a Windows-only directory traversal that could write files to the Startup folder (ZDI-reported).
- Sophos ransomware root causes: exploited vulnerabilities about 32 to 36%, compromised credentials 23 to 29%, malicious email 18 to 23%, phishing 11 to 18%, brute force 3 to 6%, downloads 1 to 2%; tracked attacks were 1,974 in 2023, 2,974 in 2024, and already 3,400 in the first half of 2025.
- Microsoft will privately preview a security platform letting antivirus run without kernel access, a response to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage that hit 8.5 million Windows machines.
- Supreme Court upheld Texas HB 1181 (6-3); Pornhub's parent blocks access in 17 states; Steve sees VPN use becoming a target of future law; Leo notes age verification is an inherent privacy loss.
- Canada ordered Hikvision to cease operations; Berlin's regulator reported DeepSeek to app stores for GDPR violations and the EU may follow; Russian ISPs reportedly throttle Cloudflare traffic to 16 KB per page.
- Atlantic Council paper (Winnona DeSombre Bernsen): zero-day acquisition is a billion-dollar opaque market; roughly half of in-the-wild zero-days Google tracked in 2023 and 2024 came from commercial vendors; middlemen mark prices up (about $100,000 to $750,000 to $1 million); China uses decentralized contracting and n-days; U.S. talent pipeline is weak and hackers distrust the government (Operation Sundevil history). Recommendations: government vulnerability broker, hacker legal protections, accelerators, and counterintelligence-aware policy. Steve concludes the U.S. should openly buy zero-days directly.
