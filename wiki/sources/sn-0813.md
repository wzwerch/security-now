# Summary

Jason Howell sits in for Leo. Steve covers the Ubiquiti breach: a whistleblower told Brian Krebs the December 2020 intrusion was far worse than disclosed, with attackers gaining root-level access to Ubiquiti's AWS accounts, including secrets for forging single sign-on cookies, which matters because newer firmware forces cloud-account logins. Data on 533 million Facebook users, scraped in 2019 through an Add Friend flaw, was posted for free, and Troy Hunt added phone-number search to Have I Been Pwned. A 22-year-old former employee was indicted for shutting down disinfection at a Kansas rural water district. Google will restrict the Android QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission that lets apps list every installed app. Fake Call of Duty: Warzone cheats are being distributed as malware kits, and QNAP NAS devices have two unfixed critical flaws that the finders chose not to detail after a 120-day grace period. The main topic is Trinity College Dublin research showing iPhones and Pixel phones contact Apple and Google about every 4.5 minutes even when idle and opted out of telemetry, sending device identifiers and, for iOS, nearby device MAC addresses with location, which Steve says is hard to avoid and deserves explanation.

# Key points

- Ubiquiti: January 2021 disclosure spoke of unauthorised access at a third-party cloud provider; the insider account says attackers got admin to AWS (S3 buckets, databases, logs, SSO cookie secrets, source code and signing keys) via credentials from an employee's password-manager account, and executives downplayed it to protect the stock. Ubiquiti maintains no evidence of customer data access; the attacker was allegedly someone with intricate cloud knowledge who tried extortion.
- Advice: change passwords, enable two-factor, delete and recreate device access profiles with fresh credentials, update firmware, and disable remote access unless needed; Steve criticises mandatory cloud login for local devices and recommends network segmentation for IoT.
- Facebook leak: 533,313,128 records (names, IDs, phone numbers, locations, some emails; 32 million in the US) from the 2019 Add Friend scraping flaw; Facebook called it old data. Only about 2.5 million records have emails, so Have I Been Pwned added phone-number search.
- Kansas Post-Rock Rural Water District: Wyatt Travnichek, a former employee with remote access that was never revoked, is charged with tampering with a public water system after shutting down cleaning and disinfecting on March 27; maximum 25 years and a fine. Contrasted with the Oldsmar, Florida incident.
- Google Play policy: QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES for apps targeting API 30 is limited to apps whose core purpose needs broad visibility (launchers, security apps); others must use scoped queries or face removal; installed-app lists are treated as sensitive data used for fingerprinting.
- Warzone cheats: fake cheat tools tell players to disable antivirus and run as admin, installing remote access trojans; sold as an affiliate kit on malware forums with promotional videos, per Activision.
- QNAP (about 69 percent NAS share per Steve): SAM Seamless Network reported a web-server command execution flaw (port 8080) and a DLNA/UPnP arbitrary file write that can become code execution (port 8200) in firmware 4.3.6.1446; unfixed after four months, details withheld. Steve urges keeping QNAP off the public internet.
- Trinity College Dublin (Douglas Leith): both iOS and Android contact their vendor about every 4.5 minutes while idle; IMEI, serial number, SIM details and phone number are shared; telemetry continues despite opt-out; iOS also sends Bluetooth/Secure Element IDs and nearby device MAC addresses tagged with GPS location. A Pixel sent about 1 MB in the first 10 minutes versus about 42 KB for iPhone (Google disputed the "20 times" framing); Apple did not respond to the researchers. A de-Googled Android setup can reduce sharing; iPhone offers no opt-out.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a playground tube slide with its exit bolted shut, captioned as a backend fix for frontend issues.
- Listener joke about the two hard problems in computer science (naming things and off-by-one errors), prompting Steve's remarks on variable naming in SpinRite 6.1.
- Banter about Android versus iOS openness and the COVID exposure-notification debate.
