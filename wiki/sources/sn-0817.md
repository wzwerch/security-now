# Summary

Steve devotes the episode to ransomware. The REvil (Sodinokibi) gang breached Apple supplier Quanta Computer and leaked unreleased Mac designs before dropping them from its leak site as negotiations began; the demand fell from $50 million to $20 million, a pattern Steve notes is typical once victims open a dialogue. Scripps Health in San Diego was crippled by an attack that forced emergency diversions and manual record-keeping across four hospitals. The multinational Emotet takedown (Operation Ladybird, January 2021) is explained: law enforcement took over command servers, pushed an inert loader to about 1.6 million infections, and on April 25 it uninstalled the malware from over a million machines. The FBI and Dutch police gave 4.3 million Emotet-harvested email addresses to Have I Been Pwned; Steve explains the NotifyMe and domain-search services and what he found for grc.com. After another strike against QNAP's hard-coded backdoor in Hybrid Backup Sync, a Gravity newsreader fix, a Dan Kaminsky anecdote and sci-fi feedback, he reviews the 81-page Ransomware Task Force report from the Institute for Security and Technology and DOJ's new ransomware task force. His verdict: the effort is welcome and thorough, but with ransomware crews sheltered by hostile states and paid in hard-to-trace cryptocurrency, he doubts it will change much.

# Key points

- REvil/Sodinokibi stole Apple product designs from Quanta Computer; leak page hidden once Quanta started negotiating; the ransom fell from $50M to $20M with a May 7 deadline. Acer was earlier hit for a similar $50M demand.
- Scripps Health (four main hospitals) lost its electronic records, patient portal and telemetry; stroke, trauma and heart-attack patients were diverted. Previous comparison: Ryuk at Universal Health Services in 2020.
- Emotet began as a 2014 banking trojan and became a malware-as-a-service delivery platform (loading Trickbot, Qbot, then Ryuk, Conti, ProLock, Egregor); at its peak about 1.6 million infections.
- Takedown: control of hundreds of command servers in 90+ countries by agencies from eight countries; backups of the infrastructure were also seized; bots were redirected to German federal police servers and received an uninstall module with an April 25, 2021 timer that removed service and autorun entries only; some Ukrainian suspects were arrested.
- Malwarebytes' CEO urged transparency and called the push-update approach a one-off; Marcus Hutchins warned that secondary malware may still be present; Steve expects more such lawful interventions, as with the FBI Exchange web-shell removal.
- Have I Been Pwned loaded 4,324,770 Emotet-related addresses as a "sensitive" breach (not publicly searchable; use /NotifyMe verification or a domain search). About 39% were already in other breaches. Troy Hunt recommends Microsoft Defender for AV.
- Domain-wide HIBP notification requires proving domain control via email, meta tag, a file, or DNS TXT record; Steve found 155 grc.com addresses (nine real, the rest guessed or spoofed).
- QNAP: Steve calls the Hybrid Backup Sync authorization flaw another hard-coded credential, exploited by several ransomware families; QNAP advised moving off port 8080 (security by obscurity); his advice is to never expose QNAP devices to the internet.
- Ransomware Task Force report "Combating Ransomware: A Comprehensive Framework for Action": 60+ experts, four goals (deter, disrupt the business model, help organisations prepare, respond better); cited statistics: average downtime 21 days, 287 days to full recovery, $350M paid in 2020 (up 311%), average payment $312,493; 2,400 US governments, hospitals and schools hit in 2020.
- Report notes ransomware-as-a-service (two-thirds of attacks per Group-IB), cryptocurrency laundering tactics (chain hopping, mixers, privacy coins) and state links such as the FSB and Evil Corp; it did not reach consensus on banning ransom payments.
- DOJ memo (John Carlin) created an internal ransomware task force to target the whole ecosystem, including forums and hosting providers.
