# Summary

The first episode of 2019 covers the NSA's announcement that it will release its internal reverse engineering framework, Ghidra, at the RSA Conference in March; Steve explains what disassemblers do and expects wide adoption, noting the commercial alternative IDA Pro is expensive. Adobe issued emergency Acrobat and Reader patches. The PewDiePie hacker returned with CastHack: over 72,000 exposed Chromecast, smart TV and Google Home devices were forced to play a promotional video, because UPnP on routers had mapped their ports to the internet. Microsoft issued an out-of-cycle fix for an actively exploited Internet Explorer JScript flaw found by Google's Threat Analysis Group. SandboxEscaper released a fresh Windows Installer zero-day before her accounts were removed. The US counterintelligence center began distributing basic cyber awareness material to industry. Welcome news: Bleeping Computer joined the No More Ransom project, more ransomware decryptors exist, and Windows 10 Pro will get Windows Sandbox, a disposable lightweight VM. Steve also notes a new page-cache side channel to be covered later, and updates SQRL work: a service provider API that lets any web server add SQRL with little crypto knowledge, and praise for the Filemail large-file sharing service.

# Key points

- Ghidra: NSA reverse engineering suite (Java, cross-platform, multiple instruction sets), previously revealed in Vault 7 leaks, to be freely released; Steve describes static analysis, symbol recovery and interactive naming.
- Adobe released out-of-cycle fixes for critical Acrobat/Reader bugs reported by Trend Micro; no known exploitation.
- CastHack: devices with ports reachable (often through UPnP) were exposed; attackers could cast media, rename, reboot or reset devices but not reach other LAN devices or the microphone; advice is to disable UPnP, reboot the router, stop forwarding Chromecast ports, and update firmware.
- IE zero-day in the JScript engine allowed code execution via web pages, email or Office documents; Microsoft patched it out of cycle.
- SandboxEscaper's fourth zero-day abuses the Windows Installer advertise function to read arbitrary files with system rights; her social and code-hosting accounts were removed.
- NCSC campaign warns US companies about nation-state attackers; Steve finds the tips trivial.
- Humorous note: a legal filing's redaction could be undone by copy and paste.
- Apple's CES privacy billboard appeared on a Marriott building, ironic after Marriott's breach.
- No More Ransom (Europol, McAfee and others) lists decryption tools in 35 languages; decryptors only work for poorly implemented ransomware, while correct implementations discard the key and cannot be reversed.
- Windows Sandbox (build 18305) comes to Pro and Enterprise: pristine, disposable, hardware-virtualized; needs virtualization enabled and modest resources.
- SQRL Service Provider API documented by GRC lets server software outsource SQRL handling.
