# Summary

Steve Gibson opens with state and national moves on social media age limits: Virginia's new law restricting under-16s to one hour a day (effective 1 January 2026, widely expected to be enjoined on First Amendment grounds) and a New Zealand members' bill modeled on Australia's approach. He then covers a Python Package Index trojan disguised as a Discord debugging helper that survived over three years and more than 11,000 downloads, which he uses to restate that repository malware is constant background noise and that scanners may not catch everything. The FBI's public service announcement about proxy botnets on end-of-life routers (TheMoon) is summarized with its five precautions. A 115-page academic reverse-engineering and formal analysis of WhatsApp's multi-device group messaging found nothing alarming but could not be complete, which Steve uses to criticize Meta's secrecy compared with Signal's open specifications. The UK NCSC's assessment of AI's effect on cyber threats through 2027 is discussed with some amusement at its formal "probability yardstick". After a long listener-feedback section (speed tests, SSH ports, core memory, Microsoft passwordless accounts, Windhawk), the main topic follows up on the TeleMessage "Signal clone" breach: Steve defines true end-to-end encryption, explains why TeleMessage's archiving (plaintext chat logs forwarded by email) could never qualify, and proposes archiving via an official Signal client acting as a visible "records retention" bot participant in conversations.

# Key points

- Virginia (signed 2 May 2025) amended its consumer data protection act: platforms must apply an age screen, limit under-16s to one hour per day without parental consent, and honor device signals that a user is a minor; takes effect 1 January 2026. Steve expects immediate litigation and says laws and age thresholds are not aligned across states or countries; he favors an API on phones that lets apps ask for an age bracket.
- New Zealand's National Party introduced a members' bill (the "Social Media Age-Appropriate Users Bill") requiring platforms to take "all reasonable steps" to keep under-16s from creating accounts, with penalties; it follows Australia's lead and the prime minister floated adopting it as a government bill.
- Socket Research found the PyPI package "discordpydebug" (uploaded March 2022 as a Discord.py error logger) contained a remote access trojan with a covert command-and-control channel; it had over 11,000 downloads and no documentation. Lesson: developer machines are a pivot into enterprises, and security vendors do not necessarily find every malicious package.
- FBI PSA: end-of-life consumer routers (roughly 2010 or earlier, no more patches) with remote administration enabled are being enrolled by variants of the TheMoon botnet as criminal proxies. Advice: replace EOL routers, apply firmware updates, disable remote administration, use long unique passwords, and reboot and update if activity looks suspicious.
- Researchers from King's College London and Royal Holloway produced a 115-page formal analysis of WhatsApp multi-device group messaging (built on the Signal protocol and Sender Keys) by reverse engineering; they found no major flaw but could not examine everything, and device revocation looked less than solid. Steve argues two billion users should not depend on undisclosed "proprietary" protocol extensions.
- UK NCSC assessment: AI will almost certainly raise the frequency and intensity of cyber threats through 2027, creating a digital divide between well-defended systems and the rest, with a realistic possibility that critical systems become more vulnerable; "technical surprise is likely".
- Listener feedback on speed testing: a former ISP executive notes most slowdowns originate in home Wi-Fi or old equipment, so test from a wired connection on the best device first; testmy.net is mentioned as another test site.
- Disabling or temporarily opening SSH ports through cloud firewalls is better than merely relocating them; one listener cut 70-80k daily failed SSH attempts to near zero by moving the port and then closed it entirely, using Tailscale for access.
- Steve explains core memory's destructive read and read-modify-write cycle in response to a question about refreshing SSD cells by reading (NAND does not work that way).
- Microsoft passwordless accounts: a listener struggled with Microsoft's authenticator requirement, while Leo confirmed his account now has no password; Steve concluded Microsoft's authenticator is a passkey-style app, not just a TOTP generator.
- Windhawk (windhawk.net) is a free framework with hundreds of small source-visible mods for the Windows UI, including a community-funded "Vertical Taskbar for Windows 11".
- TeleMessage follow-up: three US departments banned the service, CBP confirmed use and disabled it, multiple hackers claimed to have breached it, and researcher Micah Lee showed the modified Signal app (TM SGNL) forwards plaintext chat logs by email. The apps were not FedRAMP-approved, contradicting a listener's claim of approval by the prior administration.
- Definition: end-to-end encryption means the provider never has access to keys or plaintext; Steve ties it to his old "Trust No One" / "Pre-Internet Encryption" ideas and to Apple's UK Advanced Data Protection dispute.
- Steve's proposal for compliant archiving: keep the official unmodified Signal app and add a secure "Federal Records Retention" Signal-bot participant (running in a secured facility) that passively archives every conversation and visibly reminds participants of retention. Leo adds that records laws require cleartext retention and that classified discussion belongs in a SCIF. Several listener companies were TeleMessage customers and are seeking alternatives.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a framed guest Wi-Fi sign with an absurdly long random password.
- Steve mentions a battery-powered e-ink "Terminal" dashboard showing GRC web traffic, and a spike from a mention of ValiDrive.
- Leo notes that Steve's earlier remarks on Microsoft passkeys drew large TikTok views.
- Steve praises a Windows perspective-correction tool (name promised for the next episode) and discusses IPv6 support in the upcoming DNS Benchmark.
