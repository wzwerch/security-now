# Summary

A news-dense episode that ends with the main topic: Google's decree that every Android developer worldwide must register centrally with Google (fee, government ID, list of application identifiers), which the F-Droid community says would end F-Droid and other open-source distribution. Earlier, Steve covers the Qantas court order against anyone viewing its leaked data (which he sees as a CEO's cover-your-assets move), Brave passing 100 million monthly users alongside a "three times faster" claim he calls nonsense because all Chromium browsers share the same engine, and the EU Chat Control vote scheduled for October 14, with Signal threatening to leave Europe and Steve arguing any content check belongs in the operating system, not in each app. Other items: Microsoft's new Security Store, Outlook blocking inline SVG images, Chrome 141 fixes, Gmail dropping POP fetching, Google Drive's ransomware-detection and sync pause, a renewed UK order aimed at Apple's iCloud encryption, the Battering RAM memory-interposer attack on Intel SGX and AMD SEV-SNP, HackerOne payouts, Imgur blocking the UK over an ICO fine notice, a Discord third-party breach exposing age-verification ID scans, Salesforce's carefully worded denial of a new breach amid a leak-site extortion deadline, and Signal's sparse post-quantum ratchet. Steve's conclusion on the Android decree is that the trend toward gatekeeping feels inevitable, though F-Droid could possibly sign builds under developer identities.

# Key points

- Qantas: breach of a call center exposed data of 5.7 million customers; a New South Wales court made permanent an injunction forbidding third parties to access or publish the data; Steve doubts attackers care.
- Brave: 100 million monthly active users and 42 million daily; claim of up to 3x speed is dismissed; real gains come only from not loading trackers/ads (about 21% on Android in one benchmark). Leo mentions Helium, a Chromium fork with uBlock Origin built in.
- Chat Control: vote October 14 requiring a qualified majority (15 of 27 states representing 65% of EU population); 12 states reportedly in favor, 8 against; Netherlands to vote no, Germany wavering; Signal's Meredith Whittaker says Signal would leave the EU rather than break encryption. Steve: legislate at OS level (abstraction layer over camera and screen), not at app level; lawmakers want privacy and scanning at once, which is impossible.
- Microsoft Security Store (securitystore.microsoft.com): marketplace for Azure/Sentinel-integrated security SaaS and AI agents from partners.
- Outlook for Web and new Outlook for Windows stop displaying inline SVG images (affects under 0.1% of images; attachments still work), after months of SVG-with-JavaScript abuse.
- Chrome 141: 21 security fixes, bounties totaling about $49K (WebGPU heap overflow earned $25K).
- Gmail ends POP fetching from external accounts in January 2026; forwarding recommended instead.
- Google Drive for desktop adds AI-based ransomware detection that pauses syncing and eases restore (versions kept 25 days).
- UK reissues a narrower order to Apple targeting UK citizens' iCloud data; Apple already disabled Advanced Data Protection for UK users and could appeal.
- Battering RAM (KU Leuven): a roughly $50 DRAM interposer that is transparent at boot, later redirects addresses to break Intel SGX and AMD SEV-SNP confidential computing; needs physical access (rogue data-center insider); mitigation would require redesigning memory encryption. Impractical form factor, proof of concept.
- HackerOne paid $81M in a year across about 85,000 valid reports; AI-product bugs (prompt injection) paid $2.1M.
- Imgur withdrew from the UK on September 30, 2025 after the ICO issued a notice of intent to fine its parent MediaLab over age checks and children's data; ICO also investigating TikTok and Reddit; regulators say exiting does not erase past liability.
- Discord: a third-party customer-support vendor was breached; stolen data included government ID scans collected for age verification. Steve: retained ID data is a liability and proper age assertion infrastructure does not yet exist.
- Salesforce: says new extortion attempts relate to past or unsubstantiated incidents and no platform compromise; the group "Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters" posted a ransom demand with a 10-10-2025 deadline claiming about a billion records; earlier attacks used phished OAuth tokens.
- Signal Sparse Post-Quantum Ratchet (SPQR): third ratchet added to the double ratchet (making a triple ratchet), rolled out compatibly; Steve plans a deep dive.
- TPM tip: a firmware TPM stuck at 1.2 may become 2.0 after a BIOS update, making a PC eligible for Windows 11; Microsoft Entra ID reportedly rejects third-party/browser-based passkey stores and pushes Microsoft Authenticator.
- Google Android developer verification: F-Droid's critique says it would block F-Droid because it cannot take over developers' application identifiers, argues Play Protect already handles malware, and calls on regulators (DMA team) to act; EU DMA team replied noncommittally; Steve notes Windows code-signing costs and Apple notarization as parallel trends and Leo suggests GrapheneOS.

# Other segments

- Listener feedback: SpinRite success reviving a TrueNAS drives, Liquid Glass accessibility settings on iOS 26 (Steve finds the animated UI cartoonish with default settings), Entra passkey issues.
- Books: Peter F. Hamilton's "Exodus: The Helium Sea" due June 16, 2026; Steve is downsizing his book and hardware collection.
