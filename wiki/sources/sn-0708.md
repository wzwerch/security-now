# Summary

The main topic is Google's Android Security 2018 year-in-review report, which Steve found far more impressive than he expected: only 0.08% of devices using Google Play exclusively were affected by potentially harmful applications (PHAs), versus roughly eight times as many for devices sideloading, and the platform now has the full modern defence stack (encryption, hardware-backed keys, sandboxing, SELinux, verified boot). Before that, FireEye's catalogue of WinRAR ACE exploitation campaigns shows how a flaw in software without auto-update lingers, and WinRAR itself emailed licensed users with a free Malwarebytes offer. A Sophos report documents widespread Russian GNSS spoofing, linked to VIP and facility protection and Syria. April 1's Android patches fixed two critical Media Framework flaws. Tesla Autopilot research from Tencent Keen Lab was widely mis-headlined; the actual finding was that a camera-based lane-recognition model could be misled by stickers into steering toward the adjacent lane. Researchers also cracked the salted hashes in Kaspersky's ShadowHammer checker to publish the targeted ASUS MAC addresses.

# Key points

- WinRAR ACE flaw (CVE-2018-20250): FireEye saw campaigns using decoy documents (an accreditation letter, SysAid documentation aimed at an Israeli defence firm, Ukraine-targeted lures, and fake credential dumps) delivering NetWire, a custom downloader named SappyCache and QuasarRAT; one lure used a remote icon to leak NTLM hashes. Behaviour-based rules need not rely on the Startup folder because arbitrary paths are possible. WinRAR 5.70 fixes it; WinRAR emailed customers with a three-month Malwarebytes Premium offer.
- Sophos "Above Us Only Stars": 9,883 suspected GNSS spoofing events at 10 locations affecting 1,311 civilian ships since 2016; spoofing correlates with movements of the Russian head of state, protects strategic facilities (including a Black Sea palace) and denies airspace around Khmeimim air base in Syria; cheap transmitters mean proliferation risk.
- Android April 2019 bulletin: two critical remote code execution flaws in the Media Framework (Android 7.0 to 9) plus privilege-escalation fixes; no known exploitation; Google gave partners the patches a month earlier.
- Tencent Keen Security Lab on Tesla Autopilot (software 18.6.1): root access to the Autopilot ECU and steering via CAN with a gamepad, adversarial images that trigger the camera-based auto wipers, and three dots on the road that made lane recognition steer left. Steve stresses it did not show a car driving into oncoming traffic; Leo notes Tesla's hands-on-wheel checks.
- ShadowHammer follow-up: the targeted list used both Ethernet and Wi-Fi MACs and only laptops; Skylight Cyber reverse engineered Kaspersky's custom salted SHA-256 scheme and recovered 583 of 619 MAC addresses in under an hour on a rented AWS GPU instance (versus an estimated 162 days on local hardware), then published them.
- Windows 10 version 1809 moved to broad (Semi-Annual Channel) deployment; VMware patched the flaws shown at Pwn2Own.
- Google Android report highlights: Google Play Protect scans all installs; devices with Play-only apps 0.08% PHA rate; click fraud added as a PHA category; 84% more devices received security updates in Q4 2018 than a year earlier; over 95% of Pixel 3 devices ran a patch less than 90 days old; Build Test Suite blocked 242 OEM builds containing preinstalled PHAs; over $3 million in security rewards; no Pixel exploited at Mobile Pwn2Own; no publicly disclosed critical Android flaw lacked a patch or mitigation in 2018.
- Threat trends: preinstalled PHAs and backdoored SDKs; backdoors made up 28% of PHA installs outside Play; families named include Chamois, SnowFox, Cosiloon, BreadSMS, View SDK, Triada, CardinalFall, FlashingPuma, EagerFonts and Idle Coconut. India's PHA rate fell to 0.65%; US rose to 0.5% after the redefinition.
- Steve's verdict: Android is hard to secure with fringe vendors, but with a mainstream vendor that ships patches it is state of the art; iPhone remains more controlled.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: chart of PHA rates in and outside Google Play.
- Listener items: SQRL adoption by enterprises, alternate PDF readers (built-in browser viewers; Steve uses Adobe Reader 9.5 locked down), SpinRite pre-release testing open to owners, praise for "The Expanse".
- SQRL: finishing the "what if" content with about 40 questions; iOS and Android clients still lack some features.
