# Summary

Steve opens with the news that senior US officials planned military action in a Signal group that accidentally included a journalist; his point is that Signal's cryptography is sound but phones are not a trustworthy endpoint, which is why SCIFs exist, and he hopes the mistake teaches the lesson rather than costing jobs. He revisits the fake "I'm not a robot" prompts that make victims paste a command into the Windows Run dialog (seen on car-dealership sites via a compromised shared service) and muses that agent-style user interfaces may eventually remove such human-in-the-loop traps. Espressif replies to the ESP32 "backdoor" claims, confirming they are internal debug HCI commands that cannot be triggered remotely and promising a software removal. Other items: eleven state-backed groups abusing padded Windows LNK shortcut files that Microsoft will not fix, a one-PUT remote code execution in Apache Tomcat (CVE-2025-24813), a CVSS 10 authentication bypass in AMI MegaRAC BMCs, Google's 32 billion USD purchase of Wiz, RCS gaining MLS-based end-to-end encryption, an "AI crypto chatbot" tricked out of 105,000 USD, TikTok's possible hand-over to Oracle, 23andMe's Chapter 11 filing and how to delete data, White House guidance not to lay off cybersecurity staff, and rising AI project failure rates. Listener feedback covers cross-browser bookmark sync (xBrowserSync) and vendor end-of-life economics. The closing segment, built on an HP Threat Research article, explains why quantum-vulnerable asymmetric cryptography must be migrated now.

# Key points

- Signal leak: the app was not broken; the lesson is that sensitive planning belongs on controlled systems because endpoints are exposed to spyware such as Pegasus and the content is plaintext on screen.
- Fake CAPTCHA / paste-and-run attack: a compromised video-service supplier injected JavaScript on over 100 dealership sites; the page copies a PowerShell command to the clipboard and tells the user to press Win+R, Ctrl+V, Enter, installing the SectopRAT remote access trojan. Steve calls the human factor the unsolved problem.
- Espressif statement: the ESP32 commands are developer debug commands in its HCI implementation, not reachable over Bluetooth, radio or the Internet, present only in the original ESP32 (not the C, S or H series), and will be removed in a software update. It operates a bug-bounty program.
- Windows LNK: Trend Micro's ZDI found nearly 1,000 malicious .lnk files from 11 APT groups (North Korea, Iran, Russia, China) that pad the Target field with whitespace so the real command is invisible in Properties; Microsoft declined to patch. Advice: never open a shortcut whose Target field looks empty.
- Apache Tomcat CVE-2025-24813 (CVSS 9.8): partial PUT handling lets an attacker write a malicious serialized session file, then trigger deserialization with a JSESSIONID cookie; exploit published on a Chinese forum and used within about 30 hours. Roughly 19,000 exposed servers; applies where file-based session storage is used.
- AMI MegaRAC BMC flaw CVE-2024-54085 (CVSS 10, found by Eclypsium): authentication bypass in baseboard management controllers used by ASUS, ASRock Rack and HPE servers; over 1,000 were exposed to the Internet.
- Google agreed to buy cloud-security firm Wiz for 32 billion USD (after a 23 billion attempt failed), the largest cybersecurity acquisition.
- GSMA RCS Universal Profile 3 adds MLS-based end-to-end encryption, giving interoperable encrypted messaging between iOS and Android; Steve says it matches the Signal protocol's strength, and notes backups and device security still matter.
- An attacker used crafted Twitter replies to make an AI crypto chatbot send away about 105,000 USD in Ether.
- TikTok: Oracle is front-runner to oversee a US deal ("Project Texas 2.0"); critics note the algorithm would remain Chinese-controlled and Beijing has resisted a forced sale.
- 23andMe filed for Chapter 11; Steve advises deleting genetic data and shows the account-settings route (and a GRC shortcut), noting you can download raw data first.
- The Federal CIO told agencies to avoid cutting cybersecurity staff; CISA had lost over 130 positions. S&P survey: 42 percent of firms scrapped most AI initiatives, and 46 percent of proofs of concept never reached production.
- Listeners: xBrowserSync for open-source bookmark sync across Chrome, Firefox and Android; a service-provider insider on hardware end-of-life economics; DoD STIG documents give CIOs leverage to demand replacement of unsupported gear.
- Quantum threat (HP): two 2024 false alarms (a lattice-algorithm paper retracted after a flaw in step nine, and a D-Wave "RSA break" that only factored toy numbers) showed the community can respond but may breed complacency. The Global Risk Institute survey gave an average 27 percent chance of cryptography being broken by 2034 (a third of experts at 50 percent or more); BSI says at most 16 years. Progress: Google Willow (105 qubits), IBM Heron (156), neutral atoms, Microsoft's topological qubit proof of concept.
- Migration is not like patching a zero-day because every implementation of vulnerable asymmetric crypto, including hardware and firmware roots of trust, must be replaced. Timelines: US national security systems quantum-resistant for new acquisitions from 2027 and phase-out by 2030; NIST draft to deprecate RSA and ECC from 2030 and disallow after 2035; Australia and 18 EU states target 2030. Estimated US government migration cost about 7.1 billion USD. HP announced PCs and printers with quantum-resistant firmware integrity.
