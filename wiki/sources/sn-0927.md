# Summary

The episode opens with a Stable Diffusion trick that turns photos into scannable QR codes, then covers Google's Cryptomining Protection Program (up to $1 million coverage for Security Command Center Premium customers) and reports that Twitter was withholding payments to Google Cloud and AWS. Nokia's threat report shows IoT-botnet DDoS bots rising from about 200,000 to roughly one million devices. A Chinese certificate authority, HiCA, was found to require the acme.sh client because it exploited a bug in that client to run its own commands on customers' servers; the bug was fixed and the CA closed. Steve reviews Apple's WWDC 2023 privacy and security announcements and contrasts them with a French Senate amendment allowing secret remote activation of suspects' phone cameras and microphones. Russia's Roskomnadzor will block foreign vulnerability scanners. After listener feedback, science-fiction book recommendations and a SpinRite testimonial, the main segment traces how Zakir Durumeric's ZMap led to Censys, the search engine over continuous Internet-wide scans, including its scan schedules, protocol detection and opt-out options.

# Key points

- Stable Diffusion with ControlNet can render artistic images that remain valid QR codes.
- Google reports 65% of compromised cloud accounts see cryptomining; its Cryptomining Protection Program covers up to $1 million of undetected-mining compute charges for Security Command Center Premium customers, which scans VM memory agentlessly.
- Twitter reportedly withheld Google Cloud and AWS payments to renegotiate contracts; Google's contract covered spam, CSAM and account protection services; Amazon reportedly threatened to withhold ad payments.
- Nokia Threat Intelligence report: IoT botnet bots rose about fivefold to around one million and generate over 40% of DDoS traffic; banking trojans doubled to 9% of mobile malware; home network infection fell from 3% to 1.5%.
- HiCA certificate authority: documentation insisted on acme.sh; Caddy's author Matt Holt found its ACME challenge objects were not standard and made the client run shell commands, i.e. arbitrary code execution on customer servers. acme.sh fixed the bug and HiCA shut down. Advice: use Let's Encrypt.
- Apple WWDC 2023: Vision Pro eye-tracking data stays on device; Safari private browsing locks when idle and strips tracking parameters from links; narrower Photos picker with permission reminders; Communication Safety extended to video and third-party apps plus an optional Sensitive Content Warning for adults; shared password and passkey groups; mail one-time-code autofill in Safari; stronger Lockdown Mode (also on watchOS); Check In, NameDrop and Live Voicemail.
- France's Senate passed an amendment letting investigators covertly activate cameras, microphones and location on devices for serious crimes; Steve wonders whether this prepares legal cover for commercial spyware.
- Russia's Roskomnadzor will block the more than ten known foreign vulnerability scanners; Steve notes unknown scanners and Internet background radiation would continue.
- Tavis Ormandy moved from Project Zero to Google's CPU security team; a school-IT listener says districts are overwhelmed rather than uncaring.
- Listener asks why self-deleting iMessage exploits cannot be caught on a Mac; Leo and Steve discuss that iCloud message storage is end-to-end only with Advanced Data Protection.
- SpinRite: a user recovered about 35 GB from a chilled USB stick but hit the B04E division overflow (patch exists); a 6.1 tester's thumb drive read speed rose from 1.077 to 14.021 MB/s after running SpinRite.
- ZMap (2013, Zakir Durumeric, University of Michigan) can scan the IPv4 space on one port in under 45 minutes; it enabled measuring Heartbleed's impact (24 to 55% of top-million HTTPS sites initially vulnerable, 3% still vulnerable two months later).
- LZR research: only 3% of HTTP and 6% of HTTPS services run on ports 80 and 443, and services on unexpected ports tend to be less secure.
- Censys (commercial spin-off, proprietary scanner since 2019): scans IPv4 on 3,592+ ports, 137 standard ports daily, all 65,535 ports at a low rate, about 79 million IPv6 addresses, about 2.1 billion services refreshed roughly every 16 hours, from five Tier-1 ISPs, with automatic protocol detection (over 100 protocols); never attempts logins; free access for verified researchers; opt-out by blocking its scanner subnets or filtering its user agent.

# Other segments

- Sci-fi recommendations: Frontiers Saga (Ryk Brown), Silver Ships, Honor Harrington, Lost Fleet; Steve's older reading guide is at grc.sc/scifi. Brief talk of Foundation and Dune on TV.
