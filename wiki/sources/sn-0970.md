# Summary

Steve follows up on the AT&T leak: the company now says 51,226,382 people are affected, after earlier denials, and claims it first learned of the breach in March 2024 although the data was offered for sale in 2021; he repeats the advice to keep credit reports frozen by default (freeze, not lock) and gives a shortcut to a how-to. Greylock McKinnon Associates, a litigation-support consultancy for the DOJ, disclosed that attackers obtained 341,650 Social Security numbers and Medicare data in May 2023, with notices sent only months later. ETH Zurich researchers found that 65.4 percent of about 97,000 EU-popular websites collect data despite the visitor rejecting cookies, and top sites are worse. Microsoft's new Outlook for Windows shows European users that it shares data with 772 third parties. Feedback covers physical-button requirements for risky changes, the D-Link disclosure debate, and how long to run SpinRite's new RAM test. The main topic is GhostRace, a VU Amsterdam and IBM finding that all common OS synchronisation primitives (mutexes, spinlocks) can be bypassed on speculative paths, creating speculative race conditions and enabling kernel memory leaks, preceded by a tutorial on race conditions, atomic exchange and locks.

# Key points

- AT&T: 51.2 million notified; data from 2019 or earlier includes names, SSNs, birth dates, account numbers and passcodes; one year of credit monitoring offered; class actions filed; the breach seller was ShinyHunters in 2021 and a leaker "MajorNelson" posted the full set in 2024.
- Credit freezes at the three bureaus are free and the safest default; a GRC shortcut points to an Investopedia guide.
- Greylock McKinnon Associates: 341,650 individuals, notices dated after Feb 2024 although the incident was May 2023.
- Cookie compliance study (USENIX Security 2024): of 97,090 sites, 65.4 percent ignore negative consent; 32 percent lacked a notice; 56.7 percent had no reject button; widespread dark patterns. Steve sees Google's Privacy Sandbox as the practical fix; per-site cookie jars end cross-site tracking but push sites toward email collection.
- Outlook for Windows app discloses sharing with 772 third parties for ads and device scanning (shown because of GDPR transparency rules).
- A physical button press for sensitive changes can be abused by cheap remote button pushers, but even then herd immunity discourages mass remote attacks; Fritz!Box routers already work this way.
- SpinRite RAM test: DRAM does not wear out; the longer it runs the better; best practice is an overnight run on a new machine; it tests only the 54 MB SpinRite uses.
- Race condition tutorial: preemptive multithreading, context switches, lost updates on a shared counter, mutual exclusion via an atomic exchange instruction as the building block; many use-after-free bugs are race conditions (Patch Tuesday had 149 fixes with two zero-days).
- GhostRace (CVE-2024-2193): synchronisation primitives guard critical sections with a conditional branch, which a Spectre-style attacker can mistrain so the lock is skipped speculatively. 1,283 potentially exploitable gadgets found in the Linux kernel, kernel memory leaked at about 12 KB per second in a proof of concept; affects Intel, AMD, ARM and IBM architectures.
- Mitigation: serialising instruction inside every primitive, about 5.13 percent geometric-mean overhead (up to 12 percent on some operations); Linux kernel developers declined to adopt it immediately; AMD says existing Spectre-v1 mitigations apply.
- Steve notes the practical risk is mostly for shared cloud hosts, not single-user desktops, and ties it to the general trade-off between speculative performance and security.
