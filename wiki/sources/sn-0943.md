# Summary

Steve Gibson opens with the launch of GRC's ValiDrive freeware, which detects fake-capacity USB drives; about 14,000 copies were downloaded within days and all twelve drives he bought on Amazon were fraudulent. He doubts 23andMe's explanation that credential stuffing alone exposed roughly seven million customer records, since the leaked fields look like raw database records. He summarizes Microsoft's Digital Defense Report 2023 (IoT vulnerability rates, growth in attacks on open-source software, adversary-in-the-middle phishing, password and token-replay attacks, DDoS volumes). Shorter news items cover Brave's 9% layoff, Google Docs HTML exports rewriting links through Google tracking redirects, Sony being among 2,300+ MOVEit victims, Firefox 118 adding Encrypted ClientHello (ECH), Google promising seven years of updates for Pixel 8, the MACE Act passing the US House 394 to 1, and ransomware dwell time falling from 4.5 days to under one day. Listener questions deal with passkey portability, ISP motivations for traffic inspection, password entropy versus key-derivation iterations, ECH defeating pirate-site blocking, and 2038 epoch bugs in old Linux kernels. The main topic begins the NSA/CISA joint advisory on the ten most common network misconfigurations, starting with default configurations and legacy protocols such as LLMNR, NBT-NS and unsigned SMB.

# Key points

- ValiDrive (GRC freeware, about 95 KB assembler) tests whether a USB drive really holds the claimed capacity; it also reveals slow NAND write start-up caused by charge pumps. Windows Defender sometimes flags new unsigned-reputation software as a false positive.
- 23andMe says accounts were accessed through reused credentials via the DNA Relatives feature; Steve finds seven million records implausible for credential stuffing and suspects a larger problem is being downplayed.
- Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2023: 78% of IoT devices on customer networks have known vulnerabilities (46% unpatchable); attacks on open source up 742% since 2019; AiTM phishing domains grew from 2,000 to 9,000+; about 4,000 password attacks per second blocked; token-replay detections doubled; around 1,700 DDoS attacks per day.
- Brave laid off 9% of staff; its search index replaced Bing and the AI assistant is called Leo.
- Google Docs HTML exports replace link targets with google.com/url redirects containing tracking parameters; PDF exports are clean.
- Sony Interactive Entertainment disclosed about 7,000 affected people via the MOVEit SQL injection, one of more than 2,300 impacted organizations.
- Firefox 118 supports Encrypted ClientHello; ECH needs a public key fetched via DNS and should be paired with DNS over HTTPS; Cloudflare enabled it for free-tier sites and an experimental OpenSSL implementation exists.
- Pixel 8 and 8 Pro get seven years of OS and security updates.
- MACE Act (Modernizing the Acquisition of Cybersecurity Experts) passed the House 394 to 1; it lets agencies weigh only education directly relevant to the role.
- Ransomware dwell time dropped from a median 4.5 days to under one day (Secureworks); attacks are now mostly human-driven in real time, sometimes encrypting within five hours.
- Steve argues a passkey is just a private key and should be exportable (QR, text, CSV); portability gaps keep adoption low.
- Entropy versus iterations: each added bit of entropy doubles cracking cost, so seven extra bits beat raising PBKDF iterations from 5,000 to 600,000; a very strong password makes iteration counts almost irrelevant.
- ECH renders ISP and court-ordered SNI-based site blocking ineffective for sites behind Cloudflare; enterprises with TLS-intercepting proxies are not affected; OpenDNS Family Shield-style DNS filtering is the suggested home child-protection approach.
- Check Point-style appliances still run old kernels, so Unix epoch (2038) problems will linger in infrastructure.
- NSA/CISA advisory part 1: default credentials and configurations, plus legacy services (LLMNR, NBT-NS, SMB without signing) enable spoofing, hash capture and relay; both vendors (secure by design) and users share responsibility.
