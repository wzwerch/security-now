# Summary

Jason Howell hosts in place of Leo Laporte. Steve first reviews June 2023 Patch Tuesday (73 flaws, 26 remote code execution bugs including three critical ones in the PGM multicast component, a 9.8-rated SharePoint token-spoofing privilege flaw, and two Exchange RCEs). He then presents Ben-Gurion/Cornell researcher Ben Nassi's "video-based cryptanalysis": by filming a device's power LED, whose brightness tracks supply-voltage variations caused by cryptographic computation, and exploiting a camera's rolling shutter to raise sampling to about 60,000 samples per second, the team recovered a smart card's ECDSA key from 16 meters and a SIKE key from a phone via the LED of USB speakers on the same hub. After listener feedback and the announcement that the DOS side of SpinRite 6.1 is feature complete, the main topic is the MOVEit Transfer campaign: a zero-day SQL injection (CVE-2023-34362) abused by the Cl0p gang (tracked by Microsoft as Lace Tempest) to steal data from thousands of organizations and leave a web shell, followed by two further vulnerabilities found and patched in June. Steve stresses that SQL injection was documented in 1998 and remains the number one web risk because insecure usage is still the easy default.

# Key points

- June Patch Tuesday: 73 flaws (6 critical); three critical RCEs in Windows PGM (CVSS 9.8); a critical SharePoint JWT-spoofing elevation of privilege (9.8); two Exchange RCEs; Edge fixes including an exploited zero-day; no vulnerability was publicly known or exploited at release time that month.
- Video-based cryptanalysis (to be shown at Black Hat and DEF CON 2023): power-LED brightness leaks power draw; rolling shutter turns 60 fps into roughly 60,000 samples per second; no compromise of the target is needed, but the LED must fill the camera frame. Demonstrated against a smart card reader (via a hijacked security camera) and a Samsung Galaxy S8 charging on a shared USB hub. Steve notes a photodiode and telescope could do better.
- Listener notes: Google phones had live voicemail transcription earlier than Apple's Live Voicemail; Steve's grc.sc shortcut failed because Google Docs silently added "www" to the link; Certbot is the EFF's official Let's Encrypt client; HP+ printers require constant Internet and an HP account, and Brother printers are widely recommended.
- Steve's idea of an immutable on-device log for iMessage attachments as a forensic aid against zero-click exploits, with the caveat that victims rarely know they were hit and users may dislike recording attachments.
- SpinRite: the 29th alpha is out to 697 testers; no known bugs remain in the 6.1 DOS code; the integrated FAQ is being written; SpinRite 7 (UEFI, native USB) follows.
- MOVEit Transfer (Progress Software): a pre-authentication SQL injection zero-day, first patched May 31, 2023, was used to exfiltrate data, create an administrator session named "Health Check Service" and drop an ASPX web shell named human2.aspx (Mandiant names it LEMURLOOT; cluster UNC4857). Patching is not enough; systems must be checked for compromise. CISA alerted agencies; Censys found over 3,000 exposed instances.
- Cl0p posted an extortion notice asking victims to make contact by June 14 rather than contacting them, then began naming victims in batches; exact counts were still unknown. Reported victims included US federal agencies, Minnesota and Illinois, driver-license data for 3.5 million Oregonians and Louisiana residents, British Airways, the BBC and Boots (via payroll vendor Zellis), Shell, Johns Hopkins, the University System of Georgia, Ofcom, Transport for London and EY.
- Progress disclosed more flaws on June 9 and another independently published SQL injection on June 15 to 16, requiring more patches and temporarily taking MOVEit Cloud offline; researchers saw probing of MOVEit as far back as 2021. Cl0p previously hit Fortra GoAnywhere and Accellion.
- Steve's lesson: attackers now stockpile a zero-day, stage it against all vulnerable instances, and strike simultaneously; economics reward attackers (about $650 million total listed ransomware proceeds across Ryuk, REvil, LockBit and others) more than defenders and bug-bounty hunters. SQL injection (Jeff Forristal, Phrack 1998) caused breaches at 7-Eleven, MySpace and Equifax and tops the OWASP lists.
