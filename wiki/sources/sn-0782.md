# Summary

A deeper-dive episode. Steve reviews Chrome 85 additions: profile-guided optimization for faster page loads, AVIF image support, warnings for mixed-content media and blocking of risky mixed-content downloads with full blocking expected in Chrome 86, PWA shortcuts, and a postponed User-Agent Client Hints change to shrink the user-agent string. He then walks through a FBI sting in which a Russian national offered a Tesla Gigafactory employee a bribe of up to one million dollars to install malware for a ransomware-style extortion, with the employee reporting it. ETH Zurich researchers modelled the 2,000-page EMV standard in the Tamarin verifier and found that Visa contactless transactions can skip PIN verification and transaction limits with a relay of two Android phones. After feedback on archive encryption, Syncthing safety and mineral supplements, he covers the original 2012 and 2020 Mozilla replication studies showing that browser history patterns make users near-uniquely identifiable.

# Key points

- Chrome 85: PGO-compiled build loads pages up to about 10% faster; AVIF image format supported (Netflix measured 50% or more savings versus JPEG); visual warnings on mixed-content images, audio and video; blocking of risky mixed downloads such as PDFs and Office files, with all mixed downloads blocked in Chrome 86; the frozen user-agent string flag chrome://flags/#freeze-user-agent previews the privacy change.
- Tesla plot: the defendant allegedly offered 500,000 then 1,000,000 dollars, said a group had previously co-opted employees at other companies, and described malware that would first run a distracting DDoS and then exfiltrate data for extortion; the employee reported it, wore recorders for the FBI, and Elon Musk confirmed Tesla was the target. Marcus Hutchins called flying into US jurisdiction absurdly risky. Lesson: money turns insiders.
- EMV: the standard (Europay, Mastercard, Visa) was built in a closed group; the Zurich model found that the cardholder verification method in Visa contactless is neither authenticated nor protected, so a man-in-the-middle app can tell the terminal that PIN verification was done on the device; a second flaw allows unauthentic offline transactions. Fixes can be made in terminals without reissuing cards. Steve praises formal verification of protocols.
- Archive tip: protect password-manager exports with 7-Zip (SHA-256 based key derivation) before placing them in cloud storage.
- Syncthing is safe without a VPN: endpoints sit behind NAT, use TLS with pinned certificates for discovery, require approval for new devices, and relay data stays end-to-end encrypted. Leo plans to pre-encrypt sensitive folders.
- Steve recommends mineral supplements bound to amino acids (the Albion / TRAACS marking) such as zinc bisglycinate.
- AHCI driver test release 29a had no reported problems; the combined benchmark for booting USB sticks comes next.
- Browser history: the 2012 study of about 368,000 users found 69% unique histories and 97% uniqueness once four visited sites were detected; the 2020 Mozilla replication of 52,000 Firefox volunteers found 99% of distinct profiles unique, and users with 150+ domains were over 80% re-identifiable. Histories can be obtained by script probing, by ad/analytics scripts that see broad browsing, or by an ISP observing DNS lookups, reinforcing the case for encrypted DNS.
