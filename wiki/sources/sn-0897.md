# Summary

After a rant about misleading 3D election pie charts, Steve reviews November 2022 Patch Tuesday: 67 fixes including the two ProxyNotShell Exchange zero-days and four other exploited zero-days (JScript9, a Mark-of-the-Web bypass, Print Spooler and CNG Key Isolation), plus a regression that broke Kerberos authentication on domain controllers where RC4 was disabled. He warns about the open-sourced Shennina framework, which automates scanning and exploitation by wrapping Nmap and Metasploit with an AI engine, and welcomes GitHub's private vulnerability reporting. Three vulnerabilities in the LiteSpeed web server (about 1.9 million instances) need patching, and Kudelski's Shufflecake offers TrueCrypt-style hidden volumes for Linux. After the Medibank ransomware leak affecting 9.7 million Australians, Australia announced a standing offensive cyber task force, and an iOS change limits AirDrop "everyone" mode to ten minutes on China-sold iPhones. Further items include more DeFi thefts, the shutdown of The Helm personal server, Twitter's disruption under new ownership, listener feedback (including default credentials on the ZimaBoard) and a SpinRite 6.1 bug hunt. The main topic is the NSA's guidance recommending a strategic move from C and C++ to memory-safe languages, backed by static and dynamic testing and exploit mitigations.

# Key points

- November Patch Tuesday: 67 flaws, ProxyNotShell fixed, plus zero-days in JScript9 (reported by Google TAG), a MotW bypass ("ZippyReads"), Print Spooler and CNG Key Isolation. Domain controllers installing the updates could suffer Kerberos sign-in failures when RC4 had been disabled per best practice, affecting domain logons, ADFS, group managed service accounts, RDP and file shares; a Reddit thread documented workarounds. Steve notes Kerberos dates from 1988 and replaced NTLM.
- Shennina (Mazin Ahmed and Khalid Farah, Hack In The Box 2019 contest entry) is an AI-guided host exploitation framework integrating Nmap, Metasploit and a command-and-control server, with post-exploitation, ransomware simulation and exfiltration; the authors admit a hash tree would suffice and do not plan to maintain it. Steve fears script kiddies will use it; Leo notes red teams want such tools.
- GitHub added private vulnerability reporting so researchers can report issues without public issue-tracker posts.
- Palo Alto Unit 42 found three LiteSpeed/OpenLiteSpeed flaws (RCE and privilege escalation at CVSS 8.8, directory traversal 5.8) chainable into root code execution; fixed in OpenLiteSpeed 1.7.16.1 and LiteSpeed 6.0.12 on 18 October 2022; roughly 1.9 million instances are internet-visible.
- Shufflecake (Kudelski Security Research, GPLv3, Linux): up to 15 nested hidden encrypted volumes on a device, indistinguishable from random data, enabling decoy volumes and plausible deniability for journalists and activists; described as a spiritual successor to TrueCrypt and VeraCrypt.
- Medibank breach: the BlogXX group (a suspected REvil spin-off) stole data on 9.7 million Australians and leaked records after Medibank refused to pay; the AFP said the culprits are in Russia. Cyber minister Clare O'Neil announced a permanent task force of about 100 ASD and AFP experts to hunt and disrupt such gangs offensively and floated banning ransom payments; Leo doubts the legality of pre-emptive action. Precedents: recovery of the Colonial Pipeline payment and takedowns of REvil servers.
- iOS 16.1.1 limits AirDrop "Everyone" to 10 minutes on iPhones sold in China, widely read as a response to protest poster sharing; Apple reportedly plans a global optional version in 2023.
- DeFi losses: Pando (over $21.8 million stolen via an oracle attack; $50 million saved) and DFX Finance ($4.185 million, reentrancy attack). Steve advises not keeping money there.
- The Helm (a past sponsor) is shutting down by year end; users should move domains, and a firmware conversion to Armbian is promised.
- Twitter after the ownership change: switching off "microservices" reportedly broke delivery of SMS two-factor codes; Leo advises moving to an authenticator or hardware key, revoking third-party app access and deleting sensitive DMs.
- Listener notes: Cloudflare "Authenticated Origin Pulls" means many web servers do request client certificates, widening exposure to the OpenSSL 3.0 flaw; a ZimaBoard ships with CasaOS/Debian accounts using default passwords that setup does not change.
- NSA guidance: about 70% of Microsoft's vulnerabilities from 2006-2018 and a similar share in Chrome are memory safety bugs; the NSA recommends memory-safe languages (C#, Go, Java, Ruby, Rust, Swift) where possible, notes costs (garbage collection, bounds checks, retraining), and for legacy C/C++ advises using SAST and DAST tools together plus mitigations such as CFG, ASLR and DEP. Steve urges listeners to consider Rust (first released 2014, adopted by many large companies) or at least run analysis tools. Leo notes Rust avoids garbage collection through ownership and has a steep learning curve.

# Other segments

- Picture of the week: a distorted 3D election exit-poll pie chart in which a smaller slice looks bigger.
- Silver Ships and the related Pyreans series by Scott Jucha, which Steve is nearly done with.
- Humor: "nothing is more permanent than a temporary solution", and a sponsor link returning a 404.
- SpinRite 6.1: command-line drive selection finished; a whole-drive hash check exposed rare read errors in the new AHCI driver (bus-mastering IDE driver was fine), likely an end-of-transfer status issue; alpha is imminent.
