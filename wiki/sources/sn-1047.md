# Summary

The EU cancelled its planned October 14 Chat Control vote after Germany and the Netherlands announced opposition and only 12 of 27 states backed it; Steve shares an open letter from about 40 European tech firms arguing the scheme would hurt digital sovereignty and European privacy-focused businesses. Salesforce refused the extortion demand from Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters (about 989 million records from roughly 39 customers obtained via vishing-driven OAuth app authorization), and the first customer data (Qantas, Vietnam Airlines, Albertson's, Gap, Fujifilm, Engie) was then published. The Discord support-vendor breach turned out larger than first reported: attackers claim 5.5 million users and 1.6 TB from its Zendesk instance via a compromised BPO agent account, while Discord admits about 70,000 government-ID photos. Other stories: GitHub moving fully to Azure within 24 months, California's AB 566 requiring browsers to offer a Global Privacy Control opt-out from 2027, OpenAI disrupting state-linked phishing use, an Edge IE Mode exploit chain leading Microsoft to bury IE Mode, BreachForums domain seizure, a 100,000-IP botnet attacking US RDP endpoints, and the Nielsen Norman Group's critique of iOS 26 Liquid Glass. A large segment explains Texas SB2420, the App Store Accountability Act effective January 1, 2026, which demands real age verification and per-download parental consent for minors, far beyond Apple's Declared Age Range API. The episode closes with RediShell, CVE-2025-49844, a 13-year-old Lua use-after-free in Redis with CVSS 10.0 and about 330,000 exposed servers; Steve's advice is to patch, scan for port 6379 and never expose Redis publicly.

# Key points

- Chat Control: vote pulled; Germany's justice minister called suspicionless scanning a taboo for the rule of law; the Fight Chat Control site flooded MEP inboxes; open letter warns client-side scanning would hand advantage to US and Chinese firms. UK's separate Apple iCloud order still pending (US DNI had announced the UK dropped its earlier demand).
- Salesforce campaign: Mandiant tracks the actors as UNC6040; callers persuaded staff to connect an attacker-controlled app; Salesforce "will not engage, negotiate with, or pay"; DeepStrike estimated ransom payments of $813M in 2024 versus $1.1B in 2023; Cencora reportedly paid $75M. Steve notes extortionists must leak data to stay credible.
- Discord: hackers claim 8.4 million tickets, 1.6 TB, 58-hour access from September 20, ransom demand cut from $5M to $3.5M; the vendor-support tool allowed MFA resets and data lookups; question remains why age-verification IDs were retained. Steve highlights API-linked back-end systems that magnify the impact of a single compromised outsourced agent.
- GitHub (CTO memo reported by The New Stack) will move off its own data centers to Azure within 24 months, delaying features, because of capacity limits for AI/Copilot; concerns about MySQL clusters and outages.
- California AB 566 (signed October 2025, effective January 1, 2027): browsers must provide an easy opt-out preference signal; today only Brave, DuckDuckGo and Tor send Global Privacy Control by default, Firefox supports it when enabled, other Chromium browsers and Safari do not. Related bills cover account deletion and data brokers. Steve wishes for a cookie-preference signal too.
- OpenAI banned ChatGPT accounts used by Chinese state-linked actors to write spear-phishing for Taiwan's semiconductor industry and others; Steve is unimpressed because alternative AI tools abound.
- IE Mode abuse: attackers got Edge users to reload cloned sites in IE Mode and exploited an unpatched Chakra JavaScript engine flaw plus a privilege escalation; Microsoft removed the easy IE Mode buttons instead of issuing a CVE; now requires enabling in settings, restart and an allow-list.
- Texas SB2420 (App Store Accountability Act, effective January 1, 2026): app stores must verify age by a "commercially reasonable method" (self-declared birth date is not sufficient), link minors under 18 to a parent account and obtain per-download/per-purchase parental consent; developers must assign statutory age ratings and delete verification data. Apple objects, extends Declared Age Range API, and says Utah and Louisiana follow. Separate Texas HB1181 caused Pornhub to withdraw. Law is not retroactive for existing accounts and has not been challenged. Leo notes California's law uses age-bracket signals instead.
- BreachForums.hn domain seized by the FBI and international partners.
- GreyNoise: botnet of more than 100,000 IPs from 100+ countries attacking US RDP (RD Web Access timing attacks and web-client login enumeration) since October 8; Steve: never expose authenticated services such as RDP; geofence and filter inbound traffic; 2.4 to 4.5 million RDP servers are exposed.
- Nielsen Norman Group assessment of iOS 26 Liquid Glass: transparency harms legibility, animations distract.
- RediShell (CVE-2025-49844, Wiz Research via Zero Day Initiative, Pwn2Own Berlin): post-auth Lua sandbox escape via use-after-free present for about 13 years in all Redis versions; patched October 3, 2025; about 330,000 internet-exposed instances and 60,000 with no authentication; 57% of cloud environments deploy Redis from container images that default to no authentication; no public proof of concept yet. Mitigate by upgrading, restricting access and scanning internal networks for port 6379.
