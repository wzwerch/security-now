# Summary

Steve follows up on the Linux SACK panic (no widespread abuse; SentinelOne published a detect-and-protect script) and then covers two chained Firefox zero-days used against Coinbase staff, fixed in 67.0.3 and 67.0.4; he is critical that Mozilla had been told of one of them two months earlier. A used Nest Cam paired to a Wink hub could expose the new owner's images to the previous owner even after a factory reset until Google fixed it. OpenSSH added "shielding," keeping private keys encrypted in memory under a large random prekey, which defeats RAMBleed-style probabilistic leaks. BlueKeep patch rates reached about 83 percent by Microsoft's count. The big outage story was a BGP leak in which a regional ISP's faulty optimizer and Verizon's lack of route filtering sent roughly 2 percent of Internet traffic (including Cloudflare, Amazon and Facebook prefixes) through a steel company for about three hours. NASA JPL was breached for nearly a year via an unauthorized Raspberry Pi on a flat network. The episode compares ransomware payment (Riviera Beach, about 600,000 USD) against refusal (Atlanta and Baltimore spending millions) and closes with a tour of HackerOne's public bounty activity and its top payers.

# Key points

- Linux TCP SACK flaws (three CVEs): no mass exploitation reported; SentinelOne's sack-cve-fixer script can check and mitigate persistently.
- Firefox: two zero-days (code execution plus sandbox escape) used against Coinbase employees; a Project Zero researcher had reported the first on April 18; fixed in 67.0.3 and 67.0.4; Steve and Leo reiterate that browser password storage is a poor place for secrets.
- Nest Cam/Wink: a decommissioned camera's old Wink link showed still images from the new owner; Google fixed it; Steve advises caution buying or selling used cameras, microphones and locks.
- OpenSSH shielding by Damien Miller: keys encrypted at rest in RAM with a symmetric key derived from a random 16 KB prekey, so statistical bit leaks must recover every bit exactly.
- BlueKeep: Microsoft's Raviv Tamir tracked patch rates rising from 57 percent to 72.4 to 83.4 percent; several million machines still exposed; Steve still doubts a worm will materialize.
- BGP leak (June 24): DQE Communications' faulty route optimizer announced about 20,000 prefixes via its customer Allegheny Technologies to Verizon, which passed them on; Cloudflare's CEO criticized Verizon's silence; Cloudflare's blog said prefix limits would have prevented it.
- NASA JPL: April 2018 intrusion via an unapproved Raspberry Pi; attackers pivoted via a shared gateway and took about 500 MB, including ITAR-controlled Mars Science Laboratory data; OIG blamed poor network segmentation.
- Ransomware costs: Riviera Beach authorized its insurer to pay about 600,000 USD (65 bitcoin demanded); Atlanta refused 51,000 USD and spent 2.6 million; Baltimore refused 76,000 USD and spent 18.2 million.
- Microsoft's Chromium-based Edge reached Windows 7 in the Canary channel.
- HackerOne: public "Hactivity" feed shows constant payouts; ZDNet's top list led by Verizon (over 4 million USD paid), then Uber, PayPal, Shopify, Twitter and others; Steve contrasts this with Zerodium's opaque exploit purchases and notes the sobering volume of bugs.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: bomber dropping a USB key as "cyberwar", prompting a Stuxnet sneakernet discussion.
- Listener feedback on whether a BlueKeep worm would help inside LANs.
- Chat about Vector robot, JPL culture, and the film "I Am Mother".
