# Summary

A lighter news week. OpenBSD disabled Intel hyperthreading after Theo de Raadt received advance details of TLBleed (Ben Gras and VU Amsterdam, for Black Hat 2018), a side channel in Translation Lookaside Buffers that leaks a 256-bit EdDSA key across threads on the same core with high reliability; de Raadt complained that only tier-one vendors got advance warning. David Sanger's book claimed Mandiant watched Chinese hackers through their own cameras; FireEye says it was recorded network traffic from consenting victims. WebAssembly's inability to safely support shared array buffers (a back door to high-resolution timers) keeps it exposed to Spectre-style timing. Appthority found that Google Firebase databases are insecure by default and widely misconfigured. Steve describes reviving his dead XP drive as a VMware virtual machine. The Supreme Court's Carpenter decision requires a probable-cause warrant for historical cell-site location data.

# Key points

- TLBleed: leaks cryptographic signing key bits via TLB behavior on the sibling hyperthread (98% success after a single observation and 17 seconds of analysis, using machine learning); not fixable by cache isolation or microcode; Intel considered existing mitigations sufficient. Steve thinks the real fix is scheduling so that only one process shares a core's hyperthreads.
- OpenBSD (prioritizes security) turned off simultaneous multithreading by default; de Raadt called Intel's selective disclosure irresponsible.
- Mandiant / FireEye "hack back" dispute: Sanger wrote that Mandiant staff activated hackers' webcams while observing APT1 in 2013; FireEye states those images came from recorded Remote Desktop traffic on consenting victims' networks, never from hacking back. Sanger accepts passive monitoring as plausible. Steve recounts tracking an early DDoS attacker himself without hacking.
- Sanger's book "The Perfect Weapon" is recommended.
- WebAssembly is supported by all major browsers and is the basis of browser cryptominers; timer-resolution fuzzing added for Spectre can be bypassed via worker threads incrementing a SharedArrayBuffer counter; SharedArrayBuffer was pulled from JavaScript and WebAssembly will not add it until the risk is solved.
- Firebase ("HospitalGown" variant, Appthority): of about 28,500 apps using Firebase, around 9% of Android and 47% of iOS apps exposed their database; more than 100 million records (113 GB) including plaintext passwords, health information, GPS records, and OAuth tokens; cause is developers not enabling authentication, since the service is not secure by default.
- Steve's VM recovery: used Sysinternals Disk2VHD, StarWind V2V Converter and VMware vCenter Converter Standalone to turn the old XP system drive into a bootable VMware VM, recovering software licenses and registry hives.
- A reader noted Cisco acquisitions may explain unknown backdoors; Steve agrees that acquired code can carry inherited problems (like FireEye buying Mandiant).
- Carpenter v. United States: acquiring 12,898 location points over 127 days from a carrier's cell-site records was a Fourth Amendment search requiring a warrant; the Court declined to extend the third-party doctrine to such pervasive, involuntary data and kept the decision narrow, leaving exigent circumstances and other collection methods untouched. Steve welcomes the ruling.
