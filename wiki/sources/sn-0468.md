# Summary

Steve marks the end of the ninth year of the show. News: a modest Patch Tuesday (nine bulletins, with a remote-code Internet Explorer fix); the BadUSB talk at Black Hat showed the reprogramming can be done from software on an ordinary PC, USB has a sanctioned Device Firmware Upgrade mode, and signed firmware cannot help unless a non-writable boot core verifies it (Yubico says YubiKey firmware is in ROM). FireEye and Fox-IT recovered CryptoLocker's database of per-victim private keys and set up a free decryption service. Hold Security reported a Russian group of about a dozen people holding 1.2 billion unique credentials (542 million email addresses) gathered by a botnet that probes the sites its victims visit for SQL injection, then humans dump the databases; Leo is wary that the firm charges for checking. Google said HTTPS will be a lightweight search-ranking signal, which Steve sees as policy pushing with a cost to small sites. A startup selling Wi-Fi tracking laid off staff after iOS 8's MAC randomisation. An MIT, Microsoft and Adobe SIGGRAPH demo recovered sound from high-speed video of a potato chip bag, but ordinary cameras cannot do that. LastPass had a multi-hour datacenter outage, a reminder that TNO encryption does not protect availability. Listener questions cover Steve's SQRL password-strength heuristic (length plus unique characters plus twice the class transitions), whether to change passwords after a breach, USB capacity-faking fraud, stealth ports on DSL versus cable, why VPNs make Netflix better (a different, uncongested peering path), Boxcryptor Classic, pfSense, and wiring a new home.

# Key points

- BadUSB follow-up: reprogramming possible from an infected PC, no antivirus detection, the USB DFU specification, and the need for non-writable bootloader plus signature checks; IronKey says it signs firmware but Leo notes a compromised device can lie.
- CryptoLocker: infections fetch a public key from a server found via daily-generated domain names; each file gets a random AES key encrypted with that public key; researchers obtained the private-key repository and offer decryptcryptolocker.com free; Fox-IT published an intelligence report.
- Russian credential-theft ring: botnet probes visited sites for SQL injection, human operators extract the data; many small mom-and-pop sites are the weak link and reuse magnifies the harm; Steve and Leo advise changing important passwords (banks) especially if reused.
- Google announced a small search-ranking boost for HTTPS sites; Steve and Leo worry it forces paid certificates on hobby sites.
- Nomi, which tracked shoppers by Wi-Fi MAC addresses, cut about a third of staff after iOS 8 randomised MACs, and switched to Bluetooth beacons.
- Visual microphone: reconstructing speech from 2,000 to 6,000 frames per second video; Nyquist sampling means 60 fps cameras cannot eavesdrop; laser interferometry is the better spying tool.
- LastPass outage was due to a datacenter failure; Leo notes the offline export and LastPass Pocket as fallbacks; SQRL has no third-party dependency.
- Comcast customer-service dispute: record calls (and announce it) as many US states require notice.
- SQRL: identity export in file form done, QR display next; the client uses a no-forced-rules strength meter and heuristic; xkcd's four-word passwords are good but unique surprise helps.
- Forced periodic password changes make little sense; change after a breach and for critical accounts, and use a manager that audits reuse.
- A reader found a counterfeit MP3 player whose firmware over-reported memory, showing firmware tampering predates BadUSB.
- ShieldsUP showing closed rather than stealth ports after moving to DSL means the ISP's upstream equipment answers probes, not the router.
- VPN improving Netflix is likely because the VPN's route avoids the congested Verizon-Level 3 peering link.
- Boxcryptor Classic offers one-time purchase with filename encryption; Steve prefers it to annual subscriptions; pfSense is recommended for advanced home segmentation with VLANs.
- Wiring advice: run Cat6 (ideally in conduit) to fixed locations and use Wi-Fi for mobility; fibre links need switches at both ends.
