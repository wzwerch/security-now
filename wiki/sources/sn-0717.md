# Summary

Steve revisits BlueKeep: Microsoft's blog reminded everyone that nearly a million Internet-facing machines remained unpatched, but Steve explains why he expects a quiet cryptominer land-grab rather than a worm, since attackers can already scan and enter without brute forcing. He covers the announced shutdown of the GandCrab ransomware-as-a-service operation, which claimed billions in affiliate earnings. The US Senate passed the TRACED robocall bill, aided by SHAKEN/STIR caller-ID signing and an FCC move to let carriers block calls by default. Russia's military is moving to its certified Astra Linux while China reportedly plans a custom OS; Steve frames this as healthy escape from OS monoculture while doubting how feasible a from-scratch OS is. He explains Apple's Sign in with Apple, praising its random relay email addresses and noting that OAuth sign-in inherently lets the identity provider track users. The main item is Guardicore Labs' analysis of the Nansh0u campaign, which brute-forced exposed MS-SQL servers worldwide, installed a cryptominer protected by a kernel driver, and combined professional tooling with amateur mistakes.

# Key points

- BlueKeep: Robert Graham's scan found about 950,000 vulnerable hosts; Microsoft noted EternalBlue ransomware took about two months to appear after its fix; Steve predicts a cryptominer takeover where the first intruder closes the hole behind itself.
- GandCrab: affiliate model where distributors run custom builds and operators keep a commission; operators announced end of operation (May 31, 2019), told affiliates to monetize remaining victims within 20 days and said keys would be deleted; claimed more than 2 billion USD collected (unverified); BleepingComputer asked for key release.
- TRACED Act passed the Senate 97 to 1: FCC fines up to 10,000 USD per illegal robocall, longer statute of limitations, an interagency taskforce, and a push for carriers to deploy SHAKEN/STIR; FCC also proposed default carrier call-blocking and optional blocking of non-contacts.
- Russia: Astra Linux received a higher "special importance" clearance in April 2019 and the military plans to replace Windows; China reportedly plans a custom OS developed under a party-linked security group (via a Canadian magazine); Steve sees OS diversity as the real benefit and from-scratch development as risky.
- Sign in with Apple: required in apps that offer other third-party sign-in; option to hide the real email behind a randomly generated relay address; Steve notes OAuth gives the provider precise who-and-where tracking via first-party sessions.
- SQRL status: 1,275 active forum members, final explainer and protocol specification documents in progress, reference Windows client essentially finished; SQRL merchandise on Amazon.
- Nansh0u: more than 50,000 servers (healthcare, telecom, media, IT) compromised, with infections doubling from about 24,000 to about 48,000 in a month; scanned common MS-SQL ports, brute-forced credentials from large wordlists, ran scripted commands, used a 2014 privilege escalation vulnerability (CVE-2014-4113), and installed a TurtleCoin miner.
- A VMProtect-obfuscated kernel driver with a revoked VeriSign-issued signature protected the miner process; EPL-language tools and open attacker file servers suggested low-skill operators using nation-state-grade tooling.
- Steve's advice: never expose MS-SQL to the Internet and avoid guessable admin passwords.
