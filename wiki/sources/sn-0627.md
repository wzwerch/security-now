# Summary

Steve Gibson and Leo Laporte record with audio dropouts and apologize. Brian Krebs's three-week investigation suggests Marcus Hutchins sold malware as a teen and later left that life, matching the hosts' guesses. Steve examines whether leaked WikiLeaks material can be verified, covering DKIM signatures on email (valid only if the signing server was not compromised), and highlights a Princeton-led machine-checked proof of the HMAC-DRBG random generator in mbedTLS as a glimpse of verified software. The FDA recalled about 465,000 Abbott pacemakers with radio-link flaws that can be fixed by a firmware update at the doctor's office. Other items: WikiLeaks' AngelFire Windows boot-sector implant, an ISP-funded analysis showing 98.5 percent of unique FCC comments favor keeping Title II, TwoFactorAuth.org, the EFF's "stupid patent" on app permissions, and the .montblanc TLD being dropped. The main segment covers SharknAT&To: four serious problems in AT&T U-Verse gateways.

# Key points

- Krebs traced forum pseudonyms and domains tied to Hutchins's early years and concluded he had developed and sold malware in his mid-teens before changing course; Steve hopes juvenile conduct may count in his favor.
- DKIM: mail servers sign messages with a private key and publish the public key in DNS, so a signed email can be authenticated and shown unmodified (a Thunderbird add-on verifies it); this says nothing about WikiLeaks' non-email documents, and a compromised server could mean forged signatures.
- NIST DRBG background: the dual elliptic curve generator was suspect because its constants were unexplained; a team including Matthew Green proved the correctness and pseudorandomness of mbedTLS's HMAC-DRBG using the Coq proof assistant. Steve argues programs are math and software correctness proofs will become cheaper, perhaps with AI help.
- The FDA recalled 465,000 Abbott (St. Jude) pacemakers made before August 2017: authentication could be bypassed over RF, repeated wake-up commands could drain the battery, and data was sent unencrypted; the fix is a firmware update that carries a small risk of malfunction.
- WikiLeaks Vault 7 AngelFire: persistent Windows implant (boot sector loader Solartime, kernel driver Wolfcreek, in-memory user components, a hidden BadMFS file system, transient file system) for Windows XP, 7 and Server 2008 R2; Steve calls headlines claiming all Windows machines were infected clickbait.
- An ISP-funded Emprata analysis of 21.8 million FCC comments found 98.5 percent of unique, personalized comments opposed repeal of Title II rules.
- TwoFactorAuth.org (GitHub-backed) lists which sites support SMS, software TOTP and hardware tokens and helps users ask for support.
- EFF Stupid Patent of the Month: JPMorgan's US 9,747,468 on a first app asking user permission to read another app's data.
- The .montblanc top-level domain was removed, illustrating that vanity TLDs have ongoing costs.
- SharknAT&To (Nomotion write-up): (1) newest firmware on NVG589/NVG599 exposes SSH with hardcoded credentials and a restricted shell running as root with a command injection flaw, about 15,000 hosts found via Censys; (2) a "caserver" HTTPS service on port 49955 on the NVG599 with default tech credentials and command injection, able to accept firmware uploads; (3) an information disclosure service on port 61001 with hardcoded credentials that dumps configuration, WiFi credentials, logs and device MAC addresses given the modem serial number; (4) in all observed U-Verse gateways, a service on port 49152 that proxies unsolicited connections to LAN devices once a MAC address and port are guessed, bypassing the NAT firewall. Specific credentials are published in the raw transcript but omitted here.
- Advice: place your own NAT router behind the AT&T gateway (Steve suggests an EdgeRouter X); Leo notes U-Verse TV service may complicate this. Steve calls AT&T's negligence criminal and expects pressure or class action.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a heavily fortified ATM.
- Listener errata on Star Trek II (Saavik and Spock), XON/XOFF still used for terminal flow control, and what "getting a car smogged" means.
- SpinRite helped a listener recover old Eurythmics podcast files.
