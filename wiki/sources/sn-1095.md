# Summary

In an old-school breach, attackers abused a legacy Lenovo ID integration to reach nearly 5,000 Dropbox accounts in August: they registered Lenovo IDs with victims' email addresses, bypassed verification and pivoted into the linked Dropbox accounts. Microsoft will enable Memory Integrity (Hypervisor-Protected Code Integrity) by default on qualifying Windows 11 machines on 13 October. Firefox 155 adds an AI "Smart Window" that Steve finds useless. CISA, short-staffed since 2025's cuts, drops six on-site assessment services but keeps its automated Cyber Hygiene scans. OpenAI releases GPT-6 Astra, rated Critical for cyber; Steve debunks claims that its "recurrent depth" hides chain-of-thought reasoning.

NVIDIA is buying Hugging Face for $12.93 billion; Google ships Gemini 3.8 Flash and a Flash Cyber variant; Bitdefender shows the China-nexus group SilkParasite using AI to build seven RAT families and rotate indicators. Matthew Green argues AI will soon exhaust remotely exploitable bugs, so law enforcement will truly "go dark" and pressure for built-in backdoors will return; Steve links it to Utah's anti-VPN SB 73. The closing topic is an IEEE Spectrum essay by a nuclear and avionics control engineer: AI is removing the junior work through which expertise is built, so organizations should design deliberate "manual gates".

# Key points

- Dropbox/Lenovo ID: accounts without 2FA were hit between 4 and 21 August; Lenovo admitted a "legacy integration" could improperly authenticate Dropbox accounts.
- Memory Integrity/HVCI uses a hypervisor and second-level address translation to stop kernel shellcode, disabling driver signature checks and kernel patching. Old CPUs lacked per-mode execute bits (Intel MBEC since Kaby Lake, AMD GMET since Zen 2), causing slowdowns gamers disliked. Requirements: Intel 8th gen, Zen 2 or Snapdragon 8180+, 8 GB RAM, 64 GB SSD, virtualization enabled; registry or policy opt-outs are respected.
- Firefox 155's Smart Window uses Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, Qwen3 235B or (for Personal) gpt-oss-120b, later Mistral Small, or a user-provided endpoint.
- CISA ends Cyber Resilience Reviews, Ransomware Readiness Assessments and four other in-person programs; private industry is hiring former staff at high salaries.
- GPT-6 Astra: 100% on ExploitBench (Fable 5 78%, GPT-5.6 Sol 73.5%, GLM-5.3 54.4%), found two V8 zero-days during an internal benchmark, built a full browser sandbox escape and a root chain on a hardened OS. Training was paused two weeks after the Hugging Face incident; Astra refuses 91.5% of cyber jailbreaks (versus 59%); access starts with alpha testers, then Daybreak Blue.
- Recurrent depth (Universal Transformers, Google Brain/DeepMind 2018) loops some layers to get a deeper network without more weights; two or three passes is the useful limit. Since any reasoning must still be written out as tokens, Steve calls the "opaque recurrence" alarm by Buck Shlegeris and Zvi Mowshowitz hysteria.
- Hugging Face (3 million models, 18 million users) approached NVIDIA itself; valuation rose from $4.5 billion in 2023. Gemini 3.8 Flash costs $0.75/$3.75 per million tokens at launch; Flash Cyber goes to defenders via the Fairwind Program and slightly beats GPT-5.6 Sol and Mythos 5 on CyberGym.
- SilkParasite targets Central Asian governments with RATs in .NET, C++, Go and JavaScript, modular plugins and constantly rotated infrastructure; leftover test functions and two RATs built from one spec point to AI-assisted development. Steve expects espionage and extortion, not a "cyberpocalypse".
- Green ("Everything is about to go dark"): from 2010 phone encryption, through Comey's Going Dark campaign and the San Bernardino iPhone, agencies relied on exploits (GrayKey, Pegasus); with AI cleaning up code, demand for exceptional access will return and may weaken US systems most.
- IEEE essay: a Harvard study of 65 million workers found junior employment fell about 9% within six quarters after generative AI adoption while senior jobs grew. Air France 447 and FAA SAFO 17007 show automation eroding skills; the remedy is chosen manual work (for example juniors debugging with the AI switched off before comparing with the model).
