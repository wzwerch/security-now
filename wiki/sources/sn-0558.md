# Summary

The House unanimously passed the Email Privacy Act, which would require a probable-cause warrant for stored cloud communications but still has no user-notification requirement. Steve summarises the Snowden/Zakaria debate on lawful access to encrypted devices, where audience polling shifted modestly toward "yes", and sides with the view that lawful access is technically possible. Courts remain split on compelling fingerprints or passphrases. Android's monthly bulletin was renamed from Nexus to Android and again fixed critical Mediaserver (Stagefright-family) flaws. A Brazilian judge again ordered WhatsApp blocked and was again overruled. Steve is deeply sceptical of Craig Wright's claim to be Satoshi Nakamoto because he did not simply move coins signed with an early key. In errata Steve admits he should not have endorsed piping a curl download into bash for PiVPN without reading it first. The rest is a puzzle game recommendation and a listener Q&A covering SS7 and LTE, why Android attacks are rarer than expected, doubts about Signal inside WhatsApp, a Tor appliance, key hierarchies and verifying keys out of band.

# Key points

- Email Privacy Act: unanimous House vote, updates 1986 ECPA, warrant required for stored email regardless of age; EFF welcomes it but wants user notification.
- Snowden vs Zakaria debate on "lawful access to any encrypted message or device": poll moved from 13% yes to 22% yes; Steve found nothing new.
- Compelled unlocking: a court ordered a suspect's girlfriend to use a fingerprint; a Philadelphia ex-officer has been jailed for refusing to decrypt drives; fingerprints and passcodes are treated differently, Supreme Court guidance is missing.
- Android: April bulletin fixed critical remote-code-execution flaws in Mediaserver; Steve explains why media parsers with broad reach and privileges are a recurring weak spot.
- Brazil: judge ordered a 72-hour WhatsApp block again over unreadable end-to-end encrypted messages; overturned within a day.
- Satoshi claim: Craig Wright's "proof" was a sleight of hand with hashes rather than spending coins from an early block; Steve and Leo see a con; Gavin Andresen's endorsement was puzzling.
- Reader point on powering off workplace PCs: a bank employee argues shutdown clears keys and passwords from RAM when physical access is uncontrolled.
- Errata: curl-to-bash for PiVPN requires trusting the author; read the script first; one-time install is less risky than repeated fetches.
- Windows 7 update KB for the Get Windows 10 nag reappeared; a TV weather presenter's on-air Windows 10 prompt went viral.
- Q&A: TLS provides authentication so HTTPS over cellular is safe while plain voice/SMS is not; vulnerable Android devices have seen few mass attacks because attacks follow profit and mobile platforms are harder targets; trust in closed apps is unavoidable and building from source is best; a pocket Wi-Fi VPN/Tor appliance; encrypted-drive master keys can be wrapped under multiple keys (user and admin); key verification must happen out of band and key-change alerts should be on.
