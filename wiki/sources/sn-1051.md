# Summary

Steve starts with FFmpeg's new assembly-language lessons and the disputed claim of 10-50x speedups from hand-written assembly. He then walks through the State of Nevada's published after-action report on its August 2025 ransomware attack, which began with an employee downloading a spoofed admin tool in May and ended with a 28-day recovery without paying. Next come the $128 million Balancer DeFi theft through a rounding-error exploit, Chrome's new autofill of passport and driver's-licence data (which Steve links to coming age-verification demands), a UK carrier agreement to block spoofed UK numbers, and the planned removal of XSLT from Chromium, Firefox and WebKit. Shorter items cover Mozilla's paid Firefox support for organizations, Russia filtering Akamai, Google's Wiz acquisition clearing US review, the EU's draft change to let browsers send cookie preferences, further governments abandoning Microsoft Office, more countries probing Chinese buses, and Microsoft's Whisper Leak side channel that infers LLM chat topics from encrypted packet sizes and timing. After a SpinRite success story, a DNS Benchmark progress update and Steve's fix for spoofed GRC email using strict DMARC alignment, the main topic is Amazon's lawsuit against Perplexity over its Comet agentic browser. Steve calls it a grey area but expects user rights to prevail, much as sites eventually tolerate ad blockers.

# Key points

- FFmpeg published asm-lessons on GitHub (English, French, Spanish) and claims 10-50x gains from hand-written assembly; Steve thinks the comparison is against code that ignores vector instructions, though he recommends the lessons.
- Nevada: initial compromise 14 May 2025 via a spoofed admin-tool download that opened a backdoor surviving a June antivirus quarantine; attacker used remote-monitoring software, RDP lateral movement, took credentials from 26 accounts in the password vault, deleted backups and deployed ransomware at about 01:30 on Sunday 24 August. Response by pre-arranged vendors (Mandiant, Microsoft DART and others), full restoration in 28 days, about 90% of data recovered, no ransom, roughly 1.3 million dollars to partners plus about 259,000 dollars of staff overtime. Steve praises the playbooks and offline backups.
- ClickFix-style attacks (fake CAPTCHA tells users to paste a clipboard command into Run) prompt Steve's call for clipboard source tracking so browser-originated content triggers warnings.
- Balancer V2 ComposableStablePools: precision loss in upscaling at tiny balances (8-9 wei), amplified by 65 micro-swaps in one batchSwap, suppressed the pool token price and let an attacker drain about 128.6 million dollars across six chains in under 30 minutes (Check Point Research). Steve stays away from DeFi.
- Chrome enhanced autofill now stores passport, driver's licence and vehicle data; Steve fears it signals incoming ID demands and notes websites leak data. Leo keeps such data in Bitwarden.
- UK: the six largest mobile networks agreed to stop foreign call centres spoofing UK numbers within a year and to roll out call tracing and data sharing with police.
- XSLT: Chrome deprecates it with removal planned in Chrome 155 (17 November 2026); Firefox and WebKit support removal. Reasons: only XSLT 1.0 ever shipped, libxslt is aging C code with memory-safety bugs (CVE-2025-7425, CVE-2022-22834), and only about 0.02% of page loads use it.
- Mozilla opens early access to paid Firefox Support for Organizations (private tickets, response times, custom deployment) from January 2026.
- Russia is throttling Akamai traffic as part of pressing foreign cloud providers to register locally.
- Google's 32 billion dollar Wiz acquisition passed US approval, joining Mandiant.
- EU leaked draft GDPR/cookie changes: browsers would transmit a one-time cookie and tracking preference that every site must obey, ending cookie banners after a six-month grace period.
- ICC moved from Microsoft Office to openDesk; Austria's armed forces moved to LibreOffice and its economy ministry from Azure to Nextcloud.
- Norway's Chinese-bus discovery triggered inspections in Australia, Denmark, the UK and the Netherlands.
- Microsoft Whisper Leak: for streaming LLM replies, TLS packet sizes and timings let an observer flag conversations on a trained topic (100% precision in tests while catching 5-50%); OpenAI, Microsoft Azure, Mistral and xAI mitigated, notably by adding random padding in an "obfuscation" field.
- SpinRite listener story: a RAID 0 NAS drive recovered with SpinRite in about 24 hours; Steve notes data-recovery firms often run SpinRite first.
- GRC email fix: after a Gmail spam-folder glitch and spoofed mail using GRC's domain, Steve added strict alignment tags (adkim=s, aspf=s) to GRC's DMARC record; user-reported spam dropped to zero.
- Amazon v. Perplexity: Amazon says the Comet agent covertly accesses customer accounts and disguises automated activity as human browsing, ignoring cease-and-desist demands; Perplexity says an agent acting for a user has the user's permissions and accuses Amazon of protecting its advertising and product-placement revenue. Amazon argues third-party agents should identify themselves and respect each site's choice. Steve and Leo lean toward user choice but call the border soft (compare an AI browser extension).

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a half-installed emergency phone kiosk labelled "Emergency phone not installed. Please do not have an emergency at this location."
- GRC DNS Benchmark: development release 62; the commercial version adds IPv6, DNS over TLS, DNS over HTTPS, automatic sidelining of slow resolvers, five timing passes by default and adjustable run length; release expected in a week or two, followed by a mass mailing to about 150,000 subscribers.
- Banter on coffee, the weekly mailing list size (about 19,000 subscribers to the show-notes mailing) and Leo's French-language Gmail spam.
