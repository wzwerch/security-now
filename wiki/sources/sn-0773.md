# Summary

The shortening of web certificate lifetimes to 398 days, announced unilaterally by Apple for Safari, is now matched by Google and Mozilla for certificates issued from 1 September 2020. Apple also declared it will not implement 16 new W3C web APIs because they enlarge the browser fingerprinting surface, and at its developer conference announced DNS-over-HTTPS and DNS-over-TLS support in iOS 14 and macOS 11, though mainly via apps or profiles. Steve criticizes Mozilla's deal making Comcast a default Firefox DoH resolver for Comcast subscribers. Legislative news: a bill to halt federal facial recognition and biometric surveillance, the Lawful Access to Encrypted Data Act aimed at warrant-proof encryption, and Michigan moving to ban compulsory microchip implants. Steve reminds listeners of VirusTotal, which added Cynet, and shares miscellany on Edge on Windows 7, the Gravity newsreader fix, and pfSense hardware. The main segment follows up on Ripple20 with vendor lists and exposure numbers.

# Key points

- Certificates issued on or after 2020-09-01 with more than 398 days of validity will be rejected by Safari, Chrome (error ERR_CERT_VALIDITY_TOO_LONG, treated as mis-issued) and Mozilla; CAs had voted down a similar ballot, so Apple forced the issue. Steve expects CAs to sell blocks of time and stresses keeping a monitored contact email for CA notices.
- Apple's WebKit refuses APIs such as Web Bluetooth, MIDI, Magnetometer, Battery Status, Device Memory, Network Information, Ambient Light, Proximity, WebHID, Web Serial, WebUSB and User Idle Detection, citing fingerprinting. Fingerprinting has become the ad industry's main tracking method as cookies are blocked. Apple also trimmed fonts, user-agent detail and the Do Not Track flag.
- Apple's encrypted DNS in iOS 14 and macOS 11 is developer-centric: network extension apps, MDM profiles or per-app settings rather than a simple OS toggle.
- Comcast joins Mozilla's Trusted Recursive Resolver program and becomes the default Firefox DoH resolver on its network. Steve's concern is the "tyranny of the default" and Comcast's history (ad injection, lobbying against DoH); Comcast promises no tracking, no sale and deleting DNS queries every 24 hours. Users can switch resolvers.
- Facial Recognition and Biometric Technology Moratorium Act proposed; context includes a wrongful arrest in Detroit from a false match and a protested paper on predicting criminality from faces.
- Lawful Access to Encrypted Data Act (Graham, Cotton, Blackburn) would end "warrant-proof" encryption and create a prize for a lawful-access solution; separate from EARN IT.
- Michigan's house passed a bill banning compulsory microchip implants; seven states already have similar laws.
- VirusTotal adds Cynet; Steve uses it to vet an abandonware download.
- Edge on Windows 7 arrived as an unselected "important" update; it imported Chrome settings and extensions.
- Ripple20 follow-up: Treck code is licensed, rebranded and embedded under other names (for example KASAGO, Digi NET+OS), so many OEMs may not know. Forescout counted tens of thousands of matching devices, with healthcare the most exposed (infusion pumps first, then printers, UPS, networking gear); about 15,000 identifiable devices were reachable from the internet via Shodan. Affected vendors listed include Aruba/HP, Baxter, B. Braun, Cisco, Dell, Digi, Eaton, HP, Samsung, Intel AMT, Rockwell, Schneider Electric, Xerox. Advice: update firmware on printers, routers, cameras and keep devices off the internet.
