# Summary

With Jason Howell hosting, Steve follows up on the new Apache Struts flaw (scanning began three days after disclosure, then coin miners), explaining that it needs the Convention plugin or a configuration change to be exploitable. SandboxEscaper's Windows zero day turned out to be a missing permission check in the Task Scheduler RPC, was extended to Windows 7 by a small tweak, and received a third-party micropatch from 0patch before Microsoft's fix. Android versions before Pie broadcast WiFi network details and the real MAC address to any app, enabling location inference. Instagram moved to authenticator-app 2FA after SMS-protected accounts were hijacked, OpenSSH developers explained why they consider username oracles low severity, roughly 1,100 US news sites blocked EU visitors because of GDPR, and Misfortune Cookie re-emerged in a hospital device gateway. Other items cover Magento stores infected with a card skimmer, a design flaw letting the TPM 2.0 boot chain be subverted via sleep states, strain on the MITRE CVE program, and Firefox's plans to block slow and cross-site trackers by default. The headline research, SonarSnoop, uses a phone's speakers and microphones as sonar to infer unlock-pattern strokes.

# Key points

- Struts CVE flaw: vulnerable versions 2.3-2.3.34 and 2.5-2.5.16 under certain plugin/config conditions; Palo Alto Unit 42 detailed the Convention plugin condition; GreyNoise and Volexity saw scanning on August 25; the disclosure site had an elaborate logo and tutorial.
- SandboxEscaper's Task Scheduler (SchRpcSetSecurity) permission bug lets any local user set file permissions and then hijack the print spooler; Acros's 0patch published a micropatch; Steve dislikes responsible-disclosure bypass.
- Android before Pie: apps receive WiFi state broadcasts without permission (SSID, BSSID, local IP, DNS, real MAC), usable with databases like WiGLE to geolocate; Google will not backport.
- Instagram: moved to TOTP authenticators, offers verified accounts and account-information views (creation date, country, ads, former usernames). Steve expects OS-integrated authenticators eventually.
- OpenSSH: a second similar oracle found by Qualys; maintainers say fixes add attack surface (about 150 lines pre-auth) and these bugs do not warrant CVEs or secrecy.
- GDPR: about 1,149 US news sites (including large papers) block EU visitors rather than comply; third-party cookies fell about 22 percent.
- Misfortune Cookie (RomPager older than 4.07) found in the Capsule DataCaptor Terminal Server hospital gateway; vendor had not paid for updated firmware; web server only needed at setup.
- Magento: Willem de Groot found a magentocore skimmer on over 7,000 storefronts, spread via brute-forced admin panels, with a cron-based recovery backdoor; advice is to take control panels offline.
- TPM 2.0 (Korean NSRI): ACPI sleep-state handling (S3) allows boot-time measurement values to be intercepted and replaced; mitigations are TPM firmware updates or disabling S3.
- MITRE CVE funding fell from 6.7 million (2012) to 1.7 million (2015) while CVEs doubled; Senate proposed a stable DHS line item.
- Firefox: blocking trackers that load slowly (by default in Firefox 63), stripping cross-site tracker cookies (Firefox 65), and blocking fingerprinting and crypto mining; Ghostery found trackers account for over half of load time.
- SonarSnoop (USENIX): ultrasonic pings from a Galaxy S4's speakers and echoes into its microphones classified finger strokes, cutting candidate unlock patterns by up to 70 percent.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a Windows Error Reporting dialog saying Windows Error Reporting stopped working.
- Listener SpinRite story: pink-screen reboots on a gaming PC fixed after SpinRite Level 2 on a cloned SSD, plus Steve's RAID story.
- Willem de Groot and Jason's Project Soli and FingerIO comparison; Windows 10 non-security update from Microsoft.
