# Summary

Jason Howell sits in for Leo Laporte. Chrome 87 fixed 33 vulnerabilities (including the NAT Slipstream issue by blocking SIP ports), is disabling FTP for half of users ahead of removal in 88, adds page occlusion tracking and a back-forward cache on Android, and announces a privacy-practices disclosure for extensions from January 2021. Firefox 83 introduces an optional HTTPS-Only Mode and fixes the FreeType bug; Mozilla opened a public consultation on a worldwide DNS-over-HTTPS rollout. The Egregor ransomware prints its ransom note on every network printer. Wordfence saw 7.5 million attacks on 1.5 million sites probing WordPress Epsilon Framework themes, and Akamai's honeypot revealed attackers who hijack WordPress sites to host thousands of hidden fake online stores for search-ranking abuse. Steve laments how many serious flaws remain unpatched on the internet: 245,000 BlueKeep hosts, 103,000 SMBGhost hosts and long lists of others. Google began rolling out end-to-end encrypted RCS in Android Messages using the Signal protocol. The main topic is Cicada (APT10, Stone Panda), a Chinese state-linked espionage group running an 11-year, still active campaign against Japan-linked firms.

# Key points

- Chrome 87: 10 high-severity fixes among 33, bounties varied (up to 15,000 dollars for a WebCodecs use-after-free); outgoing connections to SIP ports 5060 and 5061 blocked (response to NAT Slipstreaming); FTP disabled for 50% of users; bfcache arrives on Android.
- Chrome Web Store will show an extension Privacy Practices section from January 18, 2021, paralleling Apple's App Store privacy labels due December 8.
- Firefox 83: HTTPS-Only Mode (about:preferences privacy), localhost treated as exempt; SpiderMonkey speed and memory gains; Apple Silicon support through Rosetta 2. Mozilla consultation on global DoH runs to January 4, 2021, with a canary domain for enterprise opt-out and multiple resolver choices.
- Egregor: after encrypting, it repeatedly prints the ransom demand on all reachable printers to make cover-ups impossible.
- Wordfence: attack wave against Epsilon Framework theme function-injection flaws, from over 18,000 IPs, with a confirmed full exploit chain to site takeover.
- Akamai researcher Larry Cashdollar's WordPress honeypot was brute-forced, then proxied to a command server that served over 7,000 fake ecommerce stores and fabricated sitemaps to exploit the site's search reputation.
- Unpatched exposure counts cited: BlueKeep about 246,000 hosts (port 3389 open on about 6% of IPs), SMBGhost about 103,000, Apache, Squid, IIS (374,000), Exim, Heartbleed (about 205,000) and others. Steve warns of cross-network contagion through managed service providers.
- Android Messages: RCS rollout complete; end-to-end encryption using the Signal protocol in beta, upgrading eligible conversations automatically; Steve sees it as a major step for strong encryption and a challenge to lawful-access demands.
- Cicada (APT10/Stone Panda/Cloud Hopper): active since at least 2009; this campaign ran from October 2019 through October 2020 against Japanese-linked firms in 17 regions (automotive, pharma, engineering, MSPs). Tactics: DLL side-loading, living-off-the-land tools (certutil, AdFind, ntdsutil, WMI), custom Backdoor.Hartip, Zerologon for lateral movement, RAR staging and exfiltration to cloud hosting. Symantec noticed it through suspicious DLL side-loading. Steve contrasts such quiet espionage with noisy ransomware.
