# Summary

The episode begins with a ProPublica investigation into how Microsoft's 2021 "White House Offer" of free security upgrades, made after the SolarWinds era attacks, was designed to hook federal agencies (including all military services) on paid Microsoft security products and Azure, displacing competitors; Steve sees it as a classic Gates-style move and notes procurement-law concerns. Google is reportedly preparing "Shielded Email" aliases for Gmail, similar to Apple Hide My Email and Firefox Relay. Russia plans to extend its ban on foreign hosting providers and to test its national Internet disconnect switch again in December. A Patch Tuesday zero-day (CVE-2024-43451) used by Russian actors against Ukrainians let a malicious .URL file leak NTLM hashes on right-click, delete or move, which Steve ties to old design choices in SMB, NTLM and the Windows metafile format; 0patch continues to micropatch such NTLM problems and has a free tier. Listener mail covers the value of old episodes, VeraCrypt as the TrueCrypt successor, Cloudflare Tunnel and Tailscale for remote home access without open ports, routing a spoofed command-and-control IP for malware analysis, how to hand over an unwieldy password, and searching the GRC transcripts. The main topic is Artificial General Intelligence: Steve reviews definitions (narrow AI, AGI, ASI, Google DeepMind levels), Sam Altman's claim of AGI in 2025 against expert medians around 2050-2060, and the missing ingredients (common-sense reasoning, context awareness, handling uncertainty, continual learning). His conclusion is that large language models may be a very useful dead end rather than the road to AGI, because nobody understands how they produce their answers.

# Key points

- **Microsoft "White House Offer"** (ProPublica): about 150 million USD of consulting and free licenses for a limited time led agencies, including the Department of Defense, to adopt Microsoft security tools and then pay once the trial ended; salespeople reportedly told agencies competitor products were redundant. Microsoft says it complied with all rules and invited antitrust review. Steve's concern: monoculture and vendor lock-in.
- **Gmail Shielded Email**: reported upcoming alias feature for two billion Gmail users, comparable to Apple Hide My Email and Firefox Relay, allowing per-site addresses that can be deleted if spammed.
- **Russia**: plan to ban more foreign hosting providers (Akamai and CDN77 named) over content about its army; Roskomnadzor will test the national Internet disconnect switch in December; Starlink is sanctioned there.
- **CVE-2024-43451 (NTLM hash leak)**: patched in November 2024 Patch Tuesday after ClearSky found it used against Ukrainian organizations via phishing sent from a compromised government mail server; a .URL file with a file:// target made Windows Explorer contact an attacker's SMB server on right-click, delete or move, exposing NTLM credential hashes (usable for pass-the-hash) and leading to SparkRAT delivery. Steve compares it to the legacy Windows metafile escape flaw and notes Microsoft has stopped investing in NTLM fixes while 0patch still issues micropatches (free tier exists).
- **Value of old episodes**: a new listener from Uganda found the 2005-era fundamentals episodes valuable; Steve points out all episodes remain available.
- **TrueCrypt successor**: VeraCrypt is maintained and cross-platform (works on Linux).
- **Cloudflare Tunnel**: listener describes an outbound-only tunnel from a small home server to Cloudflare, with Cloudflare Warp on the laptop and email PIN authentication for family, all on the free tier and with no router ports opened. Another listener uses Tailscale with an exit node; overlay options include ZeroTier, Nebula and Netmaker.
- **Spoofing a public C2 address in a malware lab**: Steve recommends a router with an extra physical interface (for example Netgate SG-1100 or a Protectli appliance running pfSense/OPNsense) and defining a small network that contains the target IP, rather than patching samples or ARP poisoning.
- **Typing an impossible password**: do not share a long random password with a travel agent; temporarily set a simple one and change it afterward. Steve ordered a Bluetooth-to-USB keyboard dongle to evaluate for next week.
- **Searching transcripts**: GRC's site search indexes show notes and Elaine's transcripts, which solves "which episode did we mention X" questions.
- **AGI definitions**: Wikipedia and DeepMind terminology (narrow AI, AGI, ASI, five levels from emerging to superhuman; current LLMs rated as emerging AGI).
- **Altman versus experts**: Altman predicts AGI in 2025 and says current hardware suffices; a 2022 survey of 352 AI experts gave a median of about 2060; experts cited in InformationWeek say 2025 is unrealistic because nobody understands how current models reach conclusions.
- **Steve's use of ChatGPT**: acts as a programming-language super search engine for throwaway code in unfamiliar languages (for example Visual Basic .NET), not as a finished-product generator.
- **Steve's view**: current LLMs lack understanding and never ask clarifying questions; the human brain has about 10^11 neurons with about 20,000 connections each; "more of the same" may not yield AGI. Leo adds that there is no agreed test for AGI and that useful systems need not be general.
