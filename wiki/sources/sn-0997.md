# Summary

Steve debunks headlines that Chinese researchers "broke RSA" with a D-Wave quantum annealer: they only factored a 22-bit number, nowhere near the sizes in use, and post-quantum algorithms (already used alongside classical ones in Signal) are being deployed anyway. He describes a sextortion-style scam PDF that cited a victim's real name, phone number and home address, which he attributes to leaked data such as the National Public Data breach. A large segment covers the European Union's replacement of its Product Liability Directive, which adds software (including SaaS and AI) under no-fault liability, bans contractual liability exclusions and treats missing update mechanisms as defects; open-source software outside commercial activity is exempt and the rules apply in about 24 months. Shorter items cover Microsoft losing weeks of cloud security logs, DJI suing the Pentagon over its Chinese-military listing, the Special Operations Command wish list for undetectable deepfake online personas, and Microsoft's phishing honeypot decoys. The main topic is the FIDO Alliance's Credential Exchange Protocol (CXP), announced 14 October 2024, a Diffie-Hellman-based design for securely moving passkeys and other secrets between providers, plus the Passkey Central adoption site.

# Key points

- RSA claim: researchers at Shanghai University using a D-Wave Advantage factored a 22-bit integer; real RSA moduli are 2048 bits or more, so nothing is broken; Steve blames misleading headlines and notes post-quantum migration is underway (Signal combines old and new algorithms).
- Extortion PDF: a scam email attachment contained the recipient's full name, phone number and street address (even a street view image in Leo's case) and demanded roughly $2,000 in bitcoin; Steve fears first-time recipients will be terrified and urges public awareness; Leo says he receives it daily and suspects pig-butchering style scammers.
- EU Directive on Liability for Defective Products: no-fault liability for software, firmware, apps and AI systems regardless of delivery model; vendors liable for security flaws that cause damage including loss of data or functionality; absence of an update mechanism counts as a defect; planned obsolescence and misleading durability claims covered; contractual exclusions and national liability caps not permitted; damaged consumers must prove defect but can compel evidence disclosure; applies after 24 months (autumn 2026).
- Steve links the directive to the case for memory-safe languages such as Rust over C or assembly, while noting careful assembly code can be very reliable (shuttle and Voyager computers).
- Microsoft lost security logs for some cloud customers for weeks from 2 September 2024 due to a bug in an internal monitoring agent, detected 5 September but only mitigated from 19 September.
- DJI sued the Defense Department over its listing as a Chinese military company, citing lost business and customs holds under the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act; the House voted to bar new DJI drones and Commerce is weighing restrictions. Steve suggests a US-built control board with audited firmware and US cloud as a possible compromise.
- The Intercept reported US Special Operations Command seeking technology for realistic synthetic social-media personas with ID-quality photos, video and audio that evade detection (earlier Pentagon fake-account networks were removed by Meta and Twitter); Steve links it to his own DigiCert liveness check.
- Microsoft runs bots that feed phishing sites fake credentials leading to honeypot Azure tenants; threat actors log in about 5% of the time and take around 20 days to notice, while Microsoft studies their techniques.
- BIMI follow-up: a listener fears logos give false security; Steve agrees it is only an opportunistic identity signal, while Google shows a blue checkmark and hover text for BIMI-verified senders such as Mastercard.
- CXP: FIDO's Credential Provider SIG (1Password, Bitwarden, Dashlane, NordPass, Enpass, Okta, Apple, Google, Microsoft, Samsung and others) published draft specs to replace insecure CSV exports; the importing provider creates an export request with a challenge, accepted encryption schemes and a Diffie-Hellman public key; the exporter generates its own DH pair, derives a shared migration key, encrypts the credential payload and signs the challenge; the importer reconstructs the key and decrypts. Works online or offline (for example via a USB drive). Missing from the draft: how the sender's signing key is obtained, which matters to prevent man-in-the-middle attacks, and the user-experience details.
- Passkey Central (PasskeyCentral.org) and Passkeys.dev offer adoption guidance and code samples; Steve says passkeys have arrived, and once Apple participates the main lock-in objection disappears; he wants passkeys on GRC's forums and misses SQRL's recovery mechanism.
