# Summary

The exploit market is in a bidding war: Zerodium raised its price for VMware ESXi and Hyper-V guest-to-host escapes to $500,000, outbidding Microsoft's own $250,000 Hyper-V bounty. The NSA formally released Ghidra, a free reverse-engineering framework positioned against the costly IDA Pro, partly as a recruiting tool. Firefox 67 will add an opt-in "letterboxing" anti-fingerprinting feature from the Tor Browser. Google disclosed a Chrome FileReader use-after-free (CVE-2019-5786) used in the wild together with an unpatched Windows 7 32-bit win32k.sys sandbox escape. Citrix confirmed an intrusion that the FBI flagged; a security firm attributed it to an Iranian group using password spraying and claimed several terabytes were taken. Researchers showed that popular aftermarket car-alarm apps allowed account takeover, location tracking and even engine cut-off. Marriott's CEO detailed the Starwood breach in Senate testimony, and the episode ends with SPOILER, new research showing an Intel load-store speculation leak that makes Rowhammer and cache attacks far faster, even from JavaScript.

# Key points

- Zerodium "acquisition raid": up to $500,000 for ESXi/Hyper-V escapes, citing customer demand from government and law-enforcement buyers; Microsoft pays up to $250,000 for Hyper-V bugs; VMware paid nothing at the time. Steve says he would sell to the vendor, and contrasts brokers with HackerOne's middleman role.
- Ghidra v9 (NSA, released at RSA 2019): disassembly, decompilation, scripting in Java/Python, many processor architectures including 6502; runs on JDK 11; no installer; full open-source release pending on GitHub; Steve expects it to take share from Hex-Rays IDA Pro.
- Firefox 67 (May 2019): letterboxing rounds the viewport to multiples of 200 by 100 pixels to hide window size, off by default; enable via `privacy.resistFingerprinting` in about:config. Leo notes Mozilla's Firefox Send encrypted file sharing (1 GB anonymous, 2.5 GB signed in, expiring links).
- Chrome CVE-2019-5786: use-after-free in FileReader, found by Google's Threat Analysis Group's Clement Lecigne, exploited in the wild; paired with a Windows 7 32-bit win32k.sys null-pointer-dereference privilege escalation that Microsoft was still fixing when Google disclosed it.
- Citrix: CSIO blog said the FBI alerted the company on March 6 that attackers may have taken business documents via password spraying; NBC and Resecurity reported Iranian-linked Iridium had been inside for years and took 6 to 10 TB, with Resecurity claiming it warned Citrix in December 2018.
- Pen Test Partners on Pandora and Viper (Clifford) car alarms: insecure direct object references in the API (over 3 million app downloads) allowed taking over accounts, locating vehicles, disabling alarms, unlocking, stopping engines, enabling an in-car microphone and sending CAN messages; both vendors patched within days of notice, but no full audit was done.
- Microsoft recruits Windows 10 Insiders for short phone calls with engineers (feature example Alt+Tab).
- Windows 10 will automatically uninstall updates that cause boot failure and block them for 30 days.
- Marriott/Starwood: an IBM Guardium alert on September 7, 2018 for a hand-run row-count query led to forensics that found a remote access trojan, Mimikatz and access since July 2014; two encrypted exports were identified on November 19; final figures cited were 383 million guest records, 18.5 million encrypted and 5.25 million unencrypted passport numbers, 9.1 million encrypted card numbers.
- SPOILER (Worcester Polytechnic Institute and University of Luebeck): leaks physical-address information through Intel's speculative load/store dependency (memory order buffer) logic, speeding Rowhammer and Prime+Probe preparation by large factors and working from JavaScript; affects every Intel Core generation, not ARM or AMD, and microcode cannot cheaply fix it; Intel was told in December 2018.
