# Summary

Steve argues that Google's AI Overview is fundamentally impossible today because summarising the web accurately requires comprehension that language models lack; viral examples included advice to add glue to pizza sauce, traced to an old joke post on Reddit, and false answers about elephants and presidents. Workarounds include the `udm=14` "Web" mode, the TenBlueLinks.org site and a listener's Chrome extension. LastPass announces it will finally encrypt the URLs stored in vaults, which Steve calls a decade late. Listener feedback covers why enterprise VPNs use split tunnelling and cannot always block non-tunnel traffic, replaceability of soldered eMMC chips, native vertical tabs coming to Firefox, glowing reviews of AVM's FRITZ!Box routers, a store chain with about 7,000 of 10,000 possible alarm PINs enrolled, and whether the Windows XP exposure test was fair. Steve announces that GRC's new email subscription system is live and that listeners can email securitynow@grc.com after registering their address at grc.com/mail. The main topic is Microsoft's Recall feature for Copilot+ PCs, which screenshots the screen every few seconds and stores about 90 days of searchable history locally; Steve calls it a 50 GB privacy bomb that is a gift to attackers, investigators and anyone with account access, though he allows it may prove useful.

# Key points

- Google AI Overview gave nonsensical answers (non-toxic glue in pizza sauce, elephants with two feet); Google called them uncommon queries and later scaled the feature back. Steve says keyword ranking is a solved problem while truth extraction is not.
- Suppressing AI Overview and clutter: use the `udm=14` Web mode, TenBlueLinks.org to add a default "Google Web" search in Firefox or Chrome, or avoid signing in.
- LastPass announced encryption of vault URLs to align with its zero-knowledge claims; Steve notes other managers always did this, and that vaults could have been encrypted at rest long ago. Its parent had been bought by a private-equity firm in 2019.
- GRC email: three lists (product owners, GRC news, Security Now); incoming mail to securitynow@grc.com is accepted only from addresses registered at grc.com/mail; plus-sign addresses were not yet supported; bulk sending would begin cautiously because of spam rules.
- VPN follow-up: enterprise VPNs often split-tunnel to save bandwidth (for example Teams or Slack traffic), so a blanket firewall block of non-tunnel traffic is not always feasible, while a roaming consumer VPN should enforce full tunnelling.
- Replacing BGA or soldered chips is technically possible but not practical for most people, despite YouTube demonstrations.
- FRITZ!Box (AVM) praised by a listener: updates on by default, randomised credentials, button or telephone/authenticator confirmation of sensitive changes, DNS over TLS, long support. Leo could not find a US retailer.
- Alarm-system anecdote: a grocery chain loaded nearly 7,000 four-digit PINs into each store's alarm, giving a random guess a 70% chance of success; the installer refused and the list was cut.
- The Windows XP exposure test is defended as a test of reality; NAT routers and modern default firewalls are what protect us now.
- Recall details: needs a Copilot+ PC with an NPU of at least 40 TOPS (Snapdragon X, later Intel and AMD); snapshots every few seconds stored encrypted on disk; minimum 256 GB drive with 50 GB free, default allocation 25 GB (about three months); user-profile-scoped; skips InPrivate browsing and DRM content but does not hide passwords or account numbers; data lives in a SQLite3 database that a researcher already inspected.
- Steve's concerns: a new, previously nonexistent store of everything seen on screen (including chats from privacy-focused apps like Signal) attractive to attackers, espionage and law enforcement; he sees no pressing need for it and expects the audience will not be early adopters.
