# Summary

The episode opens with the release of Anthropic's Fable model and a safety gate that diverted cybersecurity-flavoured prompts to an older model. The first news item is Weil, Gotshal & Manges, a large law firm that paid a $20 million ransom to the Silent Ransom Group to stop the leak of client data stolen from an external cloud store. Steve argues that falling ransom-payment rates push extortionists toward victims such as law firms, who cannot shrug off a breach. Next is yet another actively exploited Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN zero-day (earlier ones were CVE-2026-20182, CVE-2026-20133 and CVE-2026-20127), with CISA counting about 90 Cisco bugs exploited in the wild.

Steve then uses a CVSS 9.8 remote-code-execution flaw in the Everest Forms Pro WordPress plugin, reported by Wordfence, to explain why PHP is hard to write securely: user input was concatenated into a string passed to `eval()`. McAfee's WeedHack report describes a $5/month malware-as-a-service sold via fake Minecraft mods, with 116,000+ infections and teenagers using it to harass peers. University of Toronto and Vector Institute researchers built a lab-only adaptive AI worm that runs on an open-weight local model and so ignores vendor guardrails. Steve complains about an unsolicited Chrome "shop with confidence" notification. The HTTP/2 Bomb, found by OpenAI Codex and published by Calif, can exhaust server memory on NGINX, Apache, IIS, Envoy and Pingora; Steve condemns the uncoordinated disclosure. The main topic is Anthropic's analysis of 832 banned accounts (March 2025 to March 2026) against MITRE ATT&CK, showing medium/high-risk actors rising from 33% to 56% and the agentic GTG-1002 campaign as the real warning sign.

# Key points

- Weil, Gotshal & Manges (about $2B annual revenue) paid a $20M ransom (about 1% of revenue) to the Silent Ransom Group; the FBI had warned that group was targeting US law firms.
- Steve's takeaway: as most companies now refuse to pay, attackers target victims whose stolen data would cause fiduciary lawsuits and reputational harm, so payment is a rational gamble for them.
- Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN has had a run of exploited zero-days in 2026; CISA has flagged about 90 Cisco CVEs as exploited. Cisco said it was stress-testing its products with Claude Mythos Preview; Steve hopes AI finally improves its code quality.
- Everest Forms Pro (about 4,000 installs) had an unauthenticated RCE (CVSS 9.8): patched 18 March 2026, exploited from 13 April; Wordfence blocked 29,300+ attempts. Fixed in version 1.9.13.
- Root cause: `sanitize_text_field()` does not escape single quotes, so form values placed in an `eval()` string allowed code injection, for example creating a rogue admin account.
- Steve keeps PHP applications (XenForo forums, NuevoMailer) on a network segment isolated from the rest of GRC and recommends Wordfence for WordPress sites with plugins.
- WeedHack (McAfee Labs): MaaS spread via fake YouTube videos and SEO-poisoned mod sites; a free tier steals browser data, Discord/Steam/Telegram credentials and crypto wallets; a $5/month premium tier adds webcam, screen control, keylogging and a reverse shell. The command server is located via the Ethereum blockchain.
- McAfee advice: do not engage with people claiming to have hacked you, tell a trusted adult, and contact law enforcement.
- "AI Agents Enable Adaptive Computer Worms": a prototype worm driven by a local open-weight LLM propagated across Linux, Windows and IoT hosts in an isolated lab; it cannot be stopped by patching one flaw and bypasses cloud safety controls. Steve thinks criminals prefer extortion over worms, so state use is the likelier risk.
- Chrome showed a price-tracking notification even though Steve uses Firefox; he hopes Mozilla keeps Firefox alive.
- HTTP/2 Bomb: a compression bomb on HPACK plus a Slowloris-style zero-window hold; one client can pin about 32GB of RAM in roughly 20 seconds; 880,000+ sites on Shodan affected, many behind CDNs.
- Calif published a working exploit six days after filing an advisory (27 May to 2 June), arguing the 90-day model is outdated in the AI era; Envoy maintainers objected, noting the lack of coordination via VINCE. NGINX patched, Apache fix only a standalone module, IIS unpatched without a CVE.
- Steve suspects Stripe's repeated sales emails are AI-automated, and notes outsourcing of customer outreach (for example to Salesforce) makes business contact more homogeneous and annoying.
- Anthropic red-team report: 832 banned accounts, 13,873 observed actions, 482 techniques across 14 ATT&CK tactics (v18), scored with the AI Risk Enablement Score (ARiES).
- Medium/high-risk actors grew from 33% to 56% between the first and second half of the year; lateral movement was the strongest marker of high risk (average score 56.4 vs 46.8).
- Most common use: malware development (560 of 832 actors), obfuscation (64.7%), data from local system (55.9%), impairing defenses (about 55%); defence evasion appears in 84.4% of actors. Real-time post-compromise use was rarer (lateral movement 6.5%).
- GTG-1002 (the November 2025 espionage campaign) scored 100 using a scaffold in which Claude Code on Kali drove open-source tools via MCP servers autonomously, with a human approving only final exfiltration. Anthropic says ATT&CK has no IDs yet for autonomous orchestration.
- Steve predicts attackers will move to unrestricted local models, removing providers' ability to observe misuse, and that agentic scaffolds will be sold to less skilled actors.

# Other segments

- Picture of the Week: a "Dip Ahead" sign followed by a roadworks board reading "Bring Chips".
- Steve is working on one-click payment options (PayPal, Google Pay, Apple Pay, Venmo) for GRC's store ahead of new low-cost products and SpinRite for Windows.
