# Summary

Leo returns. Steve starts with Citizen Lab's report on three 2022 NSO Group zero-click chains against iOS 15 and 16 (LatentImage, FindMyPwn, PwnYourHome) and the observation that Apple's Lockdown Mode produced warnings and blocked successful use. The UK NCSC warns that commercial hacking tools and hacker-for-hire groups are expanding, with at least 80 countries having bought spyware. Check Point sees a growing black market for stolen ChatGPT Premium accounts. ESET bought 18 used routers and found sensitive configuration data on over half of them, and APT28 is exploiting an unpatched 2017 Cisco SNMP flaw with the Jaguar Tooth malware. A new Security Research Legal Defense Fund is announced. After brief updates (Firefox cookie bug, Kubernetes audit, a Chrome zero-day used by a surveillance vendor), Steve reviews two opposing open letters on the UK Online Safety Bill and proposes monitoring only minors via an immutable device-level age cutoff. Leo points out that the law-enforcement concern is mainly adults sharing CSAM, which Steve had not realized, so the proposal does not address the actual dispute.

# Key points

- Citizen Lab: NSO's 2022 chains LatentImage, FindMyPwn (Find My plus iMessage) and PwnYourHome (HomeKit plus iMessage). Lockdown Mode briefly gave real-time warnings and no successful PwnYourHome infection was seen on a Lockdown device; Apple fixed HomeKit issues in iOS 16.3.1.
- UK NCSC report: commercial intrusion tools rival some state APTs, at least 80 countries purchased spyware in a decade, thousands are targeted yearly, hackers-for-hire and malware-as-a-service expand reach; oversight will lack international consensus.
- Check Point: trade in stolen ChatGPT Premium accounts, brute-force and checker tools, partly to bypass geofencing; stolen accounts expose previous queries. Germany considered a ChatGPT ban after Italy.
- ESET white paper "How I (could've) stolen your corporate secrets for $100": 56% of 18 used routers still held configurations; 22% had customer data, 44% credentials for other networks, 100% had VPN credentials or hashed root passwords and identified the owner. Guidance: NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 media sanitization. Initial network access sells for about $2,800.
- Jaguar Tooth (NCSC): APT28 backdoor on old Cisco IOS routers using CVE-2017-6742 (SNMP stack overflow via return-oriented programming), which exfiltrates router data over TFTP. Steve's lesson: SNMP should never face the internet; policy failures deserve no forgiveness.
- Security Research Legal Defense Fund, backed by Google and bug bounty firms (HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, Luta Security), will fund counsel for good-faith researchers.
- Firefox 112.0.1 fixed a cookie expiry bug; NCC Group's Kubernetes audit found nothing significant; Chrome fixed a zero-day (CVE-2023-2136) exploited by a surveillance vendor. Microsoft defines "zero-day" more loosely than Google.
- Open letters: CEOs of Signal, WhatsApp, Threema, Element, Session, Viber and Wire warn the UK Online Safety Bill could force scanning of private messages and break end-to-end encryption; the Virtual Global Taskforce (law enforcement) urges Meta to keep child-safety detection if it adds end-to-end encryption, citing 29.3 million NCMEC reports in 2021.
- Steve's proposal: apply mandated monitoring only to minors using a device-bound age cutoff, leaving adults unmonitored. Leo notes the law enforcement focus is adults trading CSAM and that backdoors leak; escrow is another long-discussed idea.
