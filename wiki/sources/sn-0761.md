# Summary

The episode introduces a new "Browser News" section. Mozilla patched two use-after-free zero-days in Firefox 74.0.1 that were used in targeted attacks, Chrome shipped security fixes, and Ryan Pickren's seven-bug Safari chain that bypassed the same-origin policy to reach cameras, microphone and saved passwords earned a bounty from Apple. Chrome, Edge and Firefox all postponed the removal of TLS 1.0/1.1, and Chrome paused its SameSite cookie enforcement because of COVID-19. Edge is getting vertical tabs and became the second most used browser. The FCC mandated STIR/SHAKEN caller authentication by mid-2021, Cloudflare launched 1.1.1.2/1.1.1.3 filtering resolvers (with an initial miscategorization), and CDNs and cloud providers joined the MANRS routing-security initiative. The main topic is Zoom: explosive growth, Zoom bombing, a Windows chat link credential leak, weak or misrepresented encryption, Chinese key servers, privacy issues, and Jitsi Meet as a self-hosted alternative.

# Key points

- Firefox zero-days (CVE-2020-6819 and CVE-2020-6820) were use-after-free flaws in the docshell destructor and ReadableStream; exploited in the wild against unpatched versions before 74.0.1; reported by JMP Security researchers.
- Chrome 80 update fixed eight security bugs, none known to be exploited.
- Safari: a chain of seven flaws abusing same-origin handling (including how password autofill identifies origins) could expose camera, microphone, location and passwords from a visited site; fixed across Safari 13.0.5 to 13.1; Apple paid a $75,000 bounty.
- TLS 1.0/1.1 removal delayed: Chrome to 83 (late May), new Edge no sooner than 84, legacy Edge and IE11 disabled by default on 8 September 2020. SSL Labs shows over 97 percent of surveyed sites support TLS 1.2 or 1.3; 2018 telemetry had 0.36 to 1.2 percent of connections on old TLS depending on browser.
- Chrome temporarily rolled back its SameSite cookie enforcement (cookies without a declared policy being rejected in third-party contexts), aiming to resume in summer.
- Edge: vertical tabs and a Smart Copy feature are coming; Edge passed Firefox to become the number two browser (about 7.6 percent versus Chrome around 68 percent per the cited statistics).
- STIR/SHAKEN: the FCC unanimously required US voice providers to implement it by 30 June 2021; it only authenticates calls entirely within US IP networks, so foreign spoofing remains.
- Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 for Families: 1.1.1.2 blocks malware, 1.1.1.3 blocks malware plus adult content; an early mistake blocked LGBTQ+ sites because of a broader data-feed category and was fixed.
- MANRS (Mutually Agreed Norms for Routing Security) opened a program for CDNs and cloud providers, listing five mandatory actions such as preventing incorrect route propagation and spoofed source addresses; Steve explains BGP's trust-based design and route leaks.
- Zoom growth: from about 10 million to 200 million daily users. Zoom bombing took off because meetings were public or credentials were widely shared; the FBI issued an alert. Mitigations: passwords, waiting room, locking the meeting, restricting screen sharing.
- Zoom Windows client converted UNC paths in chat into links, leaking NTLM hashes to attackers (fixed); ISP port filtering of 445 protects many home users.
- Zoom encryption: not true end-to-end; AES-128 in ECB mode shared by participants; Citizen Lab found keys routed via servers in China. Other issues: Facebook SDK analytics, attendee attention tracking (removed), LinkedIn matching feature (disabled), leaking contacts for same-domain users, publicly findable recordings, and the zWarDial scanner for open meeting IDs. Zoom responded quickly to each.
- Jitsi Meet is recommended as an open-source, WebRTC-based self-hosted alternative; Leo runs his own server. Leo notes public recovery meetings cannot simply be password-protected.
