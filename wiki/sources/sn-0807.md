# Summary

SHAREit's publisher finally released a patch days after Trend Micro's public disclosure. Researchers at the University of Illinois at Chicago showed that browser favicon caches, which survive cache clearing and span private browsing, can store a 32-bit tracking identifier across 32 subdomains; Brave fixed it ahead of others and Firefox was spared only by a caching bug. Brave also fixed a bug where its ad-blocking DNS checks leaked .onion lookups outside Tor. Steve reminds listeners that virtually all commercial email contains tracking pixels. Microsoft's final Solorigate report says attackers viewed some source repositories (small subsets of Azure, Intune and Exchange components) but found no production secrets because policy bans secrets in code. A popular barcode scanner app on Android was sold and turned into adware overnight, a growing pattern. Steve discusses a tester's doubled SSD read speed after a SpinRite Level 3 pass and why SpinRite 6.1 needs trim awareness first. The main topic is Alex Birsan's dependency confusion research: publishing public packages under names of private internal dependencies caused build systems at more than 35 companies to download and run his code.

# Key points

- SHAREit released a fix on February 19, 2021, after 90 days of silence; Trend Micro had not yet verified. A billion-plus installs mean slow update uptake.
- Favicon tracking paper ("Tales of Favicons and Caches", NDSS 2021): identifier stored as the pattern of which subdomain favicons are cached, read back through a redirect chain; survives clearing data and works in incognito. Chrome, Safari, Edge and Brave affected; Brave patched first; Firefox not exploitable only because of a bug where its favicon cache is never read.
- Brave 1.20.108 fixes .onion domains being resolved over normal DNS in its Tor window; fix existed in nightly builds weeks earlier and Steve criticises not treating it as an emergency.
- Email tracking beacons: BBC reported "spy pixels" endemic in commercial email; mitigation is blocking remote images or reading plain text.
- Microsoft's Solorigate wrap-up: no production or customer data access; repository access limited, search terms hunted for secrets; no live credentials found; lessons are Zero Trust (assume breach) and protecting privileged credentials, especially trust delegated between on-premises and cloud.
- Barcode Scanner app (10 million installs) was bought through an intermediary and updated with adware on November 27, 2020, removed from Google Play on January 5, 2021. Steve suggests app-ownership-change notices for updates.
- SSD result: a Crucial M4 128GB went from roughly 260-310 MB/s to about 533 MB/s read after SpinRite Level 3; Steve attributes the gain to refreshing weakly charged cells rather than defragmenting the mapping, but advises waiting for a trim-aware, page-aligned SpinRite 6.1. Trimming itself does not wear the flash.
- Dependency confusion: Alex Birsan published packages on npm, PyPI and RubyGems using internal package names found in public JavaScript, GitHub and forums; his code phoned home with limited data via DNS. Over 35 organizations (including Apple, Microsoft, PayPal, Shopify, Netflix, Yelp, Uber) were affected, with bounties up to $40,000. Root causes include pip's --extra-index-url picking the higher version number across public and private indexes, and JFrog Artifactory virtual repositories using similar logic. Mitigations: use a single index, reserve internal names publicly, pin sources, and see Microsoft's guidance on private feeds.
