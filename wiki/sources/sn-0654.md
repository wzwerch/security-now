# Summary

Steve scrapped his planned memcached follow-up after CTS Labs of Tel Aviv published, with a branded website and whitepaper, 13 claimed vulnerabilities in AMD's Ryzen and EPYC platforms, giving AMD only 24 hours of notice. The flaws (named Ryzenfall, Fallout, MasterKey and Chimera) sit in the ARM-based AMD Secure Processor and in the Promontory chipset designed by Taiwan's ASMedia; Chimera includes a claimed hardware-level backdoor that may need a workaround or recall. Steve found the research credible, withheld exploit details helped, but criticizes the near-zero disclosure window, and says there is nothing users can do yet. Other items: Citizen Lab's finding that ISPs in Turkey, Syria and Egypt used Sandvine PacketLogic devices to redirect non-HTTPS downloads of popular software to spyware (FinFisher, StrongPity) and to inject Monero mining script; Microsoft folding Windows 10 S into an "S mode" for Home, Pro and Enterprise; many cryptomining stories (Check Point finds mining scripts at 42% of its customers, domain-generation algorithms evade ad blockers, miners on Redis, OrientDB and Apache Solr servers, Dofoil/Smoke Loader hitting hundreds of thousands of Windows PCs, a Mac Calendar app trading mining for features, a crypto-mining space heater); no Chinese teams at Pwn2Own; China's CNNVD vulnerability database backdating entries; a court ruling letting the Yahoo breach suit proceed; and McAfee's finding that the Necurs and Gamut botnets send 97% of spam. The memcached update: over 15,000 attacks on 7,100 sites in 10 days, a 51,000x amplification factor, turnkey attack tools, and mitigation by port 11211 throttling.

# Key points

- CTS Labs: four classes of AMD flaws, three in firmware (fixable) and one hardware backdoor (Chimera); proof-of-concept code withheld; attack requires administrative access according to later analyses (not stated in this episode); Steve urges waiting for AMD's response.
- Sandvine PacketLogic: used for on-path redirection when download links were plain HTTP even though the site pages were HTTPS (Avast, CCleaner, Opera, 7-Zip named); Sandvine disputed Citizen Lab's report. Moral: serve downloads over HTTPS and verify signatures.
- Windows 10 S becomes a mode: Store-only apps, switch-off free but returning to S mode may reinstall Windows.
- Cryptojacking tops malware charts; Coinhive, Crypto-Loot and JSECoin lead; the Redis/OrientDB campaign earned almost $1 million in a month; 1,777 Solr servers were infected in nine days.
- Calendar 2 (Mac App Store) offered features in exchange for background mining and backed off after complaints.
- Pwn2Own 2018: categories include virtualization, browsers, enterprise apps (Outlook), servers (Apache, NGINX, OpenSSL, SMB); China's government kept its researchers home.
- Recorded Future: CNNVD altered publication dates of at least 267 vulnerabilities, apparently masking Ministry of State Security vetting.
- Judge Lucy Koh refused to dismiss key claims in the Yahoo breach suit (3 billion accounts affected).
- Spam: Necurs about 60% and Gamut about 37% of Q4 2017 spam.
- Memcached: newer versions disable UDP by default; Cloudflare throttles port 11211; flush-cache commands are impractical as a defense.

# Other segments

- SpinRite story: a dentist's NAS revived and then backed up.
- Brief talk on the US blocking the Broadcom-Qualcomm merger on security grounds.
