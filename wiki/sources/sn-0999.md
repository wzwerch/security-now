# Summary

On the Election Day episode, Steve starts with housekeeping: some listeners were unsubscribed from GRC's mailing list because Outlook pre-fetches the one-click unsubscribe link, so he will add a confirmation page. He then covers Russia's fine against Google (a daily penalty doubling weekly, now around two undecillion rubles), RT's admission that some hosts are AI-generated, and the end of Windows 10 security updates in October 2025, including Microsoft's escalating Extended Security Updates pricing and 0patch's plan to micropatch Windows 10 for at least five more years. A popular Chrome extension, Hide YouTube Shorts, turned malicious after changing owners. Microsoft reports the SVR-linked Midnight Blizzard sending signed .RDP files that map victims' drives and devices to attacker servers. Firefox 132 adds post-quantum TLS key exchange and stricter third-party cookie blocking, and the UK says half its sensitive-site Chinese cameras are removed. A fake-CAPTCHA technique tricks users into pasting a malicious PowerShell command. Steve announces a paid "Plus" and "Pro" DNS Benchmark 2.0, and closes with an editorial on AI, citing Google's Big Sleep agent finding a SQLite memory-safety bug before release.

# Key points

- Mailing list: Outlook-style link scanning triggered the one-click unsubscribe; fix planned; email address changes can now be renamed on the management page; list at about 12,150 subscribers; GRC's site now handles four-digit episode numbers.
- Russia fined Google over blocked state-media YouTube channels, with penalties starting at 100,000 rubles per day in 2020 and doubling weekly (about 2 undecillion rubles); Google's Russian subsidiary is bankrupt, so payment is implausible. RT's editor-in-chief said some RT presenters are AI-generated.
- Windows 10 22H2 receives its last free updates in October 2025; Microsoft's Extended Security Updates cost $30 for consumers for one year and $61, $122, $244 per device for enterprises over three years; Canalys estimates 240 million PCs cannot run Windows 11.
- 0patch plans to adopt Windows 10 22H2 for at least five years with in-memory micropatches (no reboot), including zero-day and "Wontfix" patches such as NTLM coerced-authentication fixes; pricing about 24.95 euros (Pro) or 34.95 euros (Enterprise) per computer per year. Steve endorses it.
- Hide YouTube Shorts (100,000+ users) began sending users' full visited URLs and identifiers to an AWS server after the extension was sold to a new developer; Steve repeats the advice to minimize extensions and use uBlock Origin's element picker instead.
- Midnight Blizzard spear-phishing (since 22 October 2024, thousands of recipients at over 100 organizations): emails carried a signed .RDP file that on opening connects to an attacker's server and bidirectionally maps drives, clipboard, printers, smart cards and authentication features; Steve argues such auto-connecting files should not exist or should be disabled by default.
- Firefox 132: post-quantum key exchange for TLS 1.3, no favicons over HTTP, and Enhanced Tracking Protection Strict now actually blocks third-party cookie access.
- UK government says over 50% of Chinese-made security cameras are removed from sensitive sites, with completion expected by April 2025.
- Fake CAPTCHA/ClickFix-style social engineering: a page places an obfuscated PowerShell command on the clipboard and tells the user to press Win+R, Ctrl+V and Enter, which downloads malware; Steve notes ordinary users would comply.
- GRC's next product: DNS Benchmark 2.0 with a free edition, a $9.95 Plus edition (IPv6, encrypted DNS comparison, filtering checks) and a $19.95 Pro edition (runs as a service, logging, charts, built-in spoofability tests), one-time purchase without subscription; then Beyond Recall (secure wiping) and SpinRite 7. The Benchmark has been downloaded over 9.3 million times. A SpinRite 6.1 demo video was published.
- AI editorial: Steve is bullish on AI as transformative; Google's Big Sleep (successor to Project Naptime, Project Zero with DeepMind) found an exploitable stack buffer underflow in SQLite's development branch, fixed in early October 2024 before release, though Google says a target-specific fuzzer would likely be as effective. Steve predicts AI code verification could eventually reduce vulnerabilities, and advises young listeners that this is a promising field.
