# Summary

The episode opens with the 50th anniversary of the first Unix Programmer's Manual (3 November 1971). Steve then reviews a burst of anti-ransomware progress: the US charged the alleged Kaseya attacker Yaroslav Vasinskyi and REvil affiliate Yevgeniy Polyanin, Europol announced further arrests, Treasury sanctioned the Chatex exchange, the State Department posted large rewards for REvil and DarkSide members, and BlackMatter said it was shutting down. Pwn2Own Austin 2021 paid out over $1 million for 61 new vulnerabilities. A weird Windows 11 failure after 31 October was traced to an expired certificate. Unpatched self-hosted GitLab servers (ExifTool flaw CVE-2021-22205) are being turned into DDoS botnets exceeding 1 Tbps, two more popular npm packages (coa and rc) were hijacked, Android patched a kernel zero-day, Cisco fixed a hard-coded SSH key in Policy Suite, and CISA issued its first Binding Operational Directive with a catalog of known exploited vulnerabilities. After feedback and a story about SpinRite and misreported IRQs, the main topic is research from UC San Diego showing that physical-layer imperfections in Bluetooth Low Energy transmissions can fingerprint individual phones despite rotating addresses.

# Key points

- Law enforcement momentum: DOJ charged Vasinskyi (Kaseya attacks, detained in Poland) and Polyanin (REvil affiliate; $6.1 million in crypto seized); Europol detained seven GandCrab/REvil affiliates; Treasury sanctioned Chatex and linked firms; rewards of $10 million (leaders) and $5 million (affiliates) were offered for REvil, and similar for DarkSide; BlackMatter shut down.
- Pwn2Own Austin 2021 (22 target devices: phones, printers, smart speakers, TVs, routers, NAS): 61 new vulnerabilities, $1,081,250 in prizes. Synacktiv won Master of Pwn with 20 points; DEVCORE, STAR Labs and Pentest Ltd followed. Highlights included the Sonos One, Western Digital NAS devices, Cisco RV340 and an HP printer made to play music. Vendors get 120 days before public disclosure.
- After 31 October, Windows 11 features (touch keyboard, emoji panel, Snipping Tool, S-mode pages) failed because of an expired certificate in their signing chain; a preview update fixed most but not Snipping Tool. Steve explains that Authenticode signatures outlive the signing certificate, but an expired certificate in the chain still breaks validation.
- GitLab CVE-2021-22205 (ExifTool handling of DjVu files, patched in April) lets unauthenticated attackers take over servers; roughly half of about 60,000 internet-exposed servers were still unpatched and were being used in botnets for terabit-scale DDoS (reported by Google's Damian Menscher). Other software using ExifTool may be affected.
- npm packages coa (8.8M weekly downloads) and rc (14.2M) were hijacked via a maintainer's account and delivered a Qakbot-related loader through a post-install script; code was almost identical to the UAParser.js compromise. Steve advises scrutinizing long-dormant packages that suddenly publish an update.
- Android November patches fix 39 flaws including exploited kernel use-after-free CVE-2021-1048; Android has had only six exploited zero-days in 2021 versus 15 in Chrome.
- Cisco Policy Suite shipped with one shared hard-coded SSH key allowing root login (CVSS 9.8); the new installer generates unique keys. Steve questions why Cisco did not audit all products after earlier default-credential cases.
- CISA Binding Operational Directive 22-01 and its known-exploited-vulnerabilities catalog (about 176 older and 100 vulnerabilities from 2021) require federal agencies to patch 2021 entries within two weeks and older ones by May 2022; Steve likes that it prioritizes real-world exploitation over severity scores and gives IT staff leverage.
- UCSD research ("Evaluating Physical-Layer BLE Location Tracking Attacks on Mobile Devices"): inexpensive software-defined radios can learn a device's radio imperfections from its many beacon packets and recognise it later despite address randomisation. It works only under limited conditions (chipset similarity, transmit power and temperature affect reliability), but a case study recognised one iPhone entering and leaving a house among about 30 other devices. Steve suggests vendors could add deliberate noise to defeat it.
- Beacon rates measured: iPhone about 872 advertisements per minute, Windows laptop 864, Pixel 5 510.

# Other segments

- Unix 50th anniversary: the first edition ran on a PDP-11/20 with 8K words and was written in assembler; commentary on Unix's lineage to BSD, FreeBSD and Linux.
- Listener feedback: Excel gaining JavaScript support ("what could possibly go wrong"), GitHub now warns about hidden bidirectional Unicode text (follow-up to Trojan Source), and a joke on RAM use from Apollo to Slack.
- SpinRite: some adapters' BIOSes report the wrong hardware IRQ, causing hangs; Steve fixed this by monitoring all free interrupt lines at once, ahead of the next pre-release.
- Banter about Steve's glasses and the TWiT store.
